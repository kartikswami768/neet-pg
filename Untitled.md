# Comprehensive Pharmacology Notes: Hematology

## 1. Anemias

### 1.1 Iron Toxicity & Iron Overload

#### Pathophysiology & Clinical Manifestations

- **Acute Iron Toxicity**
  - Occurs via free radical formation and membrane lipid peroxidation.
  - Leads directly to mucosal necrosis and cell death.

##### Clinical Stages & Features

- Initially presents with:
  - Severe abdominal pain
  - Vomiting
  - Gastrointestinal bleeding
- Can rapidly progress to:
  - Anion gap metabolic acidosis
  - Multiorgan failure
- Long-term complications may include severe scarring resulting in GI tract obstruction.

> **High-Yield Exam Risk:** Accidental ingestion of adult iron supplements, which often resemble candy, is a major cause of pediatric poisoning mortality.

- **Chronic Iron Overload**
  - Results from:
    - Primary hemochromatosis — hereditary
    - Secondary hemochromatosis — frequent, long-term blood transfusions
      - β-thalassemia major
      - Sickle cell disease

##### Clinical Features

- Arthropathy
- Liver cirrhosis
- Dilated cardiomyopathy
- Diabetes mellitus with hyperpigmented skin — **"bronze diabetes"**
- Hypogonadism

### Pharmacological Management & Chelating Agents

#### Acute Poisoning Protocol

##### Gastric Lavage

- Used to evacuate unabsorbed radiopaque iron tablets from the stomach.
- **Safety rule:** In a comatose patient, gastric lavage must be preceded by endotracheal intubation with a cuffed endotracheal tube to prevent aspiration.

##### Chelation Therapy

- Systemic chelation is indicated in severe toxicity or high serum iron levels.
