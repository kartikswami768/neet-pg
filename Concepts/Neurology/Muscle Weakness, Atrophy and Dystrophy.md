# Weakness, Atrophy, and Dystrophy

## I. Core Fundamentals: Distinguishing Neurological Weakness

Before individual diseases, clinicians must localize the lesion based on clinical signs.

### 1. Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) vs. Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) Signs
* **UMN Lesions:** Characterized by **increased spasticity (tone)**, **hyperreflexia** (increased deep tendon reflexes), and the **Babinski sign** (extensor plantar response).
* **LMN Lesions:** Characterized by **decreased/flaccid tone**, **absent or decreased reflexes**, significant **muscle atrophy**, and **fasciculations** (muscle twitches).
* **Mixed Signs:** Conditions like **Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)** are high-yield for exams because they present with **both UMN and LMN signs**.

### 2. Localization Principles: Alternating Hemiplegias
In brainstem lesions, the "rule of 4s" often applies: **ipsilateral cranial nerve (CN) palsy** paired with **contralateral motor or sensory deficits**.
* **Midbrain:** Typically involves **CN III**.
* **Pons:** Often involves **CN VI or VII**.
* **Medulla:** Lateral medullary lesions cause crossed sensory loss (ipsilateral face, contralateral body).

## II. Brainstem Syndromes (High-Yield Localization)

### 1. Weber Syndrome (Superior Alternating Hemiplegia)
* **Pathology:** Occlusion of paramedian branches of the **Posterior Cerebral Artery (PCA)** affecting the ventromedial midbrain.
* **Structures:** Corticospinal tract and exiting **CN III** fascicles.
* **Presentation:** Ipsilateral **"down-and-out" eye** with dilated pupil + **contralateral spastic hemiplegia**.

### 2. Benedict Syndrome
* **Pathology:** Midbrain tegmentum lesion (more dorsal than Weber).
* **Structures:** CN III fascicles, red nucleus, and dentatorubral tract.
* **Presentation:** Ipsilateral CN III palsy + **contralateral tremor, chorea, and ataxia**.

### 3. Wallenberg Syndrome (Lateral Medullary Syndrome)
* **Pathology:** Occlusion of the **Posterior Inferior Cerebellar Artery (PICA)** or vertebral artery.
* **Presentation:** Vertigo, hoarseness, dysphagia, **ipsilateral Horner’s syndrome**, and **crossed sensory loss** (pain/temperature).

## III. Vascular Weakness: Stroke & Hemorrhage

### 1. Ischemic Stroke Territories
* **MCA (Middle Cerebral Artery):** Contralateral **face and arm weakness**; aphasia if the dominant hemisphere is involved.
* **ACA (Anterior Cerebral Artery):** Contralateral **leg weakness** and sensory deficits.
* **Lacunar Stroke:** Small vessel disease (lipohyalinosis) often causing **pure motor stroke** (internal capsule) or **ataxic hemiparesis**.

### 2. Intracranial Hemorrhages
* **Epidural Hematoma (EDH):** Usually a **middle meningeal artery** tear from a pterion fracture. Presents with a **"lucid interval"** and a **biconvex (lens-shaped)** mass on CT that doesn't cross sutures.
* **Subdural Hematoma (SDH):** Tearing of **bridging veins** (common in elderly/atrophy). Presents as a **crescent-shaped** mass on CT that can cross suture lines.
* **Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH):** Often from **berry aneurysm** rupture. Presents with **"thunderclap headache"**, meningeal signs (**Kernig’s/Brudzinski’s**), and **xanthochromia** on lumbar puncture.

## IV. Motor Neuron Diseases (MND)

### 1. Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS)
* **Key Feature:** Progressive death of **both upper and lower motor neurons**.
* **Genetics:** Linked to **SOD1, C9orf72, TDP-43, and FUS** mutations causing protein aggregation.
* **Presentation:** Asymmetric hand weakness (dropping objects), cramping, and eventual respiratory failure.

### 2. Spinal Muscular Atrophy (SMA)
* **Key Feature:** **LMN signs only**; autosomal recessive loss-of-function mutation in the **SMN1 gene**.
* **Pathology:** Death of **alpha motor neurons** in the anterior horn of the spinal cord.
* **Biopsy:** Large zones of **severely atrophic myofibers** juxtaposed with normal/hypertrophied innervated fibers.

---

## V. Demyelinating Disorders

### 1. Multiple Sclerosis (MS)
* **Pathology:** Autoimmune attack on **CNS oligodendrocytes** (Type IV Hypersensitivity).
* **Classic Signs:** **Charcot’s triad** (nystagmus, intention tremor, scanning speech) and **Lhermitte’s sign** (electric shock sensation with neck flexion).
* **Imaging:** **Dawson’s fingers** (periventricular plaques) on sagittal MRI.

### 2. Guillain–Barré Syndrome (GBS)
* **Pathology:** Acute autoimmune destruction of **PNS myelin** often following ***Campylobacter jejuni*** infection.
* **Presentation:** **Bilateral, flaccid, ascending weakness** with areflexia.
* **Lab Point:** **Albuminocytologic dissociation** in CSF (high protein, normal cell count).

---

## VI. Congenital Myopathies (Dystrophinopathies)

These are **X-linked recessive** disorders caused by mutations in the **dystrophin gene**, which normally stabilizes the sarcolemma.

* **Duchenne Muscular Dystrophy (DMD):** Severe form with **absent dystrophin** (nonsense/frameshift mutation). Signs include **Gowers' sign**, **calf pseudohypertrophy**, and onset before age 5.
* **Becker's Muscular Dystrophy (BMD):** Milder form with **"badly made" (truncated) dystrophin** (missense mutation); later onset (10–20 years).

---

## VII. Spinal Cord & Peripheral Nerve Conditions

### 1. Brown–Séquard Syndrome
* **Mechanism:** Hemisection of the spinal cord.
* **Deficits:** **Ipsilateral UMN paralysis** and loss of proprioception, with **contralateral loss of pain/temperature** 1-2 levels below the lesion.

### 2. Cauda Equina Syndrome
* **Mechanism:** Compression of multiple lumbosacral nerve roots.
* **Red Flags:** Severe back pain, **saddle anesthesia**, and **loss of bowel/bladder control**.

### 3. Syringomyelia
* **Mechanism:** Fluid-filled cyst (**syrinx**) in the spinal cord central canal.
* **Classic Sign:** **"Suspended sensory level"** (cape-like distribution of pain/temp loss) and progressive motor weakness.

### 4. Bell’s Palsy
* **Mechanism:** **LMN weakness** of the facial nerve (**CN VII**).
* **Presentation:** Unilateral facial drooping (cannot wrinkle forehead), **hyperacusis** (sensitivity to sound), and loss of taste on the anterior 2/3 of the tongue.

### 5. Charcot–Marie–Tooth Disease (CMT)
* **Mechanism:** Hereditary PNS disorder (mutations in **PMP22, MPZ, MFN2**).
* **Presentation:** Progressive distal muscle atrophy (**"stork legs"**), **pes cavus** (high arches), and hammer toes.
