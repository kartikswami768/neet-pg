# HYPERNATREMIA

> [!info] Definition & Significance
> Hypernatremia = plasma Na⁺ > 145 mM.
> - Less common than hyponatremia.
> - Mortality rate: **40–60%**, mostly due to underlying disease severity.
> - Usually results from **combined water & electrolyte deficit** with water loss > Na⁺ loss.
> - Less commonly: iatrogenic/ingestion of excess Na⁺ (e.g., IV hypertonic NaCl, NaHCO₃).

---

## Etiology

### High-Risk Groups
- **Elderly** with reduced thirst and/or diminished fluid access → highest risk.
- Rare: central defect in hypothalamic osmoreceptor function → **adipsic DI** (↓thirst + ↓AVP).
  - Causes: primary/metastatic tumor, occlusion/ligation of anterior communicating artery, trauma, hydrocephalus, inflammation.

### Water Loss Routes

#### Nonrenal Losses
- **Insensible losses** ↑ with:
  - Fever, exercise, heat exposure, severe burns, mechanical ventilation.
- **Diarrhea** (most common GI cause):
  - **Osmotic diarrhea & viral gastroenteritis** → stool Na⁺ + K⁺ < 100 mM → water loss → hypernatremia.
  - **Secretory diarrhea** → isotonic stool → hypovolemia ± hypovolemic hyponatremia (not hypernatremia).

#### Renal Losses

**1. Osmotic Diuresis**
- Causes: hyperglycemia, excess urea, postobstructive diuresis, mannitol.
- Feature: ↑ urinary solute excretion & ↑ urine osmolality.

**2. Water Diuresis**
- Occurs in **central DI** or **nephrogenic DI (NDI)**.

---

## Nephrogenic Diabetes Insipidus (NDI)

Renal resistance to AVP – can be **partial** or **complete**.

### Genetic Causes
| Defect | Inheritance |
|--------|-------------|
| Loss-of-function mutations in **X-linked V₂ receptor** | X-linked |
| Mutations in **aquaporin-2 water channel** | Autosomal recessive / dominant |
| Recessive deficiency of **aquaporin-1** | Autosomal recessive (milder concentrating defect) |

### Acquired Causes
- **Hypercalcemia** → calcium signals via Ca²⁺-sensing receptor → ↓ Na⁺/K⁺/Cl⁻ transport in TALH & ↓ water transport in principal cells → ↓ concentrating ability.
- **Hypokalemia** → 
	- inhibits renal response to AVP & ↓ aquaporin-2 expression.
	- The medullary osmotic gradient is reduced because the Na-K-2Cl cotransporter works poorly decreasing the effectiveness of water absorption by countercurrent mechanism
- **Drugs**:
  - ==Lithium==, ifosfamide, several antivirals.
  - **Lithium mechanism**:
    - Inhibits renal **GSK3** (glycogen synthase kinase-3) – required for principal cell response to AVP.
    - Lithium enters principal cells via **ENaC** (amiloride-sensitive Na⁺ channel).
    - → **Amiloride** co-therapy can mitigate lithium‑associated NDI.
    - Prolonged lithium → chronic tubulointerstitial scarring & CKD → persistent NDI even after stopping drug (reduced amiloride benefit).

---

## Gestational DI
- Rare complication of late-term pregnancy.
- ↑ activity of circulating placental protease (**vasopressinase**) → ↓ circulating AVP → polyuria + hypernatremia.
- **Treatment**: **DDAVP** (Synthetic AVP) – resistant to vasopressinase.

---

## Clinical Features

### Pathophysiology
- ↑ ECF osmolality → osmotic gradient → water efflux from ICF → **cellular shrinkage**.

### Neurologic Symptoms
- Most common: **altered mental status** (confusion → lethargy → coma).
- **Acute hypernatremia**: sudden brain cell shrinkage →
  - Parenchymal/subarachnoid hemorrhages, subdural hematomas (mainly pediatric/neonatal).
  - Rare: osmotic demyelination.
- **Muscle**: osmotic damage → **hypernatremic rhabdomyolysis**.

### Adaptation to Chronic Hypernatremia (>48 h)
- Brain cells activate transporters → influx/accumulation of **organic osmolytes**:
  - Creatine, betaine, glutamate, myoinositol, taurine.
- → ↑ ICF water → normalization of brain volume.
- → Patients with chronic hypernatremia less likely to develop severe neurologic compromise.

### Correction Risk
- Cellular adaptation predisposes **pediatric patients (especially infants)** to **cerebral edema & seizures** during overly rapid hydration.
  - Overcorrection = plasma Na⁺ correction > 10 mM/d.
- In **critically ill adults**, recent evidence shows **no higher risk** of mortality, seizure, altered consciousness, or cerebral edema with rapid correction.
  - **Prudent**: restrict correction to < 10 mM/d.
  - If rate exceeded, **do not reinduce hypernatremia**.

## Diagnostic Approach

### History
- Ask about: thirst, polyuria, extrarenal water loss (e.g., diarrhea).

### Physical Exam
- Detailed neurologic exam.
- Assess ECFV:
  - Hypovolemia → ↓ JVP, orthostasis.
- Document **daily fluid intake** & **daily urine output** (critical).

### Laboratory Workup
- Serum osmolality
- Urine osmolality
- Urine electrolytes

> [!example] Expected Response to Hypernatremia (serum osmolality >295 mOsm/kg)
> - ↑ AVP secretion
> - Urine volume < 500 mL/d
> - Urine osmolality > 800 mOsm/kg
>
> If this response is present → **extrarenal water loss** is responsible.

### Polyuric Hypernatremia

#### Osmotic Diuresis
- Daily solute excretion > 750–1000 mOsm/d (>15 mOsm/kg body water/d).
- Excessive excretion of Na⁺-Cl⁻, glucose, and/or urea.

#### Water Diuresis
- More common in polyuric hypernatremia.
- Excessive excretion of **hypotonic, dilute urine**.

## Differentiating Central vs. Nephrogenic DI

Requires:
- Measurement of **urine osmolality response to DDAVP**
- Measurement of **circulating AVP** (or **copeptin**) in setting of hypertonicity.

> [!warning] Water Deprivation Test
> **Absolutely contraindicated** in hypernatremic patients (risk of worsening hypernatremia).
> - Used only for polyuric patients **without** baseline hypernatremia.
> - “Indirect water deprivation test”: hypertonic saline infusion → measure copeptin.
>   - ↑ copeptin → **polydipsia** (not central DI).

### Test Interpretation in Hypernatremic Patient

| Condition | Serum AVP / Copeptin | Urine Osmolality Response to DDAVP |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **Nephrogenic DI (NDI)** | High | No response (↑ <50% or <150 mOsm/kg from baseline) |
| **Central DI** | Low | Responds (↑ >50%) |
| **Partial response** (↑ >50% but <800 mOsm/kg) | Distinguishes cause | NDI vs Central DI based on AVP level |

> [!note] Pregnancy
> AVP assays must be drawn in tubes containing protease inhibitor **1,10‑phenanthroline** to prevent in vitro degradation by placental vasopressinase.

### Quantifying Ongoing Losses
- For renal water loss:
  - Calculate **electrolyte-free water clearance** daily.
  - Also calculate **baseline water deficit**.
- Requires:
  - Daily urine electrolytes
  - Accurate daily urine volume

## TREATMENT

> [!tip] General Principles
> 1. Withdraw/correct underlying cause (drugs, hyperglycemia, hypercalcemia, hypokalemia, diarrhea).
> 2. Correct hypernatremia **slowly** to avoid cerebral edema.
> 3. Replace calculated free water deficit over **48 h**.
> 4. Plasma Na⁺ correction **≤ 10 mM/d**.
>    - May take >48 h if Na⁺ >160 mM.
> 5. **Exception**: acute hypernatremia (<48 h) due to sodium loading → can correct rapidly at **1 mM/h**.

### Route of Water Administration
- **Preferred**: by mouth or nasogastric tube → most direct free water (electrolyte-free).
- **Alternative**: IV dextrose solutions (e.g., **D₅W**).
  - Monitor blood glucose for hyperglycemia.
- **Hypotonic saline** (¼ or ½ normal saline) may be used initially depending on:
  - History, BP, clinical volume status.
- **Normal saline** usually inappropriate unless:
  - Very severe hypernatremia (relative hypotonicity compared to plasma)
  - Frank hypotension.

### Ongoing Losses
- Calculate **urinary electrolyte-free water clearance** daily.
- Replenish daily ongoing free water loss in patients with NDI or central DI.

## Specific Therapies

| Condition | Therapy | Notes |
|-----------|---------|-------|
| **Central DI** | DDAVP (IV, intranasal, oral) | Effective |
| **NDI due to lithium** | **Amiloride** 2.5–10 mg/d | Inhibits ENaC → ↓ lithium entry into principal cells; most patients can simply ↑ water intake |
| **NDI (general)** | **Thiazides** | Induce hypovolemia → ↑ proximal tubular water reabsorption |
| **NDI (general)** | **NSAIDs** (occasionally) | ↓ negative effect of intrarenal prostaglandins on concentrating mechanisms; risks: GI/renal toxicity |

> [!warning] Important Restriction
> **Thiazides, amiloride, NSAIDs** are only for **chronic management** of polyuria from NDI.
> - **No role in acute management** of hypernatremia.
> - Acute management focuses on replacing free water deficits and ongoing free water loss.
