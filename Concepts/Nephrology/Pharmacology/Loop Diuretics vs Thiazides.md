# Loop Diuretics vs Thiazides — Bartter & Gitelman

## 1. Big Picture

| | **Loop diuretics** | **Thiazides** | **Bartter syndrome** | **Gitelman syndrome** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Main site | **TAL** | **Early DCT** | **TAL** | **DCT** |
| Main transporter | **NKCC2** | **NCC** | NKCC2 / related TAL proteins | NCC |
| Nature | Drug inhibition | Drug inhibition | Genetic defect | Genetic defect |
| NaCl reabsorption | ↓↓↓ | ↓↓ | ↓↓↓ | ↓↓ |
| Volume | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Renin | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ |
| Aldosterone | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ |
| K⁺ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| H⁺ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Acid-base | **Metabolic alkalosis** | **Metabolic alkalosis** | **Metabolic alkalosis** | **Metabolic alkalosis** |
| **Urinary Ca²⁺** | **↑↑** | **↓↓** | **↑↑** | **↓↓** |
| **Mg²⁺** | ↓ | ↓ | Usually normal/mild ↓ | **↓↓↓** |
| BP | ↓ | ↓ | Low/normal | Low/normal |

> **Core relationship**
>
> **Loop → NKCC2 → TAL → Bartter**
>
> **Thiazide → NCC → DCT → Gitelman**

---

## 2. First understand why BOTH cause hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis

Both drugs/syndromes ultimately produce:

**NaCl loss → volume contraction → RAAS activation → ↑ distal Na⁺ reabsorption → ↑ K⁺ and H⁺ secretion**

### Step 1 — NaCl reabsorption is blocked

**Loop:**

`↓ NKCC2`

**Thiazide:**

`↓ NCC`

↓

More Na⁺ reaches the collecting duct.

### Step 2 — Collecting duct reabsorbs the extra Na⁺

Increased distal Na⁺ delivery stimulates **ENaC-mediated Na⁺ reabsorption**.

ENaC is electrogenic:

`Na⁺ enters principal cell → lumen becomes more negative`

↓

This favors:

- **K⁺ secretion → hypokalemia**
- **H⁺ secretion → metabolic alkalosis**

### Step 3 — Volume contraction activates RAAS

NaCl loss

↓

↓ ECF volume

↓

↑ Renin → ↑ Ang II → ↑ Aldosterone

↓

Further ↑ distal Na⁺ reabsorption

↓

Further ↑ K⁺ and H⁺ secretion

### Final result

> **Loop / Thiazide → NaCl loss → volume contraction → RAAS ↑ → distal Na⁺ reabsorption ↑ → K⁺ + H⁺ secretion ↑ → hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis**

The same broad mechanism operates in **Bartter** and **Gitelman**.

---

## 3. Why calcium behaves OPPOSITELY

This is one of the most important distinctions.

### LOOP → ↑ urinary Ca²⁺

The **TAL** normally reabsorbs NaCl through **NKCC2**:

`Na⁺ + K⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → cell`

K⁺ is recycled back into the tubular lumen through **ROMK**.

This generates a:

**Lumen-positive electrical potential**

↓

The positive lumen drives **paracellular reabsorption of Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺**.

Therefore:

`NKCC2 inhibition`
→ ↓ K⁺ recycling
→ ↓ lumen-positive potential
→ ↓ paracellular Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺ reabsorption
→ **↑ urinary Ca²⁺ + Mg²⁺**

### Therefore

> **Loop diuretics → hypercalciuria**

And because Bartter syndrome impairs TAL function:

> **Bartter → hypercalciuria**

---

## 4. Why thiazides cause HYPOcalciuria

Thiazides inhibit **NCC in the DCT**.

`↓ NCC`

↓

↓ intracellular Na⁺

↓

The basolateral **Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger (NCX1)** can extrude more Ca²⁺ from the cell.

↓

Intracellular Ca²⁺ decreases.

↓

This promotes Ca²⁺ entry from the tubular lumen through **TRPV5**.

↓

**Ca²⁺ reabsorption ↑**

↓

**Urinary Ca²⁺ ↓**

### Therefore

> **Thiazides → hypocalciuria**

And because Gitelman syndrome is due to defective **NCC**:

> **Gitelman → hypocalciuria**

### The high-yield contrast

| | **Loop** | **Thiazide** |
|---|---:|---:|
| Site | TAL | DCT |
| Transporter | NKCC2 | NCC |
| Ca²⁺ reabsorption | ↓ | ↑ |
| Urinary Ca²⁺ | **↑** | **↓** |

> **Loop loses Ca²⁺. Thiazide retains Ca²⁺.**

---

## 5. Magnesium: the slightly annoying part

### Loop diuretics

The TAL is an important site of Mg²⁺ reabsorption.

NKCC2 inhibition

↓

↓ lumen-positive potential

↓

↓ paracellular Mg²⁺ reabsorption

↓

**↑ Mg²⁺ urinary loss**

→ **Hypomagnesemia**

---

### Thiazides

Thiazides can also cause:

**↑ renal Mg²⁺ wasting → hypomagnesemia**

However, the finding is particularly prominent in **Gitelman syndrome**.

### Gitelman

Defective NCC

↓

Chronic DCT dysfunction

↓

Marked renal Mg²⁺ wasting

↓

**Severe hypomagnesemia**

### Therefore

> **Hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis + hypomagnesemia + hypocalciuria = Gitelman syndrome**

This combination is highly characteristic.

# 8. Adverse Effects: Loop vs Thiazide

## LOOP DIURETICS

### Major adverse effects

| Adverse effect                | Mechanism / explanation                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hypokalemia**               | ↑ distal Na⁺ delivery + RAAS activation                                      |
| **Metabolic alkalosis**       | ↑ H⁺ secretion                                                               |
| **Hypomagnesemia**            | ↓ TAL Mg²⁺ reabsorption                                                      |
| **Hypercalciuria**            | ↓ lumen-positive potential                                                   |
| **Ototoxicity**               | Particularly high-dose IV therapy; interference with inner-ear ion transport |
| **Hyperuricemia**             | Volume contraction + altered urate handling                                  |
| **Dehydration / hypotension** | Powerful natriuresis                                                         |
| **Hyponatremia**              | Excessive Na⁺ loss                                                           |
| **Interstitial nephritis**    | Can occur with some loop agents                                              |

### Classic associations

> **Loop = Ototoxicity + hypercalciuria**

---

# 9. THIAZIDES

### Major adverse effects

| Adverse effect | Mechanism / explanation |
|---|---|
| **Hypokalemia** | ↑ distal Na⁺ delivery + RAAS activation |
| **Metabolic alkalosis** | ↑ H⁺ secretion |
| **Hyponatremia** | Impaired urinary dilution |
| **Hypomagnesemia** | Renal Mg²⁺ wasting |
| **Hypocalciuria** | ↑ distal Ca²⁺ reabsorption |
| **Hyperuricemia** | ↓ urate excretion |
| **Hyperglycemia** | Impaired insulin secretion/sensitivity; K⁺ depletion may contribute |
| **Hyperlipidemia** | Usually modest |
| **Hypercalcemia** | Usually mild; due to ↓ urinary Ca²⁺ excretion |
| **Sulfonamide hypersensitivity** | Relevant to sulfonamide-containing thiazides |
| **Photosensitivity** | Can occur with thiazides |

### Classic thiazide associations

> **Thiazide → hyponatremia + hypokalemia + hypomagnesemia + hyperuricemia + hyperglycemia + hypercalcemia**

### Easy memory

**Thiazides:**

**Na⁺ ↓**

**K⁺ ↓**

**Mg²⁺ ↓**

**Ca²⁺ ↑ in blood / ↓ in urine**

**Uric acid ↑**

**Glucose ↑**

# 10. Why thiazides cause hyponatremia more commonly

This becomes easier if you distinguish:

### TAL → concentrates urine

The TAL is **impermeable to water** and contributes to the medullary osmotic gradient.

Loop diuretics inhibit NKCC2.

↓

Medullary concentration gradient decreases.

↓

**Urinary concentrating ability decreases.**

---

### DCT → dilutes urine

The DCT also reabsorbs NaCl without water.

Therefore, the DCT contributes importantly to the generation of **dilute urine**.

Thiazides inhibit NCC.

↓

Ability to excrete electrolyte-free water decreases.

↓

**Impaired urinary dilution**

↓

Water is retained relative to Na⁺.

↓

**Hyponatremia**

### Important comparison

| | **Loop** | **Thiazide** |
|---|---|---|
| Main effect on medullary gradient | **↓** | Relatively preserved |
| Urinary concentrating ability | **↓** | Relatively preserved |
| Urinary dilution | ↓ | **↓↓** |
| Hyponatremia | Less characteristic | **More common** |

> **Loop → concentration impaired**
>
> **Thiazide → dilution impaired**

# 12. The conceptual trick for exams

Start with:

## Hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis

Then ask:

### Is BP high or low/normal?

**High BP**

→ Think of **mineralocorticoid excess / ENaC activation** (like Hyperaldosteronism or Liddle Syndrome)

**Low or normal BP**

→ Think of **renal salt wasting**

---

### If renal salt wasting, check urinary Ca²⁺

**Hypercalciuria**

→ **Bartter / Loop**

**Hypocalciuria**

→ **Gitelman / Thiazide**

---

### Then use Mg²⁺ to strengthen the diagnosis

**Hypomagnesemia + hypocalciuria**

→ **Gitelman**

**Hypercalciuria**

→ **Bartter**

---

> **LOOP → TAL → NKCC2 → Ca²⁺ loss → Hypercalciuria → BARTTER**

> **THIAZIDE → DCT → NCC → Ca²⁺ retention + Mg²⁺ loss → Hypocalciuria + Hypomagnesemia → GITELMAN**

### The four-way pairing

**Loop : Bartter :: Thiazide : Gitelman**

**TAL : DCT :: NKCC2 : NCC**

**Hypercalciuria : Hypocalciuria**
