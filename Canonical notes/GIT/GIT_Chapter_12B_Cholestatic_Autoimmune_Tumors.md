# Chapter 12B: Cholestatic & Autoimmune Liver Diseases and Hepatic Tumors

## 12.3 Cholestatic & Autoimmune Liver Diseases

Cholestasis refers to impairment of bile formation or flow, leading to systemic retention of bilirubin, bile salts, and cholesterol.

### A. Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC) vs. Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)

| Feature | Primary biliary cholangitis (PBC) | Primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary ducts involved | Small intrahepatic interlobular bile ducts | Intrahepatic and extrahepatic bile ducts |
| Typical association | Middle-aged women; autoimmune disease associations | Inflammatory bowel disease, especially ulcerative colitis |
| Serology | Antimitochondrial antibody (AMA) commonly positive | p-ANCA may be positive but is not diagnostic |
| Imaging / histology | Florid duct lesions with destructive cholangitis | Multifocal strictures and dilatations (“beading”); periductal concentric fibrosis |
| Important complications | Progressive cholestasis, cirrhosis and metabolic bone disease | Cholangitis, cirrhosis and increased cholangiocarcinoma risk |
| Feature | Primary Biliary Cholangitis (PBC) | Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Target Anatomical Site** | **Intrahepatic small interlobular bile ducts** strictly | **Both Intrahepatic and Extrahepatic** large and medium bile ducts |
| **Demographics** | Middle-aged females ($9:1$ female-to-male ratio; peak age 40–60) | Young and middle-aged males ($2:1$ male-to-female ratio; peak age 30–50) |
| **Associated Conditions** | Other autoimmune diseases (Sjögren syndrome $70\%$, Hashimoto thyroiditis, Systemic Sclerosis) | **Ulcerative Colitis** ($70\%$ of PSC patients have underlying UC) |
| **Serological Markers** | **Anti-Mitochondrial Antibodies (AMA)** positive in $>95\%$ (targets pyruvate dehydrogenase E2 unit); Elevated IgM | **p-ANCA** positive ($65–80\%$); Anti-smooth muscle antibodies (ASMA); Elevated IgG |
| **Histopathology** | Granulomatous destruction of interlobular ducts (**"Florid Duct Lesions"**), ductopenia, portal inflammation | Concentric obliteration of duct lumen by segmental fibrotic scarring (**"Onion-Skinning" Fibrosis**) |
| **Radiological Findings (MRCP / ERCP)** | Normal / Non-diagnostic biliary tree | **"Beaded Appearance"** (alternating segmental strictures and saccular dilations of extra- and intra-hepatic ducts) |
| **Malignancy Risk** | Low increase in HCC risk | **High risk of Cholangiocarcinoma** ($10–15\%$) and Colorectal Carcinoma |
| **First-Line Pharmacotherapy** | **Ursodeoxycholic Acid (UDCA)** (slows progression); Obeticholic acid | No curative medical therapy; UDCA provides symptomatic relief; **Liver Transplantation** is definitive |

---

### B. Autoimmune Hepatitis (AIH)
* **Definition**: Chronic, progressive inflammatory liver disease characterized by autoimmune-mediated hepatocyte destruction.

* **Subtypes & Serology**:
  * **Type 1 AIH** ($80\%$): Classical form. Predominantly females (15–40 years). Characterized by **Anti-Nuclear Antibodies (ANA)** and **Anti-Smooth Muscle Antibodies (ASMA)** positive; elevated serum IgG.
  * **Type 2 AIH**: Children/adolescents. Characterized by **Anti-Liver Kidney Microsome-1 (anti-LKM1)** antibodies and Anti-Liver Cytosol-1 (anti-LC1) antibodies.

* **Histopathology**:
  * **Interface Hepatitis** (dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate invading across portal tract boundaries into the periportal parenchyma).
  * **Abundant Plasma Cells** within the portal and periportal infiltrate.
  * Hepatocyte **Rosettes** (regenerative hepatocytes arranged in pseudo-glandular rings).

* **Treatment**: Immunosuppression with **Systemic Corticosteroids** (Prednisone) + **Azathioprine**.

## 12.4 Hepatic Tumors

### A. Benign Hepatic Tumors
1. **Cavernous Hemangioma**:
   * Most common benign tumor of the liver.
   * *Histology*: Large, blood-filled vascular channels lined by a single layer of benign endothelium supported by dense fibrous stroma.
   * *Clinical Caution*: **Percutaneous needle biopsy is CONTRAINDICATED** due to high risk of severe retroperitoneal hemorrhage.

2. **Hepatic Adenoma (Hepatocellular Adenoma)**:
   * Benign epithelial neoplasm occurring predominantly in young females with a history of **Oral Contraceptive Pill (OCP) use** or anabolic steroid use.
   * *Subtypes*: *HNF1A*-inactivated, $\beta$-catenin-activated (highest malignant transformation risk), and Inflammatory.
   * *Clinical Pearl*: High risk of **spontaneous intra-tumoral or intraperitoneal rupture** during pregnancy, causing severe life-threatening shock. Subcapsular tumors $>5\text{ cm}$ require surgical resection.

3. **Focal Nodular Hyperplasia (FNH)**:
   * Non-neoplastic hyperplastic response to a localized vascular malformation.
   * *Radiological/Pathological Landmark*: Well-circumscribed lesion containing a characteristic **Central Stellate Scar** with radiating fibrous septa on CT/MRI.

---

### B. Malignant Hepatic Tumors

| Feature | Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) | Cholangiocarcinoma |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origin | Hepatocytes | Biliary epithelium |
| Major risk associations | Cirrhosis, chronic HBV/HCV, aflatoxin exposure and metabolic liver disease | PSC, choledochal cysts and liver-fluke infection in endemic regions |
| Morphology | Trabecular or pseudoglandular growth patterns | Adenocarcinoma with desmoplastic stroma |
| Markers / imaging clues | AFP may be elevated; arterial phase hyperenhancement with washout in appropriate at-risk patients | CA 19-9 may be elevated; imaging depends on intrahepatic/perihilar/distal subtype |
#### 1. Hepatocellular Carcinoma (HCC)
* **Epidemiology & Risk Factors**:
  * **Chronic Viral Hepatitis**: **HBV** (can cause HCC *without prior cirrhosis* via integration of HBx protein into host genome) and **HCV** (causes HCC almost exclusively via intermediate cirrhosis).
  * **Cirrhosis of any etiology** ($80–90\%$ of HCCs arise in cirrhotic livers).
  * **Environmental Toxins**: **Aflatoxin $\text{B}_1$** (produced by *Aspergillus flavus* contaminating stored grains/peanuts; induces a specific **$TP53$ codon 249 mutation**).
  * Metabolic: Hemochromatosis, MASH/NAFLD, $\alpha_1$-AT deficiency.

* **Gross & Microscopic Morphology**:
  * *Gross*: Unifocal large mass, multifocal nodules, or diffusely infiltrative. Propensity for **vascular invasion** into portal vein or IVC, extending to right atrium.
  * *Microbiology*: Malignant cells resemble hepatocytes, arranged in **trabecular**, cord-like, or pseudo-glandular patterns; produces **intracellular bile** (pathognomonic).

* **Diagnostic Workup & Imaging**:
  * **Serum Tumor Marker**: **Alpha-Fetoprotein (AFP)** ($>400\text{ ng/mL}$ is highly specific).
  * **Multiphasic Contrast-Enhanced CT / MRI** (*LIRADS System*): Pathognomonic vascular signature: **Early intense Arterial Phase Hyperenhancement** followed by **Portal Venous / Delayed Phase "Washout"** and a pseudocapsule. *Biopsy is not strictly required if imaging signature is definitive in a cirrhotic patient*.

* **Management Options**:
  * *Curative*: Surgical resection (non-cirrhotic), **Liver Transplantation** (Milan Criteria: single lesion $\le 5\text{ cm}$ or up to 3 lesions $\le 3\text{ cm}$ without vascular invasion), Radiofrequency Ablation (RFA).
  * *Palliative / Locoregional*: Transarterial Chemoembolization (**TACE**), Transarterial Radioembolization (TARE).
  * *Systemic*: Multikinase inhibitors (**Sorafenib**, Lenvatinib) or Combination Immunotherapy (**Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab**).

---

#### 2. Cholangiocarcinoma & Other Malignancies
* **Cholangiocarcinoma**:
  * Adenocarcinoma arising from intrahepatic or extrahepatic bile duct epithelium.
  * *Locations*: Extrahepatic ($80–90\%$, including **Klatskin Tumors** at the confluence of right and left hepatic ducts) vs. Intrahepatic.
  * *Risk Factors*: **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis (PSC)**, Choledochal cysts, **Liver Fluke infections** (*Clonorchis sinensis*, *Opisthorchis viverrini*), Thorotrast exposure.
  * *Histology*: Mucin-producing glandular adenocarcinoma embedded in a dense, scirrhous **desmoplastic stroma**.

* **Hepatic Angiosarcoma**:
  * Highly aggressive endothelial malignant tumor.
  * Strongly linked to occupational/environmental exposures: **Vinyl Chloride monomer** (rubber industry), **Thorotrast** (historical contrast), **Arsenic**.
  * Tumor cells express endothelial markers: **CD31** and **Factor VIII-related antigen**.

* **Metastatic Liver Disease**:
  * **Far more common than primary liver tumors** (20:1 ratio).
  * Primary sources: **Colon**, Stomach, Pancreas, Breast, Lung.
  * *Gross*: Multiple, well-circumscribed nodules with central necrosis (**"umbilication"**).
