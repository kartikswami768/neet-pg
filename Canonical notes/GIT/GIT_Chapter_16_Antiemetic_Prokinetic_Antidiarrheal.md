# Chapter 16: Antiemetic, Prokinetic & Antidiarrheal Agents

## 16.1 Antiemetic Pharmacology

The vomiting center (Nucleus Tractus Solitarius - NTS) in the medulla receives emetic inputs from four major pathways:
1. **Chemoreceptor Trigger Zone (CTZ)** (Area postrema in floor of 4th ventricle, outside BBB): Expresses $D_2$, $5\text{-HT}_3$, $\text{NK}_1$, and opioid receptors.
2. **Vestibular System**: Expresses $M_1$ muscarinic and $H_1$ histaminic receptors.
3. **Vagal & Sympathetic GI Afferents**: Express $5\text{-HT}_3$ receptors (triggered by mucosal serotonin release from enterochromaffin cells).
4. **CNS / Cortical Structures**: Sensory/emotional input.

- Cortical Structures (Memory, Fear, Anticipation)
- Chemoreceptor Trigger Zone (CTZ) < > Vagal GI Afferents (5-HT3)
- (Outside BBB: D2, 5-HT3, NK1) (Enterochromaffin Cell Damage)
- MEDULLARY VOMITING CENTER (NTS)
- Vestibular System (H1, M1) (Motion Sickness)

### Major Antiemetic Classes

#### 1. $5\text{-HT}_3$ Receptor Antagonists (Ondansetron, Granisetron, Palonosetron)
* **Mechanism**: Selective competitive blockade of peripheral vagal $5\text{-HT}_3$ receptors on intestinal primary afferent nerves and central $5\text{-HT}_3$ receptors in the CTZ and NTS.
* **Clinical Utility**: First-line for **Chemotherapy-Induced Nausea and Vomiting (CINV)**, Post-Operative Nausea and Vomiting (PONV), and Radiation-Induced Emesis. *Ineffective in motion sickness.*
* **Adverse Effects**: Headache, constipation, dizziness, and **dose-dependent QTc Interval Prolongation** (presents risk of Torsades de Pointes; IV Ondansetron capped at $16\text{ mg}$ single dose).

#### 2. Dopamine $D_2$ Receptor Antagonists
* **Agents**: Metoclopramide, Domperidone, Chlorpromazine, Prochlorperazine, Haloperidol.
* **Mechanism**: Block $D_2$ receptors in the CTZ.
* **Metoclopramide**: Crosses blood-brain barrier.
  * *Adverse Effects*: Extrapyramidal Motor Symptoms (acute dystonic reactions, parkinsonism, akathisia), **Tardive Dyskinesia** (Black Box Warning for continuous use $>12$ weeks), and hyperprolactinemia (galactorrhea, gynecomastia, amenorrhea).
* **Domperidone**: Does **not** readily cross the blood-brain barrier (peripheral $D_2$ antagonist at CTZ); minimal extrapyramidal side effects, but carries risk of QT prolongation.

#### 3. Neurokinin-1 ($\text{NK}_1$) Receptor Antagonists (Aprepitant, Fosaprepitant, Rolapitant)
* **Mechanism**: Crosses BBB and selectively blocks central $\text{NK}_1$ receptors, inhibiting binding of **Substance P**.
* **Clinical Utility**: Combined with a $5\text{-HT}_3$ antagonist and Dexamethasone for prevention of **Delayed CINV** induced by highly emetogenic chemotherapy (e.g., Cisplatin).
* **Drug Interactions**: Aprepitant is a substrate, inhibitor, and inducer of CYP3A4.

#### 4. Muscarinic ($M_1$) & Histamine ($H_1$) Antagonists
* **$M_1$ Antagonist (Scopolamine / Hyoscine)**: Transdermal patch; choice agent for **Motion Sickness** prevention. Side effects: Dry mouth, cycloplegia, urinary retention.
* **$H_1$ Antagonists (Promethazine, Diphenhydramine, Meclizine, Dimenhydrinate)**: Block vestibular $H_1$ and $M_1$ pathways. Used for motion sickness and morning sickness in pregnancy.

## 16.2 Prokinetic Agents

Prokinetic agents enhance GI transit by augmenting uncoordinated motility into coordinated propulsive patterns.

### 1. Metoclopramide
* **Prokinetic Mechanism**: Dual mechanism—Selective antagonist at enteric $D_2$ receptors (removing dopamine-mediated inhibition of ACh release) and agonist at pre-synaptic $5\text{-HT}_4$ receptors $\rightarrow \uparrow$ Acetylcholine release from myenteric motor neurons.
* **Actions**: Increases resting Lower Esophageal Sphincter (LES) tone, enhances gastric antral contractions, accelerates gastric emptying, and relaxes the duodenal bulb. Does not stimulate colonic motility.
* **Indications**: **Diabetic Gastroparesis**, GERD, and symptomatic nausea.

### 2. Erythromycin
* **Mechanism**: Direct small-molecule agonist at smooth muscle **Motilin Receptors**.
* **Action**: Induces powerful Phase III Migrating Motor Complex (MMC) contractions in the stomach and duodenum.
* **Indications**: Acute diabetic gastroparesis, acute upper GI bleeding (clears stomach of blood clots prior to emergency endoscopy).
* **Limitation**: Rapid tachyphylaxis develops within 7–10 days due to motilin receptor downregulation.

### 3. Prucalopride
* **Mechanism**: Highly selective, high-affinity $5\text{-HT}_4$ receptor agonist.
* **Action**: Stimulates colonic mass movements and accelerates total gut transit without hERG channel cardiotoxicity (unlike cisapride/tegasrod).
* **Indication**: Chronic Idiopathic Constipation refractory to laxatives.

## 16.3 Antidiarrheal & Anti-Constipation Drugs

### A. Antidiarrheal Agents

#### 1. Peripheral Opioid Agonists (Loperamide, Diphenoxylate)
* **Mechanism**: Act as selective agonists at $\mu$-opioid receptors located on the myenteric plexus of the enteric nervous system. Activation inhibits presynaptic ACh release, decreasing circular and longitudinal smooth muscle peristalsis, increasing intestinal transit time, and enhancing segmental tone and fluid/electrolyte reabsorption.
* **Loperamide Pharmacokinetics**: Substrate for P-glycoprotein efflux transporter; cleared efficiently from CNS. Does **not** cross blood-brain barrier at therapeutic doses (lacks central analgesic/addictive potential).
* **Diphenoxylate**: Formulated with sub-therapeutic atropine (Lomotil) to deter abuse; central opioid effects occur at high doses.
* **CONTRAINDICATION**: Strictly contraindicated in **invasive bacterial dysentery** (*Shigella*, *Salmonella*, *C. jejuni*) and *C. difficile* colitis, as slowing intestinal transit delays pathogen clearance and precipitates **Toxic Megacolon**.

#### 2. Bile Acid Sequestrants (Cholestyramine, Colestipol, Colesevelam)
* **Mechanism**: Bind unabsorbed bile acids in the intestinal lumen, forming an insoluble non-absorbable complex excreted in feces.
* **Indication**: Bile acid diarrhea caused by terminal ileal disease or resection (loss of ASBT transporter).

#### 3. Octreotide
* **Mechanism**: Synthetic long-acting **Somatostatin analog**. Suppresses GI hormone release (VIP, gastrin, 5-HT) and intestinal fluid secretion, while inhibiting motility at pharmacological doses.
* **Indications**: Severe secretory diarrhea associated with **VIPomas**, Carcinoid Syndrome, and refractory AIDS diarrhea.

### B. Anti-Constipation / Laxative Pharmacology

The principal laxative groups include bulk-forming agents, osmotic laxatives, stimulant laxatives, stool softeners and intestinal secretagogues. Lactulose has an additional role in hepatic encephalopathy because bacterial fermentation acidifies colonic contents and promotes conversion of diffusible ammonia ($\text{NH}_3$) to ammonium ($\text{NH}_4^+$), which is less readily absorbed. The comparison below summarises the major classes.

| Class | Mechanism of Action | Representative Agents | Key Clinical Considerations & Side Effects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bulk-Forming** | Non-digestible hydrophilic colloids that absorb water, expand, and form a gel, causing colonic distension and triggering peristalsis. | Psyllium (Ispaghula), Methylcellulose, Polycarbophil | Requires adequate fluid intake; causes bloating and flatulence. First-line for chronic constipation. |
| **Osmotic Laxatives** | Non-absorbable osmotically active solutes that draw water into the intestinal lumen, increasing stool volume and liquidity. | Polyethylene Glycol (PEG), Lactulose, Magnesium Citrate | **Lactulose**: Fermented by colonic bacteria to organic acids; lowers lumen pH, converting $\text{NH}_3 \rightarrow \text{NH}_4^+$ (trapped in stool). First-line in **Hepatic Encephalopathy**. |
| **Stimulant Laxatives** | Direct chemical irritation of enteric mucosa and myenteric plexus, inducing fluid secretion and active peristaltic contractions. | Senna, Bisacodyl, Sodium Picosulfate | Long-term use of Senna causes **Melanosis Coli** (benign dark pigmentation of colonic mucosa due to lipofuscin in macrophages). |
| **Stool Softeners** | Surface-acting detergent agents that lower surface tension, allowing water and lipids to penetrate stool. | Docusate Sodium, Mineral Oil | Useful in avoiding straining post-MI or perianal surgery. |
| **ClC-2 Secretagogues** | Local activation of apical type-2 chloride channels (ClC-2) on enterocytes $\rightarrow \uparrow \text{Cl}^-$ & water secretion into lumen. | **Lubiprostone** | Indicated for Chronic Idiopathic Constipation and IBS-C in females. Causes nausea. |
| **GC-C Agonists** | Binds guanylyl cyclase C (GC-C) on enterocytes $\rightarrow \uparrow \text{cGMP} \rightarrow$ activates CFTR chloride channel $\rightarrow \uparrow$ fluid secretion and accelerates transit. | **Linaclotide**, Plecanatide | Indicated for IBS-C and chronic idiopathic constipation. Contraindicated in pediatric patients $<6$ years. |
