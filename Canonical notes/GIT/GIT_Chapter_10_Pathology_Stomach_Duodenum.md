# Chapter 10: Pathology of the Stomach & Duodenum

## 10.1 Gastritis & Gastropathy

Gastritis refers to histologically proven mucosal inflammation, whereas gastropathy describes mucosal damage and epithelial regeneration without significant inflammatory infiltrate.

### A. Acute Gastritis & Stress-Related Mucosal Disease
* **Acute Gastritis**: Transient acute mucosal inflammatory process.
  * *Pathophysiology*: Disruption of the gastric mucosal defense mechanisms:
    1. Mucus-bicarbonate layer breakdown.
    2. Reduced mucosal blood flow (ischemia).
    3. Direct toxic cellular injury.
  * *Etiology*: NSAID abuse (inhibits COX-1 $\rightarrow \downarrow \text{PGE}_2/\text{PGI}_2 \rightarrow \downarrow$ mucus/$\text{HCO}_3^-$ secretion and blood flow), heavy alcohol ingestion, direct chemotherapy, heavy smoking, radiation.

* **Stress-Related Mucosal Disease**:
  * **Curling Ulcers**: Acute gastric/duodenal ulcers occurring in patients with **severe thermal burns**. Driven by massive systemic hypovolemia $\rightarrow$ splanchnic vasoconstriction $\rightarrow$ mucosal ischemia.
  * **Cushing Ulcers**: Acute ulcers occurring in patients with **intracranial lesions, head trauma, or brain surgery**. Driven by elevated intracranial pressure $\rightarrow$ direct vagal core stimulation $\rightarrow$ massive hypersecretion of gastric $\text{HCl}$. High risk of perforation.

---

### B. Chronic Gastritis: Autoimmune (Type A) vs. *H. pylori* (Type B)

**CHRONIC GASTRITIS CLASSIFICATION**
- AUTOIMMUNE (TYPE A) H. PYLORI (TYPE B)
- Fundus & Body - Antrum (Primary)
- Anti-Parietal & Anti-IF Abs - Microaerophilic spiral rod
- Achlorhydria + Pernicious Anemia - Peptic Ulcer Disease & MALToma

| Feature | Autoimmune Gastritis (Type A) | *H. pylori* Gastritis (Type B) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anatomical Location** | **Body and Fundus** (spares Antrum) | **Antrum** (antral-predominant; can spread to body as pangastritis) |
| **Pathophysiology** | T-cell-mediated CD4+ autoimmune destruction of Parietal Cells and Intrinsic Factor | *H. pylori* colonization of surface mucus layer; urease & CagA/VacA toxin damage |
| **Serological Markers** | Autoantibodies to **Parietal cells** ($\text{H}^+/\text{K}^+$-ATPase) and **Intrinsic Factor** | Anti-*H. pylori* IgG, Stool Antigen, Urea Breath Test |
| **Gastric Acid Secretion** | **Profound Hypochlorhydria / Achlorhydria** ($\text{pH} >6$) | **Elevated or Normal Acid** (antral); Decreased acid if pangastritis |
| **Serum Gastrin** | **Markedly Elevated (Hypergastrinemia)** due to loss of negative feedback on G-cells | Normal to slightly elevated |
| **Hematologic Manifestation** | **Pernicious Anemia** (Vitamin $\text{B}_{12}$ malabsorption) + Megaloblastic Anemia | Iron deficiency anemia (due to occult blood loss) |
| **Associated Neoplasms** | **Neuroendocrine (Carcinoid) Tumors** (driven by hypergastrinemia) & Gastric Adenocarcinoma | **Peptic Ulcer Disease**, **Gastric Adenocarcinoma**, **Gastric MALT Lymphoma** |

### C. Hypertrophic Gastropathies & Endocrine Disorders
* **Ménétrier Disease**:
  * *Pathophysiology*: Excessive local production of **Transforming Growth Factor-alpha (TGF-$\alpha$)**, activating the Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor (EGFR) cascade.
  * *Histology*: Massive enlargement of gastric rugal folds in body/fundus (**"cerebriform" appearance**), profound **foveolar epithelial hyperplasia**, cystic dilation of gastric glands, and severe **atrophy of parietal and chief cells**.
  * *Clinical Features*: Epigastric pain, weight loss, and **protein-losing enteropathy** leading to severe hypoalbuminemia and peripheral edema.

* **Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome (ZES / Gastrinoma)**:
  * *Pathophysiology*: Ectopic gastrin-secreting neuroendocrine tumor (Gastrinoma) located predominantly in the **Gastrinoma Triangle** (Passaro's triangle: junction of cystic/CBD, 2nd/3rd part duodenum, neck/body of pancreas). $25\%$ associated with **MEN1 Syndrome**.
  * *Pathology*: Massive hypergastrinemia drives 5-fold expansion of parietal cell mass, leading to giant rugal hypertrophy and uninhibited $\text{HCl}$ secretion.
  * *Clinical Triad*: Severe, recurrent, atypical peptic ulcers (extending into 2nd/3rd part of duodenum or jejunum), refractory to standard PPIs; chronic watery secretory diarrhea (acid inactivation of pancreatic lipases); elevated serum gastrin ($>1000\text{ pg/mL}$).
  * *Diagnostic Test*: **Secretin Stimulation Test**: IV secretin administration causes a paradoxical **surge in serum gastrin** ($>200\text{ pg/mL}$ rise) in ZES patients (normal G-cells are inhibited by secretin).

## 10.2 Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)

Chronic mucosal ulceration penetrating through the **muscularis mucosae** into the submucosa or deeper, driven by an imbalance between gastroduodenal mucosal aggressive factors ($\text{HCl}$, pepsin, *H. pylori*, NSAIDs) and protective defenses (mucus, bicarbonate, prostaglandins, mucosal blood flow).

- Aggressive Factors (Acid, Pepsin, H. pylori, NSAIDs) >>> Protective Factors (Mucus, HCO3-, PGE2, Blood Flow)
- Peptic Ulceration

### A. Comparison of Gastric vs. Duodenal Ulcers

| Parameter | Gastric Ulcer (GU) | Duodenal Ulcer (DU) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Relative Frequency** | $15–20\%$ of PUD cases | $80–85\%$ of PUD cases (4x more common) |
| **Peak Age Group** | 50–70 years | 30–60 years |
| **Primary Etiology** | Decreased mucosal protection (*H. pylori* $70\%$, NSAIDs $25\%$) | Hypersecretion of acid (*H. pylori* $90–95\%$, elevated parietal cell mass) |
| **Anatomical Site** | **Lesser curvature of stomach** (border between body and antrum) | **Anterior wall of 1st part (bulb) of duodenum** ($90\%$) |
| **Pain Relationship to Meals** | Pain is **precipitated or worsened by food** (eating $\rightarrow$ acid output $\rightarrow$ pain); leads to **anorexia and weight loss** | Pain is **relieved by food and antacids**; recurs 2–3 hours post-meal (**"hunger pain"**) and **awakens patient at night**; causes **weight gain** |
| **Malignant Potential** | **$4–8\%$ are malignant** (Mandatory endoscopic biopsy of ulcer margins required) | **Virtually NEVER malignant** (Biopsy not routinely required) |
| **Hemorrhage Vessel** | **Left Gastric Artery** (lesser curvature ulcers) | **Gastroduodenal Artery** (posterior duodenal wall ulcers) |
| **Perforation Location** | Anterior gastric wall | **Anterior duodenal wall** $\rightarrow$ Pneumoperitoneum |

---

### B. Major Complications of Peptic Ulcer Disease
1. **Hemorrhage** ($15–20\%$): Most common complication.
   * Posterior duodenal ulcers erode into the **Gastroduodenal Artery**, causing massive upper GI bleeding, hematemesis, and melena.
   * Lesser curvature gastric ulcers erode into the **Left Gastric Artery**.
2. **Perforation** ($5\%$):
   * Anterior duodenal ulcers erode into the peritoneal cavity, releasing air and acidic contents.
   * Presents with sudden, severe "thunderclap" abdominal pain, board-like abdominal rigidity, and **pneumoperitoneum** (free air under the diaphragm on upright chest X-ray).
3. **Gastric Outlet Obstruction (GOO)** ($2\%$):
   * Fibrotic scarring or inflammatory edema surrounding chronic prepyloric or duodenal bulb ulcers.
   * Clinical features: Intractable, non-bilious projectile vomiting containing food eaten hours prior, succussion splash, and **hypokalemic hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis**.

## 10.3 Gastric Neoplasms

### A. Gastric Polyps
* **Hyperplastic / Inflammatory Polyps** ($75\%$): Non-neoplastic; arise in response to chronic gastritis (*H. pylori*).
* **Fundic Gland Polyps**: Smooth, small polyps in fundus/body. Increased incidence with **chronic PPI use** (glandular dilation) or autosomal dominant **FAP**.
* **Gastric Adenoma** ($10\%$): True neoplastic polyps containing dysplastic epithelium. Premalignant precursor to gastric adenocarcinoma.

---

### B. Gastric Adenocarcinoma
Second leading cause of cancer mortality worldwide. Classified by the **Lauren Classification** into Intestinal and Diffuse types.

**GASTRIC ADENOCARCINOMA**
- INTESTINAL TYPE DIFFUSE TYPE
- Cohesive glandular structures - Signet-ring cells (mucin-filled)
- Associated with H. pylori & metaplasia - E-cadherin (CDH1) loss
- Exophytic / Ulcerated mass - "Leather-bottle" Linitis Plastica

| Feature | Intestinal-Type Adenocarcinoma | Diffuse-Type Adenocarcinoma |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Epidemiology** | Dominates in high-risk geographic areas (Japan, South America, China); older males ($>55$ yrs) | Uniform global distribution; younger patients ($<40$ yrs); equal male/female incidence |
| **Molecular Pathogenesis** | **Epidermal Growth Factor / HER2** amplification, $TP53$ mutations, $\beta$-catenin activation | Mutation or loss of **E-cadherin (*CDH1*)** gene, leading to loss of intercellular cell adhesion |
| **Microscopic Morphology** | Cohesive malignant epithelial cells forming **glandular structures** producing mucin | Single, non-cohesive cells infiltrating stomach wall; **Signet-Ring Cells** (large intracellular mucin vacuole displacing nucleus to periphery) |
| **Gross Appearance** | Exophytic nodular mass or large ulcer with irregular, heaped-up margins | Diffuse fibrous thickening and rigidity of entire stomach wall (**Linitis Plastica** or **"Leather-Bottle Stomach"**) |
| **Environmental Associations** | Chronic *H. pylori* infection, dietary nitrites/nitrosamines, smoked/salted foods, smoking, chronic gastritis | NOT linked to *H. pylori*, chronic gastritis, or environmental factors (primarily genetic/familial) |

* **Metastatic Manifestations of Gastric Adenocarcinoma**:
  * **Virchow's Node**: Enlarged, non-tender left supraclavicular lymph node (via thoracic duct drainage).
  * **Sister Mary Joseph Nodule**: Subcutaneous metastatic nodule at the umbilicus.
  * **Krukenberg Tumor**: Bilateral ovarian metastases composed of mucin-secreting signet-ring cells.
  * **Blumer's Shelf**: Rigid tumor shelf palpable on digital rectal or vaginal examination in the pouch of Douglas.
  * **Leser-Trélat Sign**: Sudden appearance of multiple seborrheic keratoses (paraneoplastic).

---

### C. Gastric Lymphoma & Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST)
* **Gastric MALT Lymphoma (Extranodal Marginal Zone B-Cell Lymphoma)**:
  * Driven by chronic antigen stimulation from ** *H. pylori* ** infection.
  * Molecular genetics: Translocation $t(11;18)(q21;q21)$ fusion producing *API2-MALT1*, activating NF-$\kappa$B.
  * *Clinical Pearl*: Early-stage MALT lymphomas lacking $t(11;18)$ **regress completely following *H. pylori* eradication** antibiotic therapy alone.

* **Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumor (GIST)**:
  * Most common mesenchymal tumor of the GI tract ($60\%$ occur in the stomach).
  * *Origin*: Derived from the **Interstitial Cells of Cajal (ICCs)** (pacemaker cells of the gut).
  * *Genetics*: Gain-of-function oncogenic mutations in **c-KIT (CD117)** ($85\%$) encoding a receptor tyrosine kinase, or **PDGFR$\alpha$** ($10\%$).
  * *Diagnostic Immunohistochemistry*: Strongly positive for **CD117 (c-KIT)** and **DOG-1**.
  * *Targeted Therapy*: High response rate to **Imatinib Mesylate** (tyrosine kinase inhibitor targeting c-KIT).
