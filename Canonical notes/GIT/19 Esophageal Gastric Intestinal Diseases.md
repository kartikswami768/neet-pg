# Chapter 19: Esophageal, Gastric & Intestinal Diseases

## 19.1 Management of Peptic Ulcer & *H. pylori*

Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD) encompasses mucosal defects penetrating through the muscularis mucosae in the stomach or duodenum, driven primarily by ***Helicobacter pylori*** infection and **Non-Steroidal Anti-Inflammatory Drug (NSAID)** use.

### A. Diagnostic Approach to Dyspepsia & Indication Criteria

**CLINICAL MANAGEMENT OF DYSPEPSIA**
- AGE > 55 YEARS OR ALARM FEATURES PRESENT AGE < 55 YEARS WITHOUT ALARM FEATURES
- Progressive dysphagia / odynophagia
- Persistent / recurrent vomiting v
- Unexplained weight loss (>10%) "TEST-AND-TREAT" STRATEGY
- GI bleeding or iron-deficiency anemia
- Palpable abdominal mass / lymphadenopathy
- Family history of GI malignancy
- v H. PYLORI POSITIVE H. PYLORI NEGATIVE
- URGENT UPPER ENDOSCOPY (EGD)
- (With gastric mucosal biopsies) v v
- 14-Day Eradication Trial of Empiric
- Therapy PPI for 4-8 Weeks

#### 1. "Test-and-Treat" Strategy
Indicated in uninvestigated dyspeptic patients aged **$<55$ years** (or $<45–60$ years depending on local gastric cancer incidence) **without alarm features**.
* Non-invasive testing for *H. pylori* via **Urea Breath Test (UBT)** or **Stool Antigen Test (SAT)**.
* If positive: Administer 14-day *H. pylori* eradication therapy.
* If negative or symptoms persist post-eradication: Trial of empiric PPI for 4–8 weeks.

#### 2. Immediate Upper Endoscopy (EGD) Indications
Indicated in any patient presenting with dyspepsia who is **$\ge 55$ years old** OR presents with **any** of the following **Alarm Features**:
* Unexplained significant weight loss ($>10\%$ body weight).
* Progressive dysphagia or odynophagia.
* Persistent or recurrent bilious/feculent vomiting.
* Evidence of overt GI bleeding (hematemesis, melena) or unexplained Iron Deficiency Anemia.
* Palpable abdominal mass or supraclavicular lymphadenopathy (Virchow node).
* Personal or family history of upper GI malignancy.

### B. Evidence-Based *H. pylori* Eradication Regimens

Due to rising global resistance rates to Clarithromycin ($>15\%$), guidelines from the American College of Gastroenterology (ACG) and Maastricht VI consensus strongly recommend **Bismuth Quadruple Therapy** as the preferred first-line empiric regimen.

| Regimen | Components | Duration | Indications & Clinical Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bismuth Quadruple Therapy** *(Preferred 1st-line)* | - **Standard-dose PPI** BID<br>- **Bismuth Subsalicylate** 120–300 mg QID<br>- **Metronidazole** 250 mg QID or 500 mg TID-QID<br>- **Tetracycline** 500 mg QID | **14 Days** | **First-line choice** in regions with high clarithromycin resistance or prior macrolide exposure. Highly effective against resistant strains. |
| **Non-Bismuth Concomitant Quadruple Therapy** | - **Standard-dose PPI** BID<br>- **Amoxicillin** 1000 mg BID<br>- **Clarithromycin** 500 mg BID<br>- **Metronidazole** 500 mg BID | **14 Days** | Preferred alternative first-line option if bismuth is unavailable. |
| **Clarithromycin Triple Therapy** *(Restricted)* | - **Standard-dose PPI** BID<br>- **Amoxicillin** 1000 mg BID (or Metronidazole 500 mg BID if penicillin-allergic)<br>- **Clarithromycin** 500 mg BID | **14 Days** | **Only** recommended in regions where local Clarithromycin resistance is documented to be $<15\%$ and patient has no prior macrolide exposure. |
| **Rifabutin Triple Therapy** *(Salvage)* | - **Standard-dose PPI** BID<br>- **Amoxicillin** 1000 mg BID<br>- **Rifabutin** 150 mg BID | **14 Days** | Effective **rescue / salvage therapy** for patients who have failed multiple prior eradication attempts. |

#### Confirmation of Eradication
Mandatory in all treated patients, performed at least **4 weeks post-completion of antibiotic therapy** and at least **2 weeks after stopping PPI therapy** (PPIs suppress bacterial load, causing false-negative test results). Confirmed via **Urea Breath Test** or **Stool Antigen Test**. Serology cannot be used for confirmation of eradication because anti-*H. pylori* IgG antibodies persist for years post-cure.

## 19.2 Clinical Management of Inflammatory Bowel Disease

Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD) comprises **Ulcerative Colitis (UC)** and **Crohn Disease (CD)**, characterized by chronic, relapsing immune-mediated intestinal inflammation.

### A. Phenotypic Differentiation & Disease Activity Assessment

**INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE (IBD)**
- ULCERATIVE COLITIS (UC) CROHN DISEASE (CD)
- Mucosal & Submucosal only - Transmural (full thickness)
- Continuous from Rectum proximally - Mouth to Anus; Skip lesions
- Symmetrical circumferential - Asymmetrical, cobblestoning
- Spares upper GI & small bowel - Terminal ileum most common
- Crypt abscesses, pseudopolyps - Non-caseating granulomas
- Cured by colectomy - Recurs post-surgery

#### 1. Ulcerative Colitis Activity: Truelove and Witts Criteria
Used to grade acute UC severity to guide hospitalization and rescue therapy decisions:

| Parameter | Mild UC | Moderate UC | Severe UC |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bloody Stools / Day** | $< 4$ | $4–5$ | **$\ge 6$** |
| **Pulse Rate (bpm)** | Normal ($<90$) | $90–100$ | **$> 90$ bpm** |
| **Temperature ($^\circ\text{C}$)** | Normal | $37.1–37.8^\circ\text{C}$ | **$> 37.8^\circ\text{C}$** |
| **Hemoglobin (g/dL)** | Normal | $10–11.5\text{ g/dL}$ | **$< 10.5\text{ g/dL}$** |
| **ESR (mm/1st hr)** | $< 30$ | $30–40$ | **$> 30\text{ mm/hr}$** |

---

### B. Therapeutic Algorithms (Step-Up vs. Top-Down)

**IBD THERAPEUTIC STEP-UP PYRAMID**
- / \ BIOLOGICS & SMALL MOLECULES
- / \ (Anti-TNF, Vedolizumab, Ustekinumab,
- / \ Tofacitinib, Ozanimod)
- / \ IMMUNOMODULATORS
- / \ (Azathioprine, 6-MP, Methotrexate)
- / \ SYSTEMIC CORTICOSTEROIDS
- / \ (Prednisone, Budesonide - Flares Only)
- / \ 5-AMINOSALICYLATES (5-ASA)
- / \ (Mesalamine, Sulfasalazine - UC Mainstay)

#### 1. Management of Ulcerative Colitis
* **Mild-to-Moderate UC**:
  * *Proctitis / Left-Sided*: Topical (rectal) **Mesalamine suppositories or enemas** (superior to oral 5-ASA).
  * *Extensive / Pancolitis*: Combined oral **Mesalamine ($2.4–4.8\text{ g/day}$)** PLUS topical rectal Mesalamine.
* **Moderate-to-Severe UC**:
  * Oral systemic corticosteroids (**Prednisolone 40 mg/day**) or oral **Budesonide MMX** for acute flare induction.
  * Rapid initiation of Biologic / Small Molecule therapy: **Infliximab**, **Adalimumab**, **Vedolizumab** (gut-selective anti-$\alpha_4\beta_7$), **Ustekinumab** (anti-IL-12/23), or **Tofacitinib** (JAK inhibitor).
* **Acute Severe Ulcerative Colitis (ASUC)**:
  * Hospitalization, high-dose **IV Hydrocortisone 100 mg Q6H** or Methylprednisolone.
  * Evaluate response at **Day 3** (Oxford Criteria: stool frequency $>8$/day or 3–8/day with CRP $>45\text{ mg/L}$). If failed: Emergency rescue therapy with **IV Infliximab** ($5\text{ mg/kg}$) or **IV Cyclosporine**. If failed rescue therapy at Day 7: **Emergency Subtotal Colectomy**.

#### 2. Management of Crohn Disease
* 5-ASAs are generally ineffective for Crohn disease and are not recommended by guidelines.
* **Mild-to-Moderate Ileocecal CD**: Oral **Budesonide (9 mg/day)** ileal-release (Entocort EC) for 8 weeks.
* **Moderate-to-Severe CD**: Early "Top-Down" initiation of Biologics (**Infliximab**, **Adalimumab**, **Ustekinumab**, or **Risankizumab**) combined with thiopurines (**Azathioprine**) to prevent stricturing and penetrating complications.
* **Perianal Fistulizing CD**: Surgical drainage of abscesses with non-cutting Seton placement PLUS **Infliximab** (biologic of choice for fistulizing disease).

### C. Extraintestinal Manifestations of IBD

IBD is a systemic disorder involving multiple organ systems beyond the gut. Manifestations are classified based on whether their clinical course correlates with intestinal luminal inflammation.

| Organ System | Extraintestinal Manifestation | Correlation with Gut Inflammation | Key Pathological Features & Clinical Management |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Musculoskeletal** | **Type 1 Peripheral Arthritis** | **Parallel** (Correlates) | Pauciarticular ($<5$ large joints); flares during bowel flares. Treat by controlling intestinal disease. |
| | **Type 2 Peripheral Arthritis** | **Independent** | Polyarticular (small hand joints); independent course. |
| | **Ankylosing Spondylitis** | **Independent** | Strongly associated with **HLA-B27**; axial skeleton involvement. Treated with Anti-TNF agents (NSAIDs contraindicated as they flare IBD). |
| **Dermatologic** | **Erythema Nodosum** | **Parallel** (Correlates) | Tender, red subcutaneous nodules on anterior shins. Parallel to bowel activity. |
| | **Pyoderma Gangrenosum** | **Independent** | Severe, painful, necrotizing violaceous ulcerations with undermined borders; typically on lower extremities. Treated with systemic steroids or Infliximab. |
| **Ocular** | **Episcleritis** | **Parallel** (Correlates) | Scleral hyperemia and irritation without vision loss; parallel to bowel activity. |
| | **Anterior Uveitis** | **Independent** | Deep eye pain, photophobia, headache, miosis; requires urgent topical/systemic steroids to prevent blindness. |
| **Hepatobiliary** | **Primary Sclerosing Cholangitis** | **Independent** | Strongly associated with **Ulcerative Colitis ($70\%$)**. Concentric "onion-skin" ductal fibrosis. Increased risk of Cholangiocarcinoma. |
| | **Cholelithiasis** | **Independent** | **Crohn Disease** (ileal disease/resection impairs bile acid reabsorption $\rightarrow$ depleted bile salt pool $\rightarrow$ cholesterol gallstones). |
| **Renal** | **Nephrolithiasis** | **Independent** | **Calcium Oxalate Stones** in Crohn disease (malabsorbed fatty acids bind luminal calcium $\rightarrow$ unabsorbed free oxalate is excessively absorbed in colon $\rightarrow$ hyperoxaluria). |
