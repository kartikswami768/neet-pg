# 60. Structure of DNA

Deoxyribonucleic Acid (**DNA**) is the primary carrier of genetic information in all cellular organisms and many viruses. DNA is a polymer composed of monomeric **deoxyribonucleotides** joined together covalently by phosphodiester linkages.

---

## 1. Chemical Composition of DNA

Each nucleotide building block consists of three components:
1. **Nitrogenous Base:**
   * *Purines:* Adenine (A), Guanine (G).
   * *Pyrimidines:* Cytosine (C), Thymine (T).
2. **Pentose Sugar:** 2'-deoxy-D-ribose (lacks hydroxyl group at $C2'$ position, imparting greater chemical/hydrolytic stability than RNA).
3. **Phosphate Group:** Attached to the $5'$ hydroxyl of deoxyribose.

```
Base + Deoxyribose = Deoxyribonucleoside
Deoxyribonucleoside + Phosphate = Deoxyribonucleotide
```

* **Phosphodiester Bond:** Joins the $3'$-hydroxyl group of one deoxyribose to the $5'$-phosphate group of the adjacent sugar, giving directionality ($5' \rightarrow 3'$) to the polynucleotide chain.

---

## 2. Chargaff's Rules of DNA Composition

Derived by Erwin Chargaff via quantitative chromatographic analysis of double-stranded DNA:
1. **$\text{Purines} = \text{Pyrimidines}$:** Sum of adenine and guanine equals sum of thymine and cytosine ($A + G = T + C$).
2. **Base Equivalences:** $[A] = [T]$ and $[G] = [C]$.
3. **Ratio Immutability:** The ratio $(A+T)/(G+C)$ varies across species but is constant within a given species.
4. *Rule Exception:* Applies **only to double-stranded DNA**; single-stranded viral DNA (e.g., $\phi X174$) does not obey Chargaff's rules.

---

## 3. Watson-Crick Model of B-DNA Double Helix

In 1953, James Watson and Francis Crick elucidated the secondary structure of double-stranded **B-DNA** based on Rosalind Franklin and Maurice Wilkins' X-ray diffraction patterns.

```
       5' P---[Sugar]---(A) === (T)---[Sugar]---P 3'
                |                        |
       5' P---[Sugar]---(G) === (C)---[Sugar]---P 3'
```

### Key Structural Parameters of B-DNA
* **Helical Conformation:** Two antiparallel polynucleotide strands wrapped around a central axis in a **right-handed helix**.
* **Base Pairing & Complementarity:** Purine on one strand pairs specifically with Pyrimidine on opposite strand inside hydrophobic core:
  * **Adenine = Thymine:** Joined by **2 Hydrogen Bonds**.
  * **Guanine $\equiv$ Cytosine:** Joined by **3 Hydrogen Bonds** (higher GC content correlates with increased DNA thermal stability and melting temperature $T_m$).
* **Dimensions:**
  * **Helical Diameter:** $2.0\text{ nm}$ ($20\text{ \AA}$).
  * **Helix Pitch (Turn Length):** $3.4\text{ nm}$ ($34\text{ \AA}$) per complete $360^\circ$ turn.
  * **Base Pairs per Turn:** $10–10.5\text{ bp}$.
  * **Axial Rise per Base Pair:** $0.34\text{ nm}$ ($3.4\text{ \AA}$).
* **Grooves:** Alternating **Major Groove** ($22\text{ \AA}$ wide) and **Minor Groove** ($12\text{ \AA}$ wide) expose sequence-specific hydrogen bonding groups for transcription factor and drug binding.

## 4. Alternative DNA Structural Conformations

| Parameter | A-DNA | B-DNA (Standard) | Z-DNA |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Helix Sense** | **Right-handed** | **Right-handed** | **Left-handed** |
| **Conditions** | Dehydrated / High salt | Physiological aqueous | Alternating $d(GC)_n$ repeats / Methylation |
| **Diameter** | $2.3\text{ nm}$ ($23\text{ \AA}$) | $2.0\text{ nm}$ ($20\text{ \AA}$) | $1.8\text{ nm}$ ($18\text{ \AA}$) |
| **bp per Turn** | 11 | 10–10.5 | 12 (6 dimers) |
| **Base Pair Tilt** | $20^\circ$ | $1^\circ$ (Perpendicular to axis) | $9^\circ$ |
| **Sugar Pucker** | $C3'$-endo | $C2'$-endo | $C2'$-endo (Py), $C3'$-endo (Pu) |
| **Backbone Path** | Smooth | Smooth | **Zig-zag** |

## 5. Thermal Denaturation and Hyperchromic Effect

* **Denaturation (Melting):** Heating or extreme pH disrupts hydrogen bonds and base stacking forces, separating double-stranded DNA into single strands without breaking phosphodiester bonds.
* **Hyperchromic Shift:** Single-stranded DNA absorbs significantly **more UV light at $260\text{ nm}$** than native double-stranded DNA because unstacked nitrogenous bases are exposed.
* **Melting Temperature ($T_m$):** Temperature at which $50\%$ of DNA helical structure is lost. High $G+C$ content increases $T_m$ due to extra hydrogen bonding and stronger base-stacking interactions.
