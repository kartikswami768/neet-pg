# 68. Transcription of DNA to Make Transfer RNA

Transfer RNAs (**tRNAs**) serve as essential adapter molecules during translation, matching specific mRNA codons with their corresponding amino acids. All tRNAs share a highly conserved cloverleaf secondary structure and an L-shaped tertiary conformation.

---

## 1. tRNA Transcription and Structural Domains

tRNAs are synthesized in the nucleoplasm by **RNA Polymerase III**.

```
                           Amino Acid Attachment Site
                                 5'-CCA-3' (3' End)
                                     |
                             [Acceptor Stem]
                                  /     \
                       [D-Arm]           [T-psi-C Arm]
                          |                    |
                  (Dihydrouridine)      (Pseudouridine &
                                        Ribothymidine)
                                  \     /
                              [Variable Loop]
                                     |
                              [Anticodon Arm]
                                     |
                             (3-Base Anticodon)
```

### Key Structural Arms
1. **Acceptor Stem (3' End):** Consists of a $7\text{-bp}$ stem terminating in a single-stranded **$5'\text{-CCA-3'}$ sequence**. The $3'$-terminal hydroxyl group ($2'$ or $3'$-OH) forms an ester linkage with its specific amino acid.
2. **Anticodon Arm:** Contains the 3-base **Anticodon loop** that antiparallelly base-pairs with complementary $mRNA$ codons.
3. **D-Arm (DHU Loop):** Contains modified base **Dihydrouridine**. Serves as recognition site for specific **Aminoacyl-tRNA Synthetase**.
4. **$T\Psi C$ Arm:** Contains **Ribothymidine ($T$)** and **Pseudouridine ($\Psi$)**. Responsible for binding tRNA to the ribosomal $5S$ rRNA subunit.
5. **Variable Loop:** Varies in length ($4–21\text{ nt}$) and categorizes tRNAs into Class 1 or Class 2.

---

## 2. Post-Transcriptional tRNA Processing

Primary pre-tRNA transcripts synthesized by RNA Pol III undergo extensive maturation:

1. **$5'$ Leader Cleavage:** Removed by **RNase P** (a catalytic **Ribozyme** composed of RNA and protein components).
2. **$3'$ Trailer Trimming & CCA Addition:** $3'$ end trimmed, and invariant **$5'\text{-CCA-3'}$ sequence** added sequentially by **tRNA Nucleotidyltransferase** (without DNA/RNA template).
3. **Intron Splicing:** Certain pre-tRNAs contain small introns within the anticodon loop that are excised by specialized tRNA endonucleases and ligases.
4. **Base Modification:** Approximately $10–15\%$ of bases undergo post-transcriptional chemical modification:
   * Uracil $\rightarrow$ **Dihydrouridine** (hydrogenation).
   * Uracil $\rightarrow$ **Pseudouridine** ($\Psi$, isomerization).
   * Adenine $\rightarrow$ **Inosine** (deamination by ADAT; critical for wobble base pairing).
