# Orthopaedics Chapter 05: Joint Disorders & Arthritides

## Overview & Classification of Joint Diseases

Articular pathology in orthopaedics and rheumatology is broadly categorized based on the primary site of tissue involvement and the underlying pathophysiological mechanism:
* **Degenerative Joint Disease (Cartilage Primary)**: Characterized by mechanical degradation of hyaline articular cartilage without primary systemic inflammation (e.g., Osteoarthritis).
* **Synovial Inflammatory Diseases (Autoimmune / Systemic)**: Characterized by chronic autoimmune inflammation of the synovial membrane, leading to destructive pannus formation, marginal erosions, and systemic extra-articular manifestations (e.g., Rheumatoid Arthritis).
* **Seronegative Spondyloarthropathies (Entheseal / Axial Primary)**: Rheumatoid factor-negative inflammatory disorders demonstrating strong HLA-B27 association, enthesitis, dactylitis, and prominent axial skeleton involvement (e.g., Ankylosing Spondylitis, Psoriatic Arthritis, Reactive Arthritis).
* **Crystal Deposition Arthropathies**: Microcrystal-induced acute and chronic joint inflammation triggered by metabolic saturation (e.g., Monosodium Urate in Gout, Calcium Pyrophosphate Dihydrate in Pseudogout).
* **Neuropathic (Charcot) Arthropathies**: Rapid, painless joint disorganization and destruction resulting from the loss of sensory pain and proprioceptive feedback.

## 1. Osteoarthritis (Degenerative Joint Disease)



*Osteoarthritis versus rheumatoid arthritis — schematic.*


### 1.1 Pathophysiology & Etiology
Osteoarthritis (OA) is the most prevalent joint disorder globally. It is fundamentally a disease of the entire joint complex, initiated by mechanical stress and metabolic chondrocyte dysfunction:
* **Chondrocyte-Mediated Cartilage Degradation**: Chronic mechanical load causes chondrocyte injury, triggering the release of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and aggrecanases. This leads to the loss of proteoglycans and Type II collagen, enzymatic softening (chondromalacia), surface fibrillation, and progressive vertical fissuring of articular hyaline cartilage.
* **Subchondral Bone Remodeling**: Denudation of cartilage exposes subchondral bone to abnormal focal pressure. Subchondral bone undergoes microfractures, reactive osteosclerosis (eburnation), and fluid intrusion through micro-fissures forming intraosseous subchondral cysts (geodes).
* **Osteophytosis**: Mechanical traction at joint margins stimulates periosteal chondro-osteoprogenitor cells, leading to peripheral bony outgrowth formation (osteophytes) in an attempt to increase joint surface area and redistribute mechanical load.

### 1.2 Clinical Presentation & Physical Signs
* **Symptom Complex**: Deep, aching pain in weight-bearing joints that characteristically **worsens with joint use/weight-bearing** and is **relieved by rest**. Transient morning stiffness or gel phenomenon lasts $<30\text{ minutes}$.
* **Physical Examination Findings**:
  * Coarse articular crepitus during passive range of motion.
  * Joint line tenderness and palpable bony enlargement.
  * Periarticular muscle atrophy (most notably **Vastus Medialis** atrophy in knee OA).
  * Restricted range of motion and joint deformities (e.g., bilateral **Genu Varum** / bow legs in knee OA due to predominant medial compartment cartilage loss).
* **Nodal Deformities of the Hand**:
  * **Heberden's Nodes**: Bony osteophytic swellings over the **Distal Interphalangeal (DIP)** joints.
  * **Bouchard's Nodes**: Bony osteophytic swellings over the **Proximal Interphalangeal (PIP)** joints.
  * **First CMC Joint Involvement**: Squaring of the wrist/hand base due to carpometatarseal osteoarthritis.
  * *Joint Sparing*: OA characteristically **spares the Metacarpophalangeal (MCP)** joints and wrist joints.

### 1.3 Radiological Features (Mnemonic: LOSS)


![Knee osteoarthritis radiograph: assess compartmental narrowing, osteophytes and sclerosis. ](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Osteoarthritis%20left%20knee.jpg)

*Visual reference: Knee osteoarthritis radiograph: assess compartmental narrowing, osteophytes and sclerosis. [Original image and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Osteoarthritis_left_knee.jpg). Online image; internet required.*
Plain radiography (weight-bearing AP and lateral views) remains the diagnostic gold standard:
1. **L — Loss of Joint Space**: Asymmetric, focal joint space narrowing corresponding to regional cartilage destruction.
2. **O — Osteophytes**: Marginal bony spurs developing along joint boundaries.
3. **S — Subchondral Sclerosis**: Increased radiodensity (eburnation) of subchondral bone beneath destroyed cartilage.
4. **S — Subchondral Cysts**: Well-defined lucent lesions (geodes) within subchondral trabecular bone.
* *Loose Bodies*: Fragmented osteophytes or detached cartilage fragments circulating within the joint space ("joint mice").

### 1.4 Management Protocols
* **Non-Pharmacological / Conservative**: Weight reduction, activity modification, quadriceps strengthening exercises, unloader knee braces, walking aids (cane held in contralateral hand).
* **Pharmacological**: First-line oral analgesics (Acetaminophen), topical NSAIDs (Diclofenac gel), oral non-selective or COX-2 selective NSAIDs for acute symptom flares. Intra-articular corticosteroid or hyaluronic acid injections for short-term symptomatic relief.
* **Surgical Interventions**:
  * *High Tibial Osteotomy (HTO)*: Realigns mechanical axis in young active patients with isolated unicompartmental (medial) knee OA.
  * *Total Joint Arthroplasty (TKA / THA)*: Total Knee or Total Hip Replacement indicated for end-stage refractory pain, severe functional limitation, and advanced joint destruction.

## 2. Rheumatoid Arthritis (Systemic Autoimmune Synovial Disease)

### 2.1 Etiology, Pathogenesis & Pathology
Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA) is a chronic, systemic autoimmune inflammatory disease predominantly targeting synovial membranes:
* **Genetic Predisposition**: Strong association with **HLA-DR4** (specifically *HLA-DRB1* alleles containing the "shared epitope") and *PTPN22* gene mutations. Female to male ratio is approximately $3:1$, with peak incidence between 40 and 60 years.
* **Immune Cascade & Pannus Formation**:
  1. Environmental triggers (e.g., smoking, periodontitis) induce **citrullination** of self-proteins by peptidylarginine deiminase (PAD) enzymes.
  2. Loss of self-tolerance leads to the formation of Anti-Citrullinated Protein Antibodies (**Anti-CCP** / ACPA) and Rheumatoid Factor (**RF**; IgM antibody targeting the Fc portion of IgG).
  3. CD4+ $Th_1$ and $Th_{17}$ helper T-cells infiltrate the synovium, secreting pro-inflammatory cytokines (**TNF-$\alpha$**, **IL-1**, **IL-6**, **IFN-$\gamma$**).
  4. Synovial lining hyperplasias, forming an aggressive, vascularized inflammatory granulation tissue mass termed **Pannus**.
  5. Pannus releases collagenases and stromal metalloproteinases, directly destroying articular cartilage, while TNF-$\alpha$ activates RANKL on osteoclasts to induce marginal subchondral bone erosions.
  6. End-stage disease progression leads to fibrous ankylosis and eventual **bony ankylosis**.

### 2.2 Serological & Laboratory Markers
* **Anti-CCP Antibodies**: Highest clinical specificity ($>95\%$) for RA; present in early pre-clinical disease and correlates with erosive radiographic progression.
* **Rheumatoid Factor (RF)**: Present in $80\%$ of patients (seropositive RA), but lower specificity as it occurs in Sjogren's syndrome, Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), Subacute Bacterial Endocarditis, and Hepatitis C.
* **Inflammatory Markers**: Marked elevation of Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR) and C-Reactive Protein (CRP) reflecting systemic inflammatory activity.
* **Synovial Fluid Analysis**: Inflammatory profile (WBC count $2,000\text{--}50,000/\mu\text{L}$ with PMN predominance; clear/cloudy yellow, reduced viscosity, no crystals). Differentiates inflammatory arthritis from non-inflammatory OA ($<2,000/\mu\text{L}$) and purulent Septic Arthritis ($>50,000/\mu\text{L}$).

### 2.3 Articular Manifestations & Hand Deformities


![Clinical hand changes associated with rheumatoid arthritis. ](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Severely%20deformed%20hands%20from%20Rheumatoid%20Arthritis.jpg)

*Visual reference: Clinical hand changes associated with rheumatoid arthritis. [Original image and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rheumatoid_Arthritis_Hands.jpg). Online image; internet required.*
* **Pattern of Involvement**: Chronic, **symmetric polyarthritis** predominantly involving small peripheral joints of the hands and feet.
* **Joint Predilection**: Metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints, Proximal Interphalangeal (PIP) joints, wrists, Metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joints, knees, ankles, and cervical spine. Characteristically **spares the Distal Interphalangeal (DIP)** joints and lumbar spine.
* **Symptom Timing**: Prominent **morning stiffness lasting $>1\text{ hour}$** that noticeably **improves with physical activity**.
* **Pathognomonic Deformities**:
  * **Swan Neck Deformity**: Hyperextension at the PIP joint with secondary compensatory flexion at the DIP joint (caused by volar plate attenuation and dorsal slip retraction).
  * **Boutonniere Deformity**: Fixed flexion at the PIP joint with compensatory hyperextension at the DIP joint (caused by rupture of the central extensor tendon slip over the PIP joint).
  * **Ulnar Deviation**: Subluxation and ulnar drift of fingers at the MCP joints, accompanied by compensatory radial deviation of the wrist (**Zig-zag deformity**).
  * **Z-Deformity of Thumb**: Fixed flexion at 1st MCP joint with hyperextension at 1st IP joint.
  * **Piano Key Deformity**: Prominent dorsally subluxed distal ulna due to rupture of the Extensor Carpi Ulnaris (ECU) tendon and distal radioulnar joint instability.
* **Spine Involvement**: High risk of **Atlantoaxial Subluxation ($C_1\text{--}C_2$ instability)** due to transverse ligament erosion. Poses severe risk of cervical compressive myelopathy during neck extension (e.g., endotracheal intubation).

### 2.4 Extra-Articular Manifestations
* **Cutaneous**: Subcutaneous **Rheumatoid Nodules** (painless, firm juxta-articular fibrinoid necrosis nodules surrounded by palisading histiocytes; strongly associated with high RF titers).
* **Ocular**: Keratoconjunctivitis sicca (secondary Sjogren's syndrome; most common), episcleritis, scleritis, and **scleromalacia perforans** (painful anterior necrotizing scleritis).
* **Pulmonary**: Rheumatoid interstitial lung disease (ILD), pleural effusions (characteristically low glucose), Caplan syndrome (RA + pneumoconiosis with rheumatoid lung nodules).
* **Vascular & Systemic**: Cutaneous leukocytoclastic vasculitis, pericarditis, secondary AA amyloidosis, **Felty's Syndrome** (Triad: Severe RA + Splenomegaly + Neutropenia).

### 2.5 Pharmacological Management
* **Disease-Modifying Antirheumatic Drugs (DMARDs)**: Must be initiated immediately upon diagnosis to arrest structural joint damage.
  * *Conventional Synthetic DMARDs*: **Methotrexate** (first-line anchor drug; dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor; requires folate supplementation), **Sulfasalazine**, **Leflunomide**, **Hydroxychloroquine**.
  * *Biological DMARDs*: **TNF-$\alpha$ Inhibitors** (Infliximab, Adalimumab, Etanercept; require screening for latent TB via QuantiFERON/Mantoux prior to therapy), **IL-6 Receptor Antagonists** (Tocilizumab), **B-Cell Depleting Agents** (Rituximab), **T-Cell Costimulation Blockers** (Abatacept).
  * *Targeted Synthetic DMARDs*: **JAK Inhibitors** (Tofacitinib, Baricitinib).
* **Symptom Control Bridge**: Short-term oral low-dose corticosteroids or NSAIDs while waiting for DMARD onset (typically 4–12 weeks).

## 3. Seronegative Spondyloarthropathies (RF-Negative, HLA-B27 Associated)



*Ankylosing spondylitis: sacroiliac joint — schematic.*


### 3.1 Shared Pathological Spectrum
The seronegative spondyloarthropathies represent an interconnected group of inflammatory joint disorders characterized by:
1. Absence of Rheumatoid Factor and Anti-CCP antibodies.
2. Strong genetic association with **HLA-B27** (MHC Class I molecule).
3. Primary pathology centered at the **Enthesis** (inflammation at the site of ligament, tendon, or capsule insertion into bone).
4. Asymmetric oligoarthritis, prominent axial skeleton involvement (sacroiliitis, spondylitis), dactylitis ("sausage digit"), and anterior uveitis.

### 3.2 Ankylosing Spondylitis (Marie-Strümpell / Bechterew Disease)


![Classical syndesmophyte bridging and bamboo-spine appearance. ](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Bamboo%20spine%20ankylosing%20spondylitis.jpg)

*Visual reference: Classical syndesmophyte bridging and bamboo-spine appearance. [Original image and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bamboo_spine_ankylosing_spondylitis.jpg). Online image; internet required.*
* **Demographics & Genetics**: Predominantly affects young males (15–30 years; $M:F = 3:1$). Over $90\%$ of patients are **HLA-B27 positive**.
* **Clinical Course**: Insidious onset of chronic inflammatory low back pain and stiffness lasting $>3\text{ months}$. Pain is worst in the morning or after rest, and **improves significantly with exercise**.
* **Anatomical Progression**:
  * **Sacroiliitis**: Inflammatory involvement of Sacroiliac (SI) joints is the **earliest clinical and radiological feature** (bilateral symmetric buttock pain).
  * Ascending involvement of lumbar, thoracic, and cervical spine.
  * Loss of spinal mobility in all planes; exaggerated thoracic kyphosis and flattened lumbar lordosis ("Stooped posture").
* **Clinical Examination Tests**:
  * **Schober's Test**: Quantifies lumbar spine flexion loss. Marks placed $5\text{ cm}$ below and $10\text{ cm}$ above $S_2$ spinous process ($15\text{ cm}$ baseline); distance increases to $<20\text{ cm}$ on full forward flexion in AS.
  * **Chest Expansion Test**: Restricted chest expansion ($<2.5\text{ cm}$ measured at 4th intercostal space) due to costovertebral joint ankylosis.
* **Radiological Signs**:
  * **Sacroiliitis**: Erosion, sclerosis, and joint space pseudowidening progressing to complete bony ankylosis.
  * **Romanus Sign**: Erosions at anterior margins of vertebral endplates ("Shiny Corner Sign").
  * **Syndesmophytes**: Vertical, thin, marginal bony bridges forming between adjacent vertebral bodies.
  * **Bamboo Spine**: Complete ossification of annulus fibrosus, anterior longitudinal ligament, and marginal syndesmophytes.
  * **Dagger Spine Sign**: Single central radiodense line on AP spine view due to ossification of supraspinous and interspinous ligaments.
  * **Trolley Track Sign**: Three parallel vertical radiodense lines on AP radiograph caused by simultaneous ossification of central interspinous ligaments and bilateral facet joint capsules.
* **Extra-Articular Manifestations**: Acute anterior uveitis (iridocyclitis; most common, 25–30%), aortic regurgitation, ascending aortitis, cardiac conduction defects (AV block), upper lobe apical pulmonary fibrosis.
* **Treatment**: First-line: High-dose continuous **NSAIDs**. Biologic therapy: **TNF-$\alpha$ Inhibitors** (Adalimumab, Etanercept) or **IL-17A Inhibitors** (Secukinumab) for active disease refractory to NSAIDs.

### 3.3 Reactive Arthritis (Reiter's Syndrome)
* **Etiology**: Sterile post-infectious inflammatory autoimmune arthritis developing 1–4 weeks following a antecedent mucosal infection:
  * *Genitourinary*: **Chlamydia trachomatis** (most common worldwide).
  * *Gastrointestinal*: *Salmonella*, *Shigella*, *Yersinia*, *Campylobacter*.
* **Classic Diagnostic Triad**:
  1. **Urethritis** / Cervicitis
  2. **Conjunctivitis** / Anterior Uveitis
  3. **Asymmetric Oligoartritis** (predominantly weight-bearing lower limb joints: knee, ankle)
  * Mnemonic: *"Can't see, can't pee, can't climb a tree"*.
* **Cutaneous & Mucosal Signs**:
  * **Keratoderma Blennorrhagica**: Hyperkeratotic, vesicle-like pustular papules on palms and soles.
  * **Circinate Balanitis**: Painless superficial ring-like erosions on the glans penis.
  * Painless oral mucosal ulcers and dactylitis.
* **Treatment**: NSAIDs for articular symptoms; systemic antibiotics to treat underlying active GU *Chlamydia* infection (does not alter arthritis duration).

### 3.4 Psoriatic Arthritis
* **Clinical Features**: Inflammatory arthritis occurring in $5\text{--}30\%$ of patients with cutaneous psoriasis. Cutaneous lesions precede joint symptoms in $60\%$ of cases, appear simultaneously in $20\%$, or follow arthritis in $20\%$.
* **Skeletal Patterns (Wright & Moll Classification)**:
  1. *Asymmetric Oligoarticular Arthritis* (50%; most common): Involves hands and feet.
  2. *Symmetric Polyarthritis* (40%): Resembles RA but RF-negative.
  3. *Distal Interphalangeal (DIP) Joint Arthritis* (5%): Pathognomonic DIP involvement associated with nail dystrophy.
  4. *Spondylitis / Axial Disease* (40%): Asymmetric sacroiliitis and non-marginal bulky syndesmophytes.
  5. *Arthritis Mutilans* (5%): Severe destructive osteolytic deformity featuring telescoping digits ("Opera-glass fingers").
* **Key Clinical & Radiographic Signs**:
  * **Dactylitis**: Diffuse, uniform inflammatory swelling of an entire digit ("Sausage digit").
  * **Nail Dystrophy**: Pitting, onycholysis, oil-drop discoloration, and subungual hyperkeratosis.
  * **Pencil-in-Cup Deformity**: Radiographic erosion of the distal end of a proximal phalanx into a sharp point ("pencil") tapering into the expanded cup-like base of the adjacent distal phalanx.
* **Treatment**: Methotrexate, TNF-$\alpha$ inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors (Secukinumab, Ixekizumab), IL-12/23 inhibitors (Ustekinumab), or JAK inhibitors (Tofacitinib).

### 3.5 Enteropathic Arthritis
* Associated with Inflammatory Bowel Disease (Crohn's Disease > Ulcerative Colitis).
* *Type 1 (Pauciarticular)*: Involves $<5$ large joints (knees, ankles); flares parallel intestinal IBD activity.
* *Type 2 (Polyarticular)*: Involves small hand joints; runs an independent course from bowel inflammation.

## 4. Crystal-Induced Arthropathies

### 4.1 Gout (Monosodium Urate Crystal Deposition)


![Clinical example of tophaceous gout. ](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Tophaceous%20gout.jpg)

*Visual reference: Clinical example of tophaceous gout. [Original image and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tophaceous_gout.jpg). Online image; internet required.*
* **Pathophysiology**: Disease of purine metabolism resulting in persistent hyperuricemia (serum uric acid $>6.8\text{ mg/dL}$, exceeding saturation threshold). Monosodium Urate (MSU) crystals precipitate in cold, peripheral articular tissues.
  * *Underexcretion of Uric Acid* (90% of cases): Idiopathic renal tubular defect, impaired renal function, thiazide/loop diuretics, low-dose aspirin, pyrazinamide.
  * *Overproduction of Uric Acid* (10% of cases): Cytotoxic tumor lysis syndrome, myeloproliferative disorders, HGPRT deficiency (Lesch-Nyhan syndrome), high purine diet, alcohol abuse.
* **Stages of Clinical Gout**:
  1. **Asymptomatic Hyperuricemia**.
  2. **Acute Gouty Arthritis**: Rapid onset of excruciating, agonizing monoarticular pain, erythema, warmth, and marked swelling. First Metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint is the initial site in $>50\%$ of cases (**Podagra**).
  3. **Intercritical Gout**: Symptom-free intervals between acute flares.
  4. **Chronic Tophaceous Gout**: Nodular collections of MSU crystals surrounded by giant cell granulomatous inflammation (**Tophi**) deposited in periarticular tendons, synovial membranes, olecranon bursa, and helix of the ear.
* **Diagnostic Gold Standard**: Joint fluid aspiration and **Polarized Light Microscopy**:
  * **MSU Crystals**: **Needle-shaped**, displaying **strong negative birefringence** (crystals align yellow when parallel to the red compensator filter axis, and blue when perpendicular).
* **Radiological Findings**: Punched-out subchondral lytic bone erosions with overhanging sclerotic bone margins (**Martel's G Sign** / overhanging edge sign) located juxta-articularly.
* **Management Protocols**:
  * *Acute Gouty Attack*: High-dose **NSAIDs** (Indomethacin, Naproxen), **Colchicine** ($0.6\text{ mg}$ doses; inhibits neutrophil microtubule assembly), or oral/intra-articular **Corticosteroids**.
    * *Critical Rule*: **Never initiate or discontinue Allopurinol during an acute attack**, as sudden shifts in serum uric acid levels trigger further crystal mobilization and prolong acute inflammation.
  * *Chronic / Prophylactic Urate-Lowering Therapy (ULT)*: Target serum uric acid $<6.0\text{ mg/dL}$ (or $<5.0\text{ mg/dL}$ in tophaceous disease).
    * *Xanthine Oxidase Inhibitors*: **Allopurinol** (first-line; requires *HLA-B*5801 screening in high-risk populations to prevent Stevens-Johnson syndrome) or **Febuxostat**.
    * *Uricosuric Agents*: **Probenecid** (promotes renal uric acid excretion; contraindicated in nephrolithiasis).

### 4.2 Pseudogout (Calcium Pyrophosphate Dihydrate — CPPD)


![Annotated knee radiograph demonstrating chondrocalcinosis. ](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Chondrocalcinosis%20-%20annotated.jpg)

*Visual reference: Annotated knee radiograph demonstrating chondrocalcinosis. [Original image and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chondrocalcinosis_-_annotated.jpg). Online image; internet required.*
* **Pathophysiology**: Deposition of Calcium Pyrophosphate Dihydrate (CPPD) crystals within articular cartilage and fibrocartilage (chondrocalcinosis). Predominantly affects elderly individuals ($>60\text{ years}$).
* **Associated Metabolic Conditions**: Aging, Primary Hyperparathyroidism, Hemochromatosis, Hypomagnesemia, Hypophosphatasia, Gitelman syndrome.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Acute inflammatory monoarthritis or oligoarthritis simulating acute gout. **Knee joint is the most common site** ($>50\%$), followed by wrists, MCP joints, and symphysis pubis.
* **Polarized Light Microscopy**:
  * **CPPD Crystals**: **Rhomboid-shaped or polygonal**, displaying **weak positive birefringence** (crystals align blue when parallel to the red compensator filter axis).
  * *Diagnostic Mnemonic*: The *"Blue P's of CPPD"* — **B**lue when parallel, **P**ositively birefringent, **P**olygonal/rhomboid, **P**yrophosphate, **P**seudogout.
* **Radiological Hallmark**: **Chondrocalcinosis** — linear, punctate radiodense calcifications within hyaline or fibrocartilage (knee menisci, triangular fibrocartilage complex [TFCC] of wrist, symphysis pubis).
* **Treatment**: Intra-articular steroid injection, oral NSAIDs, or systemic colchicine.

## 5. Neuropathic Joint Disease (Charcot Joint)

### 5.1 Pathophysiology & Mechanics
Charcot Arthropathy is a progressive, highly destructive form of chronic joint degeneration resulting from underlying neurosensory impairment:
1. **Neurotraumatic Mechanism**: Loss of deep pain sensation and proprioceptive feedback eliminates protective joint reflexes.
2. The patient subjects the unperceived, insensitive joint to repetitive microtrauma, unaligned mechanical loads, and recurrent intra-articular microfractures.
3. **Neurovascular Mechanism**: Autonomic neuropathy induces hyperemic bone resorption and osteoclast activation.
4. Concomitant capsular stretching, ligamentous laxity, bone fragmentation, and complete joint disorganization ensue.

### 5.2 Etiology & Anatomical Distribution
The underlying neurological disease dictates the specific joint distribution:

| Neurological Etiology | Mechanism of Neuropathy | Predominant Joint Affected |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Diabetic Neuropathy** | Peripheral sensory and autonomic polyneuropathy | **Tarsal & Tarsometatarsal Joints** (Foot / Ankle; Lisfranc complex) |
| **Tabes Dorsalis (Neurosyphilis)** | Posterior column sensory degeneration | **Knee Joint** |
| **Syringomyelia** | Central canal cervical cord cavitation | **Shoulder & Elbow Joints** (Upper Extremity) |
| **Meningomyelocele / Spina Bifida** | Congenital spinal cord dysraphism | **Ankle & Foot Joints** in pediatric age group |
| **Leprosy (Hansen's Disease)** | Peripheral nerve trunk destruction | **Hands & Feet** |

* *Note*: **Tuberculosis is NOT a cause** of neuropathic Charcot joint disease.

### 5.3 Clinical Features & Radiology


![Radiograph of destructive neuropathic arthropathy (Charcot joint). ](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Charcot%20arthropathy%20X-ray.jpg)

*Visual reference: Radiograph of destructive neuropathic arthropathy (Charcot joint). [Original image and attribution](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Charcot_arthropathy_X-ray.jpg). Online image; internet required.*
* **Clinical Presentation**: Paradoxically **painless, massively swollen, unstable, hypermobile joint**. Characterized by gross deformity, severe joint disorganization, thermal warmth, and coarse crepitus during movement ("Bag of bones" sensation).
* **Radiological Features**: Dramatic joint space collapse, massive osteophytosis, extensive subchondral bone destruction, bone fragmentation, subluxation/dislocation, and periarticular soft tissue debris.
* **Management**: Immobilization and total contact casting (TCC) during acute inflammatory phases to prevent collapse; specialized accommodative orthoses / custom footwear; surgical arthrodesis (fusion) reserved for severe deformity or mechanical instability.
