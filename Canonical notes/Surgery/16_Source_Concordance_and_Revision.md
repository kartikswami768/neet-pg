## 2. Important Source Differences

| Topic | Source discrepancy or limitation | Notes treatment |
|---|---|---|
| CRC screening | Older surgical revision notes start at 50; First Aid 2025 gives 45 | Both identified; screening depends on risk and guideline |
| Gastric/colorectal staging | Some revision sections identify PET-CT as universal investigation of choice | CT, EUS, MRI and PET-CT separated by staging purpose |
| Achalasia | Manometric descriptors differ in abbreviated revision material | Retained Chicago classification distinctions from the GIT notes |
| Barrett management | Some revision algorithms suggest ablation too broadly | Surveillance versus treatment separated by dysplasia |
| Pyloric stenosis | Ultrasound thresholds vary with age and reference | Classical values retained as examination guides |
| H. pylori | Older treatment regimens may not account for antibiotic resistance | Regimen selection requires resistance and patient context |
| Bleeding peptic ulcer | Historical transfusion-count thresholds are used for surgery | Emphasized ongoing instability, failed hemostasis and bleeding source |
| GIST | Fixed 2-cm surgical margin in abbreviated notes | Negative-margin resection and avoidance of tumor rupture emphasized |
| Gastric cancer | D1/D2 nodal classifications simplified in revision diagrams | Explained regional principles and minimum staging-node concept |
| Appendicular lump | Routine interval appendectomy appears in classical teaching | Described selective reassessment and management |
| Appendiceal NET | Resection determined solely by tumor size in abbreviated notes | Location, size and histological risk must be considered |
| MUTYH polyposis | Revision notes state “APC mutation not identified” without clearly explaining the gene | First Aid identifies MUTYH-related DNA repair defect |
| Rectal cancer | Fixed 5-cm distance used to distinguish LAR from APR | Explained sphincter involvement and oncological margins |
| IBD | Extraintestinal manifestations sometimes listed broadly as surgical indications | Indications tied to intestinal complications, refractory disease and neoplasia |
| Hemorrhoids | Stapled hemorrhoidopexy presented as a universal treatment of choice in some material | Several options described without universal superiority |
| Anorectal malformations | Older sources emphasize invertogram | Classical examination method retained, with modern anatomical imaging distinguished |
| Alvarado score | Earlier chat called the 10-point original Alvarado table “modified” | Table preserved with explicit correction in Chapter 14; modified score omits left shift and is out of 9 |
