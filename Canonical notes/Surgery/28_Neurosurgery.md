# 28 — Neurosurgery

> [!info] Source basis
> **Doctut Merged—“NEUROSURGERY”:** hydrocephalus, shunts, hematomas, tumors, congenital neural-tube defects and brain surgery. **World of Revision—Surgery “SPECIALITY SURGERY”:** berry aneurysm, SAH and CNS tumors; trauma chapters for head injury and GCS. **Robbins Pocket Companion 9e—CNS:** raised intracranial pressure, malformations, trauma, vascular disorders and CNS tumors. **First Aid 2025—Neurology and Special Senses.** This chapter consolidates neurosurgical decision concepts rather than providing a procedural operative manual.

## 1. Intracranial pressure, cerebral perfusion and herniation

### Monro–Kellie doctrine
- Rigid adult cranium contains **brain tissue + blood + cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)**; total volume is relatively fixed.
- Increase in one compartment can initially be offset by CSF displacement and reduced venous volume; exhaustion of compensation → steep increase in **intracranial pressure (ICP)**.
- **CPP = MAP − ICP** (approximately; actual venous pressure may matter). Declining CPP → cerebral ischemia → cellular edema → further ICP rise.

### Causes
- Expanding hematoma (EDH/SDH/intraparenchymal), tumor, cerebral edema, hydrocephalus, impaired venous drainage, CNS infection/abscess.
- **Vasogenic edema:** blood–brain barrier disruption (tumors, inflammation) → extracellular fluid accumulation, especially white matter.
- **Cytotoxic edema:** ATP pump failure/hypoxic–ischemic injury → intracellular swelling.

### Clinical signs
- Progressive headache, vomiting, altered consciousness, papilledema in appropriate chronic settings, sixth-nerve palsy, focal neurologic deficits.
- **Cushing response:** hypertension + bradycardia + irregular respirations suggests severe brainstem compression/raised ICP (**late ominous sign**).
- Herniation: **uncal** (ipsilateral CN III palsy/dilated pupil and possible hemiparesis), **central transtentorial**, **subfalcine** (ACA compression) and **tonsillar** (medullary compression).

### Management principles
1. Airway/oxygenation, protect cerebral perfusion and prevent hypotension/hypoxia.
2. Urgent CT/MRI according to stability and suspected lesion; involve neurosurgery early.
3. Elevate head when appropriate, maintain neutral neck/venous drainage; analgesia/sedation and temperature control.
4. Hyperosmolar therapy under specialist protocols for acute cerebral edema/impending herniation when indicated.
5. Drain CSF for selected obstructive/communicating hydrocephalus; evacuate hematoma or resect decompressive lesion when indicated.
6. **Do not perform lumbar puncture in a patient with suspected focal mass effect/obstructive hydrocephalus until safe anatomy is established**; risk of herniation.

> [!warning] Steroids nuance
> Corticosteroids can reduce **tumor-associated vasogenic edema**, but should **not** be generalized to traumatic brain edema; they are not standard for severe TBI.

## 2. Hydrocephalus: mechanism, classification and treatment

### CSF physiology
- Mostly produced by choroid plexus; flows **lateral ventricles → foramen of Monro → third ventricle → aqueduct of Sylvius → fourth ventricle → foramina of Magendie/Luschka → subarachnoid space**, then absorbed via arachnoid/granulation and lymphatic pathways.

### Classification

| Type | Cause / site | Exam clue |
|---|---|---|
| **Noncommunicating/obstructive** | Obstruction within ventricular pathway (aqueductal stenosis, posterior fossa tumor, colloid cyst) | Proximal ventricular dilation; lumbar drainage hazardous with mass effect |
| **Communicating** | Impaired CSF absorption after meningitis/SAH, sometimes excess production | Ventricular dilation despite patent internal pathways |
| **Normal-pressure hydrocephalus (NPH)** | Disturbed CSF dynamics, mostly older adults | **Gait impairment, cognitive decline, urinary urgency/incontinence** |
| **Hydrocephalus ex vacuo** | Loss of brain volume causes compensatory ventricular dilation | Enlarged sulci + ventricles; **not** high-pressure obstruction |

### Presentation
- **Infants:** increasing head circumference, tense/bulging fontanelle, separated sutures, **sunset eyes**, irritability/vomiting.
- **Older children/adults:** headache, papilledema, vomiting, visual deterioration, gait/mental-status changes.
- Imaging documents ventricular enlargement, obstruction and underlying lesion; **MRI** for congenital/anatomical causes and tumor assessment; CT for emergencies.

### Management
- **Endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV):** creates alternate CSF route for selected noncommunicating hydrocephalus; sometimes combined with choroid-plexus cauterization in particular pediatric settings.
- **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt:** proximal catheter in ventricle, valve/regulated drainage, distal catheter into peritoneal cavity. **Ventriculoatrial** route in selected situations.
- Treat cause where feasible (tumor, infection/hemorrhage); external ventricular drain for selected acute conditions.

### VP shunt complications

| Problem | Clue |
|---|---|
| Obstruction | Recurrent symptoms of high ICP, ventricular enlargement (not always prominent) |
| Infection | Fever, irritability, meningitis/peritonitis and shunt dysfunction |
| Overdrainage | Positional headache, **slit ventricles**, subdural hygroma/hematoma |
| Mechanical | Disconnection, migration, fracture, abdominal pseudocyst |

**Clinical point:** sudden vomiting/lethargy in a child with a shunt is a possible **shunt emergency**, even without fever.

## 3. Head injury and intracranial hematomas

### Primary vs secondary injury
- **Primary:** contusion, laceration, axonal shear, hemorrhage at impact.
- **Secondary:** hypoxia/hypotension, edema, seizures, excitotoxicity, ischemia, infection.
- Preventing secondary injury is the major initial lifesaving intervention.

| Condition | Mechanism | Imaging | Key treatment concept |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epidural hematoma** | Commonly middle meningeal artery associated with temporal bone injury | **Lens/biconvex**, limited by sutures | Rapid neurosurgical evacuation when size/physiology indicate |
| **Acute subdural hematoma** | Bridging veins and/or cortical vessel injury | **Crescentic**, may cross sutures | Evaluate mass effect, neurology, evacuation criteria |
| **Chronic SDH** | Slow venous collection, elderly/anticoagulated | Hypodense/mixed crescent | Selected burr-hole drainage; manage recurrence |
| **Contusion** | Focal bruised cortex, often frontal/temporal | Hemorrhagic parenchymal lesions | Observation vs surgery for mass effect/progression |
| **Diffuse axonal injury (DAI)** | Rotational/shearing injury | MRI sensitive; CT may be subtle | Supportive neurocritical care; prevent secondary injury |

### GCS and severity
- **E4 + V5 + M6 = 15**; mild GCS 13–15, moderate 9–12, severe ≤8 in classic classification.
- Document component scores and not-testable verbal components (e.g., intubated).
- Clinical deterioration, unequal pupils, new focal deficits, seizures or progressive hematoma → urgent neuroimaging and specialist evaluation.

### Neurosurgical access names
- **Burr hole:** small cranial opening for drainage/monitor, often chronic SDH.
- **Craniotomy:** bone flap temporarily removed and **replaced** after operation.
- **Craniectomy:** bone left **off** to permit decompression, replaced in later cranioplasty if appropriate.
- **Decompressive craniectomy** reduces ICP from refractory swelling in carefully selected situations; not equivalent to evacuation of every small hematoma.

## 4. Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) and cerebral aneurysms

- **Berry/saccular aneurysm:** classically arises at Circle of Willis bifurcations; anterior communicating/anterior cerebral junction commonly tested in source.
- Rupture causes **sudden thunderclap “worst headache of life”**, meningism, vomiting, photophobia and possible reduced consciousness.
- **Initial investigation:** urgent **noncontrast CT head**; further testing (vascular imaging/LP when appropriate and safe) depends on timing and clinical scenario.
- **CT angiography/digital subtraction angiography** characterize aneurysm for treatment planning.
- Secure aneurysm with **endovascular coiling** or **microsurgical clipping** based on lesion anatomy and team assessment.
- **Nimodipine** is used to lower risk of delayed cerebral ischemia after aneurysmal SAH; it **does not simply prevent the angiographic occurrence of all vasospasm**.
- Complications: rebleeding, **vasospasm/delayed cerebral ischemia**, acute hydrocephalus, hyponatremia, seizures.

## 5. Brain tumors: location and pattern recognition

### Basic classification
- **Primary CNS tumors** arise from glial, meningeal, neuronal, embryonal or other CNS structures; **metastasis** is an important cause of intracranial tumor in adults.
- **MRI with contrast** is central to evaluation where appropriate; neuroimaging alone does not always substitute for molecular/histologic diagnosis.
- Surgical goals: obtain tissue diagnosis, reduce mass effect, preserve neurologic function, maximize safe resection.

| Tumor | Characteristic notes from sources | Important surgical concept |
|---|---|---|
| **Pilocytic astrocytoma** | Pediatric, often cerebellar; cyst + **mural nodule**; classic grade 1 | Resect if safe; prognosis often favorable |
| **Glioblastoma** | Aggressive diffuse astrocytic tumor; **butterfly lesion** crossing corpus callosum | Maximal safe resection when possible + multimodal therapy |
| **Oligodendroglioma** | Fried-egg cells, chicken-wire vessels and calcification in classic histology | Integrated molecular diagnosis matters; surgery + oncology |
| **Meningioma** | Dural-based extra-axial, possible **dural tail**, whorls/psammoma bodies | Observe selected small lesions vs resection/radiation |
| **Schwannoma (vestibular)** | Cerebellopontine angle/internal acoustic meatus; CN VIII hearing loss | Microsurgery or radiosurgery depending on size/function |
| **Medulloblastoma** | Pediatric malignant posterior fossa embryonal tumor | Resection/staging and adjuvant therapy; CSF dissemination risk |
| **Ependymoma** | Ventricular/central canal origin; posterior fossa in children | Extent of resection and spinal-axis assessment |
| **Pituitary region lesion** | Visual field defect/endocrine changes; suprasellar pressure | Endoscopic transsphenoidal approaches common in appropriate cases |
| **Brain metastasis** | Adults, often multiple at gray–white junction | Selective surgery for symptomatic/accessible lesions, stereotactic radiation and systemic management |

### High-yield pathology links
- **NF1:** optic pathway glioma, neurofibromas; **NF2:** bilateral vestibular schwannomas, meningiomas/ependymomas in classic exam associations.
- **Von Hippel–Lindau:** cerebellar hemangioblastoma; **Turcot** syndromic links include CNS tumors.
- **Meningioma:** EMA/other appropriate markers; **oligodendroglioma:** characteristic 1p/19q codeletion and IDH alteration in integrated diagnosis.

> [!warning] Source ranking cautions
> Older notes state blanket “most common primary brain tumor = glioma,” “most common child = medulloblastoma,” etc. The answer depends on **age, benign/malignant, intra- vs extra-axial, and tumor classification year**; do not reproduce an unqualified rank as universal.

## 6. Spinal cord compression and spinal tumors

- **Emergency signs:** progressive limb weakness, hyperreflexia/long-tract signs, saddle sensory deficits, urinary retention or incontinence, severe back pain with neurologic deficit.
- Causes: metastatic epidural spinal cord compression, vertebral fracture/hematoma, abscess, disc disease and primary tumors.
- **MRI spine** is the crucial investigation when available and clinically feasible; suspected compression with neurologic dysfunction is urgent.
- Treat cause with decompression/stabilization, appropriate oncological therapy, antimicrobials/drainage or other specialist interventions.

### Tumor location classes
- **Extradural:** vertebral metastases common in adults; may cause mechanical instability.
- **Intradural extramedullary:** **meningioma, schwannoma**.
- **Intramedullary:** **ependymoma/astrocytoma**, syringomyelia differential.

## 7. Neural tube defects and Chiari malformations

| Lesion | Anatomy | Clinical concern |
|---|---|---|
| **Spina bifida occulta** | Vertebral arch defect, skin often intact | Cutaneous tuft/dimple; possible tethered cord |
| **Meningocele** | Meninges/CSF herniate | Usually no neural tissue inside sac |
| **Myelomeningocele** | Neural tissue/meninges within defect | Neurologic deficit, bowel/bladder problems; infection risk |
| **Chiari II** | Hindbrain displacement associated with open spinal dysraphism | **Hydrocephalus**, brainstem signs |
| **Tethered cord** | Abnormally fixed cord stretched during growth | Progressive neurologic/bladder/foot symptoms |

- For open myelomeningocele: protect neural sac, prevent infection/heat loss, assess motor/bladder function and **early multidisciplinary neurosurgical care**.
- Prenatal folate deficiency association is a key prevention point in obstetric/embryology sources.

## 8. Other neurosurgical emergencies

- **Intracranial abscess/subdural empyema:** fever/headache/focal deficits ± seizures; antibiotic therapy plus urgent drainage/source control for indicated cases. Avoid lumbar puncture with mass effect.
- **Acute obstructive hydrocephalus:** can cause rapid deterioration; emergency CSF diversion when appropriate.
- **Cauda equina syndrome:** low back pain with saddle anesthesia, bilateral weakness and urinary changes → emergency MRI and decompression when compressive cause confirmed.
- **Intracranial vascular malformation:** AVM/cavernoma can present with hemorrhage/seizures; management tailored to lesion and risk.

### Exam recognition matrix

| Stem | Think |
|---|---|
| Dilated ipsilateral pupil + deteriorating consciousness | **Uncal herniation** |
| Elderly with gait difficulty → dementia → urinary issues | **Normal-pressure hydrocephalus** |
| CSF collection overdrained by VP shunt | **Subdural hygroma/hematoma** |
| Thunderclap headache | **Aneurysmal SAH** until excluded |
| Lens hematoma after temporal impact | **EDH** |
| Crescentic extra-axial blood | **SDH** |
| Child with cerebellar cyst and mural nodule | **Pilocytic astrocytoma** |
| Adult cross-midline infiltrative lesion | **Glioblastoma** |
| Intradural extramedullary spinal mass | **Meningioma/schwannoma** |
