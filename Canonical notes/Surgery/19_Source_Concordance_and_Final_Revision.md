## 2. Source conflicts: what NOT to memorize blindly

| Topic | Conflicting/old source statement | Carefully framed conclusion in notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Number of Couinaud segments** | Doctut OCR repeatedly says “2” | Actual enumerated map is **I–VIII, with IVa/IVb**; use the correct segment table |
| **Child-Pugh scale** | Doctut OCR has “Child C 20–25,” maximum 25 | WOR shows **5 criteria ×1–3 = 5–15; A 5–6, B 7–9, C 10–15** |
| **Liver abscess organisms** | WOR says *E. coli* common globally and *Klebsiella* in Asia; Doctut cites mixed routes and cryptogenic cases | State organism and likely source **by geography, route and culture**, not universal single organism |
| **Amoebic abscess treatment** | Old fixed metronidazole doses/duration and drainage “>5 cm” used as absolute | **Amoebicide + luminal cure**, and select drainage by size, location, risk and response; do not turn a revision example into a prescription |
| **Hydatid PAIR** | WOR calls PAIR “first line,” while both sources list numerous contraindications | **WHO cyst stage, biliary communication and accessibility** determine whether medical/PAIR/catheter/surgical strategy is appropriate |
| **Hydatid life cycle** | Some Doctut OCR reverses hosts or names | **Dog definitive, sheep usual intermediate, humans accidental intermediate** |
| **Benign liver adenoma** | Doctut says always excise, WOR highlights size >5 cm; Robbins emphasizes molecular subtypes | **Bleeding/malignancy risk is subtype-, sex-, size- and growth-dependent**; not all adenomas behave identically |
| **HCC marker** | Some notes call AFP >400 “conclusive”; elsewhere “biopsy confirms dx” while others say “no biopsy” | **Multiphase imaging in appropriate liver/clinical context** and selective biopsy; AFP alone is not definitive |
| **BCLC therapy** | WOR lists sorafenib for all stage C | Historical exam schema, **not** comprehensive modern systemic treatment guidance |
| **HCC metastasis vs primary** | “HCC most common malignant liver tumor” sometimes stated | **HCC most common primary** malignant liver tumor; **metastases are commonest overall** |
| **Portal pressure thresholds** | Doctut describes high portal pressures with absolute number; WOR gives **HVPG** stages | Differentiate **direct portal pressure** from **HVPG**, especially in presinusoidal disease |
| **Variceal bleed** | Doctut mentions NG and PPI in a universal emergency sequence | Most specific measures are **bleeding resuscitation, vasoactive therapy, endoscopic source control, and TIPS rescue**; do not treat NG placement as mandatory |
| **Portal shunts** | Older texts emphasize Sugiura, Eck, Linton, Warren as routine options | Learn **connections and selective/nonselective anatomy**; endoscopy, TIPS and transplantation have changed indications |
| **Asymptomatic gallstones** | WOR/Doctut list **diabetes**, stone size >2 vs >2.5 cm, typhoid and porcelain GB as universal indications | These are **historic selective considerations** and not universal instructions to remove GB |
| **Cholecystitis timing** | “Immediate for all” or “conservative in all 1st/3rd trimester” | Source pathway should be adapted to severity/physiology; **early surgery** in suitable cases and **drainage** in high-risk severe disease |
| **Gallstone ileus** | “Radiopaque stone” often presented as required element of Rigler triad | Many stones are radiolucent; **pneumobilia + obstruction + ectopic stone** is conceptual triad, CT may define it |
| **ERCP** | Often labeled diagnostic “gold standard” for every biliary problem | **MRCP** performs noninvasive imaging; **ERCP primarily therapeutic** and has postprocedural pancreatitis risk |
| **Choledochal cyst IVb** | WOR lists **Kasai** for IVb; Doctut type IV/V → universal transplant | **Todani morphology**, bile-duct excision/reconstruction, and extent of intrahepatic disease should guide individual planning; no single operation suits all IV/V |
| **BDI timing** | Sharp cutoffs “24–48 h” or “2–12 weeks” in notes | Anatomy, sepsis control and experienced HPB reconstruction matter more than an isolated date |
| **Pancreatitis nutrition** | Older Robbins paragraph mentions blanket “rest the pancreas” | Source also emphasizes nutrition; do **not** equate all acute pancreatitis with prolonged mandatory fasting |
| **Pancreatitis severity** | WOR calls CT severity index the “best” score | Different scores predict different risks; **persistent organ failure >48 h** is central to revised Atlanta severe designation |
| **Pseudocyst operation** | “6 cm, 6 weeks, wall >6 mm” listed as mandatory indication | These are **classic historical exam cues**; symptoms, infection, bleeding, obstruction, maturity and duct anatomy determine decisions |
| **Chronic pancreatitis duct thresholds** | WOR uses >5 mm, Doctut >7 mm for Puestow-like drainage | **Dilated duct** is key principle; choose operation by head disease, duct diameter and anatomy |
| **Whipple reconstruction** | Some lists include a **fourth jejunojejunostomy** as always required | Main reconstruction conceptual triad **PJ + HJ + GJ/DJ**; variations exist |
| **Pancreatic vessel contact** | WOR/Doctut use inconsistent degrees for borderline vs unresectable | Treat as **resectability concept**, not a universal current numeric protocol |
| **Splenic injury grades** | Legacy AAST sizes/locations lack newer vascular refinement | Use sources for **classic injury grades** but prioritize **hemodynamic stability** and expert review |
| **ITP splenectomy** | Doctut gives highly variable platelet thresholds and outdated HIV/ITP statements | Splenectomy is **selected second-line** treatment; do not act on those numeric OCR statements |
| **OPSI timing/vaccine dates** | Some sources fix vaccination to POD2/3 and quote mortality numbers without context | Plan **preop when possible and postop when safe**, and follow actual immunization schedule; risk lasts for life |

## 3. Integrated must-remember numbers and landmarks

| Recall | Number / pattern |
|---|---|
| **Functional segments of liver** | **I–VIII** (IV split IVa/IVb) |
| **Right hepatectomy** | **V–VIII** |
| **Left hepatectomy** | **II, III, IV** |
| **GB cancer hepatic bed** | **IVb + V** |
| **Child-Pugh** | **5–15**; A5–6, B7–9, C10–15 |
| **Milan** | **1 lesion ≤5 cm OR up to 3 lesions each ≤3 cm**, no macrovascular invasion/extrahepatic spread |
| **HVPG normal** | **1–5 mmHg** in WOR |
| **Clinically significant portal HTN** | **HVPG ≥10 mmHg** |
| **Variceal bleeding risk** | HVPG about **≥12 mmHg** (WOR) |
| **SAAG portal HTN** | **≥1.1 g/dL** |
| **Choledochal cyst most common** | **Todani I** |
| **Biliary atresia most common** | **Type III** |
| **GB cancer T1a** | **Mucosa/lamina propria → simple cholecystectomy** in appropriate cases |
| **Mirizzi I** | **External compression only** |
| **Charcot triad** | **Fever + jaundice + RUQ pain** |
| **Reynolds pentad** | Triad + **shock + confusion** |
| **Acute pancreatitis diagnosis** | **2 of 3:** typical pain, lipase/amylase ≥3×ULN, typical imaging |
| **Severe revised Atlanta** | **Persistent organ failure >48 h** |
| **Pseudocyst / WON** | Mature collection generally **>4 weeks**, fluid versus necrotic debris |
| **Insulinoma** | **Whipple triad; 72-hour supervised fast** |
| **Whipple reconstructions** | **PJ, HJ, GJ/DJ** |
| **Splenic rib levels** | **9–11** (long axis near 10th rib) |
| **ITP vs hereditary spherocytosis** | Immune platelet removal **vs** deformable RBC defect/hemolysis |
| **OPSI organisms** | **Pneumococcus, meningococcus, Hib** |

## 5. High-yield final comparison set

| Distinguish | Core discriminating feature |
|---|---|
| **Amoebic vs pyogenic abscess** | Portal amoeba, solitary classic **vs** bacterial/biliary, toxic/multiple common |
| **Hydatid CE2 vs CE3a** | Honeycomb daughter cysts **vs** floating detached membrane |
| **FNH vs adenoma** | Scar/Kupffer uptake, usually observe **vs** OCP-related tumor with hemorrhage/malignancy risk |
| **HCC vs metastasis** | Arterial enhancement/washout in at-risk liver **vs** secondary deposits commonly portal phase hypovascular |
| **NCPF/EHPVO vs cirrhosis** | Portal varices with relatively preserved liver reserve **vs** fibrosis/nodules, synthetic impairment |
| **HPS vs PoPH** | Vascular dilatation/hypoxemia **vs** pulmonary arterial hypertension |
| **Biliary colic vs acute cholecystitis vs cholangitis** | Transient obstruction **vs** GB inflammation **vs** infected CBD obstruction |
| **Mirizzi vs choledochal cyst** | Impacted neck stone compresses duct **vs** congenital duct dilatation |
| **Gallstone ileus vs Bouveret** | Small-bowel obstruction **vs** proximal duodenal/gastric outlet obstruction |
| **Pseudocyst vs WON** | Mature homogeneous **fluid** **vs** encapsulated **necrotic debris** |
| **Pancreatic SCN vs MCN vs IPMN** | Benign microcystic/central scar **vs** ovarian stroma **vs** ductal mucin/fish-mouth ampulla |
| **Puestow vs Frey vs Beger vs Whipple** | Duct drainage **vs** head coring+drainage **vs** duodenum-preserving head resection **vs** pancreatoduodenectomy |
| **Accessory spleen vs splenosis** | Congenital near hilum/tail **vs** post-trauma implants |
| **Hypersplenism vs OPSI** | Cytopenias from spleen sequestration **vs** infection after splenic loss |

> [!summary] Revision sequence
> **1.** Segmental anatomy/portal pressure → **2.** liver masses/HCC → **3.** gallstone pathways and BDI → **4.** acute/chronic pancreatitis and named operations → **5.** pancreatic cancers → **6.** spleen/trauma/splenectomy. For image-based questions, prioritize US/CT/MRCP phase recognition and duct diagrams, not memorized labels alone.
