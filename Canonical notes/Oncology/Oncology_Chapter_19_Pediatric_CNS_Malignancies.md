# Chapter 19: Pediatric Malignancies & Central Nervous System Tumors

## 19.1 Pediatric Embryonal & Abdominal Tumors

### 1. Overview of Pediatric Oncology
Pediatric malignancies differ fundamentally from adult cancers: they originate predominantly from embryonal or mesenchymal tissues (blastemas and sarcomas rather than carcinomas) and demonstrate higher responsiveness to chemotherapy and radiation.

---

### 2. Wilms Tumor (Nephroblastoma)
Wilms tumor is the most common primary renal tumor in children (85% of cases; peak incidence age 2–5 years).

#### Genetic Associations & Syndromes
* Driven by mutations or epigenetic alterations at chromosome 11p13 (***WT1*** gene) and 11p15.5 (***WT2*** / *IGF2* locus).
* Classic Associated Syndromes:
  * **WAGR Syndrome**: **W**ilms tumor, **A**niridia (absence of iris), **G**enitourinary anomalies, **R**etardation (microdeletion at 11p13 involving *WT1* and *PAX6*).
  * **Denys-Drash Syndrome**: Point mutation in *WT1*; triad of Wilms tumor, early-onset nephrotic syndrome (diffuse mesangial sclerosis), and male pseudohermaphroditism.
  * **Beckwith-Wiedemann Syndrome (BWS)**: Genomic imprinting defect at 11p15.5 (*IGF2* overexpression); triad of **Macroglossia**, **Omphalocele / Umbilical hernia**, and **Hemihyperplasia**; high risk of Wilms tumor and Hepatoblastoma.

#### Clinical Presentation
* Asymptomatic, smooth, firm **abdominal mass** discovered incidentally by parents during bathing.
* **Critical Physical Exam Warning**: **Pre-operative abdominal palpation MUST be performed with extreme gentleness (or avoided)** to prevent rupture of the fragile tumor capsule and stage spillage into the peritoneal cavity.
* Hypertension (50% of cases due to renin secretion/renal ischemia), hematuria, abdominal pain.
* **Mass Behavior**: Does **NOT** cross the abdominal midline (contrasts with neuroblastoma).

#### Histopathology & Management
* **Triphasic Histology**: Mixture of **Blastemal** (sheets of small blue cells), **Epithelial** (primitive tubules/glomeruli), and **Stromal** (fibrous/myxoid tissue) elements.
* Anaplasia (TP53 mutation) defines high-risk unfavorable histology.
* Treatment:
  * North American (COG) Protocol: Primary Radical Nephrectomy $\rightarrow$ Adjuvant Chemotherapy (Vincristine + Dactinomycin $\pm$ Doxorubicin $\pm$ Radiotherapy).
  * European (SIOP) Protocol: Pre-operative Chemotherapy $\rightarrow$ Delayed Nephrectomy.

---

### 3. Neuroblastoma
Neuroblastoma is an embryonal malignancy of the sympathetic nervous system arising from **neural crest cells**; it is the most common extracranial solid tumor of infancy and childhood (peak age <2 years; 30% diagnosed in infancy).

#### Anatomical Origin & Clinical Features
* **Primary Sites**: Adrenal Medulla (50%), retroperitoneal sympathetic chain (30%), posterior mediastinum (15%), neck/pelvis (5%).
* **Presentation**: Firm, nodular, irregular **abdominal mass that FREQUENTLY CROSSES THE MIDLINE** and encases major retroperitoneal vessels (aorta, SMA).
* Paraneoplastic & Metastatic Phenomena:
  * **Opsoclonus-Myoclonus-Ataxia Syndrome ("Dancing Eyes, Dancing Feet")**: Autoimmune paraneoplastic cerebellar ataxia.
  * **Periorbital Ecchymosis ("Raccoon Eyes")**: Orbital metastases.
  * **Blueberry Muffin Baby**: Multiple cutaneous metastatic nodules in infants (Stage MS).
  * Intraspinal extension through neural foramina causing spinal cord compression (**"Dumbbell Tumor"**).

| Feature | Wilms tumor (nephroblastoma) | Neuroblastoma |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | Renal parenchyma | Sympathetic neural-crest lineage; commonly adrenal medulla or sympathetic chain |
| **Mass character** | Usually smooth and well circumscribed | Often irregular / nodular; may encase major vessels |
| **Midline behavior** | Usually does **not** cross the midline | Frequently crosses the midline |
| **Histology** | Triphasic: blastemal, epithelial, and stromal elements | Small round blue cells; neuropil and rosettes may be seen |
| **Urinary catecholamine metabolites** | VMA / HVA usually normal | VMA / HVA frequently elevated |

#### Diagnostic Biomarkers & Molecular Risk
* **24-hour urinary catecholamine metabolites**: Elevated **Vanillylmandelic Acid (VMA)** and **Homovanillic Acid (HVA)** present in >90% of patients.
* Imaging of choice: Contrast CT/MRI + **Technetium-99m / $^{123}\text{I}$-MIBG (Metaiodobenzylguanidine) Scintigraphy** (specifically taken up by sympathoadrenal cells).
* **Single Most Critical Genetic Biomarker**: ***MYCN* Gene Amplification** (>10 copies on 2p24); automatically classifies patient into **High-Risk category** regardless of age or stage, requiring aggressive high-dose chemo, autologous stem cell rescue, surgery, RT, and **anti-GD2 immunotherapy (Dinutuximab)**.

---

### 4. Retinoblastoma
* Most common primary intraocular malignancy of childhood (peak age <2 years).
* Driven by loss-of-function mutations in the ***RB1* gene** (13q14).
* **Hereditary (40%)**: Germline mutation (first hit) + somatic mutation (second hit); bilateral, multifocal; high risk of secondary osteosarcoma. **Sporadic (60%)**: Two somatic hits; unilateral.
* Clinical Signs: **Leukocoria** (white pupillary reflex / "cat's eye reflex") and Strabismus.
* Histology: **Flexner-Wintersteiner Rosettes** (columnar cells surrounding a central clear lumen).

## 19.2 Central Nervous System Tumors & Carcinoma of Unknown Primary

### 1. Primary Central Nervous System Tumors
Brain tumors are the most common solid tumors of childhood and the second most common childhood malignancy overall (after leukemia). In adults, brain metastases exceed primary CNS tumors by 10:1.

#### Anatomical Distribution Rule
* **Pediatric Brain Tumors**: 70% are **Infratentorial** (posterior fossa: cerebellum, 4th ventricle).
* **Adult Brain Tumors**: 70% are **Supratentorial** (cerebral hemispheres).

---

### 2. Adult Brain Tumors

#### Glioblastoma (GBM - Astrocytoma Grade 4)
* Most common and most aggressive primary malignant brain tumor in adults (peak age 50–70 years).
* Molecular Classification (WHO 2021): Defined as **IDH-wildtype**.
* Imaging: Ring-enhancing lesion with central necrosis and extensive perilesional vasogenic edema; frequently crosses the corpus callosum (**"Butterfly Glioma"**).
* Histology: Dense anaplastic astrocytic cells with **Pseudopalisading Necrosis** around ischemic centers and prominent **Microvascular / Endothelial Proliferation**.
* Treatment: Maximum safe surgical resection followed by the **Stupp Protocol** (Concomitant Radiotherapy + Daily **Temozolomide** followed by 6 cycles of adjuvant Temozolomide).
* Biomarker: **_MGMT_ Promoter Methylation** silences DNA repair enzyme O-6-methylguanine-DNA methyltransferase, predicting high responsiveness to Temozolomide.

| Glioblastoma histological feature | Typical appearance | Pathological significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pseudopalisading necrosis** | Tumor cells arranged in a dense band around a central necrotic area | Often reflects tumor-cell migration away from hypoxic / necrotic regions |
| **Microvascular proliferation** | Glomeruloid clusters of proliferating endothelial cells | Reflects prominent tumor-driven angiogenesis, including VEGF signaling |

#### Oligodendroglioma
* Adult tumor (peak age 35–50 years); commonly involves frontal lobes; presents with seizures.
* Pathognomonic Molecular Signature: **Co-deletion of chromosomes 1p and 19q (1p/19q codeletion)** combined with **_IDH1_ or _IDH2_ mutation**. Highly chemosensitive.
* Histology: Monomorphic cells with spherical nuclei and clear perinuclear halos (**"Fried-Egg" appearance**) embedded in a delicate capillary network (**"Chicken-Wire" vasculature**).

#### Meningioma
* Most common primary non-malignant CNS tumor in adults (female predominance 2:1; express progesterone receptors).
* Arises from **arachnoid cap cells**; extra-axial location attached to the dura.
* MRI: Broad dural attachment (**"Dural Tail Sign"**); homogeneous enhancement.
* Histology: Concentric whorls of meningothelial cells and calcified **Psammoma Bodies**.

### 3. Pediatric Brain Tumors

| Tumor Type | Anatomical Location | Key Histological / Molecular Features | Clinical Presentation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pilocytic Astrocytoma** (WHO Grade 1) | Cerebellum (most common site) | Cystic mass with an **enhancing mural nodule**; **Rosenthal Fibers** (corkscrew eosinophilic rods) and eosinophilic granular bodies; *BRAF* fusions. | Benign, slow-growing; excellent prognosis post-resection. |
| **Medulloblastoma** (WHO Grade 4) | Cerebellar vermis / Roof of 4th ventricle | Most common malignant brain tumor in children; highly aggressive; **Small round blue cells** forming **Homer-Wright Rosettes**; high propensity for CSF seeding (**"Drop Metastases"** to spine). | Increased ICP, truncal ataxia; treated with surgery + chemo + craniospinal irradiation. |
| **Ependymoma** (WHO Grade 2/3) | Floor of 4th ventricle | Arises from ependymal lining; **Perivascular Pseudorosettes** (tumor cells arranged around blood vessels with an intervening nuclear-free zone); plastic tumor squeezing through Foramina of Luschka/Magendie. | Hydrocephalus; incomplete resection common due to brainstem adherence. |
| **Craniopharyngioma** | Suprasellar region (derived from Rathke pouch) | Bimodally distributed; calcified cystic mass containing **"machine-oil" fluid**; wet keratin and palisaded epithelium. | Bitemporal hemianopia, hypopituitarism, diabetes insipidus. |

### 4. Carcinoma of Unknown Primary (CUP)
Carcinoma of Unknown Primary is defined as biopsy-proven metastatic carcinoma in which the primary anatomical site cannot be identified after standardized diagnostic evaluation (5% of all solid tumors).

#### Diagnostic Evaluation Workflow
1. Comprehensive physical exam (including pelvic, rectal, breast, head/neck).
2. Computed Tomography (CT) Chest, Abdomen, and Pelvis with IV contrast.
3. Immunohistochemistry (IHC) Lineage Panel on core biopsy:
   * **CK7 / CK20 Profile**:
     * **CK7+ / CK20-**: Breast, Lung, Thyroid, Ovary, Endometrium.
     * **CK7- / CK20+**: Colorectal carcinoma.
     * **CK7+ / CK20+**: Pancreatic, Biliary, Urothelial carcinoma.
     * **CK7- / CK20-**: Prostate, Renal cell, HCC.
   * Add specific markers: TTF-1 (lung/thyroid), PSA (prostate), GATA3 (breast/urothelial), PAX8 (renal/gynecologic).
4. Molecular Tumor Profiling / NGS.
5. PET/CT imaging.

#### Specific Treatable Subsets
* **Isolated Axillary Lymphadenopathy in Females**: Managed as Stage II/III Breast Cancer (MRI Breast + Mastectomy/BCS + Axillary dissection).
* **Peritoneal Carcinomatosis in Females**: Managed as Stage III Epithelial Ovarian Cancer (Cytoreductive surgery + Carboplatin/Paclitaxel).
* **Midline Nodal Disease in Young Males**: Screen for occult Germ Cell Tumor (elevated AFP/$\beta$-hCG; treated with PEB chemotherapy).
