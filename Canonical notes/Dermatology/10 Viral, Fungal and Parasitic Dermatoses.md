# Chapter 10: Viral, Fungal, and Parasitic Dermatoses

## 10.1 Cutaneous Viral Infections

### Molluscum Contagiosum
* **Etiology**: **Molluscum Contagiosum Virus (MCV)**, a large double-stranded DNA virus belonging to the *Poxviridae* family.
* **Clinical Morphology**: Firm, smooth, pearly-white or flesh-colored, dome-shaped papules with central **umbilication**. Expressing the lesion yields a white curd-like material (**molluscum body**).
* **Anatomic Distribution**: Children (trunk and face); Adults (sexually transmitted in anogenital region).
* **Histopathology**: Large, crater-like invaginated epidermal lobules containing prominent, oval eosinophilic intracytoplasmic inclusion bodies (**Henderson-Paterson Bodies**) that compress the keratinocyte nucleus.

### Human Papillomavirus (HPV) Manifestations
* **Verruca Vulgaris (Common Warts)**: Hyperkeratotic, exophytic papules caused primarily by **HPV types 2 and 4**. Histology shows hyperkeratosis, papillomatosis, hypergranulosis, and vacuolated keratinocytes (**koilocytes**).
* **Verruca Plana (Flat Warts)**: Smooth, flat-topped, flesh-colored papules caused by **HPV types 3 and 10**.
* **Condyloma Acuminata (Anogenital Warts)**: Cauliflower-like soft papules caused by low-risk **HPV types 6 and 11**. High-risk types **16 and 18** cause cervical dysplasia and Anogenital Squamous Cell Carcinoma.

## 10.2 Fungal Dermatoses

### Superficial Mycoses & Tinea Infections
* **Dermatophytosis (Ringworm)**: Cutaneous fungal infections restricted to keratinized structures (stratum corneum, hair, nails) caused by three dermatophyte genera: ***Trichophyton*, *Microsporum*, and *Epidermophyton***.
  * **Morphology**: Annular erythematous plaques with active, scaly, vesicular advancing borders and central clearing.
* **Pityriasis (Tinea) Versicolor**:
  * Etiology: Overgrowth of lipophilic commensal yeast ***Malassezia furfur***.
  * Clinical Morphology: Macules and patches ranging from hypopigmented to hyperpigmented or erythematous with fine, bran-like scale (**furfuraceous scale**). Demonstrates yellow-gold fluorescence under Wood's lamp.
  * **KOH Examination**: Direct $10\%$ KOH mount demonstrates characteristic short, thick, unbranched hyphae mixed with round budding yeast cells (**"spaghetti and meatballs"** or "banana and grapes" pattern).

**KOH mount in pityriasis (tinea) versicolor**

The characteristic “spaghetti and meatballs” appearance consists of short, curved, coarse hyphae mixed with clusters of round budding yeast cells.

### Subcutaneous Mycoses

| Entity | Etiological Agent | Classic Clinical Features | Pathognomonic Histology / Marker |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sporotrichosis** | *Sporothrix schenckii* (Dimorphic fungus; thermal dimorphism) | Inoculated via rose thorns/splinters; primary nodule at puncture site followed by linear **lymphocutaneous nodular spread**. | Cigar-shaped budding yeasts and **Asteroid Bodies** (Splendore-Hoeppli phenomenon). |
| **Mycetoma (Madura Foot)** | Actinomycetoma (Bacterial) or Eumycetoma (Fungal) | Triad: Painless subcutaneous mass, multiple draining sinus tracts, and **discharge containing granules/grains**. | Colored grains (white, yellow, black, red) examined microscopically. |
| **Chromoblastomycosis** | Dematiaceous (pigmented) fungi (*Fonsecaea pedrosoi*) | Verrucous, cauliflower-like crusted plaques on lower extremities. | Thick-walled, chestnut-brown, muriform cells (**Medlar bodies / "Copper pennies"**). |

## 10.3 Ectoparasitic Infestations & Cutaneous Protozoa

### Scabies
* **Etiology**: Infestation by the human itch mite ***Sarcoptes scabiei* var. *hominis***. Female mites burrow into the stratum corneum to deposit eggs and scybala (feces).
* **Clinical Presentation**:
  * **Severe Nocturnal Pruritus**: Driven by a Type IV hypersensitivity reaction to mite proteins and feces.
  * **Pathognomonic Burrows**: Serpentine, thread-like gray-white intraepidermal tracks ending in a tiny vesicle or papule.
  * **Classic Distribution**: Interdigital web spaces of hands, flexor wrists, axillae, umbilicus, belt line, and genitalia (**Circle of Hebra**).
* **Norwegian (Crusted) Scabies**: Hyperkeratotic, heavily crusted form containing millions of mites; seen in immunocompromised or elderly patients; minimal pruritus due to diminished cell-mediated immunity.
* **Treatment**: Topical **Permethrin 5% cream** applied neck-down for 8–12 hours (repeated in 7 days) OR oral **Ivermectin** ($200\,\mu\text{g/kg}$).

| Anatomical level | Scabies finding |
| :--- | :--- |
| Stratum corneum | The female mite burrows within the stratum corneum, forming an intraepidermal track. |
| Within the burrow | Eggs and faecal pellets (scybala) may be present behind the mite. |
| Deeper epidermis | The burrow does not normally extend into the deeper viable epidermis. |

### Cutaneous Leishmaniasis
* **Etiology & Vector**: Transmitted by the bite of infected female **phlebotomine sandflies** (*Phlebotomus* in Old World; *Lutzomyia* in New World) carrying ***Leishmania tropica / L. major***.
* **Clinical Presentation**:
  * **Old World Leishmaniasis**: Oriental sore / Delhi boil — begins as an erythematous papule that enlarges into a painless, crusted, volcaniform ulcer with raised indurated borders.
  * **Chiclero Ulcer**: Destructive ulceration of the ear pinna caused by *L. mexicana*.
* **Diagnosis**: Tissue smear from ulcer margin demonstrating intracellular **Leishman-Donovan (LD) bodies** (amastigote form within macrophages containing a nucleus and distinctive rod-shaped kinetoplast).

## 10.4 Diagnostic Summary of Cutaneous Parasitic & Fungal Infections

| Disease | Causative Agent | Key Diagnostic Test | Pathognomonic Finding |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Molluscum Contagiosum** | Poxvirus (MCV) | Biopsy / Smear | Eosinophilic **Henderson-Paterson bodies** |
| **Tinea Versicolor** | *Malassezia furfur* | 10% KOH Mount | **"Spaghetti and meatballs"** hyphae & spores |
| **Chromoblastomycosis** | Dematiaceous fungi | HPE / Direct Mount | Chestnut-brown **Medlar bodies / Copper pennies** |
| **Scabies** | *Sarcoptes scabiei* | Scraping of burrow | Mites, ova, or scybala in stratum corneum |
| **Cutaneous Leishmaniasis** | *Leishmania* spp. | Giemsa Stain | Intracellular **LD bodies with kinetoplast** |
