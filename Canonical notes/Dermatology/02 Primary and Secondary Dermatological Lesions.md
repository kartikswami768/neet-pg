# Chapter 02: Primary & Secondary Dermatological Lesions

Dermatological diagnosis relies on systematic physical examination and accurate morphological identification of cutaneous lesions. Skin lesions are broadly categorized into **primary lesions** (initial clinical manifestations of a disease process) and **secondary lesions** (evolutionary changes resulting from natural disease progression, trauma, or secondary infection).

---

## 2.1 Primary Skin Lesions

Primary skin lesions represent the earliest observable morphological changes generated directly by underlying cutaneous pathology.

| Primary lesion category | Lesion | Typical size / description |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flat and non-palpable | Macule | < 1 cm; flat colour change. |
| Flat and non-palpable | Patch | > 1 cm; flat colour change. |
| Solid and elevated | Papule | < 1 cm; palpable, solid elevation. |
| Solid and elevated | Plaque | > 1 cm; palpable, plateau-like elevation. |
| Solid and elevated | Nodule | A deeper solid lesion; depth is greater than or comparable to its surface dimensions. |
| Fluid- or pus-filled | Vesicle | < 1 cm; small fluid-filled cavity. |
| Fluid- or pus-filled | Bulla | > 1 cm; large fluid-filled cavity. |
| Fluid- or pus-filled | Pustule | A pus-filled lesion. |

### 1. Flat, Non-Palpable Color Changes
- **Macule**: A flat, circumscribed area of skin color change measuring **$<1\text{ cm}$** in diameter without elevation or depression relative to surrounding skin.
  - *Examples*: Freckle (ephelis), labial macule, vitiligo focal macule, junctional nevus.
- **Patch**: A flat, non-palpable circumscribed area of skin color change measuring **$>1\text{ cm}$** in diameter.
  - *Examples*: Vitiligo patch, Herald patch of Pityriasis rosea, Café-au-lait patch, Congenital nevus.

### 2. Circumscribed, Solid, Raised Lesions
- **Papule**: A solid, elevated, palpable lesion measuring **$<1\text{ cm}$** in diameter.
  - *Examples*: Acne papule, Lichen planus polygonal papule, Neurofibroma, Verruca vulgaris.
- **Plaque**: A solid, elevated, flat-topped, plateau-like lesion measuring **$>1\text{ cm}$** in diameter, often formed by confluence of papules.
  - *Examples*: Psoriasis vulgaris plaque, Lichen planus plaque, Mycosis fungoides plaque.
- **Nodule**: A circumscribed, solid, elevated, dome-shaped or spherical lesion measuring $>0.5-1\text{ cm}$ where **depth of involvement is greater than width**. Involves dermis or subcutis.
  - *Examples*: Dermatofibroma, Rheumatoid nodule, Erythema nodosum, Keratoacanthoma.

### 3. Fluid-Filled Blistering Lesions
- **Vesicle**: A small, circumscribed, elevated, clear fluid-filled blister measuring **$<1\text{ cm}$** in diameter.
  - *Examples*: Varicella (chickenpox "dewdrops on rose petal"), Herpes zoster, Herpes simplex, Dyshidrotic eczema.
- **Bulla**: A large, circumscribed, elevated, clear fluid-filled blister measuring **$>1\text{ cm}$** in diameter.
  - *Examples*: Bullous pemphigoid (tense bulla), Pemphigus vulgaris (flaccid bulla), Friction bulla.

### 4. Pus-Filled & Edematous Lesions
- **Pustule**: A circumscribed, elevated blister containing purulent exudate (pus composed of leukocytes and cellular debris), ranging from pinpoint-sized to several millimeters.
  - *Examples*: Pustular psoriasis, Acne pustule, Folliculitis.
- **Abscess**: A focal, localized collection of purulent exudate situated deep within the dermis or subcutaneous tissue, usually accompanied by acute inflammatory signs.
- **Wheal (Urticaria)**: An erythematous, edematous, evanescent (fleeting) papule or plaque exhibiting **central blanching pallor** and peripheral erythema. Caused by acute dermal edema secondary to histamine-mediated capillary permeability. **Characteristically lasts $<24\text{ hours}$ in a single location**.
  - *Examples*: Acute urticaria, Dermographism.
- **Cyst**: An elevated, circumscribed, wall-encapsulated cavity lined by true epithelium containing liquid or semisolid material.
  - *Examples*: Epidermoid cyst (exhibits a central black punctum), Pilar cyst.

## 2.2 Secondary Skin Lesions

Secondary lesions arise as a result of evolution, regression, trauma, rubbing, or secondary infection of primary lesions.

| Secondary Lesion | Definition & Morphological Characteristics | Clinical Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scale** | Visible desquamation or flaking of dead corneocytes (stratum corneum) composed of abnormal keratinocytes. | Silvery scales in Psoriasis, collarette scales in Pityriasis rosea, greasy yellow scales in Seborrheic dermatitis. |
| **Crust** | Dried surface deposit composed of serum, blood, or purulent exudate overlying an injured or eroded area. | Honey-colored crusts in Impetigo, hemorrhagic crusts in Herpes zoster. |
| **Erosion** | Focal partial loss of epidermis without damage to the basement membrane. Heals **without scarring**. | Ruptured pemphigus bulla, erosive GERD / mucositis. |
| **Ulcer** | Full-thickness loss of epidermis extending into the dermis or subcutis with basement membrane disruption. Heals **with scarring**. | Arterial ulcer, Venous stasis ulcer, Neuropathic diabetic ulcer, Pyoderma gangrenosum. |
| **Fissure** | A sharp, linear vertical cleaving or split extending through the epidermis into the dermis. | Palmoplantar eczema, angular cheilitis, perianal fissure. |
| **Excoriation** | Linear or punctate traumatic loss of epidermis caused by mechanical scratching or rubbing. | Neurotic excoriations, severe pruritus in Scabies/Pediculosis. |
| **Lichenification** | Thickened, leathery skin with hyperpigmentation and **exaggerated skin markings**, produced by chronic, repetitive rubbing. | Lichen simplex chronicus, chronic flexural Atopic dermatitis. |

## 2.3 Vascular & Extravasation Lesions

Red cutaneous lesions are categorized based on whether blood remains inside blood vessels (blanchable) or has extravasated into the dermis (non-blanchable).

| Feature | Blanchable lesions | Non-blanchable lesions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Underlying mechanism | Blood remains within dilated vessels; pressure displaces intravascular blood. | Red cells have extravasated into the dermis; pressure does not clear the colour. |
| Examples | Erythema; telangiectasia. | Petechiae (<2 mm); purpura (approximately 2–5 mm); ecchymoses (>5 mm). |
| Important qualification | Diascopy may help determine whether erythema is intravascular. | Palpable purpura suggests a raised inflammatory process such as small-vessel vasculitis. |

- **Petechiae**: Pinpoint, non-blanchable macules measuring **$<2\text{ mm}$** caused by capillary RBC extravasation (thrombocytopenia, meningococcemia).
- **Purpura**: Non-blanchable red-purple macules or papules measuring **$2\text{--}5\text{ mm}$**. **Palpable purpura** indicates inflammatory vessel damage seen in **cutaneous leukocytoclastic vasculitis**.
- **Ecchymosis**: Flat, non-blanchable extravasation of blood into skin measuring **$>5\text{ mm}$** (bruise).
- **Telangiectasia**: Permanently dilated, superficial small blood vessels (capillaries, venules) visible on skin surface that **blanch under pressure**.

## 2.4 Diagnostic Comparison Matrix: Tissue Loss Spectrum

| Lesion | Tissue involvement | Basement membrane | Healing |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Erosion | Loss of epidermis only. | Remains intact. | Usually heals without scarring. |
| Ulcer | Full-thickness epidermal loss with dermal involvement. | Disrupted. | Heals with scarring. |
| Fissure | Linear split extending into the dermis. | May be disrupted depending on depth. | May scar if sufficiently deep. |
