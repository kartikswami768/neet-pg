# Chapter 01: Anatomy, Histology & Physiology of Skin

Skin is the largest organ of the human body, serving as a dynamic physiological barrier against mechanical trauma, ultraviolet (UV) radiation, pathogen invasion, and transepidermal water loss. Understanding the structural architecture, cellular kinetics, and intercellular adhesion complexes of skin is fundamental to clinical dermatology and dermatopathology.

---

## 1.1 Layers of Epidermis & Epithelial Kinetics

The epidermis is a stratified squamous keratinized epithelium derived from ectoderm. It consists of five distinct histological layers in thick skin (palms and soles) and four layers in thin skin.

The epidermis is arranged from superficial to deep as follows:

| Epidermal layer | Key histological features |
| :--- | :--- |
| Stratum corneum | Anucleate corneocytes; cornified layer. |
| Stratum lucidum | Clear layer present in thick skin of palms and soles only. |
| Stratum granulosum | Keratohyalin granules and lamellar granules. |
| Stratum spinosum | Prickle cells connected by desmosomes. |
| Stratum basale | Mitotically active basal cells/stem cells; melanocytes are also present. |

> [!NOTE]
> **Mnemonic for Epidermal Layers (Superficial to Deep)**:
> *"**C**ome, **L**et's **G**et **S**un**B**urned"*
> 1. **C**orneum
> 2. **L**ucidum
> 3. **G**ranulosum
> 4. **S**pinosum
> 5. **B**asale

### Histological Breakdown of Epidermal Layers

1. **Stratum Corneum (SC)**:
   - Most superficial layer composed of flattened, fully keratinized, anucleate dead cells termed **corneocytes**.
   - Acts as the primary physical and chemical permeability barrier.
   - Constantly sheds via programmed desquamation.

2. **Stratum Lucidum**:
   - Translucent, clear cell layer present **selectively in thick skin** (palms and soles).
   - Contains eleidin, a transformation product of keratohyalin.

3. **Stratum Granulosum (SG)**:
   - Composed of 1–3 layers of flattened keratinocytes containing dark, basophilic granules.
   - Contains two distinct granule types:
     - **Keratohyaline Granules**: Contain **profilaggrin**, the precursor to **filaggrin** (*Filament Aggregating Protein*). Filaggrin aggregates keratin intermediate filaments into dense bundles. Mutations in the filaggrin gene (*FLG*) lead to **Ichthyosis vulgaris** and **Atopic dermatitis**.
     - **Lamellar Granules (Odland Bodies / Membrane-Coating Granules)**: Produce lipid-rich extracellular sheets (ceramides, cholesterol, free fatty acids) responsible for the epidermal water-barrier function.

4. **Stratum Spinosum (Prickle Cell Layer)**:
   - Polyhedral keratinocytes connected by prominent intercellular bridges or "spines" under light microscopy.
   - These spines correspond to **desmosomes**, which provide high mechanical tensile strength.
   - Together with the stratum basale, it is referred to as the *Stratum Malpighii*.

5. **Stratum Basale (Stratum Germinativum)**:
   - Deepest single-cell layer composed of cuboidal to low columnar stem cells with central hyperchromatic nuclei.
   - Continuously dividing cells attached to the underlying basement membrane zone via **hemidesmosomes**.
   - Contains melanocytes (melanin-producing cells) and Merkel cells.

### Epidermal Kinetics & Transit Time
- **Normal Epidermal Transit Time**: The time taken for a basal cell to divide, differentiate, migrate through the epidermal layers, and shed from the stratum corneum is **28 days** (approximately 14 days from basale to granulosum, and 14 days through the stratum corneum).
- **Psoriasis Transit Time**: In psoriasis vulgaris, epidermal hyperplasia accelerates transit time dramatically to **3 to 5 days**, leading to incomplete keratinization and parakeratosis.

## 1.2 Adhesion Molecules & Intercellular Junctions

Keratinocyte structural integrity depends on specialized cell-to-cell (desmosomes) and cell-to-matrix (hemidesmosomes) adhesion complexes. Autoimmune targeting or genetic mutations of these junctional proteins result in severe blistering diseases.

Desmosomes form strong intercellular junctions between adjacent keratinocytes.

| Desmosomal component | Structural role / association |
| :--- | :--- |
| Desmoplakin | Links the desmosomal plaque to intracellular keratin intermediate filaments. |
| Plakoglobin | Plak component linking desmosomal cadherins to plaque proteins. |
| Desmoglein 1 and 3 | Transmembrane desmosomal cadherins that mediate adhesion between adjacent keratinocytes; autoantibodies cause acantholysis in pemphigus. |
| Desmocollin | Transmembrane desmosomal cadherin contributing to intercellular adhesion. |

### Desmosomes (Intraepidermal Intercellular Junctions)
- **Function**: Provide strong intercellular cohesion between adjacent keratinocytes in the stratum spinosum and granulosum by linking transmembrane cadherins to intracellular keratin intermediate filaments.
- **Molecular Components**:
  - Transmembrane Cadherins: **Desmoglein 1 (Dsg1)** and **Desmoglein 3 (Dsg3)**, Desmocollin.
  - Plakins & Armadillo Proteins: Desmoplakin, Plakoglobin, Plakophilin.
- **Topographical Distribution & Clinical Relevance**:
  - *Desmoglein 1*: Predominantly expressed in superficial layers (**stratum granulosum**). Target of autoantibodies in **Pemphigus Foliaceus** (subcorneal blister) and Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS exfoliative toxin A/B).
  - *Desmoglein 3*: Predominantly expressed in basal/suprabasal layers (**stratum spinosum**) and mucosa. Target of autoantibodies in **Pemphigus Vulgaris** (suprabasal flaccid blister with mucosal involvement).

### Hemidesmosomes & Basement Membrane Zone (BMZ)
- **Function**: Anchor basal keratinocytes to the underlying papillary dermis across the dermoepidermal junction (DEJ).
- **Sub-Layer Architecture of BMZ**:
  1. **Intra-epidermal Component**: Keratin intermediate filaments (K5/K14) attached to hemidesmosomal inner plaque (**BPAG1 / BP230** and **Plectin**).
  2. **Transmembrane Component**: **BPAG2 / BP180** (Type XVII Collagen) and **Integrin $\alpha_6\beta_4$**.
  3. **Lamina Lucida**: Electron-lucent upper basement membrane zone containing **Laminin 332 (Laminin 5)**. Target of autoantibodies in Bullous Pemphigoid ("hemidesmosomes are down bullow").
  4. **Lamina Densa**: Electron-dense zone composed primarily of **Type IV Collagen** and heparan sulfate proteoglycans.
  5. **Sublamina Densa / Anchoring Fibrils**: Composed of **Type VII Collagen** ($COL7A1$), anchoring the lamina densa to dermal collagen. Target in Dystrophic Epidermolysis Bullosa and Epidermolysis Bullosa Acquisita.

## 1.3 Cellular Constituents of Skin

The epidermis is composed of 90% keratinocytes and 10% non-keratinocytes (melanocytes, Langerhans cells, and Merkel cells).

| Cell Type | Origin | Epidermal Location | Key Histological & Functional Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Keratinocytes** | Ectoderm | All layers | Produce keratin intermediate filaments; undergo cornification to form protective skin barrier. |
| **Melanocytes** | Neural Crest | Stratum Basale | Clear cells with dendritic processes (1 per 6–10 basal cells). Synthesize melanin inside melanosomes and transfer pigment to ~36 surrounding keratinocytes (*Epidermal Melanin Unit*). |
| **Langerhans Cells** | Bone Marrow | Stratum Spinosum | Antigen-presenting dendritic cells. Contain pathognomonic tennis-racket-shaped **Birbeck granules** (Langerin / CD207+). Proliferate in Langerhans Cell Histiocytosis (LCH). |
| **Merkel Cells** | Ectoderm / Neural Crest | Stratum Basale | Slow-adapting Type I mechanoreceptors for fine touch. Associated with free nerve endings. Malignant transformation forms Merkel Cell Carcinoma (polyomavirus-linked). |

## 1.4 Dermis, Subcutis & Skin Appendages

| Compartment | Connective tissue | Important structures |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Papillary dermis | Loose connective tissue; predominantly type III collagen | Capillary loops; Meissner corpuscles. |
| Reticular dermis | Dense irregular connective tissue; predominantly type I collagen | Hair follicles; sweat glands; sebaceous glands. |
| Hypodermis (subcutis) — septal compartment | Collagenous connective-tissue septa | Fibrous bands separating fat lobules. |
| Hypodermis (subcutis) — lobular compartment | Mature adipose tissue | Clusters of adipocytes. |

### 1. Dermis Anatomy
The dermis is divided into two continuous structural zones:
- **Papillary Dermis**: Superficial thin layer projecting into epidermal rete ridges. Composed of loose areolar connective tissue, fine **Type III collagen** fibers, elastic fibers, capillary loops, and Meissner touch corpuscles.
- **Reticular Dermis**: Deep thick layer extending to subcutis. Composed of dense, irregular connective tissue with thick **Type I collagen** bundles, coarse elastic fibers, larger blood vessels, and cutaneous appendages.

### 2. Hypodermis (Subcutaneous Tissue)
- Composed of two main compartments:
  1. **Septal Compartment**: Fibrous collagenous septa carrying blood vessels and nerves.
  2. **Lobular Compartment**: Clusters of mature unilocular adipocytes providing thermal insulation and shock absorption.
- Inflammation of subcutaneous fat is termed **Panniculitis** (Septal: Erythema nodosum; Lobular: Erythema induratum of Bazin / Weber-Christian disease).

### 3. Cutaneous Appendages & Glandular Secretion Types
- **Eccrine Sweat Glands**: Merocrine secretion (exocytosis without cell loss); distributed over entire body; innervated by sympathetic cholinergic fibers; key in thermoregulation.
- **Apocrine Sweat Glands**: Apocrine secretion (decapitation / pinching off of apical cell cytoplasm); located in axillae, anogenital region, and nipples; open into hair follicles; produce odorless secretion that becomes malodorous upon bacterial degradation.
- **Sebaceous Glands**: **Holocrine secretion** (entire disintegrating cell forms lipid sebum); androgen-dependent; associated with hair follicles (*Pilosebaceous Unit*).

## 1.5 Key Anatomical Levels and Blister Split Patterns

| Anatomical level (superficial to deep) | Potential split level / junction | Classic associations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stratum corneum / subcorneal region | Subcorneal split | Pemphigus foliaceus; staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome (SSSS). |
| Stratum spinosum / suprabasal epidermis | Desmosomal adhesion; suprabasal acantholysis | Pemphigus vulgaris; classic “row of tombstones” basal cells. |
| Stratum basale / dermoepidermal junction | Basement membrane zone; lamina lucida and hemidesmosomal attachments (BP180/BP230) | Bullous pemphigoid targets hemidesmosomal proteins. |
| Subepidermal region / papillary dermis | Subepidermal split | Bullous pemphigoid and dermatitis herpetiformis (different target antigens and mechanisms). |
