# Chapter 08: Subepidermal Autoimmune and Genetic Blistering Disorders

## 8.1 Bullous Pemphigoid (BP)

Bullous Pemphigoid is the most common autoimmune subepidermal blistering disease, occurring predominantly in elderly patients (typically $>70$ years).

> [!NOTE]
> **Structural Distinction**: Unlike Pemphigus (which involves intraepidermal desmosomal destruction), Bullous Pemphigoid targets hemidesmosomes, leaving the full epidermal roof intact over the subepidermal blister cavity.

### Pathophysiology & Target Antigens
* **Hypersensitivity Mechanism**: **Type II Antibody-Mediated Hypersensitivity**.
* **Target Antigens**: IgG autoantibodies directed against hemidesmosomal proteins at the dermoepidermal junction:
  * **BP180 (BPAG2 / Type XVII Collagen)**: Transmembrane component; antibody titers correlate directly with disease activity.
  * **BP230 (BPAG1)**: Intracellular plakin family component.
* **Inflammatory Cascade**: Complement activation (C3) recruits neutrophils and **eosinophils**, releasing proteases (gelatinase B, human leukocyte elastase) that degrade the basement membrane zone.

| Structure / location | Protein | Clinical association / relevance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Intracellular hemidesmosomal plaque (basal keratinocyte cytoplasm) | BP230 / BPAG1 | Autoantigen in bullous pemphigoid. |
| Transmembrane hemidesmosomal component (type XVII collagen) | BP180 / BPAG2 | Major autoantigen in bullous pemphigoid. |
| Anchoring fibrils beneath the basement membrane | Type VII collagen | Autoantigen in epidermolysis bullosa acquisita. |

### Clinical Presentation
* **Prodromal Phase**: Intense pruritus accompanied by urticarial, eczematous, or targetoid erythematous plaques preceding blister formation by weeks to months.
* **Bullous Phase**: Large, **tense, firm bullae** containing clear or hemorrhagic fluid arising on normal or erythematous skin.
* **Mucosal Involvement**: **Rare ($<10-20\%$)** and mild; oral lesions are transient and non-scarring.
* **Physical Signs**:
  * **Nikolsky Sign Negative $\ominus$**: Firm pressure does not detach normal-appearing skin because the split is subepidermal.
  * **Asboe-Hansen Sign Negative $\ominus$**.

### Histopathology & Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF)
* **Histopathology**:
  * **Subepidermal Split / Cleft**: Complete detachment of intact epidermis from the underlying dermis.
  * **Eosinophil-Rich Infiltrate**: Dense accumulation of eosinophils within the dermal papillae and blister cavity.
* **Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF)**: Demonstrates a **continuous, smooth linear band** of IgG and C3 deposition along the basement membrane zone (BMZ).

## 8.2 Dermatitis Herpetiformis (DH)

Dermatitis Herpetiformis (Duhring Disease) is an intensely pruritic, chronic subepidermal papulovesicular disorder representing the cutaneous manifestation of **Celiac Disease** (Gluten-sensitive enteropathy).

### Pathophysiology & Immunological Axis
* **Gluten Sensitivity**: Ingestion of dietary gluten (gliadin) triggers mucosal IgA anti-tissue transglutaminase (**tTG-2**) antibody synthesis in the gut.
* **Cross-Reactivity**: IgA autoantibodies cross-react with epidermal transglutaminase (**eTG / tTG-3**), forming IgA-eTG immune complexes that deposit in the dermal papillae.
* **Complement & Neutrophil Recruitment**: Deposited IgA activates complement, attracting neutrophils to the tips of dermal papillae.

### Clinical Features & Diagnosis
* **Symmetrical Distribution**: Intensely pruritic, burning, grouped (herpetiform) papules, urticarial plaques, and small vesicles symmetrically distributed over **extensor surfaces**:
  * Extensor elbows and forearms
  * Extensor knees
  * Buttocks and sacrum
  * Posterior scalp and shoulders
* **Histopathology**: Accumulation of neutrophils and eosinophils forming **neutrophilic microabscesses at the dermal papillary tips**, leading to subepidermal separation.
* **Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF)**: **Gold Standard Diagnostic Test** — reveals **granular IgA deposits clustered at the tips of dermal papillae**.

| Compartment | Characteristic finding in dermatitis herpetiformis |
| :--- | :--- |
| Epidermis | Usually intact overlying epidermis. |
| Dermal papillary tips | Granular IgA deposition with neutrophilic microabscesses. |
| Dermis | Subepidermal blister formation may develop beneath affected papillae. |

### Therapeutic Management
1. **Strict Gluten-Free Diet**: Primary lifelong intervention; heals both mucosal enteropathy and cutaneous IgA deposition (though skin clearance may take months to years).
2. **Dapsone (Diaminodiphenyl Sulfone)**: Rapidly controls pruritus and cutaneous lesions within 24–48 hours by inhibiting neutrophilic myeloperoxidase. *Must screen for G6PD deficiency prior to initiation to prevent severe hemolytic anemia*.

## 8.3 Epidermolysis Bullosa (EB) Spectrum

Epidermolysis Bullosa comprises a group of inherited mechanobullous disorders characterized by marked skin fragility and blistering triggered by minimal mechanical trauma.

### Major EB Subtypes Classified by Anatomic Split Level

| EB Subtype | Inheritance Pattern | Gene & Protein Defect | Anatomic Level of Split | Clinical Features & Prognosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EB Simplex (EBS)** | Autosomal Dominant | *KRT5* or *KRT14* (**Keratin 5 or 14**) | **Intraepidermal** (lysis of basal keratinocytes). | Mildest form; blisters on palms/soles post-friction; heals without scarring. |
| **Junctional EB (JEB)** | Autosomal Recessive | *LAMA3, LAMB3, LAMC2* (**Laminin-332**) or *COL17A1* | **Intra-lamina lucida** of basement membrane. | Severe Form (Herlitz): Enamel hypoplasia, laryngeal granulation, early infant mortality. |
| **Dystrophic EB (DEB)** | AD (Dominant) / AR (Recessive) | *COL7A1* (**Type VII Collagen**) | **Sublamina densa** (anchoring fibrils). | Recessive DEB: Severe scarring, **mitten deformities** of hands/feet, esophageal strictures, high risk of aggressive **Squamous Cell Carcinoma**. |

## 8.4 Subepidermal Blistering Disease Diagnostic Matrix

| Disease Entity | Target Antigen | Blulla Type | DIF Pattern | Primary HPE Infiltrate | Associated Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bullous Pemphigoid** | BP180 & BP230 (Hemidesmosome) | Tense, large | Smooth linear IgG/C3 along BMZ | **Eosinophils** | Elderly patients; Nikolsky negative. |
| **Dermatitis Herpetiformis** | Epidermal Transglutaminase (tTG-3) | Grouped vesicles | Granular IgA at dermal papillary tips | **Neutrophils** | Celiac disease; extensor distribution; responds to Dapsone. |
| **Epidermolysis Bullosa Acquisita** | Type VII Collagen (Anchoring Fibrils) | Tense, mechanobullous | Linear IgG/C3 along BMZ | Variable / Minimal | Trauma-induced blisters on acral sites; scarring and milia. |
