# Chapter 03: Dermatopathology, Clinical Signs & Diagnostic Procedures

Dermatology relies heavily on pathognomonic physical signs, histopathological patterns, and specialized bedside diagnostic procedures to establish precise diagnoses.

---

## 3.1 Histopathological Terminology

Dermatopathology uses standardized descriptive terms to define microscopic epidermal and dermal changes.

| Histological Term | Pathological Process & Definition | Classic Disease Associations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hyperkeratosis** | Thickening of the stratum corneum. | Psoriasis, calluses, Verruca vulgaris, Ichthyosis. |
| **Parakeratosis** | Retention of pyknotic nuclei in the stratum corneum, reflecting accelerated epidermal turnover and incomplete cornification. | Psoriasis vulgaris, Pityriasis rosea, Actinic keratosis. |
| **Acanthosis** | Epidermal hyperplasia marked by thickening of the stratum spinosum. | Psoriasis ("camel-toe" rete ridges), Acanthosis nigricans, Lichen simplex chronicus. |
| **Spongiosis** | Intercellular edema within the epidermis (stratum spinosum), widening interkeratinocyte spaces and highlighting desmosomal bridges. | Acute eczematous dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis. |
| **Acantholysis** | Loss of intercellular desmosomal connections between keratinocytes, leading to intraepidermal cell detachment and blister formation. | Pemphigus vulgaris, Pemphigus foliaceus, Hailey-Hailey disease. |
| **Dyskeratosis** | Premature, abnormal keratinization of individual cells below the stratum corneum (eosinophilic cytoplasm with condensed pyknotic nucleus). | *Benign*: Darier disease (**corps ronds & grains**), Hailey-Hailey.<br>*Malignant*: Squamous cell carcinoma, Bowen disease. |
| **Liquefaction Degeneration** | Hydropic vacuolization and destruction of the basal cell layer, characteristic of interface dermatitis. | Lichen planus, Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE), Graft-versus-Host Disease. |

## 3.2 Pathognomonic Microabscesses in Dermatology

Infiltration of specific inflammatory cells into distinct anatomical compartments of the epidermis forms diagnostic microabscesses:

| Microabscess type | Cell type / location | Classic association |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Munro microabscess | Neutrophils in the stratum corneum. | Psoriasis. |
| Kogoj spongiform pustule | Neutrophils within the stratum spinosum (spongiform pustules). | Psoriasis. |
| Pautrier microabscess | Intraepidermal collections of atypical lymphocytes. | Mycosis fungoides. |
| Papillary-tip neutrophilic microabscess | Neutrophils at dermal papillary tips. | Dermatitis herpetiformis. |
| Eosinophilic collections | Eosinophils in epidermal / pustular lesions. | Pemphigus vegetans (classically eosinophilic spongiosis / pustules). |

1. **Munro's Microabscesses**: Accumulations of **neutrophils within the stratum corneum** (parakeratotic scale). Pathognomonic of **Psoriasis vulgaris**.
2. **Kogoj Spongiform Pustules**: Accumulations of **neutrophils within the spinous layer (stratum spinosum)**. Pathognomonic of **Psoriasis vulgaris** (especially pustular variants).
3. **Dermal Papillary Tip Microabscesses**: Collections of **neutrophils and fibrin at the tips of dermal papillae**. Pathognomonic of **Dermatitis Herpetiformis**.
4. **Pautrier's Microabscesses**: Intraepidermal clusters of **atypical CD4+ T-lymphocytes**. Pathognomonic of **Mycosis Fungoides** (Cutaneous T-cell Lymphoma).
5. **Eosinophilic Microabscesses**: Intraepidermal collections of **eosinophils** seen in **Pemphigus Vegetans**.

## 3.3 Classic Clinical & Physical Signs

### 1. Psoriasis Clinical Signs (Grattage Sequence)
Scraping a chronic psoriatic plaque sequentially with a glass slide yields a characteristic 3-step physical progression:
1. **Candle-Drop Sign**: Scraping produces white, powdery, stearin-like scales resembling scraped candle wax.
2. **Bulkeley's Membrane**: Continued scraping removes all scales to expose a shiny, transparent, moist sub-scaling membrane.
3. **Auspitz Sign**: Peeling off Bulkeley's membrane produces **pinpoint bleeding spots**. This occurs due to rupture of dilated, tortuous capillary loops within dermal papillae beneath a markedly thinned suprapapillary epidermis.

### 2. Isomorphic Response (Koebner Phenomenon)
- **Definition**: Development of classic disease-specific lesions along lines of uninvolved skin that have undergone mechanical trauma, thermal injury, or surgical incision.
- **True Koebner Phenomenon Positive**:
  - **Psoriasis vulgaris**
  - **Lichen Planus**
  - **Vitiligo**
  - **Verruca Vulgaris (Warts)** & **Molluscum Contagiosum** (autoinoculation)

### 3. Blistering & Skin Fragility Signs
- **Nikolsky Sign**:
  - *Method*: Firm tangential sliding pressure applied with a finger to normal-appearing skin adjacent to a blister.
  - *Positive Result*: Dislodgement/sloughing of the top layer of epidermis due to acantholysis (loss of interkeratinocyte desmosomes).
  - *Positive in*: **Pemphigus vulgaris**, **Staphylococcal Scalded Skin Syndrome (SSSS)**, **Toxic Epidermal Necrolysis (TEN)**.
  - *Negative in*: **Bullous Pemphigoid** (subepidermal split with intact desmosomes).
- **Asboe-Hansen / Bulla Spread Sign**: Direct vertical pressure applied to the roof of an intact bulla causes lateral extension of the fluid blister into surrounding unblistered skin.

### 4. Paraneoplastic & Miscellaneous Signs
- **Darier Sign**: Rubbing or stroking a cutaneous macule causes immediate localized erythema, edema, and **wheal formation** within minutes. Caused by degranulation of mast cells releasing histamine in **Urticaria Pigmentosa / Mastocytosis**.
- **Leser-Trélat Sign**: Sudden, rapid appearance of multiple eruptive **Seborreic Keratoses** on the trunk. Serves as a cutaneous paraneoplastic marker for underlying visceral malignancy (most commonly **Gastric Adenocarcinoma**).
- **Dimple Sign (Fitzpatrick's Sign)**: Lateral compression/pinching of a cutaneous nodule causes central invagination/dimpling. Positive in **Dermatofibroma**.

## 3.4 Diagnostic Procedures & Bedside Modalities

### 1. Diascopy (Vitropression)
Pressing a transparent glass slide or clear plastic lens firmly against a red cutaneous lesion:
- **Erythema**: Blanches completely under pressure (vascular dilation with intact blood vessels).
- **Purpura**: Does **NOT blanch** (RBC extravasation out of blood vessels into dermis).
- **Lupus Vulgaris**: Reveals underlying golden-yellow / **"apple-jelly" nodules**.
- **Nevus Anemicus vs. Nevus Depigmentosus**: Diascopy causes surrounding skin to blanch so nevus anemicus merges completely, whereas nevus depigmentosus remains clearly demarcated.

### 2. Tzanck Smear
Diagnostic cytological test for rapid evaluation of acantholytic and viral blistering disorders:
- *Method*: Scrape the base of a freshly unroofed vesicobullous lesion, smear onto a glass slide, fix with heat/methanol, and stain with Giemsa or Wright stain.
- *Findings*:
  - **Acantholytic Tzanck Cells**: Large, rounded, solitary keratinocytes with hyperchromatic nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and a dark peripheral cytoplasm rim (seen in **Pemphigus vulgaris**).
  - **Multinucleated Giant Cells**: Large syncytial cells exhibiting nuclear molding, chromatin margination, and multinucleation (pathognomonic for **Herpes Simplex Virus [HSV-1/2]** and **Varicella-Zoster Virus [VZV]**).

### 3. Wood's Lamp Examination
Utilizes filtered long-wave ultraviolet light ($\sim 365\text{ nm}$) to induce characteristic fluorescence:
- **Coral-Red Fluorescence**: **Erythrasma** (caused by *Corynebacterium minutissimum* producing porphyrins).
- **Golden-Yellow / Orange Fluorescence**: **Tinea / Pityriasis Versicolor** (caused by *Malassezia* species).
- **Bright Blue-Green Fluorescence**: **Tinea Capitis** (caused by *Microsporum* species).
- **Chalk-White / Bright White**: **Vitiligo** (complete absence of epidermal melanin accentuates contrast).
