# Chapter 15: Malignant Cutaneous Neoplasms

## 15.1 Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC)

> [!NOTE]
> **Epidemiological Benchmark**: Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC) is the **most common cutaneous malignancy** and the most frequent human cancer overall worldwide. Although locally invasive and destructive ("rodent ulcer"), it exhibits an extraordinarily low rate of distant metastasis ($<0.1\%$).

| Clinical presentation | Histopathology | Molecular genetics / association |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pearly translucent nodule. | Nests of basaloid cells. | PTCH1 inactivation; Sonic Hedgehog pathway activation. |
| Surface telangiectasia. | Peripheral palisading of nuclei. | Hedgehog pathway inhibitors include vismodegib and sonidegib. |
| Rolled, bead-like border. | Peritumoral retraction clefts; mucinous stroma. | Associated with Gorlin syndrome. |
| Central ulceration (“rodent ulcer”). | Apoptotic bodies may be present. |  |

### Risk Factors & Pathophysiology
* **Ultraviolet Radiation**: Cumulative and intermittent intense UV exposure (UVA and UVB) is the primary driver. UV-induced pyrimidine dimer formation leads to characteristic $C \rightarrow T$ or $CC \rightarrow TT$ transition mutations in tumor suppressor genes.
* **Sonic Hedgehog (SHH) Pathway Activation**: The key molecular driver of BCC is loss-of-function mutation in the **$PTCH1$** (Patched-1) gene or gain-of-function mutation in **$SMO$** (Smoothened). $PTCH1$ normally inhibits $SMO$; loss of $PTCH1$ disinhibits $SMO$, triggering downstream $GLI$ transcription factor activation and uncontrolled basal cell proliferation.
* **Genodermatoses**:
  * **Gorlin Syndrome (Nevoid Basal Cell Carcinoma Syndrome)**: Autosomal dominant mutation in $PTCH1$ (chromosome 9q22). Characterized by multiple early-onset BCCs, odontogenic keratocysts of the jaw, palmar/plantar pits, calcification of falx cerebri, and bifid ribs.
  * **Xeroderma Pigmentosum**: Defective nucleotide excision repair (NER) resulting in early development of multiple BCCs, SCCs, and melanomas.

### Clinical Subtypes & Anatomical Distribution
BCC occurs predominantly on sun-exposed sites, particularly the head and neck (80%), classically **above the anatomical line connecting the angle of the mouth to the earlobe** (tragus).

| Clinical Subtype | Morphological Presentation | Histopathological Characteristics | Clinical Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nodular BCC** | Most common subtype (~60%). Pearly, translucent papule or nodule with surface telangiectasias and rolled borders. May ulcerate centrally (**noduloulcerative / rodent ulcer**). | Large, well-defined island nests of basaloid cells extending into dermis with peripheral palisading and retraction artifact. | Indolent local growth; highly amenable to excision or Mohs surgery. |
| **Superficial BCC** | Erythematous, scaly patch or thin plaque with thread-like rolled borders; often resembles eczema or psoriasis. Common on the trunk. | Multiple small buds of basaloid cells attached to the undersurface of the epidermis, extending no deeper than papillary dermis. | Low risk; manageable with topical 5-FU, Imiquimod, or electrodessication & curettage. |
| **Morpheaform / Sclerosing** | Waxy, indurated, yellowish or flesh-colored plaque resembling a scar or localized scleroderma; borders are clinically ill-defined. | Narrow, elongated strands and cords of basaloid cells embedded in a dense, sclerotic fibrous stroma. | **Aggressive local invasion**; high recurrence rate; requires **Mohs Micrographic Surgery**. |
| **Pigmented BCC** | Brown, blue, or black nodule/plaque due to abundant melanin; clinically simulates Malignant Melanoma. | Basaloid islands containing abundant intra-tumoral and stromal melanocytes and melanophages. | Indolent behavior similar to nodular BCC. |
| **Infiltrative / Micronodular** | Firm plaque or nodule; aggressive microscopic extension beyond visible clinical margins. | Small, irregular nests (micronodules) or spiky cords penetrating deeply into collagen. | High risk of local recurrence and deep structural invasion. |

### Histopathology Highlights
* **Basaloid Nests**: Monomorphic proliferation of small cells with hyperchromatic, elongated nuclei, scanty cytoplasm, and poorly defined cell borders.
* **Peripheral Palisading**: The outermost layer of cells in each tumor nest is arranged parallel to one another in an orderly, column-like pattern.
* **Retraction / Cleavage Artifact**: During tissue processing, the mucinous stroma surrounding the tumor island shrinks, creating distinct peritumoral clear lacunae/clefts separating the tumor from the dermis.

| Histopathological component | Description in basal cell carcinoma |
| :--- | :--- |
| Tumour nests | Basaloid cell islands in the dermis; cells have hyperchromatic nuclei and scant cytoplasm. |
| Peripheral palisading | Columnar tumour cells arranged in a palisading pattern at the edge of the nests. |
| Peritumoral retraction cleft | Clear space between tumour islands and surrounding stroma, partly related to stromal mucin / processing artefact. |
| Overlying epidermis | The tumour is associated with the epidermis or adnexal epithelium. |
### Management Protocols
* **Surgical Excision**: Standard choice for low-risk primary lesions (4 mm surgical margin).
* **Mohs Micrographic Surgery (MMS)**: Gold standard for high-risk regions (H-zone of face: nasolabial fold, eyelids, ears, lips), recurrent BCC, morpheaform/infiltrative subtypes, or lesions with ill-defined borders. Achieves 100% surgical margin evaluation.
* **Hedgehog Pathway Inhibitors**:
  * **Vismodegib** & **Sonidegib**: Small-molecule $SMO$ inhibitors used for advanced, metastatic, or unresectable BCCs, and severe Gorlin syndrome.
  * Side effects: Muscle spasms, dysgeusia, alopecia, weight loss.

## 15.2 Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC)

> [!WARNING]
> **Metastatic Potential**: Unlike BCC, Cutaneous Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cSCC) carries a definite risk of regional lymph node and distant metastasis ($2\text{--}5\%$ overall). The risk is higher when adverse clinical or pathological features are present; see the high-risk feature list below.

### Risk Factors & Precursor Lesions
* **Ultraviolet Radiation**: Chronic, cumulative lifelong sun exposure is the single primary cause (higher incidence in farmers, outdoor workers).
* **Precursor Lesions**:
  * **Actinic Keratosis (AK)**: Dysplasia restricted to lower layers of epidermis.
  * **Bowen's Disease**: Full-thickness epidermal dysplasia (*in situ* cSCC).
* **Non-Solar Etiologies**:
  * **Marjolin's Ulcer**: cSCC arising within chronic non-healing wounds, burn scars, osteomyelitis sinus tracts, or venous stasis ulcers. Demonstrates aggressive behavior and high metastatic rates.
  * **Chemical Carcinogens**: Inorganic arsenic exposure (well water, Fowler's solution) causes palmar/plantar arsenical keratoses and multiple SCCs.
  * **Immunosuppression**: Organ transplant recipients on long-term immunosuppressants (calcineurin inhibitors) have a **65-fold to 250-fold increased risk** of cSCC.
  * **Human Papillomavirus (HPV)**: High-risk oncogenic strains (HPV 16, 18, 31, 33; and beta-HPVs 5, 8 in Epidermodysplasia Verruciformis) are linked to periungual, genital, and cutaneous SCCs.
* **High-risk clinicopathological features:** classic exam predictors include tumour thickness **>4 mm** (or **Clark level V** in the older depth classification), **perineural or vascular invasion**, high-risk anatomical sites such as the **lip, ear, and anogenital skin**, and **immunosuppression / solid-organ transplantation**. Risk frameworks differ: some contemporary schemes reserve *very-high-risk* deep invasion for thickness **>6 mm** or extension beyond subcutaneous fat ([British Association of Dermatologists cSCC guideline, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/bjd/article/184/3/401/6702204)).

### Histopathological Diagnosis
* **Dermal Invasion**: Full-thickness dysplastic, pleomorphic squamous epithelial cells breaking through the basement membrane into the dermis.
* **Keratin Pearls (Concentric Keratin Swirls)**: Concentric, laminated nests of acellular, deeply eosinophilic keratin surrounded by dysplastic squamous cells.
* **Intercellular Bridges**: Prominent desmosomal attachments visible between adjacent tumor cells under high power.
* **Grading**:
  * *Well-differentiated*: Abundant keratin pearls, prominent intercellular bridges, mild nuclear atypia.
  * *Poorly differentiated*: Marked nuclear pleomorphism, frequent atypical mitotic figures, minimal or absent keratinization.
| Histopathological component | Description in cutaneous squamous cell carcinoma |
| :--- | :--- |
| Epidermis | Full-thickness atypia / dyskeratotic cells may be present in the overlying epidermis. |
| Basement membrane | Disrupted by invasive tumour. |
| Invasive tumour | Atypical squamous cells with prominent intercellular bridges; concentric eosinophilic keratin pearls may be present. |
| Perineural invasion | Tumour may track around or invade dermal nerves. |
### Clinical Management
* **Standard Surgical Excision**: 4–6 mm margin for low-risk cSCC; 6–10 mm margin for high-risk cSCC.
* **Mohs Micrographic Surgery**: Indicated for high-risk locations (lip, ear, central face), recurrent tumors, perineural invasion, poorly differentiated histology, or immunosuppressed patients.
* **Systemic Immunotherapy**: **Cemiplimab** and **Pembrolizumab** (PD-1 inhibitors) are approved for locally advanced or metastatic cSCC ineligible for surgery or radiation.

## 15.3 Malignant Melanoma

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Prognostic Benchmark**: Malignant Melanoma accounts for only $\sim 1\%$ of skin cancers but causes the vast majority of skin cancer deaths. **Breslow Depth of Invasion** (measured in millimeters from the top of the granular layer to the deepest tumor cell) is the single most important prognostic factor.

### The ABCDE Criteria for Early Clinical Detection
* **A – Asymmetry**: One half of the lesion does not match the other half.
* **B – Border Irregularity**: Edges are ragged, notched, blurred, or poorly defined.
* **C – Color Variegation**: Pigmentation is non-uniform, featuring shades of brown, black, pink, red, white, or blue.
* **D – Diameter**: $\ge 6\text{ mm}$ (size of a pencil eraser; though melanomas can be smaller).
* **E – Evolving**: Any lesion changing in size, shape, color, or producing new symptoms (pruritus, bleeding, ulceration).
* **The "Ugly Duckling" Sign**: A pigmented lesion that looks noticeably different from all other nevi on the patient's body.
| Melanoma subtype | Typical anatomical location | Biological behaviour / distinguishing features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Superficial spreading melanoma | Back in men; legs in women (classic distribution). | **Most common subtype (~70%)**; prolonged radial growth phase before vertical invasion. |
| Nodular melanoma | Any site. | Approximately **15%**; aggressive subtype with early vertical growth and no prolonged radial growth phase. |
| Lentigo maligna melanoma | Chronically sun-damaged face / neck in older adults. | Prolonged radial growth; the in-situ phase is lentigo maligna. |
| Acral lentiginous melanoma | Palms, soles and subungual sites. | **Most frequent melanoma subtype in darker-skinned populations** (including many Asian populations), although uncommon overall in fair-skinned populations; not primarily UV-related; Hutchinson sign may be present. |

*Subtype percentages are approximate and vary by population and study.*
### Histopathology & Growth Phases
* **Radial Growth Phase**: Horizontal expansion of malignant melanocytes within the epidermis (pagetoid spread) and papillary dermis. Incapable of metastasis during pure radial growth.
* **Vertical Growth Phase**: Downward invasion of malignant melanocytes into the deep reticular dermis and subcutis. Accompanied by vascular/lymphatic invasion potential.
* **Histological Features**: Atypical melanocytes arranged in nests or single cells along the dermal-epidermal junction, exhibiting **pagetoid intraepidermal spread** ("buckshot" distribution of melanocytes migrating up into upper epidermal layers), cellular pleomorphism, macronucleoli, and atypical mitoses.

### Microscopic Staging & Depth Measurement
* **Breslow Depth**: Measured with an ocular micrometer from the top of the stratum granulosum (or base of ulcer) to the deepest point of tumor invasion.
  * $<0.8\text{ mm}$: Low risk ($\ge 95\%$ 5-year survival).
  * $0.8\text{--}1.0\text{ mm}$: Intermediate risk.
  * $>4.0\text{ mm}$: High risk of distant metastasis ($<50\%$ 5-year survival).
* **Clark Levels** (Anatomical level of invasion; largely superseded by Breslow depth):
  * Level I: In situ (confined to epidermis).
  * Level II: Invades papillary dermis.
  * Level III: Fills papillary dermis to junction of reticular dermis.
  * Level IV: Invades reticular dermis.
  * Level V: Invades subcutaneous fat.

### Molecular Genetics & Targeted Therapy
* **$BRAF$ V600E Mutation**: Present in $40\text{--}50\%$ of cutaneous melanomas. $BRAF$ is a serine/threonine kinase in the MAPK/ERK pathway. V600E substitution causes constitutive kinase activation.
  * **$BRAF$ Inhibitors**: **Vemurafenib**, **Dabrafenib**.
  * **$MEK$ Inhibitors** (combined with $BRAF$ inhibitors to prevent resistance): **Trametinib**, **Cobimetinib**.
* **$NRAS$ Mutations**: Present in $15\text{--}20\%$ of cases.
* **$c-KIT$ Mutations**: Enriched in acral lentiginous and mucosal melanomas; targeted by **Imatinib**.
* **Immune Checkpoint Blockade**:
  * **Anti-PD-1 Antibodies**: **Pembrolizumab**, **Nivolumab** (enhances T-cell antitumor response).
  * **Anti-CTLA-4 Antibody**: **Ipilimumab**.

## 15.4 Cutaneous Lymphomas

### Mycosis Fungoides (MF)
* **Definition**: The most common primary **Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma (CTCL)**, originating from skin-homing mature **$\text{CD4}^+$ helper T-lymphocytes** ($\text{CD4}^+\text{CD7}^-$ profile).
| Stage | Clinical morphology | Other features in source |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Patch stage | Erythematous, scaly, poikilodermatous patches, often in bathing-suit distribution. | May mimic eczema or psoriasis. |
| Plaque stage | Indurated, elevated red plaques with active edges. | Pruritus; atypical T cells infiltrate the papillary dermis. |
| Tumour stage | Red-brown nodular tumours with a tendency to ulcerate. | Nodal and visceral spread may occur later in the disease course. |

* **Histological Hallmarks**:
  * **Epidermotropism**: Migration of atypical CD4+ lymphocytes into the epidermis without significant spongiosis.
  * **Pautrier's Microabscesses**: Intraepidermal aggregates/clusters of atypical neoplastic lymphocytes with hyperchromatic, indented, cerebriform nuclei.
* **Treatment**: Topical corticosteroids, Mechlorethamine (nitrogen mustard), Bexarotene (topical/oral retinoid X receptor agonist), phototherapy (PUVA / NB-UVB), radiation therapy (total skin electron beam).

### Sézary Syndrome
* **Triad**:
  1. **Generalized Erythroderma** (red man syndrome covering $>90\%$ BSA with intense pruritus and scaling).
  2. **Generalized Lymphadenopathy**.
  3. **Circulating Sézary Cells**: Atypical T-lymphocytes with characteristic **cerebriform (grooved/folded) nuclei** comprising $>1000/\mu\text{L}$ or $>10\%$ of circulating peripheral blood lymphocytes.

## 15.5 Comparative Diagnostic Matrix of Primary Skin Cancers

| Feature | Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC) | Squamous Cell Carcinoma (cSCC) | Malignant Melanoma |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin Cell** | Basal layer keratinocyte stem cells | Epidermal spinous keratinocytes | Neural crest-derived melanocytes |
| **Primary Etiology** | Intermittent intense UV exposure | Chronic cumulative sun exposure | Intermittent intense blistering sunburns |
| **Classic Appearance** | Pearly nodule with telangiectasias, rolled border, central ulcer | Hyperkeratotic, ulcerated firm red nodule/plaque | Asymmetric, multicolored macule/nodule with irregular borders |
| **Histology Key** | Basaloid nests, peripheral palisading, retraction clefts | Keratin pearls, intercellular bridges, dermal invasion | Pagetoid intraepidermal spread, atypical melanocytic nests |
| **Metastatic Risk** | Extremely low ($<0.1\%$) | Low-to-moderate ($2\text{--}5\%$; higher in Marjolin ulcer) | **High** (driven by Breslow depth) |
| **Key Biomarker/Gene** | $PTCH1$, $SMO$ (Sonic Hedgehog) | $p53$ mutations | $BRAF$ V600E, $NRAS$, $c-KIT$, S100, HMB-45 |
