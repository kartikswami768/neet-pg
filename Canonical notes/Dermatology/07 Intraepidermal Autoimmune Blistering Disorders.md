# Chapter 07: Intraepidermal Autoimmune Blistering Disorders

## 7.1 Pemphigus Vulgaris (PV)

Pemphigus Vulgaris is a severe, potentially fatal intraepidermal autoimmune blistering disease targeting desmosomal adhesion molecules.

> [!WARNING]
> **High-Yield Clinical Alert**: Prior to the advent of systemic corticosteroid therapy, Pemphigus Vulgaris carried a mortality rate approaching $100\%$ due to fluid loss, electrolyte collapse, and overwhelming cutaneous sepsis.

### Etiopathogenesis & Molecular Targets
* **Hypersensitivity Mechanism**: **Type II Antibody-Mediated Hypersensitivity**.
* **Autoantibody Target**: Circulating pathogenic **IgG1 and IgG4 autoantibodies** directed against **Desmoglein 3** (expressed predominantly in mucosal epithelium and lower epidermis) and **Desmoglein 1** (expressed in upper epidermis).
* **Desmoglein Compensation Theory**:
  * *Mucosa-Dominant PV*: Autoantibodies against **Desmoglein 3 alone**. Mucosal lesions occur because Desmoglein 1 cannot compensate in mucosa; skin is spared because Desmoglein 1 compensates in epidermis.
  * *Mucocutaneous PV*: Autoantibodies against **both Desmoglein 3 and Desmoglein 1**, producing severe oral and cutaneous bullae.

| Epidermal region / antigen | Disease association | Split level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Desmoglein 1 is prominent in superficial epidermis / upper layers. | Pemphigus foliaceus | Subcorneal / superficial intraepidermal split. |
| Desmoglein 3 is prominent in deeper epidermis; desmoglein 1 may also be targeted. | Pemphigus vulgaris | Suprabasal intraepidermal split; “row of tombstones”. |

### Clinical Presentation
* **Oral Mucosal Involvement**: The **initial site of disease in $>70-80\%$ of patients**, preceding cutaneous lesions by months. Painful, persistent oral erosions that rupture easily; intact mucosal bullae are rarely seen due to thin roofs.
* **Cutaneous Lesions**: Flaccid, fragile, clear-fluid-filled bullae arising on normal or erythematous skin.
* **Clinical Physical Signs**:
  * **Nikolsky Sign Positive $\oplus$**: Firm sliding pressure applied to normal-appearing peri-lesional skin causes epidermal shearing and detachment.
  * **Asboe-Hansen Sign (Bulla Spread Sign) Positive $\oplus$**: Direct vertical pressure applied to an intact bulla causes lateral extension of the fluid collection beneath surrounding unblistered skin.

### Histopathology & Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF)
* **Histopathology**:
  * **Suprabasal Acantholysis**: Loss of desmosomal cell-to-cell adhesion between spinous keratinocytes directly above the basal layer.
  * **"Row of Tombstones" Appearance**: Basal keratinocytes remain attached to the basement membrane via intact hemidesmosomes, lining the floor of the intraepidermal cavity.
  * **Acantholytic Cells (Tzanck Cells)**: Rounded, detached keratinocytes with hyperchromatic nuclei and perinuclear halos visible on Tzanck smear.
* **Direct Immunofluorescence (DIF)**: Demonstrates intercellular **reticular / lace-like / fishnet-pattern** deposition of IgG and C3 throughout the epidermal spinous layer.

| Finding | Description in pemphigus vulgaris |
| :--- | :--- |
| Blister level | Suprabasal intraepidermal cleft due to acantholysis. |
| Blister character | Flaccid bulla that ruptures easily. |
| Tzanck cells | Rounded, detached acantholytic keratinocytes in blister fluid. |
| Basal layer | Basal keratinocytes remain attached to the basement membrane, producing the “row of tombstones” appearance. |

## 7.2 Pemphigus Foliaceus & Variants

### Pemphigus Foliaceus (PF)
* **Autoantibody Target**: Directed exclusively against **Desmoglein 1**.
* **Split Level**: **Subcorneal / Intragranular acantholytic split**.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Fragile, shallow, scaly, crusted erosions resembling "corn flakes" or "oatmeal".
* **Mucosal Sparing**: Oral mucosa is **never involved** because Desmoglein 3 compensates fully in mucosal tissues.
* **Fogo Selvaggio**: Endemic form of Pemphigus Foliaceus transmitted by *Simulium* blackfly bites in South America (Brazil).

### Paraneoplastic Pemphigus (PNP)
* Severe, recalcitrant mucocutaneous ulceration associated with underlying neoplasms (e.g., **Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma, Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia, Castleman Disease**).
* Autoantibodies target **Plakins** (Envoplakin, Periplakin) and Desmogleins.

## 7.3 Genetic Acantholytic Disorders

### Hailey-Hailey Disease (Familial Benign Chronic Pemphigus)
* **Genetics**: Autosomal dominant mutation in the ***ATP2C1* gene** encoding the secretory pathway $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{Mn}^{2+}$ ATPase 1 (SPCA1). Intracellular calcium depletion impairs desmosomal assembly.
* **Clinical Presentation**: Recurrent painful, malodorous vesicles and erosions in intertriginous areas (axillae, groin, neck).
* **Histopathology**: Extensive, full-thickness epidermal acantholysis resembling a **"dilapidated brick wall"**.

### Darier Disease (Keratosis Follicularis)
* **Genetics**: Autosomal dominant mutation in the ***ATP2A2* gene** encoding the sarco/endoplasmic reticulum $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ ATPase 2 (SERCA2).
* **Clinical Presentation**: Malodorous, greasy, hyperkeratotic papules in seborrheic areas; nail changes feature V-shaped distal nicks and alternating red/white longitudinal bands.
* **Histopathology**: Acantholysis accompanied by distinctive dyskeratotic cells:
  * **Corps Ronds**: Round cells with pyknotic nuclei surrounded by a clear halo in the upper spinous/granular layers.
  * **Grains**: Small basophilic nuclear remnants in the stratum corneum.
