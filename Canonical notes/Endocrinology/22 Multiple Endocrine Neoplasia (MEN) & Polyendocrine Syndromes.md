# Chapter 22: Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN) & Polyendocrine Syndromes

## 22.1 Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN) Syndromes

Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia (MEN) syndromes are autosomal dominant genetic disorders predisposing individuals to benign or malignant tumors in multiple endocrine glands.

| Syndrome | Genetic basis | Characteristic features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MEN 1 | MEN1 tumour-suppressor gene; menin | The three Ps: parathyroid disease, pancreatic / duodenal neuroendocrine tumours and pituitary adenoma |
| MEN 2A | Activating RET proto-oncogene mutation | Medullary thyroid carcinoma, pheochromocytoma and parathyroid hyperplasia |
| MEN 2B | Activating RET mutation, classically M918T | Aggressive medullary thyroid carcinoma, pheochromocytoma, mucosal neuromas and marfanoid habitus |

### Detailed Syndrome Comparison Matrix

| Feature | MEN 1 (Wermer Syndrome) | MEN 2A (Sipple Syndrome) | MEN 2B |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gene Mutation** | **_MEN1_** (11q13)<br>Tumor Suppressor (Menin loss) | **_RET_ Proto-Oncogene** (10q11.2)<br>Gain-of-Function Kinase Mutation | **_RET_ Proto-Oncogene**<br>Specific **M918T** Exon 16 Mutation |
| **Primary Feature #1** | **Parathyroid Hyperplasia** ($95\%$, earliest manifestation) | **Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma** ($100\%$, early onset) | **Medullary Thyroid Carcinoma** ($100\%$, highly aggressive in infancy) |
| **Primary Feature #2** | **Pancreatic NETs** ($70\%$, Gastrinoma #1, Insulinoma) | **Pheochromocytoma** ($50\%$, often bilateral) | **Pheochromocytoma** ($50\%$, bilateral) |
| **Primary Feature #3** | **Pituitary Adenoma** ($40\%$, Prolactinoma #1) | **Parathyroid Hyperplasia** ($20\text{--}30\%$) | **Mucosal Neuromas & Marfanoid Habitus** (No parathyroid involvement!) |
| **Prophylactic Surgery** | None (regular screening) | Prophylactic Thyroidectomy before age 5 | Prophylactic Thyroidectomy in **first year of life** |

## 22.2 Autoimmune Polyendocrine Syndromes (APS)

Autoimmune Polyendocrine Syndromes are hereditary or acquired autoimmune conditions characterized by immune-mediated destruction of multiple endocrine glands.

| Feature | APS type 1 (APECED) | APS type 2 (Schmidt syndrome) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Genetic basis | Autosomal recessive AIRE mutation | Polygenic association; HLA-DR3 / HLA-DR4 associations |
| Core clinical pattern | Chronic mucocutaneous candidiasis, hypoparathyroidism and primary adrenal insufficiency; classically at least two of the three | Primary adrenal insufficiency with autoimmune thyroid disease and/or type 1 diabetes mellitus |

### 1. APS Type 1 (APECED)
* **Genetics**: Autosomal recessive loss-of-function mutation in the **_AIRE_ (Autoimmune Regulator)** gene on chromosome 21q22. Loss of AIRE impairs central thymic clonal deletion of self-reactive T-cells.
* **Clinical Diagnostic Triad (Requires $\ge 2$ features)**:
  1. **Chronic Mucocutaneous Candidiasis (CMC)**: Earliest manifestation, appearing in infancy/early childhood.
  2. **Autoimmune Hypoparathyroidism**: Usually presents before age 10.
  3. **Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease)**: Develops by age 15.

### 2. APS Type 2 (Schmidt Syndrome)
* **Genetics**: Polygenic inheritance strongly associated with **HLA-DR3 and HLA-DR4** haplotypes.
* **Clinical Spectrum**:
  * **Primary Adrenal Insufficiency (Addison's Disease)** (sine qua non feature).
  * **Autoimmune Thyroid Disease** (Hashimoto Thyroiditis or Graves' Disease).
  * **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus**.
* **Clinical Management Rule**: When initiating replacement therapy in a patient with coexisting Addison's disease and hypothyroidism, **ALWAYS start Glucocorticoid replacement (Hydrocortisone) FIRST** before starting Levothyroxine. Initiating levothyroxine first increases hepatic steroid clearance and metabolic demand, triggering a fatal **Acute Adrenal Crisis**.
