# 01 — Introduction to Surgery and Perioperative Care

> [!info] Scope and source basis
> Surgical foundations are drawn principally from **World of Revision (WOR), General Surgery pp. 1–5**, its **Anaesthesia: Pre-Anaesthesia Checkup pp. 1–8**, and **Doctut Merged: Inside the OT / Operation Theatre Protocols**. This is an exam-oriented synthesis, not a substitute for local perioperative protocols.

## 1. Classifying surgical procedures

| Dimension | Classification | Essential distinction / example |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | **Emergency** | Cannot safely wait: active exsanguinating hemorrhage, peritonitis with shock, threatened limb |
| | **Urgent** | Necessary within a limited window; brief stabilization/optimization possible |
| | **Elective** | Planned after assessment and optimization |
| Admission | **Day-care / ambulatory** | Admission and discharge on the same day; selection, escort, pain and PONV control crucial |
| | **Overnight / short stay** | Observation over ~23 hours or up to ~72 hours in the revision classification |
| | **Inpatient** | Requires extended postoperative observation/treatment |
| Intention | **Diagnostic, therapeutic, palliative, reconstructive, prophylactic** | Biopsy; disease removal; symptom relief; restoration; risk-reducing surgery |
| Access | **Open, endoscopic, laparoscopic, robotic** | Differ in access trauma, visualization and equipment |
| Contamination | **Clean, clean-contaminated, contaminated, dirty/infected** | Determines SSI risk and the role of antibiotics; detailed in Ch. 03 |

### Ambulatory/day-care surgery
- **Selection:** procedure of manageable physiologic stress; expected early mobility, oral intake and adequate oral analgesia; stable comorbidities; safe home environment and responsible escort.
- WOR examination convention: ASA I–II patients are typical candidates for standalone units; selected ASA III patients may be managed in centers with greater support. Patient factors, procedure duration and institutional facilities matter more than a single numeric cut-off.
- **Common immediate problems:** postoperative nausea/vomiting (**PONV**), pain, urinary retention, dizziness and bleeding. Hemorrhage is an important cause of unexpected admission/readmission in revision notes.
- **Discharge principles:** stable observations, awake/oriented, controlled pain and nausea, acceptable wound bleeding, sufficient mobility, instructions and escort; voiding or oral intake need to be assessed when relevant to the procedure/anesthesia.

## 2. Preoperative assessment: systematic sequence

1. **Confirm the operation:** indication, anticipated benefit, alternatives, laterality/site, urgency and likely physiological burden.
2. **History:** presenting illness; past surgeries/anesthesia; difficult intubation; postoperative complications; allergies; smoking, alcohol, recreational drugs; family history of malignant hyperthermia; pregnancy possibility.
3. **Comorbidity review:** coronary disease/heart failure/arrhythmia, respiratory disease/OSA, diabetes, CKD, liver disease, anemia, bleeding disorder, VTE history, seizures, endocrine disease, infection, malnutrition.
4. **Medication reconciliation:** anticoagulants/antiplatelets, hypoglycemics, antihypertensives, steroids, antiepileptics, sedatives, opioids, hormones/herbal agents.
5. **Examination:** vital signs, hydration, cardiopulmonary and neurologic examination, edema, peripheral perfusion, airway assessment, relevant operative-site exam.
6. **Assess risk:** ASA physical status; cardiac/pulmonary risk; VTE and bleeding risk; frailty, nutrition and functional capacity.
7. **Order targeted tests and optimize:** not every patient needs a complete routine panel. Record blood availability and postoperative destination if risk is substantial.
8. **Document informed consent, site marking, fasting/medication plan, antibiotic and VTE plans.**

### Functional and cardiac assessment
- **Functional capacity:** ability to perform ordinary activities and climb stairs helps estimate cardiopulmonary reserve. Low functional capacity plus clinical risk may justify further assessment.
- **Revised Cardiac Risk Index (RCRI):** six factors: high-risk surgery; ischemic heart disease; heart failure; cerebrovascular disease; insulin-treated diabetes; creatinine >2 mg/dL. It predicts major cardiac complications, not an absolute promise of an outcome.
- **Conditions requiring serious evaluation before non-emergency surgery:** acute coronary syndrome, decompensated heart failure, dangerous arrhythmia, severe symptomatic valvular disease.
- **Pulmonary risks:** advanced age, COPD, smoking, poor functional status, low albumin, thoracic/upper abdominal surgery, emergency surgery and prolonged general anesthesia.
- **Tests when indicated:** CBC, creatinine/electrolytes, glucose, ECG, chest imaging and echocardiography. **Routine echo/ECG/CXR in every asymptomatic low-risk patient is not supported by the source framework.**

### ASA Physical Status Classification

| ASA | Meaning | Exam example |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Healthy patient | No significant systemic disease |
| **II** | Mild systemic disease, without substantial functional limitation | Well-controlled hypertension/diabetes, pregnancy, smoking |
| **III** | Severe systemic disease with substantial health burden | Poorly controlled diabetes, stable severe systemic disease, dialysis-dependent ESRD |
| **IV** | Severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life | Unstable angina, decompensated heart failure, severe ongoing organ dysfunction |
| **V** | Moribund patient, unlikely to survive without operation | Ruptured aneurysm, catastrophic ischemic bowel, massive trauma |
| **VI** | Brain-dead donor undergoing organ procurement | Organ donor |

- **E** suffix marks an emergency (e.g., ASA IIIE).
- ASA is a **physical-status descriptor**, **not** a direct score for operative risk or a measure of surgical complexity.

### Anticipating a difficult airway
- History of airway difficulty, facial/neck abnormality, trismus, obstructive sleep apnea, limited neck mobility and oral submucosal fibrosis.
- **Mouth opening:** approximately three finger breadths is desirable; marked restriction is concerning.
- **Modified Mallampati:** class I—soft palate, uvula and pillars visible; II—soft palate and uvula but pillars obscured; III—soft palate and base of uvula; IV—hard palate only.
- **Thyromental distance**, mandibular protrusion (upper-lip-bite test), neck extension, neck circumference and dentition supplement Mallampati.
- In trauma use **LEMON**: **L**ook externally, **E**valuate 3-3-2, **M**allampati, **O**bstruction, **N**eck mobility.
- **Important:** no individual bedside test reliably excludes difficult ventilation or intubation.

### Medication review: principles and exam traps

| Medication/class | Surgical concern | Revision principle |
|---|---|---|
| Long-term **beta-blockers** | Rebound tachycardia/ischemia if withdrawn | Usually continue established therapy |
| **ACE inhibitors / ARBs** | Anesthesia-related hypotension | Whether withheld depends on indication and procedure; avoid a universal rule |
| **Insulin** | Fasting hypoglycemia vs perioperative DKA | Adjust dose rather than stop basal insulin indiscriminately |
| **SGLT2 inhibitors** | Euglycemic ketoacidosis | Often withheld several days before planned surgery; exact timing is protocol/agent dependent |
| **Antiplatelets** | Thrombosis vs bleeding, especially recent coronary stents | Individualize with cardiology/anesthesia; never casually stop dual antiplatelet therapy |
| **Warfarin / DOACs** | Operative and neuraxial bleeding | Hold/restart according to agent, renal function, bleeding risk, thromboembolic risk; **bridging is not routine for everyone** |
| **Chronic steroids** | Risk of adrenal suppression | Continue baseline regimen; evaluate perioperative supplementation where warranted |
| **Antiepileptics** | Withdrawal seizures | Generally continue |
| **MAO inhibitors / lithium** | Drug and anesthesia interactions | Plan with anesthetist; variable individual approach |
| Estrogen-containing medications | VTE risk | Assess need to interrupt in high-risk immobilizing operations |

> [!warning] Medication timing is high-stakes
> Some uploaded revision charts provide fixed stopping intervals and insulin fractions that are too broad for universal prescribing. Keep such numbers as **historical exam-source material**, not as a one-size-fits-all patient order. Verify drug-specific and institution-specific protocols before use.

## 3. Consent, safety and operating-theatre workflow

### Informed consent must address
- Diagnosis and indication; proposed procedure, site, laterality and anticipated course.
- Material benefits, common complications and serious risks, including anesthetic, bleeding, infection, injury to adjacent structures and possible stoma/conversion if relevant.
- Reasonable alternatives including no operation; consequences of declining; expected recovery and follow-up.
- Capacity and voluntariness; language/interpreter; written documentation; separate anesthesia and transfusion consent when required.
- Emergency treatment without prior consent is a specific legal/ethical exception; document necessity and context.

### WHO Surgical Safety Checklist

| Stage | When | Key actions |
|---|---|---|
| **Sign in** | Before induction | Identity, planned procedure and site, consent, site mark, allergies, airway/aspiration risk, blood-loss risk, equipment and pulse oximeter checks |
| **Time out** | Before incision | Introduce team; **verbally confirm patient, operation and laterality**; surgeon's critical steps/expected duration and blood loss; anesthetic concerns; antibiotics, imaging and sterility |
| **Sign out** | Before leaving OR | Confirm operation, instrument/sponge/needle counts, specimen labeling, equipment problems and postoperative care plans |

### Asepsis and OT basics
- **Asepsis:** preventing microorganisms from entering the surgical field. **Antisepsis:** reducing microbes on living tissue. **Disinfection:** reducing pathogens on objects. **Sterilization:** eliminating all viable organisms including spores.
- **Sterile team** remains within the sterile field; minimize movement, door openings, needless handling and breaks in technique.
- Preoperative skin preparation uses an appropriate antiseptic (commonly chlorhexidine–alcohol if safe for the site, or povidone–iodine according to local policy); allow drying and avoid pooling around diathermy.
- Surgical hand hygiene, gowns/gloves, sterile draping, instrument and sponge counts, sharps safety, safe specimen handling.
- Hair removal: **clip only when necessary**; avoid routine razor shaving that causes microabrasions and SSI risk.
- OT zones as commonly described: **protective/unrestricted entry → clean/semi-restricted → aseptic/restricted operating field → disposal/dirty area**. Exact zoning nomenclature varies.


### Sterilization, disinfection and hand hygiene

| Procedure | Core principle | Examples / practical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Sterilization** | Destroys/inactivates all microbial life including spores | Steam under pressure for compatible surgical instruments; appropriately validated low-temperature methods for heat-sensitive devices |
| **High-level disinfection** | Eliminates most organisms but cannot always guarantee all spores | Selected semicritical equipment after thorough cleaning |
| **Low/intermediate disinfection** | Reduces surface microbes but does not sterilize | Environmental equipment and surfaces |
| **Antisepsis** | Applied to living tissues | Chlorhexidine or iodine-containing preparations where safe |

- **Cleaning and removal of organic material precede disinfection/sterilization**; an autoclave cannot compensate for poor cleaning/packaging.
- **Steam sterilization (autoclave)** uses moist heat under pressure; actual cycle temperature, time and validation depend on equipment/load, so do not blindly extrapolate a single recipe.
- **Chemical/low-temperature methods** are used for selected heat-sensitive devices, with strict validation and safety procedures.
- **WHO five moments of hand hygiene:** before touching a patient; before clean/aseptic task; after body-fluid exposure risk; after touching a patient; after touching patient surroundings.
- **Surgical scrub/hand preparation** covers fingertips, interdigital spaces and forearms, followed by sterile gown/gloves.
- **Spore-forming contamination / visibly soiled hands:** soap and water is emphasized in the WOR surgical hygiene summary.

## 4. Patient positioning

| Position | Typical use | Complication to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Supine | Abdominal/breast surgery | Pressure injury, nerve compression |
| Trendelenburg (head down) | Pelvic procedures | Ventilation difficulty, facial/airway edema, raised ICP/IOP |
| Reverse Trendelenburg | Upper abdominal/laparoscopic GB | Hypotension, venous pooling, sliding |
| Lithotomy | Urology, gynecology, anorectal | **Common peroneal nerve** compression at fibular neck; compartment syndrome in prolonged cases |
| Lateral / kidney | Thoracic, renal | Dependent pressure injury, brachial plexus stretch if poorly supported |
| Prone | Spine, posterior operations | Eye pressure/visual loss risk, airway access difficulty, abdominal compression |
| Sitting / Fowler | Posterior fossa surgery | **Venous air embolism** |
| Jackknife | Perianal/pilonidal procedures | Respiratory/pressure complications if positioning inadequate |

**Venous air embolism:** consider when abrupt fall in end-tidal CO₂, hypoxemia/hypotension occurs during high-risk surgery; notify the anesthetist, stop air entrainment, support oxygenation and hemodynamics, and aspirate air via existing appropriate central catheter where feasible. WOR discusses left-lateral/Trendelenburg (Durant positioning) as a classical maneuver; patient-specific management is more important than a memorized position.

## 5. ERAS — Enhanced Recovery After Surgery

**Goal:** reduce the surgical stress response and complications and shorten recovery without sacrificing safety. It is a **multidisciplinary pathway**, not a single intervention.

| Preoperative | Intraoperative | Postoperative |
|---|---|---|
| Counsel and prehabilitate; assess nutrition; smoking cessation; avoid prolonged fasting; selective carbohydrate loading | Minimally invasive approach when feasible; goal-directed fluids; normothermia; multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia; appropriate PONV and SSI prophylaxis | Early oral/enteral intake; early mobilization; remove unnecessary tubes/lines; multimodal analgesia; restore bowel function; individualized VTE prophylaxis |

- Fasting revision values for otherwise eligible elective patients: **clear liquids up to 2 h**, **light meal/solids ~6 h** and longer for fatty meals; aspiration-risk patients need individualized instructions.
- Avoid routine nasogastric tubes, drains and mechanical bowel preparation **unless indicated for the actual procedure**.
- Consider local/regional anesthesia and long-acting local analgesia; prevent hypothermia.
- Early ambulation reduces atelectasis, deconditioning and venous thrombosis.

## 6. Postoperative management

### Routine priorities
- **Airway and breathing:** level of consciousness, respiratory rate, oxygenation, aspiration, pain-limited breaths.
- **Circulation:** pulse, blood pressure, perfusion, fluid balance, drain loss, occult hemorrhage.
- **Pain/PONV:** multimodal analgesia; antiemetics in those at risk; opioid adverse effects.
- **Kidneys and electrolytes:** monitor urine output when relevant; review IV fluids and electrolytes; treat abnormalities.
- **Wound:** assess bleeding, erythema, separation and expected drain output; record type/volume.
- **Mobilization/feeding:** as early as safe; prevent DVT and pulmonary complications.

### Postoperative fever — diagnostic approach

| Timing clue | Important differential | Clinical response |
|---|---|---|
| **Within 24 h** | Inflammatory response, transfusion reaction, aspiration, infection present pre-op, malignant hyperthermia (peri-anesthetic) | Examine and assess vitals, respiratory status and operation details; do not assign a diagnosis by day alone |
| **Days 1–3** | Pneumonia/aspiration, UTI, line inflammation, thrombophlebitis | Examine lungs, urine and IV access when clinically indicated |
| **Days 3–7** | SSI, abscess, anastomotic leak, DVT, other infection | Inspect wound, assess abdomen and source of sepsis |
| **Later or persistent** | Deep/organ-space infection, abscess, drug fever, VTE, line infection | Targeted investigations and source control |

- The classic revision mnemonic **“5 Ws”** (Wind, Water, Wound, Walking, Wonder drugs) helps organize a differential, **not accurately diagnose fever by calendar day**.
- **Atelectasis** is associated with postoperative pulmonary complications, but should not automatically be declared the cause of fever.
- **Postoperative nausea/vomiting risks** in revision notes include prior PONV, female sex, nonsmoking status and perioperative opioids (Apfel factors); procedure and anesthesia characteristics also matter.

### Burst abdomen / fascial dehiscence
- Usually manifests during the first postoperative week; **serosanguineous (“salmon-pink”) discharge** may precede fascial separation.
- **Risk factors:** obesity, malnutrition, infection, coughing, raised intra-abdominal pressure, emergency operation, faulty fascial closure, hypoperfusion/steroids.
- **If evisceration:** protect bowel with sterile saline-moistened covering, urgent surgical assessment/resuscitation, operative fascial repair when possible; never attempt aggressive bedside reinsertion.
- Abdominal closure principles: small-bite fascial technique where appropriate, adequate suture:wound length ratio (commonly at least **4:1**), avoid tissue strangulation and excessive tension.

### Postoperative complications to identify rapidly
- Bleeding and shock; respiratory compromise/aspiration; venous thromboembolism; acute kidney injury; delirium; ileus; urinary retention; wound infection, anastomotic leak and intra-abdominal abscess; surgical-site dehiscence.

### Chapter 01 — rapid revision
- **ASA = systemic health status, not operative risk score.**
- **WHO checklist = sign in / time out / sign out.**
- **Lithotomy → common peroneal nerve. Sitting → venous air embolism.**
- **ERAS** = multimodal, organ-supportive, early feeding/mobilization and fewer unnecessary tubes.
- **Postoperative fever:** find the cause; avoid rigid “day = diagnosis” shortcuts.
