> [!warning] Limits of provenance
> The Doctut transcription contains OCR errors; exact figures or spellings were not accepted when unclear. No current external guideline verification was requested or performed. Where primary sources contradict each other or contain simplified legacy algorithms, they are disclosed below. These notes prioritize **internally coherent explanations and safe conceptual interpretation** rather than asserting that every uploaded recommendation is current.

## B. Cross-source disagreements and misleading one-liners

| Topic | What the sources say / conflict | How to study it |
|---|---|---|
| **Sepsis definition** | WOR/Doctut use **SIRS + infection**; this represents an older construct | Distinguish SIRS criteria from organ dysfunction and septic shock; learn which definition a question specifies |
| **Sepsis bundle** | Older charts give obligatory CVP/ScvO₂ targets | Remember the **concept** of resuscitation and lactate reassessment, not mandatory targets for every patient |
| **Postoperative day-1 fever** | Older notes frequently call atelectasis the most common cause | Treat this as a **historical exam mnemonic**, not a validated causal diagnosis |
| **Blood-transfusion Hb thresholds** | Source tables list fixed cutoffs such as <6, 6–8 or >8 g/dL | Interpret with **bleeding/hemodynamics/clinical context**, not Hb alone |
| **Hemorrhage class volumes** | Printed class tables contain inconsistent absolute volumes | Prioritize % blood-volume classes and physiological change |
| **NG tube confirmation** | Some notes list auscultation after air injection | **Do not use auscultation as a reliable placement confirmation** |
| **Albumin for nutrition** | Doctut calls albumin “best”; WOR says no single biochemical marker reliable | **Inflammation confounds albumin**; combine history, weight and functional measures |
| **Harmonic scalpel** | One note states “coagulation without heat” | Ultrasonic devices **also generate heat**; the difference is energy delivery mechanism |
| **Bipolar cautery** | Revision table treats it as coagulation-only | Classic bipolar forceps mainly coagulate; advanced devices may cut/seal too |
| **Escharotomy** | WOR describes cutting deep fascia | **Escharotomy cuts eschar; fasciotomy cuts deep fascia** |
| **Burn resuscitation** | Both 4-mL Parkland and 2-mL Brooke appear | Learn both as **initial formulas**, titrate to perfusion/urine output |
| **Burn pain** | Superficial burns painful, full thickness often painless | Nerve destruction reduces sensation in deepest part; surrounding burns can hurt |
| **Burned nasal hair** | Listed as most specific inhalation sign in one revision chart | A clue **alone** does not prove clinically important inhalation injury |
| **TXA in trauma** | WOR supports 1 g + 1 g; a Doctut OCR/transcription passage gives a different 2+2 g statement | Avoid adopting the questionable doubled regimen |
| **Chest decompression site** | Landmark differs among protocols/age groups | Clinical tension pneumothorax needs **urgent decompression**; confirm correct device/site with applicable training/protocol |
| **Flail chest definition** | WOR writes ≥2 consecutive ribs; other common teaching uses ≥3 | Recognize paradoxical unstable segment and associated contusion; exam wording matters |
| **MESS ≥7** | Some revisions imply automatic amputation | A score **cannot independently decide limb sacrifice** |
| **TRALI vs TACO** | WOR table has a normal CXR next to TACO | Both can cause pulmonary opacities; volume physiology distinguishes them |
| **Surgical-site implant window** | Some source charts say “within 1 year” | Surveillance definitions differ by protocol; do not rigidly universalize |
| **Antibiotic for every surgery** | One older source suggests antibiotics for all surgeries | Determine **wound class and operation**: prophylaxis and active infection treatment are different |
| **Perioperative drug stopping intervals** | Specific universal numbers appear for warfarin, antiplatelets, insulin and ACEi | Drug type, bleeding/thrombotic risk and anesthetic technique determine the plan |

## C. Values and equations that deserve memorization

| Concept | Value / formula | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Normal GCS | **15** | E4 V5 M6 |
| Severe TBI | **GCS ≤8** | Airway and secondary injury prevention |
| GCS-P | **GCS − unreactive pupil score (0–2)** | Pupil response supplement |
| Cerebral perfusion pressure | **CPP = MAP − ICP** | Prevent hypotension/raised ICP |
| Shock index | **HR / SBP** | ~>0.9 often flags increased risk |
| RCRI | **Six factors** | CAD, HF, CVA, insulin, creatinine >2, high-risk surgery |
| Main wound-healing cells | Neutrophils early → **macrophages ~day 3** | Angiogenesis peaks ~day 5 in Robbins clean wound example |
| Tensile strength | **~10% at 1 week; ~70–80% at 3 months** | Not 100% recovery |
| Fascial closure suture ratio | **≥4:1 suture:wound** | Adequate tension-free closure |
| Adult urine output in resuscitation | **~0.5 mL/kg/h** | Titrate to whole patient, not isolated output |
| Burns pediatric urine output | **~1 mL/kg/h** | With pediatric maintenance fluid considerations |
| Classic Parkland | **4 mL × kg × %TBSA** | LR 24-h initial estimate |
| Modified Brooke adult | **2 mL × kg × %TBSA** | Used in WOR table |
| Burn fluid timing | **50% first 8 h FROM injury; 50% next 16 h** | Formula only a starting point |
| Adult Rule of Nines | Head 9, each arm 9, each leg 18, trunk ant 18/post 18, perineum 1 | Exclude superficial epidermal burns |
| Trauma TXA (WOR) | **1 g IV / 10 min + 1 g / 8 h** | For selected major bleeding/risk, correct early timing |
| Tension pneumothorax | **Clinical diagnosis** in unstable patient | Do not wait for X-ray |
| Massive transfusion (traditional) | **>10 units RBC / 24 h** | Other definitions include rapid early need |
| TRALI timing | **Within ~6 hours** classically | Acute noncardiogenic edema |
| Refeeding marker | **Hypophosphatemia** | Also low K/Mg; thiamine prevention |

## D. Integrated exam-style one-liners

1. Elective thyroidectomy is a **clean** surgery; controlled bowel entry is **clean-contaminated**.
2. **Submucosa** contributes most to the strength of bowel anastomosis.
3. **PDS** is a delayed-absorbable monofilament; **Prolene** is nonabsorbable polypropylene.
4. In a clean incised wound, **neutrophils first → macrophages → granulation tissue → collagen remodels**.
5. **VEGF = blood vessels; TGF-β = fibroblasts and collagen; MMP = ECM degradation.**
6. **Keloid crosses margins; hypertrophic scar does not.**
7. **Burst abdomen** may present with a sudden serosanguineous gush; suspect fascial dehiscence.
8. **Abscess requires drainage**; antibiotics alone may not achieve source control.
9. **Necrotizing fasciitis:** rapidly progressive pain out of proportion, systemic toxicity → urgent surgical evaluation.
10. **Vomiting/NG suction** classically causes hypochloremic metabolic alkalosis.
11. **Refeeding syndrome:** insulin-driven intracellular phosphate/K/Mg shift.
12. **Neurogenic shock:** hypotension plus inappropriate **bradycardia**.
13. **Early warm septic shock:** low SVR, potentially high CO.
14. **Cardiogenic/obstructive shock:** consider high JVP, but patient-specific signs vary.
15. **TRALI** is inflammatory alveolar damage; **TACO** is volume overload.
16. **Tension pneumothorax = hyperresonant**; **hemothorax = dull**.
17. **EDH = lens-shaped**; **SDH = crescentic** on noncontrast CT.
18. **Pringle** stops inflow via portal triad, not all bleeding from hepatic veins.
19. **Howell–Jolly bodies** suggest hyposplenism/post-splenectomy status.
20. **Burn stasis zone** is viable but threatened; hyperemia generally recovers.
21. **Superficial partial** burn is blistered and very painful; **full thickness** is leathery and often insensate.
22. **CO toxicity** can produce normal-looking conventional SpO₂ despite hypoxia.
23. **HF exposure:** fluoride causes low serum calcium and possible arrhythmia.
24. **Escharotomy** releases constricting dead skin; **fasciotomy** decompresses muscle compartments.
