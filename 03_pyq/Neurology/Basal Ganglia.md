# Basal Ganglia — Conceptual & Clinical Understanding

---

## 1. Core Function: What Do Basal Ganglia Do?

Basal ganglia select actions.

They decide which motor, cognitive, or behavioral program is allowed to run and which are suppressed.

Cortex proposes actions → Basal ganglia choose → Thalamus executes

They do not produce movement themselves; they permit or inhibit it.

---

## 2. Key Anatomical Components (Functional Grouping)

### Input Nucleus
- Striatum
  - Caudate nucleus + Putamen
  - Medium spiny neurons (MSNs)
  - Receives excitatory (glutamatergic) cortical input

### Output Nuclei
- GPi (Globus Pallidus Internus)
- SNr (Substantia Nigra pars reticulata)

GPi/SNr provide tonic inhibitory (GABAergic) output to the thalamus and brainstem

### Modulatory / Relay Nuclei
- GPe (Globus Pallidus Externus)
- STN (Subthalamic Nucleus)
- SNc (Substantia Nigra pars compacta) – Dopamine source

---

## 3. Fundamental Principle

Basal ganglia output is tonically inhibitory.

Movement or action occurs only when this inhibition is selectively reduced (disinhibition).

---

## 4. Functional Pathways

---

### 4.1 Direct Pathway — Facilitates Selected Action

Pathway:
Cortex (+) → Striatum (D1) (−) → GPi/SNr (−) → Thalamus (+) → Cortex

Net Effect:
- Inhibits the inhibitory GPi
- Disinhibition of thalamus
- Promotes movement

Clinical Concept:
Required for initiation of voluntary movement.

---

### 4.2 Indirect Pathway — Suppresses Competing Actions

Pathway:
Cortex (+) → Striatum (D2) (−) → GPe (−) → STN (+) → GPi/SNr (−) → Thalamus

Net Effect:
- Increases GPi activity
- Strengthens inhibition
- Suppresses unwanted movements

Clinical Concept:
Improves precision and prevents overflow movements.

---

### 4.3 Hyperdirect Pathway — Emergency Stop

Pathway:
Cortex (+) → STN (+) → GPi/SNr (−) → Thalamus

Net Effect:
- Rapid, global inhibition
- Stops all ongoing or planned actions

Clinical Concept:
Involved in response inhibition and conflict resolution.

---

## 5. Role of Dopamine (SNc)

| Receptor | Pathway  | Dopamine Effect |
| -------- | -------- | --------------- |
| D1       | Direct   | Excitatory      |
| D2       | Indirect | Inhibitory      |

Overall Effect of Dopamine:
- Enhances direct pathway
- Suppresses indirect pathway
- Facilitates movement and action selection

---

## 6. Dopamine as a Learning Signal

- Encodes reward prediction error
- Reinforces useful actions
- Weakens ineffective actions

Basal ganglia function as a reinforcement-learning–based action selector.

---

## 7. Clinical Correlations

---

### Parkinson’s Disease
- Decreased dopamine due to SNc degeneration
- Decreased direct pathway activity
- Increased indirect pathway activity

Result:
- Bradykinesia
- Rigidity
- Difficulty initiating movement

---

### Huntington’s Disease
- Degeneration of D2 (indirect pathway) neurons

Result:
- Chorea
- Hyperkinesia
- Poor movement suppression

---

### Hemiballismus
- Lesion of subthalamic nucleus

Result:
- Violent, flinging movements
- Contralateral to lesion

---

## 8. High-Yield Summary

- Basal ganglia are an action selection system
- GPi/SNr output is tonically inhibitory
- Direct pathway facilitates movement
- Indirect pathway suppresses competing movements
- Hyperdirect pathway produces global inhibition
- Dopamine biases system toward action
- Disorders reflect imbalance of pathways

---

## 9. One-Liner

Basal ganglia do not create movement; they decide which movement is allowed.
