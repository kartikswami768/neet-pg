## Core Concept
Graves’ disease is an **autoimmune cause of hyperthyroidism** due to **TSH receptor–stimulating antibodies** that continuously activate the thyroid gland.

- Antibodies involved:
  - TSH receptor antibody
  - TSI (Thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin)
  - TRAb  
(All are essentially the same thing in exams)

---

## Epidemiology (PYQ Relevant)
- **Most common cause of hyperthyroidism**
- More common in **young to middle-aged women**
- Often associated with other autoimmune disorders

---

## Pathophysiology (Exam Logic)
- Autoantibodies bind to **TSH receptor**
- → Increased synthesis and release of T3 and T4
- → TSH suppressed due to negative feedback
- Antibodies also act on **orbital and dermal fibroblasts** → eye and skin manifestations

---

## Clinical Features

### 1. General Hyperthyroid Symptoms
- Weight loss with increased appetite
- Heat intolerance
- Palpitations
- Tremors
- Anxiety, irritability, insomnia

---

### 2. Thyroid Gland Findings
- **Diffuse, smooth, symmetrical goiter**
- **Thyroid bruit** (due to increased vascularity) — high-yield

---

### 3. Eye Manifestations (Very High-Yield)

#### Graves’ Ophthalmopathy
- Proptosis
- Lid retraction
- Lid lag
- Diplopia
- Exposure keratitis

**Mechanism**
- Autoimmune inflammation
- Glycosaminoglycan deposition in extraocular muscles
- Not directly due to excess thyroid hormone

**Important PYQ Point**
- Ophthalmopathy can **worsen after radioactive iodine therapy**
> RAI destroys thyroid follicles —> releases antigens —> stimulate immune system (autoimmune disease)

---

### 4. Skin Manifestations
- **Pretibial myxedema**
  - Non-pitting edema
  - Peau d’orange appearance
- Due to glycosaminoglycan deposition in dermis

---

## Investigations

### Thyroid Function Tests
- ↑ T3
- ↑ T4
- ↓ TSH

---

### Antibody Testing
- **TSH receptor antibody (TRAb)** — most specific
- Anti-TPO may be present but is not diagnostic

---

### Radioiodine Uptake Scan
- **Diffuse increased uptake** throughout the gland

**Differentiation**
- Graves → diffuse increased uptake
- Toxic adenoma → focal uptake
- Thyroiditis → low uptake

---

## Management (PYQ Focused)

### 1. Antithyroid Drugs
- **Methimazole** — drug of choice
- **Propylthiouracil (PTU)**
  - Preferred in **first trimester of pregnancy**
  - Preferred in **thyroid storm**

---

### 2. Radioactive Iodine Therapy
- Definitive treatment
- Contraindicated in:
  - Pregnancy
  - Lactation
- May worsen ophthalmopathy → steroids may be required

---

### 3. Surgery (Thyroidectomy)

**Indications**
- Large goiter
- Suspicion of malignancy
- Failure or intolerance of medical therapy
- Severe ophthalmopathy

---

## Graves’ Disease vs Thyroiditis (Common PYQ Table)

| Feature | Graves’ Disease | Thyroiditis |
|------|---------------|------------|
| Uptake scan | Diffuse increased uptake | Low uptake |
| Antibodies | TRAb positive | Usually absent |
| Pain | Painless | Often painful |
| Thyroid bruit | Present | Absent |

---

## High-Yield NEET PG Pearls
- Most common cause of hyperthyroidism → **Graves’ disease**
- Most specific antibody → **TSH receptor antibody**
- Diffuse uptake on radionuclide scan → **Graves**
- Eye disease may worsen after radioactive iodine
- Pretibial myxedema is seen in **Graves**, not hypothyroidism
