### 1. Iodide Trapping (Basolateral membrane)
**Na⁺–I⁻ symporter (NIS)**

- Brings iodide from blood into follicular cell  
- Driven by Na⁺ gradient

>**Clinical use – Thyroid scintigraphy**
>- **I-123** → Uptake study (normal: **10–15%**)  
>	- ↑ Uptake → Graves disease  
>	- ↓ / Nil uptake → Cold nodule
>- **I-124** → PET imaging  
>- **I-127** → Normal dietary iodine  
>- **I-131** → Radiotherapy
>- **Tc-99m** → Thyroid scan (trapped, not organified)

**Inhibited by**
- Thiocyanates  
- Perchlorate

---

### 2. Transport into Colloid (Apical membrane)

I⁻–Cl⁻ anti-porter (Pendrin)
**Clinical correlation**
> - **Pendred syndrome**  
>	→ Sensorineural hearing loss + goitre

---

### 3. Oxidation & Organification
(Iodide becomes usable iodine)

- **I⁻ → I₂**
- Occurs at apical membrane
- **Organification**: I₂ is attached to Tyrosine residue (not reacting, just getting close)

**Mediated by**
- **Dual oxidase-2 (DUOX2)** → provides H₂O₂  
- **Thyroid peroxidase (TPO)**

---

### 4. Iodination
- **Tyrosine residues + I₂**
	- Tyrosine residues are provided by Thyroglobulin (which is the storage protein of Thyroid)
- Formation of:
  - **MIT** (mono-iodotyrosine)
  - **DIT** (di-iodotyrosine)

---

### 5. Coupling (TPO-dependent)
- **MIT + DIT → T3**
- **DIT + DIT → T4**

(Hormones remain stored as part of thyroglobulin)

---

### 6. Endocytosis (TSH-stimulated)
- Colloid taken back into follicular cell
- **Enzyme-independent step**

Inside vesicle:
- T3 (~20%)
- T4 (~80%)
- MIT & DIT (non-hormonal)

---

### 7. Proteolysis & Release
- Lysosomal proteolysis releases **T3 & T4 into blood**
- MIT & DIT → deiodinated → iodine recycled

**Inhibition**
- Excess iodide → **Wolff–Chaikoff effect**
