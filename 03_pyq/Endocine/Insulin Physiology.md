# Insulin Synthesis

## 1. Preproinsulin
Synthesized in the rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER).

Components:
- Signal peptide
- A chain
- B chain
- C-peptide

## 2. Proinsulin
Signal peptide is removed in the RER.

Structure:
- A chain + B chain connected by C-peptide

Disulfide bonds form between A and B chains.

## 3. Formation of Insulin and C-Peptide
Occurs in Golgi secretory granules.

Proinsulin is cleaved by prohormone convertases into:
- Insulin
- C-peptide

Both are secreted in **equimolar amounts**.

### Clinical Importance of C-Peptide
- High insulin + high C-peptide → endogenous insulin secretion
- High insulin + low C-peptide → exogenous insulin administration

Useful in:
- Factitious hypoglycemia
- Assessing residual β-cell function

# Physiology of Insulin Secretion

## Stepwise Mechanism

### 1. Glucose Entry into β-cell
Transported via:
- GLUT1 and GLUT3 in humans
- GLUT2 is more important in rodents
### 2. Glucose Phosphorylation
By **glucokinase** (acts as glucose sensor).
### 3. ATP Generation
Glucose metabolism increases intracellular ATP
### 4. Closure of ATP-sensitive K⁺ Channels
↑ ATP closes KATP channels.

Result:
- Reduced K⁺ efflux
- Membrane depolarization
### 5. Opening of Voltage-Gated Ca²⁺ Channels
Depolarization opens calcium channels.

### 6. Exocytosis of Insulin Granules
Ca²⁺ influx triggers insulin release.

# Core Mechanism

```text
↑ Glucose → ↑ ATP → Closure of KATP channels
→ Membrane depolarization
→ Opening of Ca²⁺ channels
→ Ca²⁺ influx
→ Insulin secretion
```

# Biphasic Insulin Secretion

## First Phase
- Rapid release of preformed insulin
- Occurs within minutes
### Clinical Relevance
Lost early in type 2 diabetes mellitus.

## Second Phase
- Sustained slower release
- Due to:
  - Continued synthesis
  - Mobilization of reserve granules

# Incretin Physiology

## Definition
Gut hormones that enhance insulin secretion after oral glucose intake.

# 1. GLP-1 (Glucagon-like Peptide-1)

Produced by:
- L cells of ileum and colon

Actions:
- ↑ Glucose-dependent insulin secretion
- ↓ Glucagon secretion
- Slows gastric emptying
- Increases satiety

Metabolism:
- Rapidly degraded by DPP-4 enzyme

### Drugs Based on This Pathway
- GLP-1 receptor agonists
- DPP-4 inhibitors

# 2. GIP (Glucose-dependent Insulinotropic Peptide)

Produced by:
- K cells of duodenum and jejunum

Action:
- Stimulates insulin secretion

# 3. Amylin

Co-secreted with insulin from β-cells.

Functions:
- Slows gastric emptying
- Suppresses glucagon secretion
- Promotes satiety

### Clinical Relevance
In type 2 diabetes:
- Amylin forms amyloid deposits in pancreatic islets

# 4. Glucagon

Secreted by α-cells.

Stimulated by:
- Hypoglycemia
- Stress
- Exercise

Actions:
- Glycogenolysis
- Gluconeogenesis
- Ketogenesis

# 5. Somatostatin

Secreted by δ-cells.

Functions:
- Inhibits insulin
- Inhibits glucagon
- Inhibits GI secretions

Acts mainly through paracrine regulation.

# Portal Circulation and Insulin

Pancreatic venous blood drains first into the portal vein.

Therefore:
- Liver is exposed to highest insulin concentration first

Importance:
- Liver acts as primary regulator of postprandial glucose.

---

# Insulin Receptor and Signaling

## Receptor Type
- Receptor tyrosine kinase

## Signaling Pathway

1. Insulin binds receptor
2. Autophosphorylation occurs
3. IRS proteins activated
4. PI3K/Akt pathway activated

## Effects

- GLUT4 translocation
- Glycogen synthesis
- Protein synthesis
- Lipogenesis

| Transporter | Location | Important Feature |

| GLUT2 | Liver, pancreas | High capacity transporter |
| GLUT3 | Neurons | High affinity transporter |
| GLUT4 | Muscle, adipose tissue | Insulin-dependent |
| GLUT5 | Intestine | Fructose transporter |

# Metabolic States

# Fed State
Dominated by insulin.

Effects:
- Glycogenesis
- Lipogenesis
- Protein synthesis

Anabolic state.

# Fasting State
Dominated by glucagon.

Effects:
- Glycogenolysis
- Gluconeogenesis
- Lipolysis

Catabolic state.
