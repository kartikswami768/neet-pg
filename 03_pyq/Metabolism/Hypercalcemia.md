# Hypercalcemia in Malignancy

## MAJOR MECHANISMS

| Mechanism                                   | Cause                                       | Common Tumors                                               |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| PTH related peptide -mediated (Most common) | Mimics PTH → ↑ bone resorption, ↓ phosphate | Squamous cell carcinoma (lung, head & neck), renal, bladder |
| Osteolytic metastasis                       | Bone destruction → Ca²⁺ release             | Breast cancer, multiple myeloma                             |
| Vitamin D (Calcitriol) mediated             | ↑ 1-alpha hydroxylase → ↑ Vit D             | Lymphoma                                                    |
| Ectopic PTH (Rare)                          | True PTH secretion                          | Very rare tumors                                            |

## 🧠 LAB FINDINGS (VERY IMPORTANT)

| Parameter | PTHrP | Bone Metastasis | Vitamin D mediated |
|----------|------|----------------|--------------------|
| Calcium | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ |
| PTH | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ |
| Phosphate | ↓ | Normal/↓ | ↑ |
| Vitamin D | Normal | Normal | ↑ |

---

## ⚡ MOST COMMON ASSOCIATIONS

- Most common cause overall → **PTHrP (Humoral hypercalcemia)**
- Most common tumor → **Squamous cell carcinoma**
- Bone metastasis → **Breast cancer, Multiple myeloma**
- Vitamin D mediated → **Lymphoma**

## SYMPTOMS (HIGH-YIELD)

"Stones, Bones, Groans, Moans"

- Stones → Renal calculi  
- Bones → Bone pain  
- Groans → Abdominal pain, constipation  
- Moans → Confusion, depression  

## ECG CHANGES

- Shortened QT interval  (as it accelerates ventricular repolarisation due to shortene [[Cardiac Action Potential#Phase 2 – Plateau Phase|Plateau Phase]])
- Arrhythmias in severe cases  

## TREATMENT (EXAM ORDER)

1. IV Normal saline (first step)  
2. Calcitonin → fastest acting  
3. Bisphosphonates → most sustained effect  
4. Steroids → in vitamin D–mediated (lymphoma)  
5. Dialysis → severe/refractory cases  

---

## ⚡ ULTRA-FAST RECALL

- Squamous cell carcinoma → PTHrP → Hypercalcemia  
- Breast cancer → Bone metastasis  
- Lymphoma → Vitamin D ↑  
- Calcitonin → Fastest treatment  
- Bisphosphonates → Long-term control  

---
# Treatment
- MOST RAPID ACTING: **Calcitonin**
- **Bisphosphonates** are used after calcitonin and they act slowly but have most sustained effect
- **Steroids** can be used in Vitamin D related hypercalcemia
- **Dialysis** used in refractory or renal failure cases
