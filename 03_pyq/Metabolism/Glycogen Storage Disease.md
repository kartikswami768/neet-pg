There are 5 GSDs.
Out of these the most frequently asked are:
	- Type 1
	- Type 0
	- Type 5
- These 3 are the ones that can be presented as clinical case.

### Type 0
Deficient enzyme: **Glycogen Synthase**
- Body will not be able to produce Glycogen.
- It will cause hypoglycemia in fasting state. But gluconeogenesis and fat oxidation will work fine. 

### Type 1 (Von-Gierke)
Metabolism will be stuck at Glucose 6 Phosphate

#### Clinical Presentation
- Usually Infant
- With seizures, irritability, growth and development problems
- On Examination: large abdomen, hepatomegaly, Doll like facies, weak limbs
- On laboratory investigations: 
	- Hypoglycemia (especially fasting)
	- Lactic acidosis
	- Hyperuricemia (gout)
	- Hypertruglyceridemia

#### Pathology
There are two types here:
1. Type 1a: von gierke - There is deficiency of **Glucose 6 Phosphatase**
2. Type 1b: There is deficiency of transporter of G6P

Both types will produce same clinical picture. 
- Liver cannot transport G6P without first converting it into Glucose.
- And to convert it into Glucose it needs to transport G6P into lumen of endoplasmic reticulum.

In both the cases, liver will not be able to transport G6P outside.

This will create a certain situation:
- Large amounts of Glucose product inside liver 
- Hypoglycemia outside liver

The body will think it has low glucose, and since glycogenolysis is not working, it will try Gluconeogenesis (try to produce glucose from lactic acid, glycerol, amino acids).
But GlucoNEOgenesis also use Glucose 6 phosphate in one of its step. And, hence, body cannot produce glucose even from here.
**AND THAT IS WHY, TYPE 1 GSD CAUSES SEVERE HYPOGLYCEMIA**

**Hypertriglyceridemia:**
Now, when glucose is deficient, body will try to use fat stores. It will mobilize fats as triglycerides towards liver. But liver thinks there is abundance of glucose as it has plenty of glucose 6 phosphate. So it will produce more triglycerides.
This causes abundance of fat: causing doll like facies

**Lactic Acidosis**:
Body will not be able to convert lactic acid into glucose through Cori's cycle. The reason is same - this cycle also passess through G6P.

**High Uric Acid**
Two reasons:
1. Major reason: Lactic acid will compete with it to get outside through kidney.
2. The accumulating G6P pushes metabolism toward pathways that increase production of purine degradation products.

**Stunted Growth:**
Chronic hypoglycemia and metabolic stress will cause thinning of limbs and growth abnormalities.

#### Type 1b
It will specifically cause **Neutropenia** as neutrophils require high amount of glucose and aerobic metabolism. They will not be able to use whatever amount of glucose is available due to deficient transport of glucose 6 phosphate.
### Type V (McArdle)
Deficient Enzyme: **Muscle Glycogen Phosphorylase**

The first step of glycogen breakdown is defective.

**Second Wind Phenomenon**
Muscles will not be able to use their glycogen so it will cause pain due to non availability of ATP. As the patient continues to exercise slowly, muscles will start taking glucose from blood and pain will cease. It will feel like there is surge of energy somehow even after fatigue - Second wind.

**Low Serum Lactate**:
Muscles are the ones that cause lactate production on anaerobic metabolism.
Since muscles will use only that glucose that came with blood, and not its own glucose store, it will never allow anaerobic metabolism. Because the glucose will also bring oxygen with it in the blood.

**Burgundy Colored Urine:**
Muscle breakdown will occur --> Myoglobinuria --> Myoglobin will cause urine colour change
Myoglobinuria will also cause **kidney damage**.
