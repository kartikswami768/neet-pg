> **Exam focus:** Consent, rape definition, age, medico-legal examination, two-finger test, hymen, injuries, semen examination, DNA evidence, time window for evidence collection, STI/HIV prophylaxis, emergency contraception, POCSO, documentation and medico-legal opinion.

# 1. Core Concept

The medico-legal examination after sexual assault has **two simultaneous purposes**:

1. **Clinical care**
   - Treat injuries
   - Prevent pregnancy
   - Prevent/treat STIs
   - Prevent HIV/HBV transmission where indicated
   - Provide psychological support
   - Address safety and follow-up
1. **Forensic assistance**
   - Document injuries
   - Collect biological/trace evidence
   - Preserve clothing and other material
   - Establish or exclude possible associations through DNA/serology
   - Provide objective medical findings to the investigating agency/court

A very important distinction:

> **A doctor does NOT medically diagnose “rape”.**

Rape is a **legal conclusion**. The doctor documents history, examination findings and laboratory evidence, and gives a medically justified opinion. Normal findings neither confirm nor refute sexual violence.

# 3. Rape — BNS Section 63
## Definition
**BNS §63** defines rape.
Penetration, **to any extent**, is sufficient.

It includes:
### Penetration by penis into:
- vagina (including labia majora)
- mouth
- urethra
- anus

### Other penetration:
- insertion of an object or body part other than penis into 
	- vagina
	- urethra/
	- anus
- application of mouth to vagina, anus or urethra
- manipulation of a woman’s body causing penetration

The section also states that **vagina includes labia majora** for this purpose.


## Circumstances Making the Act Rape

Any of the above acts becomes rape when performed:
1. Against her will

2. Without her consent

3. Consent obtained through fear
	Consent obtained by putting the woman or someone in whom she is interested in fear of:
	- death
	- hurt

4. Mistaken identity of husband
	The man knows he is not her husband, but she believes that he is the man to whom she is lawfully married.

5. Incapacity due to mental state/intoxication/drugs
	Consent given when, because of:
	- unsoundness of mind
	- intoxication
	- administration of a stupefying/unwholesome substance
	she cannot understand the nature and consequences of the act.

6. Age <18 years
	With or without consent.

7. Unable to communicate consent

These seven descriptions are specified in **BNS §63.**

---

## Consent — Very High Yield
Consent is **unequivocal voluntary agreement** to participate in the specific sexual act.
Consent may be communicated by:
- words
- gestures
- verbal communication
- non-verbal communication

And importantly:
> **Failure to physically resist does NOT by itself constitute consent.**

### Exam trap
**“No resistance = consent” → FALSE**
A person may be unable or unwilling to resist because of:
- fear
- intoxication
- threats
- freezing/tonic immobility
- physical weakness
- coercion
- inability to communicate

So absence of injuries or resistance does not establish consent.

---

## Marital Rape Exception — Current BNS

**BNS §63 retains an exception** for sexual intercourse/sexual acts by a man with his own wife when the wife is **18 years or older**.

Thus:

> Sexual intercourse by a husband with his wife aged ≥18 is excluded from the offence of rape under this exception.

However, **BNS §67** separately **criminalizes** sexual intercourse by a husband with his **wife who is living separately, without her consent**.

### Important exam update
Old IPC questions may show **15 years** in the marital exception.
Current BNS wording uses **18 years**.

# 8. POCSO — Extremely Important

## Definition of child

Under POCSO:

> **Child = any person below 18 years.**

Therefore, for NEET-PG purposes:

> **Sexual activity with a person <18 years cannot be treated as consensual merely because the child says “yes”.**

POCSO applies separately from the general BNS rape framework.

# 11. Medical Examination of a Sexual Assault Survivor
## Golden principles
### 1. Treat first
If there is:
- hemorrhage
- shock
- airway problem
- severe injury
- altered consciousness
- poisoning/intoxication
- pregnancy-related emergency

→ stabilize first.

### 2. Examination requires informed consent
Consent is required for:
- examination
- ==evidence collection==
- treatment
- relevant procedures

==A survivor can refuse:==
- the medico-legal examination
- evidence collection
- both

==Refusal of examination/evidence collection **must not be used as a reason to deny medical treatment**.==

---

# 12. Police Requisition Is NOT a Prerequisite for Treatment
A person may present:
- directly to the hospital
- with police requisition
- under a court direction

A direct presentation does **not** justify refusal of treatment or examination merely because there is no police requisition.

# 13. BNSS Section 184 — Medical Examination of Victim of Rape
Current equivalent of old **CrPC §164A**.

The woman should be sent for medical examination within **24 hours** of receipt of information, and examination is conducted with her consent or that of a competent person where legally applicable.

## Medical report should include:
1. Name and address
2. Name/address of person bringing her
3. Age
4. Description of material taken for DNA profiling
5. Marks of injury
6. General mental condition
7. Other relevant particulars

It must also:
- explain reasons for conclusions
- specifically record consent
- record exact commencement and completion times

The RMP is to forward the report to the investigating officer **within 7 days**.

### MCQ numbers to burn into your brain

> **24 hours → patient sent for examination**

> **7 days → report forwarded by doctor**

# 17. Injuries in Sexual Assault

Possible injuries include:

- General/body injuries
- Genital injuries
- Anal injuries
### Important principle
> **Presence of injury does not automatically prove rape.**

Conversely:
> **Absence of injury does not exclude sexual assault.**

This is particularly important in delayed examinations and in children.

# 19. Hymen — Extremely High Yield

>Never use the hymen as a “virginity test”
>Intact hymen ≠ no previous intercourse
>Torn/absent hymen ≠ rape

Hymenal configuration varies naturally, and injuries can heal.

The examiner must document actual findings rather than interpret them as evidence of sexual “habituation”.


# 20. Two-Finger Test — VERY HIGH YIELD

The so-called:

> **“two-finger test” / per vaginum test to assess habituation to intercourse must NOT be performed to establish sexual assault or rape.**

No comments should be made about:

- vaginal introitus size
- vaginal elasticity
- hymenal elasticity
- “habituation” to sexual intercourse
- previous sexual experience

Similarly, no such commentary should be made about:

- anal opening size
- anal elasticity
- previous anal intercourse

### NEET-PG trap

**“Two-finger test determines habituation to sexual intercourse.”**

→ **FALSE**


# 24. Forensic Evidence Collection
Evidence collection depends mainly on:
1. Nature of assault
2. Time elapsed
3. Washing/bathing/changing clothes



# 25. Clothing Evidence
Clothes worn during the assault can contain:
- semen
- blood
- saliva
- epithelial cells
- foreign material
- hairs
- soil/grass
- tears or other damage

## Handling
- collect relevant clothing
- document stains/tears
- air dry
- fold carefully
- keep separate items separately
- use appropriate paper packaging
- seal and label
- preserve chain of custody

The stained portion should not be allowed to contact unstained portions.


# 26. Debris / Trace Evidence

Ask survivor to stand over a clean sheet when appropriate so that material falling from the body may be collected.

Possible evidence:

- grass
- soil
- fibres
- hairs
- foreign particles

The sheet can be folded and preserved for forensic examination.

---

# 27. Hair Evidence
Possible specimens:

### From survivor:
- loose scalp hair
- pubic hair
- combed hair

### Comparison samples:
- scalp hair
- pubic hair
Hair evidence can be used for trace association, although DNA-based evidence is generally much more powerful than simply comparing morphology.

---

# 28. Nail Evidence
Particularly useful when there was:
- struggle
- scratching
- defensive injury

Potential material beneath nails:
- epithelial cells
- blood
- fibres
- hairs
- foreign particles

Collect nail scrapings/clippings from both hands separately when indicated.

# 31. SEMEN — Major FMT Exam Area

The classic forensic question:

> **“How do you identify a seminal stain?”**

Think in layers:

**Screen → Confirm semen → Identify sperm → DNA**

# 33. Microscopy for Spermatozoa

The most direct classical demonstration is:

> **Identification of spermatozoa microscopically**

Usually after appropriate staining of the smear.

### Presence of spermatozoa
Strong evidence of semen.

### Absence of spermatozoa
Does **NOT** prove absence of semen.

Why?

Because semen may be present when:
- ejaculation was incomplete
- the male is azoospermic
- sperm count is very low
- time has elapsed
- sperm have degraded
- condom was used etc.

Modern semen identification therefore uses additional markers such as PSA and semenogelin.

| ---------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Microscopy       | Spermatozoa                | Direct evidence               |
| Acid phosphatase | Seminal phosphatase        | Screening/presumptive         |
| PSA / p30        | Prostate-specific antigen  | Seminal plasma marker         |

| ==Barberio==     | ==Spermine==               | ==Yellow needle crystals==    |

# 48. Pregnancy Assessment
After reproductive-age sexual assault:

> assess pregnancy possibility.

A urine pregnancy test may be performed.

However:
### Negative UPT immediately after assault does NOT exclude pregnancy
because implantation and β-hCG production take time.
The test is primarily useful for establishing:

> **baseline pregnancy status**

before/around decisions regarding emergency contraception and subsequent follow-up.


# 49. Emergency Contraception

WHO guidance:

> **Emergency contraception should be offered as soon as possible and can be provided up to 120 hours (5 days) after rape.**

### High-yield number

**Emergency contraception → ≤120 hours**

# 50. HIV Post-Exposure Prophylaxis

HIV PEP should be initiated:

> **as soon as possible**

and ideally **within 72 hours** of exposure.

NACO guidance includes sexual-assault survivors among those for whom PEP may be indicated, with decisions made within the 72-hour window.


# 60. Child Sexual Abuse Examination

Children require particularly careful handling.

# 61. POCSO §27 — Medical Examination of Child
Very important.
Medical examination of a child under POCSO:

### Even if FIR/complaint has not yet been registered
the medical examination may be conducted.

### Girl child

> Examination should be conducted by a **woman doctor**.

### Presence
Examination should occur in the presence of:

- parent, or
- another person in whom the child has trust/confidence

If such a person cannot be present:
> a woman nominated by the head of the medical institution should be present.

# 62. Child Examination — Important Trap

> “No physical findings = no sexual abuse.”

**FALSE.**

Most sexually abused children do not have diagnostic physical findings, particularly when evaluated non-acutely.

# 79. The Most Important Conceptual Distinctions

## Distinction 1

**Rape ≠ genital injury**

Rape can occur without injury.

## Distinction 2

**Semen ≠ sperm**

Semen contains seminal plasma + spermatozoa.

An azoospermic man can still leave seminal markers.

## Distinction 3

**Sperm ≠ DNA identification**

Sperm shows biological material.

DNA can provide individual association.

## Distinction 4

**Medical examination ≠ legal determination of rape**

Doctor documents evidence.

Court determines the legal offence.

## Distinction 5

**No resistance ≠ consent**

Consent must be voluntary and unequivocal.

## Distinction 6

**Normal examination ≠ no assault**

Especially important in delayed examination and children.

# 81. One Final Mental Model

When an MCQ gives you:

> **“A sexual assault survivor presents to the emergency department…”**

Run this sequence in your head:

**1. Is she stable?**
↓
**2. Consent**
↓
**3. History of what actually happened**
↓
**4. Examine for injuries**
↓
**5. Collect only relevant forensic specimens**
↓
**6. Think: DNA + semen + trace evidence + toxicology**
↓
**7. Think: pregnancy + STI + HIV PEP + HBV + tetanus**
↓
**8. Document everything objectively**
↓
**9. Do NOT perform two-finger/virginity testing**
↓
**10. Never conclude “rape” simply from the medical examination**

That is the framework from which most of the chapter’s MCQs can be solved.
