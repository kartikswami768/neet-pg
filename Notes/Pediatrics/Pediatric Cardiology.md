# ==FETAL CIRCULATION & INTRO TO CONGENITAL HEART DISEASES==

## Fetal circulation

- Placenta provides oxygen and nutrients to the baby as the lungs aren't yet functional.
- High pulmonary vascular resistance → lungs are not functional.

### Special structures

- Ductus venosus
- Foramen ovale
- Ductus arteriosus

The umbilical artery carries **deoxygenated blood**.

The umbilical vein carries **oxygenated blood**. The ductus venosus connects the umbilical vein to IVC and bypasses the liver.

### Flow of fetal circulation

**SVC → Deoxygenated blood → Right atrium**

**Oxygenated blood → Right atrium**

From the right atrium:

- **1/3 blood (oxygenated)** → Through foramen ovale → LA → LV → Aorta → Descending aorta → Oxygen saturation decreases
- **2/3 blood (mixed)** → Through tricuspid valve → RV → Pulmonary artery → Lungs
- Pulmonary artery → **Ductus arteriosus → Aorta**

> **Handwritten note:** Brain → Head; SVC

### Oxygen saturation

| Structure | Oxygen saturation |
|---|---:|
| Umbilical vein | 80% |
| Ductus venosus | 75% |
| IVC | 70% |
| LV | 70% |
| RV | 55% |
| Descending aorta | 55% (50–60%) |
| Umbilical artery | 55% (50–60%) |

# Circulatory changes at birth

### At birth

**Placenta cut off**  
→ Closure of umbilical vessels

**Baby is breathing**  
→ Oxygen enters into lungs  
→ Decreased pulmonary vascular resistance

**Clamping of umbilical cord**  
→ Increased systemic vascular resistance  
→ Increased pressure in aorta

→ Decreased pressure in RA  
→ Increased pressure in LA  
→ Closure of foramen ovale

### Functional and structural closure

| Structure | Functional closure | Structural closure after birth | Remnant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Umbilical vessels | At/soon after birth | 5–10 days | Umbilical vein → Ligamentum teres; Umbilical artery → Medial umbilical ligament |
| Ductus venosus | At/soon after birth | 3–7 days | Ligamentum venosum |
| Foramen ovale | At/soon after birth | 3 months up to few years (structurally patent foramen ovale) | Fossa ovalis |
| Ductus arteriosus | 10–14 hours after birth | 10–21 days | Ligamentum arteriosum |

### Timeline

Timeline questions can be answered on the basis of **functional closure**.

- Last structure to functionally close → **Ductus arteriosus**
- Last structure to close anatomically → **Foramen ovale**

# Congenital heart diseases / CHD

- Prevalence: **6–8/1000 live births**
- Polygenic/multifactorial inheritance.
- MC CHD: **Ventricular Septal Defect (VSD)**
- MC cyanotic congenital heart disease: **Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)**

## CHD associations

### Genetic syndromes

- **Down syndrome (Trisomy 21)** → Endocardial cushion defect
  - AV valve defects (most commonly regurgitation)
- **Turner syndrome**
  - Bicuspid aortic valve
  - Coarctation of aorta
- **Noonan syndrome**
  - Pulmonary stenosis
  - ASD
  - HOCM (Hypertrophic Obstructive Cardiomyopathy)
- **Edwards syndrome (Trisomy 18)** → VSD
- **Patau syndrome (Trisomy 13)** → VSD
- **DiGeorge syndrome** → Conotruncal defects
- **Williams syndrome** → Supravalvular aortic stenosis
- **Holt-Oram syndrome** → Radial ray abnormalities
  - Absent radius is MC
  - ASD (strongest familial association)
- **Alagille syndrome** → Pulmonary stenosis

### Prenatal maternal exposure

- **Diabetes (overt)** → VSD
- **Congenital rubella syndrome**
  - Patent ductus arteriosus
  - Pulmonary stenosis
  - VSD

### SLE

- **Lupus: Anti-Ro (SS-A) / Anti-La (SS-B)**
  - Antibodies cross placenta
  - Congenital heart disease

### Teratogens

1. Alcohol
2. Phenytoin → VSD
3. Lithium → Ebstein's anomaly

# NADA's criteria for CHD

CHD is possible if **one major or two minor criteria** are present.

### Major criteria

- Systolic murmur: Grade 3 or more
- Diastolic murmur: Any grade
- Cyanosis
- CCF

### Minor criteria

- Systolic murmur < grade 3
- Abnormal SaO₂
- Abnormal chest X-ray
- Abnormal BP
- Abnormal ECG

## Diagnostic considerations

### Systolic murmurs

- Heard throughout systole.
- They are high-grade murmurs (grade 3 or more).
- Always pathological.
- Seen in:
  - VSD
  - Mitral regurgitation
  - Tricuspid regurgitation

### Ejection systolic murmur

- Pathological: Grade 3 or more, heard when blood gets ejected out.
- Seen in:
  - Aortic stenosis
  - Pulmonary stenosis

### Normal

- < grade 3
- Also called short systolic murmur.
- Seen in:
  - High fever
  - Anemia

## Cyanosis

Cyanosis results from severe hypoxia (**SpO₂ < 85%**).

|  | Central cyanosis | Peripheral cyanosis |
|---|---|---|
| Reason | Associated with CHD. Cyanosis due to: 1. Mixing (right → left), 2. Obstruction to pulmonary blood flow | Due to conditions leading to vasoconstriction of limbs → decreased oxygen delivery to tissue |
| Site | Mucous membranes, extremities | Extremities (hands/feet) |

# Ventricular Septal Defect / VSD

## Types

- **Perimembranous VSD**
  - Overall MC type of septal defect (90%)
- **Muscular VSD**
  - High chance of spontaneous closure
- **Swiss cheese VSD** (special type)
  - Low chance of spontaneous closure
- **Subaortic / outlet / supracristal VSD**
  - Never closes spontaneously
  - Associated with increased risk of aortic regurgitation
- **Inlet VSD**
  - Associated with increased risk of mitral regurgitation

## Pathophysiology

Whenever blood flows from **left ventricle (high pressure) → right ventricle (low pressure)**, turbulence is caused.

→ Pansystolic murmur heard over the left 4th intercostal space/parasternal area (**classical murmur**).

Also called **shunt murmur** or **Roger's murmur**.

### Flow murmurs

- Ejection systolic murmur in the pulmonary region
- Increased volume of blood passes from right ventricle to pulmonary artery through pulmonary valve.

## Cardiomegaly

Due to overloading in VSD, there is an enlargement of left ventricle → downward and outward pushing of the apex.

**Characteristic feature:** Apex is displaced downward and outward.

Congestion of heart is seen as **cardiomegaly**.

### Cardiothoracic ratio

**Cardiothoracic ratio = a + b / c**

- **a + b** → Longest cardiac diameter
- **c** → Longest thoracic diameter
- Cardiothoracic ratio > **0.55**
- In older children: **0.6 in infants**

## Complications

- **Lung congestion** → Recurrent pneumonia
- **Congestive cardiac failure / CCF**
  - Occurs around 6–10 weeks after birth
  - Coincides with pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) being very low
- **Infective endocarditis**
  - MC associated complication of VSD
  - MC cause of infective endocarditis in children: **Streptococcus viridans**
- **Late complication: Eisenmenger's syndrome**
  - Constant increased blood flow in pulmonary artery for years
  - Causes irreversible changes due to endothelial dysfunction + vascular remodelling
  - Leads to increased pressure/narrowing of pulmonary artery
  - → Right ventricular hypertrophy
  - As pressure increases in right ventricle, deoxygenated blood starts flowing into the left ventricle through VSD
  - → Reversal of shunt
  - Known as **Eisenmenger's syndrome**
  - Aorta gets mixed blood from LV → cyanosis
  - Feature: Cyanosis in a previously acyanotic patient

## Treatment

### Medical

To control CCF:

- Digoxin
- Furosemide

To treat infections:

- Broad-spectrum antibiotics

### Surgical closure indications

- Features of CCF
- **Qp/Qs > 1.5**
  - Qp = pulmonary blood flow
  - Qs = systemic blood flow
- Supracristal VSD
- Subaortic VSD

### Contraindication for surgical closure

- **VSD + Eisenmenger syndrome**
  - Eisenmenger syndrome causes irreversible changes in pulmonary artery
  - No improvement even after surgery

### Surgeries

- Dacron patch closure
- Catheter-based closure → Occluder device

# Atrial Septal Defect / ASD

- 5–10% of all CHDs

## Types

1. **Ostium secundum ASD**
   - Overall MC ASD
2. **Ostium primum ASD**
   - Associated with mitral valve defects like:
     - Mitral valve prolapse
     - Mitral regurgitation
   - Also associated with increased risk of conduction defects

### Rare types of ASD

- **Coronary sinus ASD (unroofed coronary sinus)**
  - Connection between left atrium and coronary sinus
- **Sinus venosus ASD**
  - Located at the junction of SVC and right atrium

## Pathophysiology

Blood flows through atrial septal defect from **left atrium → right atrium**.

There is minimal pressure difference between the atria.

→ No shunt murmur is produced (as there is no turbulence).

### Flow murmurs

- Delayed diastolic murmur
- Ejection systolic murmur

## Characteristic feature of ASD

**Wide fixed split S₂** (second heart sound) is seen in both types of ASD.

### Normal scenario

Second heart sound is produced due to closure of aortic and pulmonary valves.

On expiration, the valves close at the same time.

On inspiration:

→ Increased venous return to RA  
→ Increased blood flow in RV and pulmonary artery  
→ Delayed closure of pulmonary valve

### ASD

Increased blood flow in right atrium through the ASD

→ Always delayed closure of pulmonary valve irrespective of inspiration or expiration.

Since both types of ASD show wide fixed split S₂, **echocardiography** needs to be done to differentiate the types.

### ECG

- Ostium secundum ASD → **Right axis deviation**
- Ostium primum ASD → **Left axis deviation**
  - Left axis deviation contributed by left anterior fascicular block and mitral regurgitation

## Complications

- Usually start by **4th decade**
- Least incidence of infective endocarditis
  - Smooth blood flow won't cause damage to RA

## Treatment

Mostly ASD close spontaneously.

- If ASD < 8 mm + asymptomatic → Observe
- If no spontaneous closure → Surgical closure
  - Done before school entry / <4 years age
  - Patch closure / catheter-based closure with occluder device
- CCF
- Size > 8 mm
- Qp/Qs > 1

# Patent Ductus Arteriosus / PDA

**Pulmonary artery (right) ← PDA → Aorta (left)**

Turbulent blood flow → **Loud, continuous murmur / machinery murmur**

Heard in the **left 2nd intercostal space (Gibson's area)**.

- Common in female and preterm babies.
- Associated infection: **Rubella**

## Features

- Loud, continuous murmur/machinery murmur

Other causes with continuous murmur:

1. Rupture sinus of Valsalva (RSOV)
2. Aortico-pulmonary window
3. AV malformations
4. Collaterals (coarctation of aorta)

## Complications

- Complications similar to other conditions like:
  - CCF
  - Eisenmenger syndrome
- **PDA + Eisenmenger syndrome**
  - Shunting PDA will cause **differential cyanosis**
  - Upper limbs → Pink
  - Lower limbs → Cyanosed

## Treatment

### Medical

To control CCF:

- Digoxin
- Furosemide

To treat infections:

- Broad-spectrum antibiotics

### Medical closure of PDA

Suitable for **preterm/term babies**.

It should be started within **2 weeks after birth**.

Drugs:

- PDE inhibitors
- NSAIDs like:
  - Indomethacin
  - Ibuprofen

Both drugs are equally effective in closure of PDA.

**Success rate: 70–80%.**

Ibuprofen is the superior drug due to a lower range of side effects, especially **necrotising enterocolitis (NEC)**.

- If child is on total enteral feeds → **Oral ibuprofen is preferred**
- If child is on total parenteral nutrition/nearly enteral feeds → **IV indomethacin is preferred**, as no IV ibuprofen is available in India.

### Contraindications for medical closure

- Platelet count <80,000/cumm
- Serum creatinine 1.8 mg/dL or more
- NEC/feed intolerance
- Any active bleed

### Surgical closure

If contraindication for medical treatment / failure of medical treatment present:

- 3 doses of ibuprofen or indomethacin given and still no closure.

Most term babies usually need surgical closure as medical closure is ineffective.

- Ligation
- Catheter + occluder device
- Coil closure can be used

# Coarctation of aorta

**Obstructive lesion / narrowing of aorta**

### Location

**Juxtaductal / at junction of arch and descending aorta**

## Features

### Lower limbs

1. Intermittent claudication pain
2. Feeble femoral pulse
3. Radio-femoral delay

### BP

**Hypertension due to:**

1. Obstruction increasing pressure
2. Renal damage due to coarctation

Features depend on severity of narrowing.

### Pathophysiology

**Decreased blood flow to descending aorta**

→ Intercostal arteries  
→ Dilatation

### X-ray

**Inferior rib notching** on lower border of ribs due to compression from dilated intercostal arteries.

### X-ray signs

- Inferior rib notching
- **3 sign**
  - Prestenotic + poststenotic dilatation

### Barium swallow

Dilatation will compress the esophagus, appearing as a **€ sign / reverse 3 sign**.

### Diagram

- Normally → SVC, RA, LA, LV
- Coarctation → **3 sign**
- Compensation due to dilatation

### Treatment

**Angioplasty → Balloon dilatation ± stenting**

## Types of cyanotic congenital heart defects

- Obstructive lesions
- Mixing lesion (increases pulmonary blood flow)

**Obstructive lesions → Mixing lesions**

### Obstructive lesions

Obstruction to pulmonary blood flow  
→ Decreased pulmonary blood flow  
→ Decreased oxygenation by lung  
→ Cyanosis

1. Tetralogy of Fallot (overall most common cyanotic heart disease)
2. Tricuspid atresia
3. Ebstein's anomaly

# Tetralogy of Fallot

## Four components of Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF)

1. **Overriding of aorta**
   - The aorta after arising from the left ventricle slightly shifts to the right side.
2. **Right ventricular hypertrophy**
   - Gives rise to right-to-left shunt.
3. **Ventricular septal defect (VSD)**
4. **Sub-pulmonary stenosis / pulmonary stenosis**

## Sub-pulmonary stenosis

- Stenosis below the pulmonary artery and valve, towards the infundibulum part of the right ventricle.
- Also called **right ventricular outflow tract (RVOT) obstruction**.
- Infundibular area hypertrophied → Thick/narrow
- RVOT obstruction (main determinant of cyanosis)
- Due to decreased pulmonary blood flow because of RVOT obstruction, the child develops cyanosis.
- Right-to-left shunt through VSD → mixing of oxygenated blood + deoxygenated blood → cyanosis.

**Pentalogy of Fallot = TOF + ASD (Atrial Septal Defect)**

## Trilogy of Fallot

- ASD
- Pulmonary stenosis with intact ventricular septum
- Right ventricular hypertrophy

## Consequences of TOF

### Cyanosis

- Age is variable as it depends on severity of RVOT obstruction.
- Most of the children develop cyanosis within 4 months after birth.
- Progressive in nature
  - As the child develops, narrowing of the RVOT also increases.

### Failure to thrive

### Single second heart sound

- Only aortic component of the heart sound is heard.
- Pulmonary component is not heard because of narrowing in the pulmonary region.

### Pulmonary area — ejection systolic murmur

Due to blood flowing through the narrow pulmonary area.

### VSD murmur not heard

Large and non-restrictive VSD → No turbulence.

## Complications of TOF

- **Infective endocarditis**
  - Turbulence → Damage to the right ventricle and predisposing to infections.
- **Polycythemia**
  - Long-standing cyanosis → Chronic hypoxia
  - Release of erythropoietin → ↑ RBC production
  - Increased RBCs in blood → Increased viscosity of blood → Thrombosis/thromboembolism
- **Paradoxical emboli**
  - An embolus from the venous system enters the arterial system via the VSD in a patient of TOF.
- **Brain abscess**
  - If a septic paradoxical embolus enters CNS → Brain abscess.
  - Location of brain abscess in TOF → **Parietal lobe**
- Excessive crying, feeding, exertion can cause spasm of infundibulum → **Tet spells / hypercyanotic spells / hypoxemic spells**

---

# Vicious cycle of hypercyanotic spell

**Spasm of infundibulum**  
→ Worsening hypoxia  
→ Acidosis  
→ Hyperventilation  
→ Increased venous return  
→ Further worsening of cycle

## Management of cyanotic spell

Treat each component of the cycle:

- **Hypoxia** → Oxygen supply is given.
- **Lactic acidosis** → Sodium bicarbonate is given.
- **Hyperventilation** → Morphine decreases stimulation of the respiratory center of the brain.
- To decrease the spasm of the infundibulum → **Beta blockers** like propranolol, esmolol may be used.
- Children during cyanotic spell adopt **squatting position**.

### Squatting

**Femoral artery compressed**  
→ Increased systemic arterial resistance  
→ Increase in afterload  
→ Decreased shunting of blood  
→ Decreased mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood  
→ Decrease in cyanosis

**Femoral vein compressed**  
→ Decreased venous return  
→ Lower blood flow to heart  
→ Decreases spasm of infundibulum

### Squatting position

- Done by older children.
- For infants, the mother is advised to bring the baby to a **knee-chest position** during a cyanotic spell.
- α-agonistic drugs like **phenylephrine** can also be given to increase systemic arterial resistance.

### X-ray

**Boot-shaped heart / Coeur-en-sabot**

Due to right ventricular hypertrophy pushing up the apex/left ventricle superiorly.

## Treatment

### Definitive treatment

- Early diagnosis is key.
- Surgery is best done within **3 months after birth** (as early as possible).

### Palliative treatment

Shunt surgeries improve pulmonary circulation.

1. **Classical Blalock-Taussig shunt**
   - Subclavian artery → Pulmonary artery
2. **Modified Blalock-Taussig shunt**
   - Subclavian artery is connected to the pulmonary artery via conduit.
   - Most commonly performed shunt surgery.
3. **Waterston shunt**
   - Ascending aorta → Pulmonary artery
4. **Potts shunt**
   - Descending aorta → Pulmonary artery

# Tricuspid Atresia

Atresia of the tricuspid valve  
→ Small-sized RV  
→ Decreased pulmonary blood flow  
→ Cyanosis

## Associated defects with tricuspid atresia

- Patent foramen ovale or ASD
- Mixing of blood from right to left side of heart via the ASD
- Low O₂ saturation → Cyanosis
- Ventricular septal defect

## Features

- Cyanosis develops at the time of birth or neonatal period.
- Blood shunts from right to left atrium and ventricle
- Overloading of left heart chambers → Congestion on the left side.

**ECG:** Left axis deviation — important distinguishing feature from other cyanotic CHD.

## Treatment

- Should be started as early as possible.
- **PGE₁ infusion** keeps the ductus patent, allowing blood from aorta to flow into the pulmonary artery.

### Definitive treatment

Series of surgery → staged shunting procedures:

**Modified Blalock-Taussig shunt → Glenn shunt → Fontan's shunt**

1. **Modified Blalock-Taussig shunt**
   - To improve pulmonary blood flow.
2. **Glenn shunt**
   - SVC is directly connected to the pulmonary artery.
   - Prevents overloading of left heart chambers and also increases pulmonary blood flow.
3. **Fontan's shunt**
   - IVC is shunted/connected directly to the pulmonary artery.

# Ebstein's Anomaly

Associated with **lithium exposure during pregnancy**.

Downward displacement/prolapse of the tricuspid valve.

Part of right ventricular volume now becomes part of atrium.

This is known as **atrialization of the right ventricle**.

## Consequences of Ebstein's anomaly

- **Tricuspid regurgitation**
  - Prolapsed valve → Blood from the right ventricle regurgitates to the right atrium.
- **RVOT obstruction**
  - Prolapsed valve partly occludes the blood flowing towards the pulmonary artery → Cyanosis.

## Features

- Cyanosis at birth or neonatal period.
- Pansystolic murmur → Due to tricuspid regurgitation.
- **Quadruple rhythm**
  - Irregular blood flow in the right heart
  - RVOT obstruction and tricuspid regurgitation
  - Multiple added sounds and ejection clicks are heard.

## X-ray finding

Due to regurgitation from right ventricle and atrialization of right ventricle, the RA is enlarged.

The RA forms the right heart border, which displaces laterally and appears on X-ray as though a box can fit in → **Box-shaped heart**.

## Ebstein's anomaly associations

- Antenatal exposure to lithium
- **Wolff-Parkinson-White (WPW) syndrome**
  - Bundle of Kent connects the atrium to ventricle.
  - In WPW syndrome → pre-excitation of the ventricle → predisposition to arrhythmias.
  - MC: Paroxysmal supraventricular tachycardia (PSVT)

### ECG findings in WPW syndrome

1. Wide QRS complex: >0.08 sec
2. Short PR interval
3. Delta wave

## Treatment for Ebstein's anomaly

- PGE₁ infusion
- Surgery: **Cone repair of the tricuspid valve**

# Truncus arteriosus

Other cyanotic congenital heart defects:

- Truncus arteriosus (TA)
- Transposition of Great Arteries (TGA)
- Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome (HLHS)
- Total Anomalous Pulmonary Venous Connection (TAPVC)

Mixing of deoxygenated blood with oxygenated blood  
→ Decreased oxygen saturation in the blood  
→ Cyanosis  
→ Increased pulmonary blood flow

Single arterial trunk into which both the ventricles drain instead of arteries arising from the LV and RV.

Deoxygenated + oxygenated blood mix  
→ Predisposes to cyanosis at birth or neonatal period.

### Association

**DiGeorge syndrome**

Conotruncal defects are also seen in this syndrome.

### Conotruncal defects include

- Truncus arteriosus
- Interrupted aortic arch
- TOF

### Treatment

**Rastelli's procedure**

# Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome

## Basic defect

Atresia of mitral valve  
→ Hypoplastic left ventricle  
→ Decreased blood flow into the aorta  
→ Small aorta  
→ Hypoplastic aorta

Most patients have **ASD**, which causes mixing of blood.

Decreased flow in the aorta  
→ Systemic circulation gets compromised  
→ Receives very less blood  
→ Shock

Due to mitral valve block  
→ Congestion of other chambers  
→ Congestive cardiac failure  
→ Seen in the neonatal period as well.

**MC cause of CCF in the first week of life → Hypoplastic left heart syndrome.**

## Treatment

Initially medical treatment is given → **PGE₁ infusion**.

This causes improved circulation in the aorta from the pulmonary artery.

Surgery → **Norwood's procedure**

# Transposition of great arteries / TGA

Change in the position of the great vessels.

- Aorta arises from the right ventricle.
- Pulmonary artery arises from the left ventricle.

**Parallel circulation** is seen in TGA, i.e. there is no crossover between the circulations.

## Types

### Pure TGA / TGA with intact ventricular septum

- Intense cyanosis at birth or soon after birth.

### TGA with VSD

- Pansystolic murmur.
- Less severe cyanosis
  - Aorta receives some amount of oxygenated blood from VSD.

### CCF

Due to increased blood entering the pulmonary artery and left heart.

## X-ray

Heart shadow with relatively narrow area above the heart chambers.

Instead of the pulmonary artery and aorta being positioned side to side, they appear positioned on top of each other in AP view.

This appears narrower than normal on X-ray, like a string attached to an egg.

**Egg on string / egg on side appearance.**

## Treatment

### Pure TGA

Emergency intervention → neonate presents with intense cyanosis.

**PGE₁ infusion** is given.

### Surgery

1. **Rashkind procedure**
   - An atrial septostomy is created at the interatrial septum causing shunt from LA to RA, hence decreasing cyanosis.
2. **Jatene's / Arterial Switch Procedure**
   - Definitive surgery.
   - Changing the position of the great arteries.
   - Done within **2–4 weeks after birth**.

### TGA with VSD

Treatment can be delayed compared to pure TGA (within **3 months after birth**).

Jatene's arterial switch procedure can be performed directly.

# Total anomalous pulmonary venous connection / TAPVC

Instead of delivering blood into the left atrium, the pulmonary vein drains into the right atrium.

## 4 types of TAPVC

- Supracardiac type (MC)
- Cardiac type
- Infracardiac type
- Mixed variety

### Supracardiac type

Pulmonary veins  
→ Vertical vein  
→ Innominate vein (brachiocephalic vein)  
→ SVC  
→ Right atrium

Therefore, oxygenated blood reaches the right atrium instead of the left atrium.

TAPVC patients have **patent foramen ovale or ASD**.

→ Oxygenated blood reaching the left side of heart leaves via aorta.

i.e. mixed blood from the RA via the VSD is supplied by the aorta.

- Blood supplied by aorta is majorly deoxygenated blood
- → Cyanosis
- Congestion in lungs and heart → CCF is also seen.
- Due to ASD, **wide fixed split S₂** is heard.

## Investigation

Estimation of O₂ saturation in different chambers of heart:

**All 4 chambers have the same O₂ saturation**, since RA supplies all the chambers of heart (RV + LA/LV via ASD).

### X-ray

- Figure of 8 appearance
- Snowman appearance
- Dilatation of SVC

---

## Cardiac type

The pulmonary veins directly connect towards the RA.

Similar features to supracardiac type:

- Mild cyanosis
- Congestive cardiac failure
- ASD

**Figure of 8 appearance is not seen** in this condition (SVC is not involved).

## Infracardiac TAPVC / Obstructive TAPVC

Pulmonary veins connect to the IVC or hepatic vein or portal vein.

At the point where the pulmonary veins join the IVC, the pulmonary veins undergo obstructive changes.

→ Decreased draining of blood from pulmonary vein  
→ Pulmonary venous hypertension  
→ Congestion in lungs  
→ Cyanosis at birth

### X-ray

**White-out lungs / ground-glass appearance of lungs** (pulmonary congestion).

Ground-glass appearance of lungs is seen in:

- Respiratory distress syndrome
- Pulmonary alveolar proteinosis
- Obstructive type of TAPVC

## Treatment of TAPVC

### Obstructive lesion

- All infracardiac TAPVCs
- Surgery done as early as possible.

### Non-obstructive lesion

- Supracardiac or cardiac
- Surgery done in first few months of life.

**PGE₁ infusion is contraindicated in infracardiac type of TAPVC** because blood flows from aorta into pulmonary artery, increasing pulmonary congestion.

# ==ACUTE RHEUMATIC FEVER==

### Acute rheumatic fever (ARF)

- Post-streptococcal disorder: **Group A beta-hemolytic streptococci**
- Age group affected: School-going children (5–15 years)
- Incidence: Equal in both males and females

### Pathogenesis

**Pharyngitis → 10 days to few weeks → Rheumatic fever**

#### Molecular mimicry

- Following streptococcal infection, there will be presence of streptococcal antigens in the body.
- The body produces anti-streptococcal antibodies against the antigens and streptolysin.
- Certain body tissues like heart, joint and skin show similarity to these antigens.
- The antibodies cross-react with body's own tissues, resulting in rheumatic fever → **molecular mimicry**.

Probability:

- Normal → 0.3%
- In epidemic → 1–3%

---

## Clinical manifestations of rheumatic fever

### Modified Jones criteria (2015)

Divided regions of world into two:

- Low-risk population
- Moderate to high-risk population (India)

**ARF incidence > 3/1 lakh school-going children**

**RHD prevalence > 1/1000 population**

### Criteria include

#### Major criteria — moderate to high-risk population

1. **Pancarditis**
   - Either clinical or subclinical (ECHO findings suggestive of carditis).
2. **Arthritis**
   - Mono/polyarthritis/polyarthralgia
   - In low-risk population, only polyarthritis is considered a part of major criteria.
3. **Subcutaneous nodules**
4. **Erythema marginatum**
5. **Sydenham's chorea**

#### Minor criteria

| Moderate to high-risk population | Low-risk population |
|---|---|
| Monoarthralgia | Polyarthralgia |
| Fever ≥38°C | Fever ≥38.5°C |
| ESR ≥30 mm/hour and/or CRP ≥3 mg/dL | ESR ≥60 mm/hour and/or CRP ≥3 mg/dL |
| Prolonged PR interval |  |

> **Handwritten notes:** Heart → Pancarditis; Joint → Arthritis; Skin → Subcutaneous nodules; CNS → Sydenham's chorea; “Modified Jones”

### Essential criteria

#### Evidence of preceding streptococcal infection

1. **Elevated ASO titer (anti-streptolysin O titer)**
   - Preferred investigation.
2. Throat swab culture (not usually preferred).
3. History of preceding sore throat (>50%).

### Diagnosis

#### First episode

- **2 major criteria** OR
- **1 major + 2 minor criteria**

#### Recurrence

- **2 major criteria** OR
- **1 major + 2 minor criteria** OR
- **3 minor criteria**

In addition to these, **essential criteria should also be fulfilled** to confirm the diagnosis.

---

## Carditis

**Pancarditis:** All 3 layers affected.

- Most common feature of ARF (90%).
- Early finding of rheumatic fever (within 4 weeks).
- Most serious.
- Permanent damage of valves can occur.

### Diagnosis

Clinical → **Pericarditis**

- Chest pain
- Frictional rub

### Valve involvement

- Most common valve: **Mitral valve > aortic valve**
- Mitral valve involvement → Common in females
- Aortic involvement → More common in males

#### Results in

1. **Mitral valvulitis**
   - Carey Coombs murmur (delayed diastolic murmur)
2. **Mitral regurgitation**
   - Soft S₁
   - Pansystolic murmur
   - Causes hemodynamic overload and precipitates left ventricular failure.

**Main reason for morbidity/mortality in ARF: Left ventricular failure.**

---

## Arthritis

- Large joints affected (knee, ankle, elbow)
- Migratory arthritis
- Early finding
- Found in 30–50% of affected children.
- Excellent/dramatic response to aspirin.
- No residual joint damage → Complete recovery.

---

## Erythema marginatum

- If present, found in early stages.
- Erythematous lesion with pale center.
- Over time the rashes coalesce with each other, forming serpiginous rash.
- Non-pruritic.
- Starts in trunk and limbs.
- Not found in face.
- Induced by warmth/heat.

---

## Subcutaneous nodules

- Late finding (4–6 weeks after onset).
- Painless nodules.
- Present around bony prominences:
  - Elbow
  - Shin
  - Occiput
  - Ankle
- Where tendons attach to bone.
- Presence of nodules is associated with high risk of carditis.

---

## Sydenham's chorea

- Last manifestation (3 months after onset).
- Semi-purposeful, jerky movements of limbs resulting in abnormal coordination and abnormal posturing.

### Associated manifestations

- Hypotonia
- Emotional lability
- Jerky speech

### Signs

- **Milkmaid grip sign**
  - Alternate tightening and loosening of grip.
- **Parting tongue sign**
  - Unable to keep the tongue protruded out for examination.

- Self-resolving (2–4 weeks).
- If not resolving, treat with:
  - Diazepam
  - Haloperidol

---

## Management

### Treatment

- Strict bed rest for **2 weeks**.

#### Penicillin

- IM Penicillin G (benzathine) single dose
- Oral Penicillin V also **4 times/day × 10 days**

---

### Allergic to Penicillin

- **Erythromycin 250 mg 4 times a day × 10 days**

### Anti-inflammatory medications

#### Steroids

- Preferred (especially in cardiac problems).
- Oral prednisolone **2 mg/kg/day to a maximum of 60 mg/day**
- For 3 weeks.
- Taper over 9 weeks.

> **Handwritten note:** ≥30 kg child

#### Aspirin

- **90–120 mg/kg/day** in 4 divided doses × 10 weeks.
- Taper over next 2 weeks.

**Total duration of treatment with anti-inflammatory medications in ARF: 12 weeks.**

---

## Secondary prophylaxis

Prophylaxis given to **prevent further episodes of ARF and cardiac issues**.

- **Penicillin (benzathine/penicillin G)**
  - >30 kg → 12 lakh IU every 3 weeks
  - <30 kg → 6 lakh IU every 2 weeks
- **Oral Penicillin V** → 250 mg twice a day
- **Allergic to Penicillin** → Erythromycin 250 mg twice a day

### Duration of prophylaxis

Depends on problems encountered in initial episode of rheumatic fever.

- **No carditis:** Prophylaxis for next 5 years or till 18 years of age, whichever is longer.
- **Carditis (without RHD):** Next 10 years or till 25 years of age, whichever is longer.
- **Established RHD/undergone surgery:** Till the age of 40 years.
  - Ideally given lifelong.
