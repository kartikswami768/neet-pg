# 1. ANTI-ASTHMA DRUGS

## 1.1 Asthma — pharmacological overview

### PDF-derived content

The PDF describes asthma as an inflammatory airway disorder associated with:

- Bronchial inflammation
- Bronchoconstriction
- Dyspnea
- Chest tightness/pain
- Wheeze

The PDF organizes anti-asthma drugs into two broad groups:

| Bronchodilators | Anti-inflammatory drugs |
|---|---|
| β2-agonists | Mast-cell stabilizers |
| Anticholinergics | Lipoxygenase inhibitors |
| Theophylline | Leukotriene receptor antagonists |
| Magnesium sulfate | Corticosteroids |
|  | Monoclonal antibodies |

### Current verification / correction

The PDF's statement that asthma is simply a **"type 1 hypersensitivity reaction"** is an oversimplification.

Asthma is a **heterogeneous chronic inflammatory airway disease**. Type 2 inflammation is important in many patients, particularly allergic/eosinophilic asthma, but not all asthma is explained by a classic IgE-mediated type I hypersensitivity mechanism.

For current treatment, the central pharmacological principle is:

> **Asthma treatment should contain an inhaled corticosteroid (ICS); bronchodilator-only treatment does not adequately address airway inflammation.**

GINA's 2026 strategy remains the current major international reference for asthma management.

# 2. ACUTE ASTHMA / ASTHMA EXACERBATION

## 2.1 PDF-derived acute-asthma framework

The PDF lists:

1. β2-agonist
2. Anticholinergic
3. Theophylline
4. Magnesium sulfate

It then describes:

- Inhaled/nebulized magnesium sulfate
- Ipratropium
- IV aminophylline, listed as **5 mg/kg**
- IV hydrocortisone to control inflammation
- General anesthesia (GA) + mechanical ventilation if deterioration continues
- Ketamine and halothane are mentioned as preferred anesthetic agents because of bronchodilator effects
- The PDF states that chest infection is a major cause of acute severe asthma and suggests antibiotics.

### Current verification / correction

The modern acute-exacerbation sequence is more accurately represented as:

```text
Acute asthma exacerbation
        ↓
Assess severity + oxygenation + lung function
        ↓
Inhaled rapid-acting β2-agonist (SABA)
        +
Controlled oxygen when indicated
        +
Systemic corticosteroid for moderate/severe exacerbation
        ↓
If severe:
add inhaled/nebulized ipratropium
        ↓
Reassess response
        ↓
If severe exacerbation persists despite initial treatment:
consider IV magnesium sulfate
        ↓
Escalate to acute/ICU care when indicated
        ↓
Respiratory support / intubation and mechanical ventilation
if impending or established respiratory failure
```

GINA 2026 recommends starting SABA, using controlled oxygen when required, introducing systemic corticosteroids early for moderate/severe exacerbations, and adding ipratropium in severe presentations. IV magnesium sulfate can be considered in severe exacerbations that fail to respond adequately to initial treatment. Antibiotics should **not** be prescribed routinely for asthma exacerbations. citeturn3search18

### Aminophylline / theophylline in acute asthma

The PDF lists:

> **Aminophylline IV — 5 mg/kg**

This dose is retained as **PDF-derived content**, but the therapeutic recommendation is outdated.

**Current correction:** GINA states that IV aminophylline/theophylline should **not be routinely used** for asthma exacerbations because of limited efficacy and a less favorable safety profile. citeturn3search19turn3search20

### Magnesium sulfate

The PDF lists magnesium sulfate as a treatment for status asthmaticus and also describes "inhaler/nebulizations."

**Current correction:**

- IV magnesium sulfate is **not routine first-line therapy**.
- It can be considered in selected patients with severe exacerbations who fail to respond adequately to initial treatment.
- GINA describes a single **2 g IV infusion over 20 minutes** in appropriate severe cases.
- Nebulized magnesium sulfate does not have a routine role in current adult asthma exacerbation management. citeturn3search18turn3search19

### Antibiotics

The PDF states:

> "MC cause of acute severe asthma is chest infection: therefore, antibiotics are started as well"

**Current correction:** Do **not** routinely prescribe antibiotics for an asthma exacerbation. Treat a documented/suspected bacterial infection when clinically indicated rather than treating asthma itself with antibiotics. citeturn3search18

### General anesthesia and mechanical ventilation

The PDF mentions:

- GA + mechanical ventilation when the condition remains uncontrolled
- Ketamine and halothane because of bronchodilator effects

These are escalation concepts rather than routine asthma pharmacotherapy.

**Clinical principle:** Mechanical ventilation is reserved for respiratory failure or impending respiratory arrest and should be performed in an appropriate critical-care setting. Ketamine has bronchodilator properties and may be useful in selected critically ill patients requiring sedation/intubation; halothane is historically important but is not a routine modern asthma treatment.

# 3. CHRONIC / PERSISTENT ASTHMA

## 3.1 PDF-derived severity ladder

The PDF presents the following simplified progression:

| Asthma category in PDF | PDF treatment |
|---|---|
| Mild | Low-dose ICS |
| Moderate | Medium-dose ICS + LABA |
| Severe | High-dose ICS + LABA |
| Refractory asthma | Oral corticosteroid + LABA |
| Corticosteroid-resistant asthma | Monoclonal antibody |

The PDF also states:

- ICS is preferred from mild through severe disease, with increasing doses.
- LABA is added for moderate-to-severe disease.
- Refractory asthma is described as uncontrolled despite the highest ICS dose.
- Corticosteroid-resistant asthma is described as uncontrolled despite oral corticosteroids.

### Current verification / correction

This ladder is useful as a pharmacology memory aid, but it is **not a current GINA stepwise treatment algorithm**.

Modern treatment is based on symptom control, exacerbation risk, adherence, inhaler technique, comorbidities, phenotype, and treatment response. GINA 2026 emphasizes **ICS-containing therapy** and uses ICS-formoterol-based approaches in its preferred treatment track. Add-on LAMA and biologics are options for selected patients with uncontrolled severe asthma despite optimized therapy. citeturn0search60turn3search5

**Important exam distinction:**

- **LABA should not be used alone in asthma.**
- When a LABA is used for asthma maintenance, it should be combined with an ICS.
- Formoterol is a LABA pharmacologically, but its rapid onset permits its use as a reliever when combined with an ICS in appropriate regimens.

# 4. β2-ADRENERGIC AGONISTS

## 4.1 Mechanism of action

### PDF-derived mechanism

β2-agonists:

1. Stimulate β2 receptors on bronchial smooth muscle.
2. β2 receptors are **Gs-coupled GPCRs**.
3. Gs activates adenylyl cyclase.
4. Intracellular cAMP increases.
5. Bronchial smooth muscle relaxes.
6. Bronchodilation occurs.

The PDF also states:

> cAMP is degraded by PDE4.

and relates PDE inhibition to theophylline.

### Pharmacology

```text
β2 receptor stimulation
        ↓
Gs
        ↓
Adenylyl cyclase
        ↓
↑ cAMP
        ↓
Smooth-muscle relaxation
        ↓
Bronchodilation
```

---

## 4.2 SABA — short-acting β2-agonists

### PDF examples

- **Salbutamol (albuterol)**
- **Terbutaline**

### Main use

- Rapid relief of bronchospasm
- Particularly important in acute asthma exacerbations

The PDF calls terbutaline the "DOC for brittle asthma type 1."

### Current verification / correction

The phrase **"brittle asthma type 1"** is an older terminology and is not a standard modern GINA treatment category.

SABA remains an important acute bronchodilator, but current asthma strategy emphasizes **ICS-containing therapy** rather than SABA-only treatment for chronic asthma management. citeturn0search0turn0search10

## 4.3 LABA — long-acting β2-agonists

### PDF examples

- Salmeterol
- Bambuterol

### PDF duration

- Approximately **12 hours**
- Traditionally dosed twice daily

### Use

- Maintenance bronchodilation in chronic obstructive airway disease
- In asthma, LABA should be combined with an ICS.

---

## 4.4 Ultra-LABA

### PDF examples

- Indacaterol
- Vilanterol
- "Vlenbuterol" — this name is unclear in the source and is likely an OCR/terminology error.

### Current correction

The intended drug is likely **clenbuterol**, a long-acting β2 agonist encountered in some older pharmacology teaching material. Because the PDF specifically says "Vlenbuterol" and gives no further identifying information, the source term should be treated as **[unclear]** rather than silently rewritten.

Indacaterol and vilanterol are established long-acting/ultra-long-acting β2 agonists used primarily in COPD.

---

## 4.5 Formoterol

The PDF states:

> "Formoterol: SABA + LABA"

### Current correction

Formoterol is pharmacologically a **LABA**, not a SABA.

The important distinction is:

- **Class:** LABA
- **Onset:** relatively rapid
- **Duration:** long
- Therefore, when combined with an ICS, formoterol can serve as both maintenance and reliever therapy in appropriate asthma regimens.

This is the pharmacological basis of **ICS-formoterol MART/SMART-type strategies**.

GINA 2026 continues to use ICS-formoterol-based reliever strategies in its preferred treatment approach. citeturn0search0turn0search60

---

## 4.6 Routes mentioned in the PDF

The PDF states:

- Inhalational administration
- Oral syrups
- Terbutaline can be given SC
- Salmeterol can be given IV

### Current correction

The **salmeterol IV statement is incorrect** for standard clinical use. Salmeterol is an inhaled LABA.

Terbutaline can be administered by routes including inhalation and, in selected settings, subcutaneous administration; however, inhaled therapy is generally preferred for routine bronchospasm treatment.

---

## 4.7 Adverse effects of β2-agonists

The PDF mnemonic is:

> **3T-2H**

The listed effects are:

### Tremor

- β2 receptors are present in skeletal muscle.
- β2 stimulation can produce fine tremor.

### Tachycardia

- May occur because of reflex effects, β2-mediated cardiac effects, and some β1 activity at higher systemic concentrations.

### Tachyphylaxis / receptor desensitization

The PDF describes rapid loss of effect due to β2-receptor desensitization.

**Important correction:** Increased corticosteroid dose is not simply a universal method to "overcome tachyphylaxis." Chronic β2-receptor responsiveness is influenced by receptor regulation and corticosteroid effects, but treatment should be guided by the overall asthma regimen rather than simply escalating ICS to counter tachyphylaxis.

### Hypokalemia

β2 stimulation promotes intracellular K+ uptake.

### Hyperglycemia

β2 stimulation can increase hepatic glycogenolysis and contribute to hyperglycemia.

# 5. ANTICHOLINERGIC / ANTIMUSCARINIC BRONCHODILATORS

## 5.1 Mechanism

The PDF states:

> "Block the M3 receptor of ACh on the bronchus."

### Core mechanism

Parasympathetic vagal stimulation releases acetylcholine, which activates muscarinic receptors and promotes bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion.

Antimuscarinic drugs reduce cholinergic bronchomotor tone, particularly through blockade of **M3 receptors** on airway smooth muscle and glands.

---

## 5.2 Classification

| Class | Drug(s) in PDF | Typical role |
|---|---|---|
| SAMA | Ipratropium | Short-acting; acute bronchospasm |
| LAMA | Tiotropium | Long-acting maintenance |
| LAMA | Umeclidinium | Long-acting maintenance |
| LAMA | Aclidinium | Long-acting maintenance |
| Newer LAMA | Revefenacin | Long-acting COPD therapy |
| Newer LAMA | Glycopyrrolate/glycopyrronium | Long-acting COPD therapy |

The PDF states that LAMAs are used particularly in COPD and that **LAMA + LABA** is highly effective in COPD.

GOLD 2026 continues to recognize LABA, LAMA, and LABA+LAMA as central COPD bronchodilator strategies. citeturn1search49turn1search3

---

## 5.3 Adverse effects

The PDF gives atropine-like effects:

- Dry eyes
- Dry mouth
- Reduced mucus production
- Constipation
- Urinary retention

These reflect peripheral antimuscarinic activity.

---

## 5.4 Paradoxical bronchoconstriction

The PDF states:

> Ipratropium can cause paradoxical bronchoconstriction by blocking M2 receptors, increasing acetylcholine release.

### Pharmacological explanation

Presynaptic **M2 autoreceptors** normally limit acetylcholine release. Nonselective muscarinic antagonism can interfere with this feedback mechanism.

However, clinically significant paradoxical bronchospasm is an uncommon adverse reaction associated with inhaled bronchodilator formulations and should not be presented as the usual action of ipratropium.

The statement:

> "Tiotropium doesn't cause this, as it does not act on M2"

is an oversimplification. Tiotropium has kinetic receptor selectivity favoring M1/M3 functional effects over M2 at the therapeutic airway level, but the important clinical point is that tiotropium is a LAMA used for maintenance therapy.

The PDF presents theophylline as a bronchodilator for asthma and COPD.

Theophylline has a much smaller clinical role in modern asthma management because of:

- Narrow therapeutic index
- Significant drug interactions
- Dose-dependent toxicity
- Better tolerated and more effective inhaled therapies

- Laxative

## 7.2 Mechanism

The PDF states:

> "Blocks calcium channel."

For asthma, magnesium produces **airway smooth-muscle relaxation**, partly through calcium antagonism and reduction of intracellular calcium-mediated contraction.

---

## 7.3 Magnesium toxicity

The PDF identifies the earliest sign as:

> **Loss of the knee-jerk reflex**

This is an important examination point.

Monitoring can include:

- Deep tendon reflexes
- Respiratory rate
- Urine output
- Serum magnesium when clinically indicated

Severe magnesium toxicity can progress to:

- Hypotension
- Respiratory depression/paralysis
- Cardiac conduction abnormalities

# 11. LEUKOTRIENE RECEPTOR ANTAGONISTS

## 11.1 Drugs

- Montelukast
- Zafirlukast

### Mechanism

They block the cysteinyl-leukotriene receptor **CysLT1**.

The PDF writes "LT1 receptor."

### Uses

Particularly useful as:

- Controller therapy in selected asthma patients
- Add-on therapy
- Exercise-induced bronchoconstriction
- Aspirin-exacerbated respiratory disease in selected patients

The PDF describes these drugs as having a **steroid-sparing effect**.

### Montelukast and EGPA / "Churg-Strauss"

The PDF states:

> "ADR: Churg-Strauss syndrome — increase in eosinophil counts"

### Current correction

Montelukast and other leukotriene modifiers have been associated with reports of **eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (EGPA)**. However, the relationship is complex and may reflect **unmasking of previously suppressed EGPA after reduction of corticosteroids**, rather than a simple direct drug-induced syndrome.

For exam purposes:

> **Montelukast → association with EGPA / Churg-Strauss syndrome**

but do not conceptualize every eosinophilia after montelukast as direct causation.

# 12. CORTICOSTEROIDS

## 12.1 Mechanism

The PDF states:

> "Inhibit phospholipase A2."

A more complete pharmacological description:

- Glucocorticoids bind intracellular glucocorticoid receptors.
- The receptor-ligand complex alters gene transcription.
- They suppress multiple inflammatory pathways.
- They reduce inflammatory cytokines, eosinophilic inflammation, airway hyperresponsiveness, and edema.
- They increase expression of anti-inflammatory proteins, including lipocortin/annexin A1, which contributes to inhibition of phospholipase A2-mediated arachidonic acid generation.

---

# 13. SYSTEMIC / ORAL CORTICOSTEROIDS

### PDF-derived uses

- Refractory asthma
- Prednisone
- IV hydrocortisone
- Status asthmaticus

### Current interpretation

Systemic corticosteroids are important for:

- Moderate/severe asthma exacerbations
- Severe acute asthma
- Selected patients with severe/refractory asthma when other treatments have not achieved control

Long-term oral corticosteroids are generally avoided when steroid-sparing alternatives are available because of systemic toxicity.

---

# 14. ICS VS ORAL CORTICOSTEROIDS

The PDF lists these advantages of ICS:

- Pregnancy: "safe" compared with oral steroids
- No risk of Cushing syndrome
- No risk of ACTH suppression

### Current correction

The **principle** is that inhaled corticosteroids have substantially less systemic exposure than chronic oral corticosteroids when used appropriately.

However:

- ICS are **not completely free of systemic effects**.
- At sufficiently high doses, systemic corticosteroid effects can occur.
- Pregnancy should not be summarized as "oral steroids are contraindicated." Systemic corticosteroids may be clinically indicated when needed; untreated severe asthma itself poses important maternal/fetal risks.

---

# 15. MONOCLONAL ANTIBODIES IN SEVERE ASTHMA

The PDF places biologics under "corticosteroid-resistant asthma."

### Current correction

Biologics are **not simply treatments for "corticosteroid-resistant asthma."**

They are add-on therapies for selected patients with **severe asthma that remains uncontrolled despite optimized standard therapy**, often guided by phenotype/biomarkers such as allergic or eosinophilic inflammation.

GINA 2026 includes anti-IgE, anti-IL-5/IL-5R, anti-IL-4Rα, and anti-TSLP approaches among biologic options for appropriately selected severe asthma. citeturn0search60turn3search5

---

## 15.1 Omalizumab

### PDF

- Anti-IgE monoclonal antibody
- Listed for corticosteroid-resistant asthma

### Correct mechanism

**Omalizumab binds free IgE**, reducing its interaction with high-affinity FcεRI receptors on mast cells and basophils.

It is used as add-on treatment in selected severe allergic asthma.

---

## 15.2 Mepolizumab

### Mechanism

- Anti-IL-5 monoclonal antibody
- Reduces eosinophil maturation, survival, and recruitment

---

## 15.3 Reslizumab

### Mechanism

- Anti-IL-5 monoclonal antibody

---

## 15.4 Benralizumab

### PDF

> Blocks the receptor of IL-5.

### Correct mechanism

Benralizumab targets **IL-5 receptor alpha (IL-5Rα)** on eosinophils and promotes eosinophil depletion, including through antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity.

---

## 15.5 Dupilumab

### PDF

> "Blocks IL-4"

### Current correction

Dupilumab binds the **IL-4 receptor alpha (IL-4Rα)** subunit and inhibits signaling from **IL-4 and IL-13**.

Thus:

> **Dupilumab → anti-IL-4Rα → blocks IL-4/IL-13 signaling**

The FDA classifies dupilumab as an IL-4 receptor alpha antagonist. citeturn2search4

---

## 15.6 Tezepelumab

### PDF

> "Blocks TSL"

### Correction

The intended target is **TSLP (thymic stromal lymphopoietin)**.

Thus:

> **Tezepelumab → anti-TSLP**

FDA describes tezepelumab as an add-on maintenance treatment for severe asthma and notes that it targets TSLP. It is not a treatment for acute asthma attacks. citeturn1search0

---

## 15.7 Current update: depemokimab

This is **not present in the PDF** but is relevant to current pharmacology.

**Depemokimab (Exdensur)** is an anti-IL-5 monoclonal antibody approved by the FDA in December 2025 for add-on maintenance treatment of severe eosinophilic asthma in adults and children ≥12 years. It is administered subcutaneously every 6 months and is **not** for acute bronchospasm/status asthmaticus. citeturn2search0turn2search38

## 17.2 N-acetylcysteine

### Mechanism

The PDF states:

> Breaks disulfide bonds and liquefies mucus.

This is a classic mucolytic mechanism.

NAC disrupts disulfide bonds within mucin glycoproteins, reducing mucus viscosity.

### Important separate pharmacological use

N-acetylcysteine is also the antidote for **acetaminophen/paracetamol toxicity** by restoring glutathione-dependent detoxification pathways.

# 18. ANTITUSSIVES

## 18.1 General principle

The PDF describes antitussives as drugs that:

- Suppress the cough reflex
- Reduce activity of respiratory/cough centers
- Are mainly used for troublesome **dry/nonproductive cough**

The PDF states they are contraindicated in productive/infective cough.

### Clinical nuance

Antitussives should be used selectively. Suppressing cough can be undesirable when cough is serving an important mucus-clearance function.

---

## 18.2 Dextromethorphan

The PDF states:

> Blocks the NMDA receptor.

### More complete mechanism

Dextromethorphan is a centrally acting antitussive. Its pharmacology includes **NMDA receptor antagonism** and interaction with sigma-1 receptors.

It is structurally related to opioid compounds but does not act as a conventional opioid analgesic at therapeutic antitussive doses.

### Important safety point

High doses can produce CNS toxicity and, particularly with serotonergic drugs, there is a risk of serotonin toxicity.

---

## 18.3 Opioid antitussives

The PDF lists:

- Morphine
- Codeine
- Pholcodine
- Levorphanol

The PDF labels levorphanol as highly addictive.

### Pharmacology

- Sedation
- Respiratory depression
- Constipation
- Dependence/misuse potential

# 19. DRUGS USED IN PULMONARY ARTERIAL HYPERTENSION (PAH)

## 19.1 Pathophysiological principle

The PDF states:

> Vasoconstriction in the pulmonary artery causes right heart failure.

The pharmacological objective in PAH is to target abnormal pulmonary vascular tone/remodeling through major signaling pathways.

The PDF mnemonic is:

> **PEPSI Coca-Cola**

The exact mnemonic is source-specific; the pharmacology is more reliably remembered by the three major PAH pathways:

1. **Endothelin pathway**
2. **Nitric oxide–cGMP pathway**
3. **Prostacyclin pathway**

The 2022 ESC/ERS guideline remains a major reference for PAH pharmacotherapy. citeturn0search5

## 23.1 Riociguat

### More precise terminology

**Riociguat is a soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC) stimulator.**

It:

- Enhances sensitivity of sGC to endogenous nitric oxide
- Increases cGMP
- Promotes pulmonary vasodilation

# 26. CALCIUM-CHANNEL BLOCKERS IN PAH

The PDF lists:

- Amlodipine
- Nifedipine

and states:

### Important correction

This is an important area where the PDF is misleading.

High-dose calcium-channel blockers are used in a **small, carefully selected subset of patients with idiopathic/heritable/drug-associated PAH who demonstrate a positive acute pulmonary vasoreactivity test**.

They are **not routine therapy simply because systemic hypertension is present**.

The 2022 ESC/ERS framework emphasizes identifying the small group of vasoreactive PAH patients who may benefit from calcium-channel blockers. citeturn0search5turn1search1

# 28. DRUGS USED IN CYSTIC FIBROSIS

## 28.1 Basic pharmacology

The PDF states:

> Defect in CFTR cells on epithelial cells, defective production of Cl− and HCO₃−.

More accurately:

- CF is caused by pathogenic variants in the **CFTR gene**.
- CFTR is an epithelial chloride and bicarbonate channel/regulator.
- Defective CFTR function alters ion and water transport.
- Airway secretions become dehydrated and viscous.
- The resulting mucus obstruction contributes to recurrent infection and progressive lung disease.

# 29. CFTR MODULATORS

## 29.1 Drugs listed in the PDF

- Ivacaftor
- Lumacaftor
- Elexacaftor
- Tezacaftor

The PDF lists the individual drugs without explaining their functional classes.

### Current classification

| Drug | Class | Core action |
|---|---|---|
| Ivacaftor | Potentiator | Increases opening/probability of responsive CFTR channels at the cell surface |
| Lumacaftor | Corrector | Improves folding/trafficking of F508del-CFTR |
| Tezacaftor | Corrector | Improves CFTR processing/trafficking |
| Elexacaftor | Corrector | Improves CFTR folding/processing; used in highly effective combinations |

The CF Foundation describes five currently available CFTR-modulator therapies, including ivacaftor, lumacaftor/ivacaftor, tezacaftor/ivacaftor, elexacaftor/tezacaftor/ivacaftor, and the newer vanzacaftor/tezacaftor/deutivacaftor combination. citeturn0search7

## 29.2 Major combinations

### Ivacaftor

Used for specific CFTR variants that are responsive to potentiation.

### Lumacaftor + ivacaftor

- Corrector + potentiator

### Tezacaftor + ivacaftor

- Corrector + potentiator

### Elexacaftor + tezacaftor + ivacaftor

# 32. HIGH-YIELD EXAM PEARLS

## Asthma

- **ICS = cornerstone of chronic asthma pharmacotherapy**
- **LABA must not be used alone in asthma**
- **Formoterol = LABA with rapid onset**
- **SABA = rapid bronchodilation**
- **Ipratropium = SAMA**
- **Tiotropium = LAMA**
- **β2 agonist → Gs → ↑cAMP → bronchodilation**
- β2 agonists can cause:
  - Tremor
  - Tachycardia
  - Hypokalemia
  - Hyperglycemia
- **Theophylline = narrow therapeutic index + major interactions**
- Theophylline toxicity:
  - GI symptoms
  - CNS excitation/seizures
  - Arrhythmias
- **Montelukast/zafirlukast → CysLT1 blockade**
- **Zileuton → 5-lipoxygenase inhibition**
- **Omalizumab → anti-IgE**
- **Mepolizumab/reslizumab → anti-IL-5**
- **Benralizumab → anti-IL-5Rα**
- **Dupilumab → anti-IL-4Rα → IL-4/IL-13 blockade**
- **Tezepelumab → anti-TSLP**
- **Depemokimab → anti-IL-5, long-interval dosing**

## Acute asthma

- First-line bronchodilator: **rapid-acting inhaled β2 agonist**
- Add **ipratropium** in severe exacerbation
- Give **systemic corticosteroid** early in moderate/severe exacerbation
- **IV magnesium sulfate** is an adjunct for selected severe refractory cases
- **Do not routinely use IV aminophylline/theophylline**
- **Do not routinely prescribe antibiotics**
- Respiratory failure → ICU-level escalation and ventilatory support

## COPD

- **LAMA + LABA** is a major bronchodilator combination
- **Roflumilast = PDE4 inhibitor**
- Long-acting bronchodilators are central to COPD maintenance therapy. citeturn1search49turn1search3

## PAH

Remember the three major pathways:

```text
ENDOTHELIN      → endothelin receptor antagonists
NO–cGMP         → PDE5 inhibitors / sGC stimulator
PROSTACYCLIN    → prostacyclin analogues / IP receptor agonist
```

- Bosentan/ambrisentan/macitentan → endothelin antagonists
- Sildenafil/tadalafil → PDE5 inhibitors
- Riociguat → sGC stimulator
- Epoprostenol/treprostinil/iloprost → prostacyclin pathway
- Selexipag → IP receptor agonist
- CCB → only selected vasoreactive PAH

## Cystic fibrosis

- **Ivacaftor = potentiator**
- **Lumacaftor/tezacaftor/elexacaftor = correctors**
- **Elexacaftor + tezacaftor + ivacaftor = highly effective triple-modulator regimen for eligible patients**
- Newer regimen: **vanzacaftor + tezacaftor + deutivacaftor**
