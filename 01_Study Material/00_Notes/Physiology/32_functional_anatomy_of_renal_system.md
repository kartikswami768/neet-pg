# 32 — Functional Anatomy of Renal System

## 19. Functional Anatomy Of Renal System
### NEPHRONS

| Parameter | Value stated in the source |
|---|---|
| Number per kidney | 0.8–1.2 million (average: 1 million) |
| Nephron length | 45–65 mm (average: 5 cm) |
| Renal blood flow | 1000–1260 mL/min (20–25% of cardiac output) |
| Distribution of renal blood flow | Cortex: 90%; medulla: 10% |
| Renal plasma flow | 625–700 mL/min |

- Nephron number decreases with age.
- After 40 years: a decrease of 10% per 10 years.
- Nephrons do not regenerate.
- Adaptive changes occur in remaining nephrons: hyperfiltration and hypertrophy.
- The source states: “Serious clinical symptoms of CKD develops” when “functional nephrons fall to at least 70%–75% below normal.” The threshold wording is retained as printed and is ambiguous.

### PARTS OF NEPHRON
- The source diagram labels: Bowman's capsule (BC), glomerulus, proximal tubule (proximal convoluted and proximal straight tubules), loop of Henle (thin descending, thin ascending and thick ascending segments), distal tubule, connecting tubule (secretes renal kallikrein), initial collecting tubule, cortical collecting tubule/ducts and medullary collecting ducts.
- Glomerulus: capillary network inside Bowman's capsule.
- Bowman's capsule: cup-shaped structure enclosing the glomerulus.
- The thin ascending segments of the loop of Henle are prominent in juxtamedullary nephrons and shorter in cortical nephrons.
- Initial collecting tubules (ICT) and cortical collecting tubules/ducts are ultrastructurally similar and contain special cells.

### COMPARISON BETWEEN THE EPITHELIAL CELLS OF PCT AND COLLECTING DUCT (CD)
| Feature | PCT epithelial cells | Collecting duct (CD) epithelial cells |
|---|---|---|

| Transport between cells | Both paracellular and transcellular transport. | No paracellular transport; only transcellular transport. |

### ACETAZOLAMIDE
- Drug that blocks carbonic anhydrase type II, IV
- Predominantly acts at level of PCT as it inhibits both types
- Secondary action at level of CD
### GLOMERULAR FILTRATION BARRIER
- The glomerular filtration barrier is the layer through which ultrafiltration occurs.
- The source states that the diameter of the efferent arteriole is smaller than the afferent arteriole and that multiple glomerular capillaries lie between them.

### BOWMAN'S CAPSULE
- The visceral layer is in contact with the glomerular capillary; the parietal layer is not in contact with the capillary.
- A substance in the glomerular capillary must cross the following layers to reach Bowman's capsule.
- The source states that all layers are negatively charged. Positively charged substances are attracted and pass more easily through the membrane.
- Filtration order by charge as given in the source: positive ≫ neutral ≫ negative.

| Layer | Description stated in the source |
|---|---|
| First: fenestrated endothelium | Gaps between endothelial cells: 70–90 nm. Lined with a negatively charged glycocalyx coat. |
| Second: glomerular basement membrane (BM) | Gap: 10 nm. Contains negatively charged heparan sulfate proteoglycan (HSPG). |
| Third: visceral layer | Specialized epithelial cells (podocytes). Podocyte foot processes line the visceral epithelium of Bowman's capsule. The gap between foot processes is the filtration slit (25 nm). The slit diaphragm is described as having a gap of 4–14 nm. The source names the negatively charged sialoglycoproteins as “podoclyxin” and “podoendin”; those spellings are retained pending source verification. |

### FILTERABILITY OF SUBSTANCES
| Category | Molecular radius | Description/examples stated in the source |
|---|---|---|
| Freely filterable substances | <1.8 nm | Pass easily irrespective of charge. Examples: Na⁺, K⁺, H₂O, urea, Cl⁻, glucose, sucrose, polyethylene glycol and inulin. |
| Selectively permeable substances | 1.8–4.2 nm | Filtration is determined by charge. The source gives the order: positively charged ≫ neutral ≫ negatively charged. Examples: albumin (molecular radius 3.55 nm), myoglobin, lactoglobulin, Bence Jones proteins and lysozyme. |
| Impermeable | >4.2 nm | Listed as impermeable in the source diagram. |

- Albumin is negatively charged and has very little filtration at the barrier.
- After filtration, most albumin is reabsorbed by endocytosis involving megalin and cubilin.
- Normal albumin level in urine: 8–10 mg/day.
