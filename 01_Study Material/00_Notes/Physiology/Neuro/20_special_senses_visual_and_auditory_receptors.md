# 20. Special Senses: Visual and Auditory Receptors

## Special senses

Special senses use specialized organs to detect and process stimuli:

- **Vision:** rods and cones. Visual transduction converts light energy to action potentials in the optic nerve (source wording).
- **Hearing and balance:** hair cells. Auditory transduction converts sound energy to action potentials in the auditory nerve.
- **Smell:** olfactory receptors.
- **Taste:** gustatory receptors.

## Visual transduction

### Mechanisms of phototransduction: layers of the retina

- The source describes the retina as consisting of 10 layers.
- **Internal limiting membrane:** anterior-most layer of the retina.
- **Pigment-cell layer:** posterior-most layer of the retina; absorbs excess light.
- Light activates rods and cones in the photoreceptor layer.
- Outer nuclear layer is formed by the nuclei of rods and cones.
- Inner nuclear layer is formed by the nuclei of bipolar cells.
- Outer plexiform layer contains synapses between rods, cones, and bipolar cells; it also contains horizontal cells.
- Inner plexiform layer contains synapses between bipolar and ganglion cells; it also contains amacrine cells.
- The optic nerve is formed by the axons of ganglion cells.
- Ganglion cells in the retina generate action potentials.

![[ch20_p144_retinal_layers_and_circuit.png]]

### Important cells of the retina

| Cell | Function/source description |
|---|---|
| Rods and cones | Receptor cells. |
| Bipolar cells | Synapse with receptor cells and determine sensitivity and acuity. |
| Horizontal cells | Contrast enhancement (lateral inhibition) and colour opponency. |
| Amacrine cells | Modify the signal from bipolar to ganglion cells. |
| Ganglion cells | Output cells of the retina; form the optic nerve. |
| Müller cells | Glial cells of the retina; help form the blood-retinal barrier. |

### Retinal cell numbers and neuronal connections

- 120 million rods and 6 million cones.
- 1.2 million nerve fibres in each optic nerve.
- The source lists the bipolar-cell-to-ganglion-cell ratio as about **105:1**.
- Visual-cortex neurons are stated to be 1,000 times more numerous than nerve fibres.
- Rods and cones synapse with bipolar cells:
  - Multiple rods converge on a single bipolar cell.
  - A single cone converges with a single bipolar cell.
  - Multiple bipolar cells synapse with a single ganglion cell.
  - Axons of ganglion cells form the optic nerve.
- Rods and cones are described as modified neuron cells and as first-order neurons; bipolar cells as second-order neurons; and ganglion cells as third-order neurons (source wording).

### Rods versus cones

| Function | Rods | Cones |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity to light | Sensitive to low-intensity light; night vision. | Sensitive to high-intensity light; day vision. |
| Acuity | Lower visual acuity; not present in the fovea (source wording). | Higher visual acuity; present in the fovea. |
| Dark adaptation | Rods adapt later. | Cones adapt first. |
| Colour vision | No. | Yes. |

## Structure of photoreceptors and visual pigment

### Rods and cones

- Rods and cones contain:
	- **Outer segment:** 
		- discs are present in rods; sacs are present in cones and ultimately form a disc-like appearance (source wording). 
		- The outer segment contain non-specific cation channels.
	- **Inner segment:** 
		- contains mitochondria and nucleus; 
		- contains a sodium–potassium ATPase pump.
	- **Synaptic terminal:** synapses with bipolar cells.
- The source labels approximately 1,000 discs in each rod or cone.

![[ch20_p146_photoreceptor_structure.png]]

### Dark current

- In dark conditions, no light falls on rods and cones.
- The **rhodopsin**, **G-protein (transducin)**, and **guanylate cyclase** are present on the outer part of the membrane and states that guanylate cyclase increases cGMP.
- cGMP stimulates the opening of a non-specific cation channel (**cGMP-gated Na⁺ channel**).
- **Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ enter (Na⁺ greater than Ca²⁺**), **==depolarizing==** rods and cones; membrane potential becomes approximately **−40 mV**.
- Na⁺ in the outer segment enters the inner segment.
- The Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump in the inner segment causes entry of K⁺ and exit of Na⁺; later, K⁺ leaves the inner segment and enters the outer segment via the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump (as worded in the source).
- The resulting **==constant current flow==** from **outer to inner segment and inner to outer segment** is called the **dark current**.
- The source states: rods and cones in dark → depolarization → hyperpolarization of bipolar cells → no action potential in ganglion cells.

![[ch20_p147_dark_current_and_phototransduction.png|800]]

### Visual pigment and rhodopsin activation

- Rhodopsin is present in the membrane of the disc.
- **Rhodopsin = 11-cis retinal + opsin** (protein).
- Cone pigments/colour-pigment composition is slightly different from rhodopsin.
- Rhodopsin activation sequence shown in the source:

  Light → rhodopsin (11-cis retinal + opsin) → bathorhodopsin (nsec) → lumirhodopsin (μsec) → metarhodopsin I (msec) → metarhodopsin II (sec) → all-trans retinal + opsin.

- Metarhodopsin II is also called activated rhodopsin and activates the G-protein **transducin**.
- Transducin is the G-protein of the retina.
- ==🟡Hyperpolarisation occurs in Light==
>==🔵Photoreceptors are the only Receptors that undergo hyperpolarisation on stimulation==

![[ch20_p146_rhodopsin_visual_pigment.png]]



### Phototransduction in light

1. Light is absorbed by the photopigment.
2. Opsin dissociates from retinal.
3. Metarhodopsin II activates the G-protein transducin.
4. The G-protein activates phosphodiesterase (PDE).
5. Breakdown of cGMP decreases the cGMP level.
6. Na⁺ channels close and rods/cones hyperpolarize (approximately −70 mV, as stated in the source).
7. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels close.
8. Neurotransmitter release decreases.
9. The bipolar cell depolarizes.
10. An action potential is generated in the ganglion cell and travels through the optic nerve.

![[ch20_p148_visual_transduction_pathway.png]]

### Key points listed in the source

- Receptor potential is a hyperpolarizing type (exception) in rods and cones.
- Action potentials are generated only in ganglion cells of the retina.
- Bipolar, horizontal, and amacrine cells produce local potentials.
- Rods and cones hyperpolarize in the presence of light.
- Bipolar cells may hyperpolarize or depolarize.
- Amacrine cells are listed as depolarizing.
- G-protein of the retina: transducin.
- Neurotransmitter of the photoreceptor cell: glutamate.

## Auditory transduction

### Anatomy of the inner ear and cochlea

- Inner ear consists of two main functional parts:
  - **Cochlea:** hearing.
  - **Vestibular system:** balance/posture.
- The cochlea is described as a system of coiled tubes with **2.75 turns**.
- Reissner’s membrane and the basilar membrane divide the bony cochlea into three parts:
  - Scala vestibuli.
  - Scala tympani.
  - Scala media.
- Scala media is rich in potassium and is described as similar to ICF; it is responsible for an endolymphatic potential of +80 to +85 mV.
- Scala vestibuli and scala tympani are rich in sodium and are described as similar to ECF.

![[ch20_p148_cochlear_anatomy.png]]

### Organ of Corti and hair cells

- The organ of Corti is the specialized organ for auditory signal transduction.
- Located on the basilar membrane.
- Contains the receptor cells: inner and outer hair cells.
- Cilia protrude from hair cells and are embedded in the tectorial membrane.

| Hair-cell type | Features listed in the source |
|---|---|
| **Outer hair cells** | Three rows; about 20,000; V- or W-shaped ranks; responsible for modulation of sound. |
| **Inner hair cells** | One row; about 3,500; straight-line ranks; mainly responsible for sound transduction. |

**Single hair cell**

- Resting membrane potential (RMP): −60 mV.
- 30–150 rod-shaped hairs (stereocilia).
- One large, non-motile kinocilium.
- Towards the kinocilium, stereocilia progressively increase in height.
- The kinocilium is present during development but regresses in mature auditory hair cells.

### Potentials in the ear

- Scala media contains endolymph; endolymphatic potential: +85 mV.
- Scala tympani and scala vestibuli contain perilymph; perilymph potential: 0 mV.
- Hair-cell RMP with respect to surrounding perilymph: −60 mV.
- Hair-cell RMP with respect to endolymph: −145 mV (140–150 mV, as given in the source).

### Afferent and efferent innervation of hair cells

- **Afferent:** Most (90–95%) sensory neurons innervate inner hair cells; only 5–10% innervate the more numerous outer hair cells.
- **Efferent:** Most efferent fibres terminate on outer rather than inner hair cells.

### Sound-transduction mechanism: inner hair cells

- The connection between stereocilia runs from the tip of the smaller stereocilium to the side of the larger stereocilium; this is the **tip link** (described in the source as made of actin).
- A non-specific cation channel is present near the tip link.
- Stretching the tip link opens the channel; relaxation of the tip link closes it.
- Stereocilia pushed towards the higher stereocilia → opening of cation channels.
- K⁺ and Ca²⁺ enter through the channel (K⁺ much greater than Ca²⁺, as shown in the source) → hair-cell depolarization → glutamate production/release → action potential in afferent neurons of cranial nerve VIII.
- After depolarization, the non-specific cation channel is closed by myosin (source wording).
