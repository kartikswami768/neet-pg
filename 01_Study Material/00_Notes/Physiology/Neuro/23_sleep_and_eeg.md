## 51. SLEEP AND EEG

### Sleep                                                                                                  00:00:45
- Sleep is a behavioural state that alternates with wakefulness
- Characterized by:
- Lying down posture
- Raised threshold to sensory stimulation
- Low level of motor output
- Unique behaviour of dreaming → NREM → Non-recallable dreaming → REM → Active dreaming, visual & emotional activity present
- Sleep vs Coma
- Sleep → Reversible, Coma → Not reversible

### Eeg & Evoked Potential                                                                         00:05:02
- Pyramidal neurons are located in the cerebral cortex
- Have long dendrites extending toward the scalp surface
- Multiple neurons form synapses with these dendrites
- Synapses can be excitatory or inhibitory
- Excitatory synapse → Generate EPSP (Excitatory postsynaptic potentials)
- Inhibitory synapse → Generate IPSP (inhibitory postsynaptic potentials)
- These local potentials are generated just beneath the cortical surface
- Electrode placement on the scalp records the summation of all dendritic potentials
- 2 (EPSP + IPSP)
- This summated electrical activity is known as EEG (Electroencephalography)
- EEG:
- Recording of dendritic potentials from surface of the scalp

![[ch23_p165_sleep_stages.png]]

- EEG is not an action potential recording
- EPSP and IPSP generate action potentials at the initial segment in deeper cortical layers
- Scalp electrodes cannot record deep cortical action potentials
- If the electrode is placed on the cerebral cortex (at Pial Surface) → Electrocorticogram

### Evoked Potential                                                                                      00:10:54
- Electrical response of the brain to a specific stimulus
- E.g., Skin stimulation → Signal reaches primary somatosensory cortex (SS1) → Electrode placed over the SS1 area records Somatosensory Evoked Potential (SSEP)
- Local potential
- Evoked potentials exhibit a time gap → Latency (generally 5-12 milliseconds)
- Recorded to check the integrity of the sensory pathway, connected with the part of cerebral cortex

### Types Of Evoked Potentials
- 1) Somatosensory Evoked Potential (SSEP) - Response to sensory stimuli
- 2) Visual Evoked Potential (VEP) - Response to visual stimuli (e.g., flashing lights).
- 3) Auditory Evoked Potential (AEP) - Response to auditory stimuli

### Mechanisms Of Sleep                                                                               00:49:53
- Hypothalamus contains:
- In Anterior Hypothalamus: Ventrolateral preoptic nucleus (VLPO) → Sleep promoting area
- In Posterior Hypothalamus: Tuberomammillary nucleus (TMN) → Wake promoting area
- Mutual inhibition exists between VLPO and TMN → when VLPO is active, TMN is inhibited, and vice versa
- These regions serve as the main centre for sleep control
