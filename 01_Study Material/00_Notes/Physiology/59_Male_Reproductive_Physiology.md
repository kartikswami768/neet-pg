# 59. Male Reproductive Physiology

## Parts of the male reproductive system

- The **testis** is the primary reproductive organ where spermatogenesis occurs.
- The epididymis continues as the vas deferens, which joins the seminal vesicle to form the ejaculatory duct; it opens into the urethra.
- Seminiferous tubules are multiple coiled tubes within the testis.
- Each testis contains approximately **900 coiled seminiferous tubules**.
- Seminiferous tubules merge to form the rete testis, which drains into the epididymis.

## Sperm inside the epididymis

### Storage and maturation in the epididymis (12–26 days)

- Sperm acquire motility.
- Sperm undergo maturation, including acrosome development.
- Cytoplasm and cell volume decrease.
- Sperm gain receptors for the zona pellucida, enabling binding.

**Important information:** Final maturation of sperm (capacitation) occurs inside the female genital tract.

## Seminiferous tubule

- Leydig cells, located outside the seminiferous tubules, produce testosterone.
- Inside seminiferous tubules, Sertoli cells are interconnected by cytoplasmic bridges, with tight junctions forming the **blood–testis barrier (BTB)**.
- Spermatogonia, responsible for sperm formation, reside outside the blood–testis barrier in the basal compartment. The source states that spermatogenesis does not occur in this compartment, but spermatogonia are maintained there.
- Spermatogonia divide and proliferate, then cross the blood–testis barrier, entering the adluminal compartment as primary spermatocytes.
- The adluminal compartment, protected by the BTB, is where spermatogenesis occurs.
- Testosterone produced by Leydig cells diffuses into and out of the adluminal compartment.
- Sertoli cells secrete androgen-binding protein (ABP), which binds testosterone and prevents its escape into the interstitial space (“testosterone trapping”).
- Adluminal compartment:
  - High testosterone levels.
  - Testosterone is converted to estrogen by aromatase, which is also active in Sertoli cells.
  - Estrogen levels increase.
  - Potassium (K⁺) ions are present.
  - The compartment is described as favourable for spermatogenesis, unlike the outside environment.
- Spermatogonia develop into primary and secondary spermatocytes while remaining attached to Sertoli cells throughout the process.
- Sertoli cells nourish developing sperm and secrete factors that maintain a favourable environment for sperm development.
- Outside the basal lamina are Leydig cells, which secrete testosterone and have LH receptors.
- Sertoli cells have FSH receptors.
- Primary spermatocytes, secondary spermatocytes and spermatids are in the adluminal compartment.
- A spermatid remains attached to Sertoli cells during maturation. Release of a spermatid from a Sertoli cell is **spermiation**.

## Steps of spermatogenesis

1. Primordial germ cells from the yolk sac migrate to the testis and form spermatogonia, which remain until puberty.
2. At puberty, under the influence of LH and FSH, spermatogonia proliferate and develop. Large numbers of spermatogonia are produced and remain in the basal compartment of seminiferous tubules.
3. Spermatogonia develop into primary spermatocytes. The blood–testis barrier opens and the primary spermatocytes cross it.
4. Primary spermatocytes undergo meiotic division I to form secondary spermatocytes.
5. Secondary spermatocytes undergo meiotic division II and form spermatids.
6. Spermatids differentiate into mature sperm/spermatozoa.

**Terminology**

- **Spermiogenesis:** conversion of spermatids to mature sperm; the final step of sperm maturation.
- **Spermatogenesis:** development of sperm.
- **Spermiation:** release of sperm from Sertoli cells.
- Total time from development of spermatogonia to mature sperm: **74 days**.

## Spermatogenesis in different species

- **Rodents:** multiple steps of spermatogonial proliferation; one spermatogonium gives rise to 512 spermatids/sperm.
- **Primates:** multiple steps of spermatogonial proliferation are absent.
- **Humans:** multiple steps of spermatogonial proliferation are absent; one spermatogonium can give rise to 8–32 sperm (average 16).

## Hormones for spermatogenesis

### Testosterone

Main targets/actions listed:

- Spermiogenesis.
- Spermiation.
- Meiosis; suppression of testosterone leads to a block in meiosis.
- Responsible for later stages of spermatogenesis.

### FSH

Main targets/actions listed:

- Sertoli-cell proliferation.
- Spermatogonial proliferation up to the primary-spermatocyte stage.
- Responsible for early stages of spermatogenesis.
- Supports meiosis.

### Estrogen

- Fluid resorption and concentration of sperm.
- Acrosome biogenesis.

## Hypothalamic–pituitary axis

- Hypothalamus secretes GnRH → anterior pituitary.
- FSH → Sertoli cells → inhibin.
- LH → Leydig cells → testosterone.

### Effects listed by hormone

**Testosterone**

- Differentiation of epididymis, seminal vesicle and vas deferens.
- Pubertal growth spurt.
- Growth of penis and seminal vesicle.
- Deepening of voice.
- Increased muscle and bone mass.
- Increased RBC number.

**Dihydrotestosterone (DHT)**

- Development of male external genitalia: differentiation of penis, scrotum and prostate.
- Growth of prostate and penis during puberty.
- Male-pattern baldness.
- Facial hair and acne.

**Estradiol**

- Cessation of pubertal growth spurt (epiphyseal closure).
- Increased male libido.
