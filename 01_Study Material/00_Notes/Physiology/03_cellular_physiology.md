## Cell membrane (*00:00:51*)

- The cell membrane is a **phospholipid bilayer**.
- Thickness of the cell membrane: **7.5 nm**.
- Proteins are embedded in the layers.
- **Fluid Mosaic Model (1972):** proposed by Singer and Nicolson; proteins are present in a fluid phase.
- Phospholipid structure:
  - Hydrophilic phosphate head: present outward.
  - Two hydrophobic tails: present inward.

![[ch03_p017_cell_membrane_model.png]]

*Cell-membrane structure and the fluid-mosaic model.*

## Composition of the cell/plasma membrane (*00:03:58*)

| Component | Percentage/details |
|---|---|
| Lipid | 42% total; phospholipids 25%; cholesterol 13%; triglyceride 0% (as listed) |
| Protein | 55% total |
| Carbohydrates | 3%; present as glycoprotein or glycolipid |

**Membrane proteins**

- **Integral/intrinsic membrane proteins:** span through the cell membrane.
- **Peripheral/extrinsic membrane proteins:** attached either to intrinsic membrane proteins or to the surface of the cell membrane.

**Protein-to-lipid ratio across membranes**

- Highest ratio: inner mitochondrial membrane (**3:2**) and sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane (**2**).
- Lowest ratio: myelin sheath (**0.23**).

## Cell cytoskeleton (*00:08:36*)

- Maintains the size and shape of the cell.
- **Dynamic cytoskeleton:** length increases proportionally with growth of the cell membrane.
- **Static cytoskeleton:** length does not change with cell growth.

| Cytoskeletal component | Diameter | General classification |
|---|---:|---|
| Microtubules | 25 nm | Dynamic |
| Microfilaments | 7 nm | Dynamic |
| Intermediate filaments | 10 nm | Static |

![[ch03_p018_cytoskeleton_structure.png]]

*Dynamic and static cytoskeleton components and filament diameters.*

### Microtubules

- Hollow tubular structures.
- Composed of **α-tubulin** and **β-tubulin** subunits.
- A total of **13 tubulin** subunits form the periphery/wall; described as a dynamic structure.
- Growing end: **positive (+) end**.
- Non-growing end: **negative (−) end**.
- Cell growth involves polymerization of tubulin.
  - Guanosine triphosphate (GTP) is the energy source for this polymerization.
- Apart from α- and β-tubulin, **γ-tubulin** is present at the level of the centrosome.
- Functions: intracellular transport; part of cilia and flagella.

### Microfilaments

- Microfilaments are **actin**.
- Composed of globular subunits (**G-actin**).
- Multiple G-actin molecules attach to form a long filament (**F-actin**).
- F-actin also helps in muscle contraction; two F-actin filaments intertwine.
- Dynamic cytoskeleton:
  - End that grows by polymerization: positive end of the microfilament.
  - Other end, without growth in length: negative end.
- **ATP** is the source of energy.
- Actin is described as the most abundant intracellular protein in a mammalian cell.

### Intermediate filaments

- Fixed structure.
- Cell-specific and used as a **cell marker** to identify different cell types.
- Connect the nuclear membrane and cell membrane; the notes state that this helps the cell withstand outside pressure.

| Cell marker | Cell type |
|---|---|
| Cytokeratin | Epithelial cell |
| Vimentin | Mesenchymal cell |
| Desmin | Skeletal muscle cell |
| Glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) | Astrocytes |

## Drugs and cytoskeleton (*00:23:07*)

- **Colchicine and vinblastine** act on microtubules and prevent their assembly (polymerization), thereby blocking mitosis.
- **Paclitaxel (Taxol)** binds to microtubules and makes them stable; the notes state that organelles cannot move, mitotic spindles are not formed, cell division is blocked, and cells die.

## Gap-junction structure

![[ch03_p020_gap_junction_structure.png]]

*Gap-junction channel, connexon, and connexin structure.*

- Gap between two plasma membranes: **20 nm**.
- Gap between two plasma membranes at a gap junction: **3.5 nm**.
- A gap junction is formed by **two hemiconnexon molecules**.
- One connexon protein is composed of **six subunits**, called connexin molecules.
- Each connexin molecule is composed of **four membrane-spanning domains**.
- Pore diameter: **0.8–1.4 nm**.
- Permeability: up to **1000 Da molecular weight**.
- **20 different genes** form connexin proteins.
- Seen in:
	1. Heart & Visceral Smooth muscles
		- like GIT, Bladder, Uterus
	2. Certain Neurons
		- Thalamus
		- Hypothalamus
		- Brainstem
		- Inferior Olivary Neurons
		- Retina
	3. Epithelium
		- For exchange of ions and signalling molecules
	4. Liver
		- Metabolic Synchronisation
	5. Osteocytes & Osteoblast

> Not seen in Skeletal muscles → So that Skeletal Muscles can act as Individual units → Graded Fine skilled movements



## Connexins in disease

Mutations of connexin proteins are stated to be responsible for approximately 20 different human diseases.

| Disease | Connexin association stated |
|---|---|
| Clouston syndrome (ectodermal dysplasia) | Connexin 30 defect |
| Erythro-keratoderma variabilis | Cx30.3 and Cx31 |
| Cataract | Cx46 and Cx50 |
| Idiopathic atrial fibrillation | Cx40 |
| X-linked Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease | Cx32 |

## MCQs

**Q1.** A research scholar studying the cell cytoskeleton finds stable strands of fibrous proteins that distribute tensile strength across tissue. Which structure best fits the description? (NEET 2021)

- A. Microfilament
- B. Microtubules
- C. Intermediate filaments
- D. All of the above

**Answer: C.**

**Q2.** Which type of amino acids are present in the transmembrane part of a membrane receptor? (INI-CET 2020)

- A. Amphipathic
- B. Hydrophobic
- C. Hydrophilic
- D. None

**Answer: B.**
