# 33 — Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)

## 20. Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR)

### Determinants of GFR

- The source compares the diameter of the afferent arteriole (AA) with that of the efferent arteriole (EA) as `AA diameter > EA diameter`.
- The AA supplies blood to the glomerular capillaries; the EA drains blood.
- Glomerular filtration pressure terms:
  - \(P_c\): hydrostatic pressure generated by the water content of plasma.
  - \(\pi_c\): colloidal oncotic pressure of plasma.
  - \(P_B\): hydrostatic pressure of Bowman's capsule.
  - \(\pi_B\): oncotic pressure of Bowman's capsule.
- Net filtration pressure at the glomerular capillaries is determined by Starling's law/Starling forces.

![[ch33_p255_glomerular_starling_pressure_schematic.png]]

### Starling forces

These forces apply to all capillaries.

- \(P_c\): hydrostatic pressure of the capillary; pushes fluid out of capillaries.
- \(P_t\): hydrostatic pressure of tissue; pushes fluid into capillaries.
- \(\pi_c\): oncotic pressure of the capillary; retains fluid in capillaries.
- \(\pi_t\): oncotic pressure of interstitial fluid; draws fluid out of capillaries.

\[
\text{Net filtration force} = (P_c-P_t)+(\pi_t-\pi_c)
\]

- If the net force is positive, fluid moves out of capillaries.
- If the net force is negative, fluid enters capillaries.
- Filtration = \(K_f\times\text{NPF}\), where \(K_f\) is the filtration coefficient.

![[ch33_p255_general_starling_forces_schematic.png]]

### Net filtration pressure at the glomerular capillary

\[
\text{NPF}=(P_c-P_B)+(\pi_B-\pi_c)
\]

Normal values given in the source:

- \(P_c=60\text{ mmHg}\)
- \(P_B=18\text{ mmHg}\)
- \(\pi_c=32\text{ mmHg}\)
- \(\pi_B=0\text{ mmHg}\)

\[
(60-18)+(0-32)=10\text{ mmHg}
\]

### Glomerular filtration rate

\[
\text{GFR} = \text{NPF}\times K_f
\]

- GFR = 125 mL/min.
- NPF = 10 mmHg.
- \(K_f=125/10=12.5\text{ mL/min/mmHg}\).
- The filtration coefficient \(K_f\) depends on:
  - Surface area of capillaries.
  - Permeability of capillaries.
- GFR is directly proportional to \(K_f\) and NPF.
- If NPF or \(K_f\) decreases, GFR also decreases.
- Example from the source: obstruction to urinary flow causes fluid to accumulate in Bowman's capsule, increasing \(P_B\), decreasing NPF, and decreasing GFR.

### Net filtration pressure along the glomerular capillary

- Normal renal plasma flow (RPF): 700 mL/min.
- NPF gradually decreases from the afferent end to the efferent end of the glomerular capillary.
- NPF at the afferent end = 10–15 mmHg.
- NPF at the efferent end = 0 mmHg.
- Average NPF = 10 mmHg.

## Regulation of GFR

Major regulators:

1. Autoregulation.
2. Resistance of the afferent and efferent arterioles.

### Autoregulation

- In the graph, the x-axis is mean arterial pressure and the y-axis is RPF.
- The example on the source slide describes BP increasing from 0 to 180 mmHg:
  - RPF initially increases from 0 to 80 mmHg.
  - RPF remains constant from 80 to 180 mmHg.
  - Above 180 mmHg, the source shows the autoregulatory range being exceeded.
- Autoregulation is described as constant maintenance of renal blood flow (RBF) and GFR despite changes in BP, within a particular range of 80–180 mmHg.
- Outside this range, the source states that decreased BP can cause renal shutdown and increased BP leads to autoregulatory failure.

![[ch33_p256_renal_autoregulation_graph.png]]

### Mechanisms of autoregulation

The source lists three mechanisms:

| Mechanism | Mediator given in the source |
|---|---|
| Myogenic mechanism | Calcium |
| Tubuloglomerular feedback mechanism | Adenosine |
| Angiotensin II-mediated vasoconstriction | Angiotensin II |

![[ch33_p256_autoregulation_mechanisms_flowchart.png]]

#### Myogenic mechanism

- Mediated by calcium.
- Increased BP stretches smooth muscle in the afferent arterioles.
- Stretch-sensitive Ca²⁺ channels open; Ca²⁺ enters the cells; smooth muscle contracts; the afferent arterioles constrict.

### Afferent and efferent arteriolar resistance

\[
\text{Filtration fraction (FF)}=\frac{\text{GFR}}{\text{RPF}}
\]

Normal FF = 0.16–0.2 (16–20%).

| Arteriole | Change | RPF | GFR | FF as shown in source |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Afferent | Constriction | ↓↓ | ↓↓ | Normal |
| Afferent | Relaxation | ↑↑ | ↑↑ | Normal |
| Efferent | Constriction | ↓↓ | Mild constriction: ↑↑; severe constriction: ↓↓ | Not stated in table |
| Efferent | Relaxation | ↑↑ | ↓↓ | Not stated in table |

![[ch33_p257_afferent_efferent_resistance_table.png]]

### Efferent arteriole and RPF

- Efferent arteriolar constriction increases efferent arteriolar resistance and decreases glomerular plasma flow.
- An increase in resistance of either the afferent (R1) or efferent (R2) arteriole increases total renal resistance and decreases blood flow to the kidney and glomerular plasma flow.

#### Mild versus severe efferent arteriolar constriction

| Mild constriction | Severe constriction |
|---|---|
| RPF decreases slightly (700 mL → 500 mL). | RPF decreases markedly (700 mL → 200 mL). |
| Plasma accumulates in capillaries; capillary hydrostatic pressure \(P_c\) increases; GFR increases. | Plasma accumulates less in capillaries; capillary hydrostatic pressure \(P_c\) decreases; GFR decreases. |

![[ch33_p257_mild_severe_efferent_constriction_table_and_curves.png]]

### Effects of substances on GFR and RPF

| Category | Substance | GFR | RPF | Mechanism stated in source |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Vasoconstrictor | Angiotensin II | ↓ | ↓ | Constricts AA and EA |
| Vasoconstrictor | Sympathetic nerves | ↓ | ↓ | Constricts AA and EA |
| Vasoconstrictor | Endothelin | ↓ | ↓ | Constricts AA and EA |
| Vasodilator | Prostaglandins (PGE₁, PGE₂, PGI₂) | ↑ or no change | ↑ | Counteracts Ang II- and sympathetic nerve-mediated vasoconstriction |
| Vasodilator | Nitric oxide (NO) | ↑ | ↑ | Counteracts Ang II- and sympathetic nerve-mediated vasoconstriction |
| Vasodilator | Bradykinin | ↑ | ↑ | Increases production of prostaglandins and NO |
| Vasodilator | Natriuretic peptides (ANP, BNP) | ↑ | Little change | Dilates AA and constricts EA |

### Angiotensin II effects on GFR

- Angiotensin II is a potent vasoconstrictor.
- Angiotensin II influences GFR through the following effects:
  - Constriction of AA → ↓ GFR.
  - Contraction of mesangial cells → ↓ GFR.
  - Constriction of EA → ↑ GFR.
  - Net effect: slight decrease in GFR under normal conditions.
- Clinical note in the source:
  - In renal artery stenosis or hypotension, the effect of Ang II on EA predominates, so Ang II increases GFR.
  - Blocking RAAS may lead to acute kidney injury in patients with bilateral renal artery stenosis, unilateral stenosis, or a single kidney.
