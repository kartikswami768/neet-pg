# Gastrointestinal Hormones

## Main hormones

| Hormone | Source listed in the source |
|---|---|
| Gastrin | G cells, located in the antrum |
| Cholecystokinin (CCK) | I cells of the upper small intestine |
| Secretin | S cells of the upper small intestine |

## Gastrin

### Stimuli for release

- Peptides (most potent), calcium and stretching/distension of the stomach.
- GRP (gastrin-releasing peptide) from the parasympathetic system.
- Fat and carbohydrate: no effect, according to the source.

### Actions

- G cells produce gastrin, which acts on parietal and chief cells located in the fundus and body of the stomach.
- Stimulates gastric acid and pepsin secretion.
- Trophic action: stimulates growth of the mucosal layer of the stomach and small and large intestine.
- Affects motility of the pyloric region.

### Negative feedback mechanism

Gastrin enters the circulation and reaches parietal/chief cells, stimulating H⁺ production. The source describes H⁺ as acting on G cells to inhibit gastrin secretion.

### Pernicious anaemia

- Autoimmune destruction of parietal cells.
- The source states that loss of H⁺ secretion removes inhibition of G cells, increasing gastrin.

## Cholecystokinin (CCK)

- Produced in the upper part of the small intestine.
- **Stimuli:** peptides (most potent) and free fatty acids; carbohydrates have no effect, according to the source.
- **Functions:**
  - Stimulates pancreatic enzyme secretion.
  - Contracts the gallbladder.
  - Relaxes the sphincter of Oddi.
  - Inhibits gastric emptying.
  - Exerts a trophic effect on the pancreas. The source adds that gastrin and CCK deficiencies can cause gastric mucosal atrophy; this wording is retained as printed.
  - Enhances motility of the small intestine and colon.
  - Augments the action of secretin.
- The source describes a positive-feedback sequence: peptides stimulate I cells → CCK acts on pancreatic acinar cells → pancreatic enzyme secretion increases → enzymes reach the second part of the duodenum → protein and fat digestion and peptide production increase → further stimulation of I cells.

## Secretin

- Discovered by **Bayliss and Starling**.
- **Stimulus:** H⁺ ions are the most potent stimulus.
- **Negative feedback:** high H⁺ concentration in the duodenum stimulates S cells to produce secretin. Secretin acts on pancreatic duct cells to secrete HCO₃⁻; bicarbonate neutralises H⁺ in the duodenum, reducing secretin secretion.

### Stimuli for secretion

- Acid (most potent).
- Protein digestion products.
- Carbohydrate: no role.
- Fat: minor role.

### Functions

- Stimulates HCO₃⁻ secretion by pancreatic ducts and the biliary tract.
- Augments the action of CCK.
- Inhibits gastric acid secretion.
- Causes contraction of the pyloric sphincter.

## Other gastrointestinal hormones

| Hormone | Origin listed in the source | Major actions listed in the source |
|---|---|---|
| Pancreatic polypeptide | Pancreatic PP cells | Inhibits pancreatic bicarbonate secretion, gallbladder contraction and gut motility |
| Neuropeptide Y (NPY) | Neurotransmitter in CNS and PNS | Potent stimulator of food intake |
| Peptide YY (PYY) | L cells of distal small intestine (ileum) and colon | The source lists acid secretion and motility of stomach, enzyme and fluid secretion (pancreas), and reduced food intake; wording is preserved as presented in the table |
| Oxyntomodulin | L cells | Inhibits food intake, gastric motility and acid production |
| Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) | L cells | Promotes insulin secretion from pancreatic β cells (incretin effect) and promotes β-cell growth |
| GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide) | K cells of small intestine | Exocrine: increases fluid absorption; endocrine: increases insulin release from pancreas |
| Guanylin | Ileum and colon (goblet cells) | Increases fluid absorption in small and large gut |
| Motilin | Mo cells of stomach and duodenum, as listed in the source | Increases smooth-muscle contraction (MMC) and intestinal motility |
| Somatostatin | D cells of the GI tract and pancreas; hypothalamus | Lowers gastric acid secretion and gastric motor activity; affects cholangiocyte HCO₃⁻ secretion |


## Active neuropeptides of the GIT

| Neuropeptide | Functions listed in the source |
|---|---|
| Bombesin (gastrin-releasing peptide) | Increases gastrin release, gastric acid secretion, pancreatic enzyme secretion and gallbladder contraction |
| Substance P | Increases smooth-muscle contraction |
| Vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP) | Increases smooth-muscle relaxation and pancreatic bicarbonate secretion |
| Calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) | Increases somatostatin release and smooth-muscle contraction |

## MCQs

**1. Which of the following is an incorrect match?** (INICET 2023)

- a. Gastrin: G cells from the stomach
- b. Motilin: M cells from duodenum and jejunum
- c. CCK: I cells of duodenum and pancreas
- d. Secretin: S cells of duodenum and jejunum

**Answer: c. CCK: I cells of duodenum and pancreas**

**2. Which of the following does not act on the pancreas?** (AIIMS 2019)

- a. Gastrin
- b. Secretin
- c. GIP
- d. Cholecystokinin

**Answer: a. Gastrin**
