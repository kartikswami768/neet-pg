# Lung Volume and Capacities

## Spirometer

- Contains a floating drum that contains breathing gas and is inverted over a water chamber.
- A tube connects the mouthpiece to the gas chamber, and a drum attached to a thread linked to a pen records the changes.
- During inspiration:
  - The floating drum moves downward.
  - The pen records an upward curve on the recording drum.
- During expiration:
  - The floating drum moves upward.
  - The pen records a downward curve on the recording drum.

## Lung volumes and capacities

- **Lung volumes:** Volume of air in the lungs at a specific point during breathing.
- **Lung capacities:** Sum of different lung volumes.

| Type | Measurement | Units stated in the source |
|---|---|---|
| Static lung volumes | Measured without considering time | mL or L |
| Dynamic lung volumes | Measured with respect to time | mL/s or L/min |

## Static lung volumes

![[ch42_p303_static_lung_volumes_graph.png]]

| Volume | Definition | Value stated in the source |
|---|---|---:|
| Residual volume (RV) | Air remaining after full expiration | 1,300 mL |
| Tidal volume (TV) | Air exchanged during normal breathing | 500 mL |
| Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV) | Extra air inhaled with maximum inspiratory effort | 2,000–3,000 mL |
| Expiratory reserve volume (ERV) | Extra air exhaled with maximum expiratory effort | 1,000 mL |

## Static lung capacities

- Inspiratory capacity (IC) = TV + IRV.
- Vital capacity (VC) = TV + IRV + ERV.
- Functional residual capacity (FRC) = ERV + RV.
  - FRC is also known as resting lung volume or relaxation lung volume.
  - FRC is the amount of air remaining in the lungs after normal expiration.
- Total lung capacity (TLC) = TV + IRV + ERV + RV.

## Residual volume (RV)

- Cannot be measured by a spirometer.
- Measured using:
  - Helium dilution.
  - Nitrogen washout.
  - Body plethysmography (stated as the best method).

## Closing volumes and capacities

- During forceful expiration, the basal alveoli close while alveoli in the apical and middle parts remain open. The air remaining in the lungs is called closing capacity.
- With continued forceful expiration, air from the apical and middle alveoli is expelled, leaving the residual volume (RV).
- The source states: **CC >> RV**.

![[ch42_p303_closing_capacity_diagram.png]]

- CC = RV + CV (closing volume).
- For a normal adult, FRC > CC (CC is 50% of FRC).
- With age, CC becomes nearly equal to FRC by age 66.
- In small-airway disease of the lung, CC >> FRC.
- Measurement: single-breath nitrogen washout method (Fowler's method).

## Dynamic lung volumes and capacities

**Process**

1. The subject performs normal breathing, followed by deep inspiration to reach TLC.
2. A brief breath-hold is followed by forceful expiration until RV is reached.

**Measurement**

- Mark the start of expiration with a perpendicular to the X-axis.
- Mark the end of expiration with a perpendicular to the Y-axis.
- The intersection of these lines identifies the starting point of the manoeuvre.
- Complete expiration takes 3–6 seconds.

## Forced vital capacity (FVC)

- The total forcefully expired volume from the beginning to the end of the manoeuvre represents FVC.

![[ch42_p304_forced_vital_capacity_spirogram.png]]

| Measurement | Definition / value stated in the source |
|---|---|
| FEV₁ | Forced expired volume in 1 second; 80% of FVC |
| FEV₂ | Forced expired volume in 2 seconds; 95% of FVC |
| FEV₃ | Forced expired volume in 3 seconds; 99–100% of FVC |
| Tiffeneau ratio | FEV₁ / FVC; normal 80% |

## Obstructive and restrictive diseases

- **Obstructive:** Obstruction mainly during expiration.
  - Emphysema.
  - Chronic bronchitis.
  - Asthma.
  - Cystic fibrosis.
- **Restrictive:** Restriction mainly during inspiration.
  - Parenchymal: fibrosis.
  - Extra-parenchymal: kyphosis, obesity and myasthenia gravis.

## Dynamic compression of airways

Occurs during forceful expiration after deep inspiration.

**Mechanism**

1. **Forceful expiration:** During deep expiration, the muscles of the chest wall contract, increasing intrathoracic pressure. This generates pressure inside the alveoli and bronchi to push air out of the lungs.
2. **Elastic recoil of lung tissues:** Elastic fibres in the lungs recoil inward, adding to the pressure inside the airways. The source gives an example of elastic recoil contributing about 5 mmHg to the total force.
3. **Equal pressure point (EPP):** As air moves from alveoli to bronchi, airway pressure progressively drops due to airway resistance. At a certain point, the pressure inside the bronchioles equals the surrounding intrapleural pressure. This is the EPP.
4. **Collapse beyond EPP:** Beyond the EPP, pressure outside the airways (intrapleural pressure) becomes higher than pressure inside the bronchioles, leading to bronchiolar collapse and dynamic airway compression.

## Obstructive lung disease and emphysema

Examples of obstructive lung disease: emphysema, bronchitis, asthma and bronchiectasis.

**Emphysema**

- Alveoli become over-expanded, leading to rupture of some alveoli and elastic tissue.
- Loss of elastic recoil increases lung compliance, making the lungs easier to expand but harder to deflate.
- During inspiration, the lungs fully expand due to increased compliance.
- During expiration:
  - Elastic tissue destruction reduces elastic recoil force (approximately 0 mmHg in the source).
  - Expiration relies on chest-wall force.
  - Airway resistance reduces pressure as air exits the lungs.
  - When intrabronchiolar pressure falls below external pressure, bronchioles close prematurely during early expiration.
  - This causes air trapping.
  - RV and TLC increase due to trapped air.

![[ch42_p305_barrel_chest_pursed_lip_breathing.png]]

- **Barrel chest:** Increased anteroposterior chest diameter to accommodate extra air.
- **Pursed-lip breathing:** Maintains positive airway pressure to prevent premature airway collapse.

## Restrictive lung disease

- Restriction during inspiration limits lung expansion.

| Parenchymal restriction | Extra-parenchymal restriction |
|---|---|
| In fibrosis, elastic tissue surrounding alveoli is replaced by fibrous tissue, increasing stiffness and decreasing compliance, making inspiration difficult. | The source states that forceful expiration is limited, leading to increased RV. |
| TLC decreases and RV decreases. | TLC decreases but RV increases. |

## Digital spirometry

- Only the expiratory portion is recorded in digital spirometry.

![[ch42_p307_digital_spirometry_system.png]]

**Recording**

- Y-axis: expiratory volume; X-axis: time.
- In a digital spirometer, an upward curve indicates the expiratory phase.
- Total expired volume within 5 seconds = FVC.
- FEV₁: volume expired in the first second.
- Advantage: FEV₁ and FVC are directly measurable from the curve.

![[ch42_p307_spirogram_volume_time_graphs.png]]

## Peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR)

- Measures the slope of the volume-time curve.
- Indicates the rate of volume change over time.

## Flow-volume loop

![[ch42_p308_flow_volume_loop.png]]

- X-axis: volume.
- Y-axis: flow.
- Inspiration: downward flow curve.
- Expiration: upward flow curve.

**Inspiratory flow**

- During normal inspiration, flow increases and then decreases.
- Forceful inspiration causes a continuous rise in flow, peaking before gradually decreasing.

**Expiratory flow**

- During normal expiration, flow increases and then decreases.
- During forceful expiration, flow rises to a peak and then decreases due to dynamic airway compression.

**Lung volumes and measured volumes**

- TV: volume of air entering or leaving the lungs during normal breathing.
- TLC: total air in the lungs at the end of maximal inspiration (approximately 5 L).
- RV: air remaining in the lungs after maximal expiration.
- From TV to TLC: inspiratory reserve volume (IRV).
- From TV to RV: expiratory reserve volume (ERV).
- From RV to TLC: forced vital capacity (FVC), as stated in the source.
- Peak inspiratory flow rate (PIFR): maximum flow rate during inspiration.
- Peak expiratory flow rate (PEFR): maximum flow rate during expiration.
- The entire respiratory cycle forms a loop representing the relationship between airflow and lung volume.

## Maximal mid-expiratory flow (MMEF)

![[ch42_p308_mmef_fef_graph.png]]

- Average flow during the middle half of FVC.
- Described as effort-independent.
- A sensitive marker for detecting early airway obstruction.
- The source also depicts a volume-time FEV₁/FVC graph and a set of effort-dependent to effort-independent flow-volume curves.

![[ch42_p308_fev1_fvc_time_graph.png]]
![[ch42_p308_effort_dependence_curve.png]]

- During less forceful expiration, peak expiratory flow (PEF) rises continuously.
- With increasing forceful expiration, the left side of the curve shows a rise in PEF, indicating effort dependence.
- On the right side of the curve, dynamic compression occurs, making expiratory flow effort-independent.

## Abnormal flow-volume loop

| Obstructive disease | Restrictive disease (parenchymal) | Restrictive disease (extra-parenchymal) |
|---|---|---|
| PEFR decreases; TLC and RV increase; depression in the expiratory portion of the curve (“scooped out”). | Very narrow curve; PEFR, TLC and RV decrease. | Very narrow curve; TLC decreases; RV is higher than normal. |

## Upper airway obstruction

Types:

- Fixed upper-airway obstruction.
- Variable upper-airway obstruction:
  - Variable extra-thoracic obstruction.
  - Variable intra-thoracic obstruction.

**Fixed upper-airway obstruction**

- Obstruction affects both inspiratory and expiratory phases.
- Box-pattern: flattened inspiratory and expiratory curves.
- Examples: foreign bodies, scarring of the airway and enlarged thyroid.

![[ch42_p309_fixed_upper_airway_obstruction_curves.png]]

**Variable extra-thoracic obstruction**

- Obstruction affects the inspiratory phase.
- Examples: paralysed vocal cords, enlarged lymph nodes and flabby pharyngeal muscles (obstructive sleep apnoea).

![[ch42_p310_variable_extrathoracic_obstruction_curve.png]]

**Variable intra-thoracic obstruction**

- Obstruction affects the expiratory phase.
- Examples: tumours (stated as the most common cause) and tracheomalacia.

![[ch42_p310_variable_intrathoracic_obstruction_curve.png]]
