# Compliance

## Concept of compliance

- **Compliance:** Stretchability or extensibility of a structure (distensibility of tissue).
  - Easily stretchable structures are highly compliant, and vice versa.
- Compliance is the change in volume divided by the change in pressure:

$$
  C = \frac{\Delta V}{\Delta P}
$$

- **Lung compliance:** Change in lung volume / pressure change.
- The pressure change may be intrapleural pressure or transmural pressure.

## MCQ example

A person inhales a tidal volume of 600 mL. During inspiration, intrapleural pressure changes from −4 cm H₂O to −8 cm H₂O. Calculate the compliance of the respiratory system.

- Volume change (ΔV) = tidal volume = 600 mL.
- Intrapleural pressure change (ΔP) = 4 cm H₂O.
- Compliance = 600 / 4 = **150 mL/cm H₂O**.

## Measurement of compliance

![[ch41_p297_compliance_measurement_diagram.png]]

**Tools used**

- Spirometer measures ΔV.
- Pressure transducer measures intrapleural pressure (ΔP) by measuring intra-oesophageal pressure, a surrogate for intrapleural pressure.
  - The oesophagus is closed at both ends with sphincters.
  - The middle part is a collapsible structure and measures pressure.

**Procedure**

1. The subject takes a deep inspiration, reaching total lung capacity (TLC).
2. The subject exhales slightly, holds their breath, and volume (V₁) and pressure (P₁) are recorded.
3. The subject exhales another small amount, and volume (V₂) and pressure (P₂) are recorded.
4. From the curve, ΔV and ΔP are measured:
   - ΔV = difference between V₁ and V₂.
   - ΔP = difference between P₁ and P₂.
   - Compliance = ΔV / ΔP.

## Compliance and elasticity

- Elasticity is inversely proportional to compliance.
- More elasticity means less compliance.
- High elasticity makes structures harder to stretch and less compliant.
- Example: two balloons with the same volume but different wall thickness:
  - Thin wall: high compliance, low elasticity.
  - Thick wall: low compliance, high elasticity.

## Surface tension

- Surface tension is a cohesive force at the air–water interface.
- The inner lining of alveoli contains a thin layer of water.
- Water molecules in alveoli attract each other with cohesive forces.
- This causes an inward-directed force (surface tension) that tends to collapse alveoli.
- Surface tension is a key factor resisting alveolar expansion.
- Increased surface tension leads to alveolar collapse.
- Surfactant decreases surface tension and is secreted by type II pneumocytes.

## Surfactant

**Composition**

- Phospholipids (90%).
	- Major component: DPPC / lecithin (phosphatidylcholine).
- Proteins (~10%): surfactant proteins A, B, C and D.

**Production and maturation**

- Production starts at 20–24 weeks of gestation.
- Matures between 35–37 weeks.
- A baby born before 34 weeks may have surfactant deficiency, leading to hyaline membrane disease / infant respiratory distress syndrome.
- Surfactant appears in amniotic fluid between 28 and 32 weeks.
	- Legal Viability age → 28 weeks
- Lecithin/sphingomyelin ratio greater than 2 to 2.5 indicates lung maturity.

**Major component and mechanism**

- The major component is a phospholipid with a hydrophilic head and a hydrophobic tail.
- At the alveolar level, the hydrophilic head inserts between water molecules.
- This disrupts cohesive forces, breaking the continuous water layer in alveoli.
- Surface tension is reduced, aiding alveolar expansion.

## Compliance in pathology

Alveolar expansion is opposed by:

- Elastic tissue recoil (1/3).
- Surface tension (2/3).

If the opposing force is greater, compliance is lower, and vice versa.

| High compliance | Low compliance |
|---|---|
| Increased surfactant | Surfactant deficiency (e.g., hyaline membrane disease) |
| Emphysema | Fibrosis and other restrictive lung diseases |
| Old age | |
| Asthma | |

The source distinguishes bronchitis from emphysema, noting that bronchitis patients have normal compliance.

## Compliance curve

![[ch41_p299_hysteresis_inflation_deflation_curve.png]]

**Inflation curve**

- Initially requires high pressure due to collapsed alveoli.
- As alveoli open, compliance increases.
- Plateau phase: full alveolar expansion.

**Deflation curve**

- Tissue elasticity and surface tension aid lung collapse.
- Hysteresis is the gap between the inflation and deflation curves.
- The deflation curve differs from the inflation curve due to differences in surface tension.

**Important information**

- Hysteresis is not seen with a saline-filled lung.
- When the lung is filled with saline, there is no air–water surface tension and no hysteresis property.
- The curve follows almost the same path.
- The small gap between curves is due to elastic recoiling force.

## Comparing compliance curves

![[ch41_p300_compliance_pathology_curves.png]]

- The source describes the normal curve as representing the expiratory phase.
- Compliance is calculated by plotting two perpendiculars to the exponential part of the curve.
- Difference in Y-axis values gives ΔV (2 L); difference in X-axis values gives ΔP (10 cm H₂O).
- Compliance = ΔV / ΔP = 2,000 mL / 10 cm H₂O = **200 mL/cm H₂O**.
- A curve above the normal curve indicates high compliance (emphysema).
- A curve below the normal curve indicates low compliance (fibrosis and restrictive lung disease).

## Normal compliance

- Chest wall: 200 mL/cm H₂O.
- Lungs only: 150–200 mL/cm H₂O.
- Respiratory system: 85 mL/cm H₂O.
- Compliance of adult lung is greater than that of a child.
- Compliance of the base is greater than that of the apex.
- Compliance is maximum at FRC.

## Types of compliance

| Type | Description in the source |
|---|---|
| Static compliance | Gives the status of lung alveoli, lung and chest wall. Low static compliance: e.g., fibrosis. |
| Dynamic compliance | Includes airway resistance and the static-compliance component. Airway resistance alone can make dynamic compliance low while static compliance remains normal. In fibrosis, both dynamic and static compliance are low. |
| Specific compliance | Compliance / FRC. Used to compare compliance between individuals of different sizes. |

## MCQs

**1. In cases of infant respiratory distress syndrome, which lipid is deficient?** (NEET 2023, NEET 2020)

- a. Lecithin
- b. Sphingomyelin
- c. Phosphatidylserine
- d. Phosphatidylethanolamine

**Answer: a. Lecithin**

**2. Which cells are deficient in a premature infant with RDS?** (NEET 2020)

- a. Alveolar capillary endothelial cells
- b. Type I alveolar cells
- c. Type II alveolar cells
- d. Alveolar macrophages

**Answer: c. Type II alveolar cells**
