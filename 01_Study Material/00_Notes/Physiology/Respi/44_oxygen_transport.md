# Oxygen Transport

## Partial pressure of oxygen along the respiratory pathway

- Atmosphere: PO₂ = 160 mmHg.
- Conducting zones: inspiratory PO₂ decreases to 149–150 mmHg.
- Alveoli: PO₂ decreases further to 100–104 mmHg.
- Arterial blood: PO₂ = 95–98 mmHg due to venous shunts.

## Methods of oxygen transport

| Dissolved oxygen | Haemoglobin-bound oxygen |
|---|---|
| Only 1% of oxygen is transported in dissolved form. | 99% of oxygen is carried bound to haemoglobin (Hb). |
| Calculated using Henry's law: DO₂ = 0.003 × PO₂. | Depends on Hb saturation (SO₂). |
| Arterial DO₂: 0.29 mL/dL. | SO₂ depends on PO₂. |
| Venous DO₂: 0.12 mL/dL. | SO₂ is measured as SpO₂ (pulse oximeter) or SO₂ by ABG analysis. |
| | Sequence: PO₂ → SO₂ → Hb-bound O₂. |

## Partial pressure and saturation

- PO₂ → SO₂ → Hb-bound O₂.
- Ideal scenario: 1 g of pure Hb can carry 1.39 mL of O₂ when 100% saturated.
- Real-life scenario: 1.34 mL of O₂ is carried by 1 g Hb.

| | Arterial blood | Venous blood |
|---|---|---|
| PO₂ and Hb saturation | PO₂ = 98 mmHg; Hb saturation = 97% (SaO₂) | PO₂ = 40 mmHg; Hb saturation = 75% (SvO₂) |
| O₂ carried by 1 g Hb | (1.34 × 97/100) = 1.29 mL O₂ | (1.34 × 75/100) = 1.001 mL O₂ |

## Oxygen content calculation

- Normal Hb = 15 g/dL.

| | Arterial blood | Venous blood |
|---|---|---|
| Hb-bound oxygen per gram Hb | 1.29 mL | 1.001 mL |
| Hb-bound O₂ | (1.29 × 15) = 19.5 mL/dL | 15.1 mL/dL (source value) |
| Dissolved O₂ (DO₂) | 0.29 mL/dL | 0.12 mL/dL |
| Total O₂ content | 19.5 + 0.29 = 19.8 mL/dL (≈20 mL/dL) | 15.2 mL/dL |

## Oxygen extraction ratio

- Tissue extracts 5 mL of oxygen from 100 mL of blood.
- Extraction ratio = O₂ uptake ÷ O₂ supply = 5 ÷ 20 = **25%**.

| Venous blood | Arterial blood |
|---|---|
| PvO₂ = 40 mmHg | PaO₂ = 98 mmHg |
| SvO₂ = 75% | SaO₂ = 97% |
| CvO₂ = 15 mL/dL | CaO₂ = 20 mL/dL |

## Oxygen–haemoglobin dissociation curve (OHDC)

![[ch44_p318_oxygen_hemoglobin_dissociation_curve.png]]

- At 100% Hb saturation, a perpendicular from the plateau to the X-axis gives PO₂ = 120 mmHg.
- At 75% Hb saturation, PO₂ = 40 mmHg (venous blood point).
- At 50% Hb saturation (P50 of Hb), PO₂ = 26 mmHg.
- P50 of Hb ranges between 23 and 26 mmHg.
- Shape: sigmoid or S-shaped due to cooperative binding of Hb with O₂.
  - First O₂ binds with difficulty.
  - Subsequent O₂ binding becomes easier, resulting in a sigmoid curve.

## Right shift of OHDC

A rightward shift occurs in certain conditions.

| Significance | Causes listed in the source |
|---|---|
| Increased P50 | Decreased body temperature (example given: exercise) |
| Decreased O₂ affinity | Increased CO₂ production (increased PCO₂) |
| Increased O₂ unloading | Increased H⁺ (acidosis) |
| | Increased 2,3-DPG (BPG) |

- **Bohr effect:** Rightward shift of OHDC due to increased CO₂ production or increased H⁺.

## 2,3-BPG production: Luebering–Rapoport pathway

![[ch44_p319_luebering_rapoport_pathway.png]]

- Chronic acidosis inhibits 2,3-DPG production, causing a left shift.
- Acidosis:
  - Direct effect (acute acidosis) → right shift of OHDC.
  - Indirect effect (chronic acidosis → decreased 2,3-BPG) → left shift.

**Causes of increased 2,3-DPG**

1. Chronic hypoxia.
2. Exercise.
3. Anaemia.
4. Hormones (↑ thyroid, ↑ GH and ↑ androgens).
5. Increased body temperature.
6. Increased inosine, pyruvate and phosphate of RBCs.

**Right shifting**

- Hb contains two α chains and two β chains.
- For 2,3-DPG binding, Hb should be partially deoxygenated.
- 2,3-DPG binds with the β chain of Hb.
- Molar ratio of Hb:2,3-DPG = 1:1.
- 2,3-DPG stabilises the low-affinity state of Hb, facilitating O₂ release.

**Left shift of OHDC**

- Left shift increases Hb oxygen affinity and reduces oxygen unloading to tissues.
- Causes listed in the source:
  1. Decreased temperature.
  2. Alkalosis (increased pH / reduced H⁺).
  3. Decreased PCO₂.
  4. Fetal Hb = α₂γ₂ (no 2,3-DPG binding).
  5. Stored blood (decreased 2,3-DPG).
  6. CO poisoning (high O₂ affinity and decreased 2,3-DPG).
  7. Met-Hb (high O₂ affinity).
  8. Thalassaemia.

## 2,3-BPG and blood storage

- Storage below 6°C → glycolysis → decreased 2,3-DPG.
- Storage in an acidic solution inhibits the mutase enzyme → decreased 2,3-DPG.
- 2,3-DPG reaches zero after 1–2 weeks of storage, according to the source.
- After transfusion, 2,3-DPG is restored:
  - 50% restored after 7 hours.
  - 100% restored after 48 hours.
- Citrate–phosphate–dextrose (CPD) solution reduces the rate of 2,3-DPG depletion:
  - Citrate acts as an anticoagulant.
  - Phosphate increases 2,3-DPG levels.
  - Dextrose stimulates the glycolytic pathway.

## Carbon monoxide (CO) and OHDC

- CO has approximately 250 times higher affinity for Hb than oxygen.
- CO binds at the same site on Hb as oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin.
- In the presence of CO, Hb affinity for O₂ increases, causing defective O₂ release and decreased O₂ content in blood, resulting in hypoxia.

## Myoglobin and OHDC

- Myoglobin is abundant in muscle.
- It resembles Hb but binds one rather than four molecules of O₂ per molecule.
- It lacks cooperative binding.
- Its dissociation curve is a rectangular hyperbola, not a sigmoid curve.
