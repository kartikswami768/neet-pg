# 26. Mechanics of Respiration

## Pressure system

### Resting condition

- Holding of breathing at the end of normal expiration.
- **Functional residual capacity (FRC) = residual volume (RV) + expiratory reserve volume (ERV).**
  - FRC is the resting lung volume.
- Alveoli are surrounded by elastic tissues connected to the visceral pleura by an elastic septum.
  - Elastic tissues of the lungs recoil inward, collapsing alveoli.
  - The chest wall recoils outward, counteracting lung recoil.
- The visceral pleura follows lung recoil (inward).
- The parietal pleura follows chest-wall recoil (outward).
- The interpleural space between the pleurae contains 10–20 mL of fluid.

![[ch39_p286_pleural_pressure_alveoli_open_schematic.png]]

### Intrapleural pressure (IPP)

- Pressure in the space between the lungs and chest wall.
- Normal intrapleural pressure is negative (subatmospheric).
  - Elastic recoil of the lung is opposite to the elastic recoil of the chest wall; the opposite pulling produces negative intrathoracic/intrapleural pressure (ITP/IPP).
  - At FRC, elastic recoil of the lung equals elastic recoil of the chest wall.
- Normal value: **−2.5 mmHg or −5 cm H₂O**.
- **Fluid in the intrapleural space:**
  - Secreted by capillaries of the parietal pleura.
  - Net force for secretion = 9 cm H₂O.
  - Absorbed by lymphatics in the visceral pleura.
  - Net force for absorption = 10 cm H₂O.
- **Causes/features of IPP:**
  - Created by elastic recoiling of the lung.
  - Maintained by lymphatic drainage of the visceral pleura.
  - Advantage: keeps alveoli open.

![[ch39_p286_intrapleural_pressure_schematic.png]]

### Intra-alveolar pressure

- At rest, 760 mmHg = atmospheric pressure (ATM).
- Intra-alveolar pressure (IAP) relative to ATM is 0 mmHg.
- The source states that IPP relative to ATM is “−2.5 mmHg or +2.5 mmHg sub ATM”; the sign wording is retained as printed.

## Pneumothorax and IPP

| Simple pneumothorax | Tension pneumothorax |
|---|---|
| Chest-wall injury creates an opening in the chest wall; atmospheric air enters the interpleural space. | Laceration injury to the lung creates a gap in the visceral pleura, acting as a flap-like valve. |
| IPP becomes 0 mmHg and alveoli collapse. | During inspiration, air enters but cannot exit the pleural space. IPP becomes positive; positive IPP compresses the lungs and other thoracic structures. Entrapped air continues to accumulate, worsening lung collapse. |

![[ch39_p287_simple_vs_tension_pneumothorax.png]]

## Transmural pressure

- **Definition:** Pressure difference across a wall or structure.

| Pressure | Definition/value given in source |
|---|---|
| Transpulmonary pressure (a) | Intra-alveolar pressure − intrapleural pressure; at rest: +5 cm H₂O. |
| Transthoracic pressure (b) | Difference between intrapleural pressure and outside chest-wall pressure; at rest: −5 cm H₂O. |
| Trans-respiratory pressure (c) | The source gives the wording “Skin beneath the nail plate.” |

![[ch39_p287_transmural_pressure_illustrations.png]]

## Transmural pressure and lung-volume change

### Resting condition (FRC)

- Lungs and chest wall are intact.
- At FRC, trans-respiratory pressure is 0 mmHg.
  - If trans-respiratory pressure is positive, lung and chest-wall volume increase.
  - If trans-respiratory pressure is negative, lung and chest-wall volume decreases.
- When the lungs and chest wall are intact:
  - Resting volume: FRC.
  - Maximum volume: up to TLC.
  - Minimum volume: down to RV.

### Chest wall removed

- If the lung is allowed to recoil without the chest wall, at expiration and zero pressure the lung is at its minimal volume (approximately 500 mL).
- The pressure difference between intra-alveolar pressure and outside atmospheric pressure gives a transpulmonary pressure of 0 cm H₂O.
- **Transpulmonary pressure:**
  - When 0: volume is minimal (approximately 500 mL).
  - When positive: expansion of the lung.
  - When negative: lung remains at minimal volume.

### Lung removed

- The chest wall expands outward until the volume inside the thoracic cavity reaches 70% of TLC.
- Resting volume = 70% of TLC.
- The pressure difference between the inside of the thoracic cavity and the chest wall outside is the transthoracic pressure.
- Transthoracic pressure becomes 0 mmHg.
- **Transthoracic pressure:**
  - When 0 mmHg: thoracic volume is 70% of TLC.
  - When positive: thoracic volume increases.
  - When negative: thoracic volume decreases toward RV.

![[ch39_p288_pressure_volume_relationship.png]]

## Functional residual capacity (FRC)

- Obtained by adding residual volume + expiratory reserve volume.
- Trans-respiratory pressure is zero.
- Elastic recoil of the lung and chest wall are equal but in opposite directions.
- FRC is known as the relaxation/resting volume of the lungs.
- At FRC:
  - Intrapleural pressure: −2.5 mmHg or −5 cm H₂O.
  - Intra-alveolar pressure: 0 mmHg.

## Mechanism of normal respiration

### Inspiration

- **Muscles:**
  - Diaphragm (main muscle of inspiration) contributes 75% of inspiratory effort.
  - External intercostals assist (25%).
- During inspiration, diaphragm contraction expands intrapleural volume; intrapleural pressure decreases from −2.5 to −6 mmHg.
- Stretch of the alveoli lowers intra-alveolar pressure from 0 to −1 mmHg, drawing air into the lungs.
- End of inspiration: when IAP = 0 mmHg.

![[ch39_p289_inspiration_diagram.png]]

### Expiration

- Passive process as inspiratory muscles relax.
- The diaphragm ascends, compressing the expanded alveoli; alveolar volume decreases.
- Intra-alveolar pressure increases to +1 mmHg, expelling air.
- At the end of expiration, IAP returns to 0 mmHg.

### Intra-alveolar pressure-change curve

- Intra-alveolar pressure oscillates between −1 and +1 mmHg.
- Intrapleural pressure oscillates between −6 and −2.5 mmHg.
- Pressure ensures that alveoli remain open and air moves in and out effectively.

![[ch39_p289_alveolar_and_pleural_pressure_curve.png]]

## Forceful inspiration/expiration

1. **Valsalva maneuver:** Forceful expiration against a closed glottis; intrapleural pressure rises to +100 mmHg.
2. **Müller's maneuver:** Forceful inspiration against a closed glottis; intrapleural pressure drops to −80 mmHg.

## MCQs

### 1. FRC is the volume which remains in the lungs after which of the following? *(NEET 2019)*

- a. Full inspiration
- b. Full expiration
- c. Normal inspiration
- d. Normal expiration

**Answer: (d)**

### 2. Relaxation pressure is zero at which of the following lung volumes? *(NEET 2023)*

- a. FRC
- b. TLC
- c. RV
- d. IC

**Answer: (a)**

### 3. What does zero trans-respiratory pressure indicate in the pressure-volume curve of the lung and chest wall? *(INI-CET 2022 Nov)*

- a. Residual volume
- b. Functional residual capacity
- c. Tidal volume
- d. Inspiratory reserve volume

**Answer: (b)**

### 4. If the lung was allowed to recoil without the chest wall during expiration, what would be the lung volume? *(AIIMS 2019 Nov)*

- a. Residual volume
- b. FRC
- c. Minimal value
- d. Zero

**Answer: (c)**
