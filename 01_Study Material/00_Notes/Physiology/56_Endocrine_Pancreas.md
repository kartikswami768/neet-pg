# 56. Endocrine Pancreas

## Islets of Langerhans

- The source gives **1–2 million** islets of Langerhans, containing α, β and δ cells.
- The maximum number is present in the tail of the pancreas.
- The illustration labels:
  - Islets of Langerhans: 2% of total volume.
  - Exocrine pancreas (acini): 80% of total volume.
  - Remainder: ducts and blood vessels.

### Cells of the islets of Langerhans

| Cell type | Quantity | Location | Hormones / features |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **A / α** | 20% | Peripheral | Glucagon |
| **B / β** | 70% | Central | Insulin, amylin, C-peptide. Amylin:insulin ratio = 1:100. C-peptide:insulin ratio = 1:1. |
| **D / δ** | <10% | Variable | Somatostatin |
| **F (PP cell)** | Rare | Peripheral to central | Pancreatic polypeptide; ↓ HCO₃⁻ secretion from pancreatic acini and ↓ gut motility. |

- **C-peptide** has a longer half-life than insulin and is a surrogate marker of insulin secretion in the body.
- **Amylin** decreases glucagon release and food intake.
  - Amylin analogue: **pramlintide**, listed as improving HbA1c and used in treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus.


## Insulin

### Synthesis and structure
- Preproinsulin is synthesized by the rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) → proinsulin.
- Proinsulin contains A, B and C chains.
- A chain: 21 amino acids.
- B chain: 30 amino acids.
- A and B chains are connected by disulphide bonds.
- The C chain lies between A and B chains.
- Proinsulin → insulin, consisting of the A and B chains only.
- Human insulin contains 51 amino acids.

### Animal and human insulin
- Bovine insulin: 51 amino acids, with 3 amino acids different from human insulin.
- Porcine insulin: 51 amino acids, with 1 amino acid different from human insulin.
- Both retain intact biological activity.
- Long-term use may cause an immunogenic reaction and antibody formation.
- Human insulin is produced using recombinant DNA technology.

## Insulin receptor and signaling

- Insulin receptor is a tyrosine kinase receptor with α and β subunits (the source text renders these as “2α, 2β”).
- The β-subunit crosses the cell membrane; its intracellular portion has tyrosine kinase activity.
- Insulin receptor is similar to the IGF-1 receptor.
- IGF-1 cannot compensate for insulin deficiency; the source states that IGF-1 has stronger affinity for its own receptor than for the insulin receptor.
- Insulin stimulates the receptor by binding to its extracellular portion.
- The pathway labels in the source include:
  1. Insulin binding.
  2. Receptor autophosphorylation.
  3. Phosphorylation of insulin-receptor substrates (IRS).
  4. Phosphorylation/activation of PI3-kinase.
  5. Translocation of GLUT-4 to the cell membrane.
  6. Glucose uptake.
- Various physiological effects listed include cell growth and protein synthesis.

## Actions of insulin

| Timing | Actions listed |
|---|---|
| **Rapid** | Transport of glucose, amino acids and K⁺ into the cell |
| **Intermediate** | Increased protein synthesis; prevention of protein breakdown; activation of glycolytic enzymes and glycogen synthesis; inhibition of phosphorylase and gluconeogenic enzymes |
| **Long-term** | Upregulation of mRNA for lipogenic and other enzymes |

**Net effect:** storage of carbohydrate (glycogen), protein and fat; anabolic effects.

## Tissue-specific actions

| Adipose tissue | Muscle | Liver |
|---|---|---|
| GLUT-4 stimulation. | GLUT-4 stimulation. | Stimulation of glucokinase → phosphorylation of glucose → intracellular free glucose decreases → glucose entry into the cell. |
| Activation of lipoprotein lipase. | After a meal, increased glucose entry through GLUT-4. | Increased glycogen synthesis (increased glycogen synthase activity). |
| ↑↑ triglyceride deposition. | During exercise, glucose entry is described as insulin-independent through increased 5′-AMP-kinase activity and increased GLUT-4 at different sites. | Increased lipid synthesis: excess glucose → fatty acids → triglycerides (VLDL) → blood → adipose tissue. |
| Inhibition of hormone-sensitive lipase. | Increased amino-acid uptake and protein synthesis. | Increased protein synthesis (the source includes “by ↓ gluconeogenesis”). |
| Increased K⁺ uptake. | Prevents protein catabolism; ↑ ketone, ↑ K⁺ uptake. | Decreased ketogenesis. |

## Regulation of insulin secretion

### Glucose-dependent secretion
- Blood glucose >70 mg% → GLUT-2 opens / glucose enters β-cells → glycolysis and citric-acid cycle → increased ATP.
- ATP inhibits the ATP-sensitive K⁺ channel → β-cell depolarization.
- Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → Ca²⁺ enters.
- Vesicles move close to the cell membrane; the cell membrane fuses with the vesicular membrane → insulin secretion.

### Incretins and ATP-sensitive K⁺ channels
- Incretin receptor activation enhances Ca²⁺ entry.
- The most potent incretin listed is **GLP-1**.
- GLP-1 agonist listed: **exenatide**.
- ATP-sensitive K⁺ channel is associated with the sulfonylurea receptor (SUR).
- Sulfonylureas increase insulin secretion.

### Stimulators and modulators of insulin release
- Glucose and mannose → increased ATP.
- Amino acids and β-keto acids → increased ATP.
- Intestinal peptides: GLP-1, GIP, gastrin, secretin and CCK.
- Parasympathetic stimulation: acetylcholine acts on M3 receptors → Gαq activation → increased insulin release.
- Sympathetic stimulation: α₂-adrenergic receptor stimulation → decreased cAMP → decreased insulin release.
- After α₂ blockade, sympathetic stimulation of β₂ receptors increases cAMP and insulin release.
- Theophylline, glucagon and sulfonylurea are listed as increasing cAMP.

## Glucagon

- The rough endoplasmic reticulum produces preproglucagon.

### Preproglucagon processing
- In **α-cells**, the source lists glucagon, MPGF and GRPP.
- In **intestinal L-cells**, the source lists glicentin, GLP-1, GLP-2, oxyntomodulin and GRPP.
- The processed-fragment schematic also labels signal peptide, GRPP, glucagon, GLP-1, GLP-2, oxyntomodulin, glicentin and MPGF (major proglucagon fragment).

### Actions of glucagon
- Glycogenolytic.
- Gluconeogenic.
- Lipolytic.
- Ketogenic.
- Insulin is described as the hormone of energy storage.
- Glucagon is described as the hormone of energy release.

## MCQs

### Rapid infusion of insulin
**Question:** Rapid infusion of insulin causes? (AIIMS 2010)

- a. Hyperkalemia
- b. Hypokalemia
- c. Hypernatremia
- d. Hyponatremia

**Answer as printed:** (b) Hypokalemia.

### Insulin secretion after a carbohydrate-rich meal
**Question:** After a meal rich in carbohydrate, insulin secretion is stimulated by which option? (JIPMER 2003)

- a. CCK
- b. Serotonin
- c. VIP
- d. GLP-1 (7–36)

**Answer as printed:** (d) GLP-1 (7–36).
