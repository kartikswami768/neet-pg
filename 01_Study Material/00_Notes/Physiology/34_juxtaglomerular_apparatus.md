# 34 — Juxtaglomerular Apparatus

## 21. Juxta-Glomerular Apparatus

The juxtaglomerular (JG) apparatus is located:

- Between the afferent and efferent arterioles of the glomerulus.
- At the junction of the thick ascending limb (TAL) of Henle's loop and the distal convoluted tubule (DCT).

## Components of the JG apparatus

### Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells

- Modified smooth-muscle cells of the afferent arteriole.
- Contain granules filled with renin.
- Also known as granular cells of the kidney.
- Function as intrarenal baroreceptors, activated by a decrease in BP.

### Macula densa cells

- Columnar epithelial cells of the TAL, in contact with the afferent and efferent arterioles.
- Located at the junction between TAL and DCT.
- Act as an intrarenal chemoreceptor, sensing NaCl concentration in the tubular lumen.
- Cl⁻ is a more potent stimulus than Na⁺ in the source description.

### Lacis cells

- Extraglomerular mesangial cells located outside the glomerular capillaries.
- Described in the source as anti-inflammatory cells.

### Intraglomerular mesangial cells

- Found between the glomerular capillaries.
- The source states that they regulate GFR through contraction and relaxation of afferent arterioles.
  - Relaxation: increased surface area of the basement membrane → increased GFR.
  - Contraction: decreased surface area of the basement membrane → decreased GFR.

## JG cells and renin secretion

### cAMP

- JG cells contain GPCRs that generate cAMP; cAMP leads to degranulation of JG cells and stimulates renin secretion.
- Factors increasing cAMP production inside JG cells:
  1. Hypotension.
  2. Sympathetic stimulation.
  3. PGE₂ and PGI₂.
- High intracellular Ca²⁺ inhibits renin secretion; the source calls this the **calcium paradox of JG cells**.

![[ch34_p261_jg_cell_camp_renin_schematic.png]]

### Regulation of renin

| Inhibitors of renin production | Stimulators of renin production |
|---|---|
| Angiotensin II, adenosine, ATP and ADH levels → increased intracellular Ca²⁺ in JG cells → inhibition of renin secretion | Intrarenal hypotension (BP < 80 mmHg) |
| Increased NaCl at the macula densa → increased adenosine → decreased renin secretion | Sympathetic activation (catecholamines) |
|  | Prostaglandins (PGE₂, PGI₂) |

Factors associated with increased renin secretion in the source:

- Na⁺ depletion.
- Hypotension and haemorrhage.
- Diuretics.
- Dehydration.
- Cardiac failure.
- Cirrhosis.
- Constriction of the renal artery or aorta.
- Psychological stimuli and upright posture.

## Renal artery stenosis (RAS)

### Right renal artery stenosis

1. Narrowing of the right renal artery → increased resistance to blood flow → decreased BP at the renal artery → increased renin.
2. Increased renin enters the right renal vein and then the systemic circulation.
3. Renin in the systemic circulation → angiotensin I → angiotensin II → severe hypertension due to vasoconstriction.

### Effect on the left renal artery

- Angiotensin II reaches the left renal artery and inhibits JG cells on the left side.
- Renin secretion on the left side decreases, resulting in decreased renin in the left renal vein.
- In renal artery stenosis, renin is increased on the stenotic side and decreased on the contralateral side.

![[ch34_p261_renal_artery_stenosis_renin_diagram.png]]

### Diagnosis and surgery

- Measure renin levels from both renal veins.
- Significant stenosis is identified by a higher renin level on the affected side.
- Surgical intervention is considered if the renin ratio (right:left) is > 1.5.

## Macula densa cells

- Macula densa is located at the junction of TAL and DCT.
- Detects increased NaCl load and produces adenosine.
- The luminal surface of the macula densa cell contains an NKCC transporter (Na⁺, K⁺ and Cl⁻).
- K⁺ diffuses back into the lumen.
- Na⁺ is reabsorbed from the basal aspect via the Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump, generating ADP; ADP → AMP → adenosine.

### Adenosine action

- Acts on Lacis cells via the A1 receptor, leading to Ca²⁺ influx in JG and smooth-muscle cells through gap junctions.
- Increased Ca²⁺ influx in JG cells → decreased renin.
- Increased Ca²⁺ influx in smooth-muscle cells → afferent arteriole contraction.

## Tubuloglomerular feedback

1. Increased ECF → increased GFR → increased filtration of NaCl.
2. Increased NaCl load at macula densa cells → increased adenosine.
3. Adenosine causes contraction of smooth muscle in afferent arterioles.
4. Afferent arteriolar constriction → decreased GFR and decreased renal blood flow.

- This is a negative-feedback mechanism operating from the tubules to the glomerulus.
- It is an autoregulatory mechanism for GFR control.

## MCQs

### 1. Macula densa is made of specialized cells located at which site?

(a) Junction of thick descending limb and DCT; (b) Junction of thick descending limb and PCT; (c) Junction of thick ascending limb and DCT; (d) Junction of DCT and collecting duct.

**Answer: (c)** *(INI-CET 2022)*

### 2. Macula densa is formed by which part of the nephron?

(a) PCT; (b) DCT; (c) Afferent arteriole; (d) Bowman's capsule.

**Answer: (b)** *(INI-CET 2020, November)*

A. Mild constriction of the efferent artery increases GFR.  
B. Efferent arteriolar constriction decreases RBF.  
C. Mesangial-cell contraction increases glomerular hydrostatic pressure.  
D. Mesangial-cell contraction increases Bowman's capsule hydrostatic pressure.

**Answers:** A — True; B — True; C — False; D — False. *(AIIMS 2019)*
