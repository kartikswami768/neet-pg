# Hypertensive Crisis — Preferred Drugs

> **Hypertensive emergency = severe BP elevation + acute target-organ damage**
> → **IV, rapidly titratable drug**
> → Usually reduce MAP by **~20–25% in first hour**, then to ~160/100–110 over next 2–6 h
> → Exceptions: aortic dissection, eclampsia, stroke, etc.

| Clinical scenario                                  | Preferred drug                                | Why / key rationale                                                               |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Most hypertensive emergencies**                  | **Nicardipine IV**                            | Potent, predictable arterial vasodilator; easily titratable                       |
| **Acute aortic dissection**                        | **Esmolol IV → add nicardipine if needed**    | Rapid β1 blockade ↓ HR/contractility → ↓ aortic shear stress                      |
| **Acute pulmonary edema / LV failure**             | **IV nitroglycerin**                          | Venodilation → ↓ preload; at higher doses also ↓ afterload                        |
| **ACS with severe hypertension**                   | **Nitroglycerin ± β-blocker**                 | ↓ preload/afterload and myocardial O₂ demand                                      |
| **Acute ischemic stroke + BP requiring treatment** | **Labetalol or nicardipine**                  | Controlled BP reduction without excessive cerebral hypoperfusion                  |
| **Intracerebral hemorrhage**                       | **Nicardipine**                               | Smooth, titratable BP reduction; avoids major ICP effects                         |
| **Hypertensive encephalopathy**                    | **Nicardipine**                               | Controlled reduction reverses cerebral hyperperfusion                             |
| **Eclampsia / severe pre-eclampsia**               | **IV labetalol**                              | Rapid BP control; established obstetric safety                                    |
| **Eclampsia with seizures**                        | **MgSO₄ + antihypertensive**                  | MgSO₄ prevents/treats seizures; labetalol controls BP                             |
| **Pheochromocytoma crisis**                        | **Phentolamine IV**                           | α-blockade directly reverses catecholamine-mediated vasoconstriction              |
| **Pheochromocytoma + tachycardia**                 | **α-blockade first → β-blocker**              | Prevents unopposed α-mediated vasoconstriction                                    |
| **Sympathomimetic/cocaine toxicity**               | **Benzodiazepine + nicardipine/phentolamine** | ↓ sympathetic drive + control vasoconstriction                                    |
| **MAOI–tyramine crisis**                           | **Phentolamine**                              | α-blockade counteracts catecholamine excess                                       |
| **Acute renal failure / renal dysfunction**        | **Nicardipine or clevidipine**                | Easily titratable; avoids reliance on renal clearance                             |
| **Perioperative hypertensive crisis**              | **Clevidipine / nicardipine**                 | Very rapid onset/offset and titratability                                         |
| **Aortic dissection + severe bronchospasm risk**   | **Esmolol cautiously / alternative strategy** | Short half-life allows rapid titration; avoid β-blockade if true contraindication |
| **Pregnancy-associated severe hypertension**       | **Labetalol / hydralazine / nifedipine**      | Established pregnancy safety; rapid BP control                                    |

## High-yield drug selection

### 1. Nicardipine
**General-purpose hypertensive emergency drug**

> IV dihydropyridine CCB → arterial vasodilation → ↓ SVR

Best remembered as:
**"Most hypertensive emergencies → nicardipine."**

### 2. Esmolol
**Aortic dissection**

> Ultra-short acting β1 blocker → ↓ HR + ↓ contractility → ↓ dP/dt

**Give β-blocker BEFORE vasodilator** to prevent reflex tachycardia.

### 3. Nitroglycerin
**ACS / acute pulmonary edema**

> Predominantly venodilator → ↓ preload → ↓ LV filling pressure

At higher doses:
> ↓ afterload + coronary vasodilation

### 4. Labetalol
**Pregnancy + neurologic hypertensive emergencies**

> α1 + β blockade → ↓ SVR without marked reflex tachycardia

### 5. Phentolamine
**Catecholamine excess**

> Competitive α-blocker → rapidly reverses catecholamine-mediated vasoconstriction

Think:
**"Pheochromocytoma = Phentolamine."**

### 6. Clevidipine
**Very rapid perioperative BP control**

> Ultra-short acting IV dihydropyridine CCB → extremely rapid titration/on-off

---

# Important exceptions

### Aortic dissection
**Do NOT start with a pure vasodilator alone.**

> **Esmolol → then nicardipine/nitroprusside if needed**

### Pheochromocytoma
**Never give β-blocker before α-blockade.**

> **α first → β later if required**

### Acute ischemic stroke
BP is **not automatically lowered** just because it is high.

- Thrombolysis candidate → BP generally must be **<185/110 mmHg before thrombolysis**
- After thrombolysis → maintain **<180/105 mmHg**
- Otherwise, BP reduction is generally reserved for specific indications/extreme elevations.

### Intracerebral hemorrhage
Early controlled BP reduction is generally favored, but the exact target depends on the clinical situation.
