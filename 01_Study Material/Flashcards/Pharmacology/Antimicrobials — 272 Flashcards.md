# Antimicrobials — 273 Flashcards

These cards are built from the supplied Antimicrobials Part 1 and Part 2 notes. Priority is given to mechanisms, drug-of-choice associations, resistance mechanisms, adverse effects, contraindications, interactions, organism–drug pairs, and classic clinical vignettes.

#flashcards/Pharmacology/Antimicrobials

### AM-001 — General classification · Drug targets · mechanism · target: bacterial cell wall · difficulty: 1
Which major antimicrobial classes primarily inhibit bacterial cell-wall synthesis?::β-lactams, bacitracin, cycloserine, vancomycin and fosfomycin.

### AM-002 — General classification · Protein synthesis · target: 30S/50S · difficulty: 1
Which major antimicrobial classes act on bacterial protein synthesis?::Tetracyclines, aminoglycosides, macrolides, clindamycin, streptogramins, chloramphenicol, linezolid and tigecycline.

### AM-003 — General classification · Folate pathway · target: folate synthesis · difficulty: 1
Which antimicrobial classes interfere with microbial folate synthesis?::Sulfonamides, dapsone, trimethoprim and pyrimethamine.

### AM-004 — General classification · DNA/nucleic acid · target: DNA · difficulty: 1
Which two major antimicrobial groups in the notes directly target bacterial DNA/nucleic-acid processes?::Nitroimidazoles and fluoroquinolones.

### AM-005 — β-lactams · Mechanism · target: PBP/transpeptidation · difficulty: 1
What is the core mechanism of β-lactam antibiotics?::They bind penicillin-binding proteins and inhibit transpeptidation, preventing peptidoglycan cross-linking and causing bacterial cell-wall failure.

### AM-006 — β-lactams · Pharmacodynamics · bactericidal action · target: dividing bacteria · difficulty: 1
Why are β-lactams most effective against actively dividing bacteria?::They inhibit cell-wall assembly during growth and are generally bactericidal.

### AM-007 — Penicillin G · High-yield uses · drug-use · target: susceptible spirochetes/bacteria · difficulty: 1
What is the classic high-yield infectious-disease association for penicillin G?::Syphilis; it is also used for selected susceptible streptococcal infections and several other infections listed in the notes.

### AM-008 — Penicillin G · Pharmacokinetics · limitation · target: oral stability · difficulty: 1
Why is penicillin G poorly suited for oral administration?::It is acid-labile and therefore has poor oral stability.

### AM-009 — Penicillin G · Pharmacology · limitation · target: spectrum/duration · difficulty: 1
What are the major pharmacological limitations of penicillin G?::It is acid-labile, short-acting, narrow-spectrum and susceptible to resistance such as β-lactamase production or altered PBPs.

### AM-010 — Probenecid · Interaction · pharmacokinetics · target: renal tubular secretion · difficulty: 1
How does probenecid prolong penicillin exposure?::It inhibits renal tubular organic-anion transport and reduces penicillin excretion.

### AM-011 — Penicillin classes · Comparison · classification · target: β-lactamase resistance · difficulty: 1
Which oral penicillin is the acid-stable alternative to penicillin G?::Penicillin V.

### AM-012 — Aminopenicillins · Drugs · classification · target: β-lactam/PBP · difficulty: 1
Which drugs are the classic aminopenicillins?::Amoxicillin and ampicillin.

### AM-013 — Antistaphylococcal penicillins · Drugs · classification · target: penicillinase · difficulty: 1
Which penicillins are classically resistant to staphylococcal penicillinase?::Oxacillin, cloxacillin, dicloxacillin and nafcillin.

### AM-014 — Benzathine penicillin · Formulation · route · target: depot absorption · difficulty: 1
What is the key formulation and route of benzathine penicillin G?::It is a depot formulation given intramuscularly with slow absorption.

### AM-015 — Benzathine penicillin · Clinical use · drug-use · target: Treponema pallidum · difficulty: 1
Which syphilis treatment is classically associated with benzathine penicillin G?::Primary, secondary and early latent syphilis are treated with benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM once in the notes.

### AM-016 — Piperacillin · Spectrum · antipseudomonal · target: Pseudomonas · difficulty: 1
Which penicillin is the major clinically relevant antipseudomonal ureidopenicillin?::Piperacillin, commonly used as piperacillin-tazobactam.

### AM-017 — Ampicillin · Meningitis · drug of choice · target: Listeria · difficulty: 1
Which antibiotic is the classic drug association for Listeria monocytogenes meningitis?::Ampicillin.

### AM-018 — Ampicillin · EBV · adverse effect clue · target: rash · difficulty: 1
What classic reaction occurs when ampicillin is given during infectious mononucleosis due to EBV?::A characteristic maculopapular rash.

### AM-019 — Penicillin allergy · Type I hypersensitivity · mechanism · target: IgE · difficulty: 1
What are the classic manifestations of a type I penicillin hypersensitivity reaction?::Urticaria, angioedema and anaphylaxis from IgE-mediated immediate hypersensitivity.

### AM-020 — β-lactam allergy · Cross-reactivity · comparison · target: side chain/immunology · difficulty: 2
How should third-generation cephalosporin cross-reactivity with confirmed IgE-mediated penicillin allergy be remembered for exams?::It is low, with the notes giving a risk of less than 1% for agents such as ceftriaxone and cefixime.

### AM-021 — Aztreonam · Allergy · comparison · target: β-lactam cross-reactivity · difficulty: 2
What is the important allergy exception associated with aztreonam?::Aztreonam generally has minimal cross-reactivity with penicillin allergy, but caution is needed with ceftazidime allergy because of shared side-chain similarity.

### AM-022 — Syphilis · Early disease · regimen · target: Treponema pallidum · difficulty: 1
What regimen is used for uncomplicated early syphilis in the notes?::Benzathine penicillin G 2.4 million units IM once.

### AM-023 — Syphilis · Late latent/tertiary disease · regimen · target: Treponema pallidum · difficulty: 1
What is the benzathine penicillin regimen for late latent or tertiary syphilis without neurosyphilis?::2.4 million units IM weekly for 3 doses, for a total of 7.2 million units.

### AM-024 — Neurosyphilis · Treatment · drug of choice · target: CNS penetration · difficulty: 1
Which penicillin formulation is used for neurosyphilis?::Aqueous crystalline penicillin G IV, rather than benzathine penicillin.

### AM-025 — Neurosyphilis · Regimen · dosing · target: CNS Treponema · difficulty: 2
What is the high-yield aqueous penicillin G regimen for neurosyphilis in the notes?::18–24 million units/day IV for 10–14 days, given as divided doses or continuous infusion.

### AM-026 — Syphilis · Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction · differentiation · target: inflammatory response · difficulty: 1
What is the key distinction between the Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction and penicillin allergy?::Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction is an acute febrile inflammatory reaction after syphilis treatment and is not a penicillin allergy.

### AM-027 — Syphilis · Pregnancy · management · target: penicillin · difficulty: 1
What should be done when a pregnant patient with syphilis has a true penicillin allergy?::Penicillin desensitization is used because penicillin remains essential for syphilis in pregnancy.

### AM-028 — MRSA · Resistance · mechanism · target: PBP2a/mecA · difficulty: 1
What is the classic molecular mechanism of MRSA resistance to most β-lactams?::Acquisition of mec determinants such as mecA leading to altered PBP2a with reduced β-lactam affinity.

### AM-029 — MRSA · β-lactam exceptions · comparison · target: PBP2a · difficulty: 1
Which cephalosporins in the notes retain activity against MRSA?::Ceftaroline and ceftobiprole.

### AM-030 — MSSA · Penicillinase · drug choice · target: β-lactamase · difficulty: 1
Which penicillins are used against penicillinase-producing MSSA?::Cloxacillin, oxacillin, nafcillin and dicloxacillin.

### AM-031 — Cephalosporins · Generations · classification · target: spectrum · difficulty: 1
Which cephalosporin generation is classically associated with cefazolin and strong Gram-positive activity?::First generation.

### AM-032 — Cephalosporins · Surgical prophylaxis · drug of choice · target: MSSA/streptococci · difficulty: 1
Which cephalosporin is the major surgical prophylaxis association?::Cefazolin.

### AM-033 — Cephalosporins · LAME mnemonic · noncoverage · target: organism spectrum · difficulty: 1
What does LAME remind you about cephalosporin noncoverage?::Listeria, atypical organisms, MRSA and Enterococci.

### AM-034 — Cephalosporins · Pseudomonas · spectrum · target: Gram-negative bacilli · difficulty: 1
Name important antipseudomonal cephalosporins emphasized in the notes.::Ceftazidime, cefoperazone, ceftolozane and cefepime.

### AM-035 — Ceftriaxone · Elimination · pharmacokinetics · target: biliary excretion · difficulty: 1
Why is ceftriaxone important in renal impairment?::It has substantial biliary elimination and can be relatively useful when renal dose adjustment is a concern.

### AM-036 — Ceftriaxone · Neonates · adverse effect · target: bilirubin · difficulty: 2
Why is ceftriaxone used cautiously in neonates?::It can displace bilirubin and is associated with bilirubin encephalopathy risk.

### AM-037 — Cefoperazone · Adverse effect · coagulation · target: vitamin K · difficulty: 1
Which characteristic adverse effect is associated with cefoperazone?::Bleeding tendency or hypoprothrombinemia from impaired vitamin-K-dependent clotting factor function in susceptible patients.

### AM-038 — Carbapenems · Clinical role · spectrum · target: ESBL Enterobacterales · difficulty: 1
Why are carbapenems important in severe infections due to many ESBL-producing Enterobacterales?::They have very broad β-lactam activity and are stable to many ESBL enzymes.

### AM-039 — Ertapenem · Spectrum · comparison · target: Pseudomonas · difficulty: 1
Which important Gram-negative organisms are not reliably covered by ertapenem compared with imipenem or meropenem?::Pseudomonas aeruginosa and Acinetobacter.

### AM-040 — Imipenem/cilastatin · Mechanism · pharmacokinetics · target: DHP-I · difficulty: 1
Why is cilastatin combined with imipenem?::Cilastatin inhibits renal dehydropeptidase-I, reducing imipenem degradation and nephrotoxic metabolite formation.

### AM-041 — Carbapenems · Imipenem · adverse effect · target: CNS · difficulty: 1
Which carbapenem is particularly associated with seizures/neurotoxicity?::Imipenem.

### AM-042 — ESBL · Resistance mechanism · target: β-lactams · difficulty: 1
Which major β-lactam groups are hydrolyzed by ESBLs?::Many penicillins, cephalosporins and aztreonam.

### AM-043 — β-lactamase inhibitors · Drugs · classification · target: β-lactamase · difficulty: 1
Which classic β-lactamase inhibitors are listed in the notes?::Clavulanic acid, sulbactam and tazobactam.

### AM-044 — Carbapenemases · Resistance · exam association · target: carbapenem hydrolysis · difficulty: 2
What is the key concept represented by the term “Delhi-superbug” in antimicrobial pharmacology?::Carbapenemase-producing organisms, especially the NDM-associated resistance problem.

### AM-045 — Carbapenemase · Current therapy · resistance · target: KPC/NDM/OXA-48 · difficulty: 2
What current resistance-treatment point replaces the old blanket “carbapenemase → colistin” rule?::Treatment is pathogen-specific; the notes emphasize modern agents such as ceftazidime-avibactam, imipenem-relebactam, meropenem-vaborbactam, aztreonam-avibactam and cefiderocol depending on the resistance mechanism.

### AM-046 — Aztreonam · Spectrum · classification · target: aerobic Gram-negatives · difficulty: 1
What is the antimicrobial spectrum of aztreonam?::Primarily aerobic Gram-negative bacilli, including many Enterobacterales and Pseudomonas; it lacks useful Gram-positive and anaerobic coverage.

### AM-047 — Glycopeptides · Mechanism · target: D-Ala-D-Ala · difficulty: 1
What common mechanism do vancomycin and other glycopeptides share?::They bind the D-Ala-D-Ala terminus of peptidoglycan precursors and inhibit cell-wall assembly.

### AM-048 — Vancomycin · Spectrum · comparison · target: Gram-positive cell wall · difficulty: 1
Why is vancomycin not used routinely for Gram-negative infections?::Gram-negative outer membranes limit access of vancomycin to its target.

### AM-049 — Vancomycin · Oral versus IV · route · target: gut lumen/systemic infection · difficulty: 1
Why is oral vancomycin used for intestinal C. difficile infection rather than systemic MRSA infection?::Oral vancomycin is poorly absorbed and therefore stays in the gut lumen.

### AM-050 — Vancomycin · Infusion reaction · adverse effect · target: mediator release · difficulty: 1
What is the classic “red-man” or vancomycin infusion reaction?::Rapid IV infusion can cause flushing, erythema, pruritus and hypotension due to mediator release.

### AM-051 — Vancomycin · Infusion reaction · Prevention · target: infusion rate · difficulty: 1
How can vancomycin infusion reaction be reduced?::Administer the drug more slowly with appropriate supportive measures.

### AM-052 — C. difficile · Antibiotic risk · epidemiology · target: microbiome disruption · difficulty: 1
Which antibiotic is the classic high-yield risk factor for C. difficile infection?::Clindamycin; third-/fourth-generation cephalosporins, fluoroquinolones and carbapenems are also high-risk groups.

### AM-053 — C. difficile · Initial episode · treatment · target: toxin-producing C. difficile · difficulty: 2
What treatment is currently preferred for an initial adult C. difficile episode when feasible?::Fidaxomicin 200 mg orally twice daily for 10 days; oral vancomycin 125 mg four times daily for 10 days remains an acceptable alternative.

### AM-054 — Toxic shock syndrome · Toxin suppression · mechanism · target: protein synthesis · difficulty: 1
Why is clindamycin used in toxin-mediated staphylococcal or streptococcal disease?::Protein-synthesis inhibition can reduce bacterial toxin production; it is combined with appropriate bactericidal therapy and source control.

### AM-055 — Daptomycin · Mechanism · target: bacterial membrane · difficulty: 1
How does daptomycin kill susceptible Gram-positive bacteria?::It causes bacterial membrane depolarization and rapid bactericidal activity.

### AM-056 — Daptomycin · Pneumonia · contraindicated use · target: pulmonary surfactant · difficulty: 1
Why is daptomycin not useful for pneumonia?::Pulmonary surfactant inactivates daptomycin.

### AM-057 — Colistin · Spectrum · target: Gram-negative membrane · difficulty: 1
What is the main antimicrobial target and spectrum of colistin?::It disrupts Gram-negative bacterial membranes and is reserved for selected multidrug-resistant Gram-negative infections.

### AM-058 — Colistin · Adverse effects · safety · target: kidney/nervous system · difficulty: 1
What are the two major toxicities of colistin?::Nephrotoxicity and neurotoxicity.

### AM-059 — Colistin · Intrinsic resistance · organisms · target: outer membrane · difficulty: 1
Which three organisms are classic intrinsic resistance associations for colistin in the notes?::Burkholderia, Serratia and Proteus.

### AM-060 — Nitroimidazoles · Mechanism · target: DNA · difficulty: 1
How do nitroimidazoles such as metronidazole damage susceptible organisms?::They are reductively activated to reactive intermediates that damage DNA.

### AM-061 — Nitroimidazoles · Spectrum · comparison · target: anaerobic reduction pathways · difficulty: 1
Why do nitroimidazoles have strong activity against anaerobes but poor activity against typical aerobes?::Activation requires intracellular reduction pathways that are characteristic of susceptible anaerobic organisms and protozoa.

### AM-062 — Metronidazole · Clinical associations · drug-use · target: anaerobes/protozoa · difficulty: 1
What are four classic metronidazole associations?::Anaerobic infections, Trichomonas vaginalis, Giardia and amoebiasis; it also appears in H. pylori regimens and selected CDI settings.

### AM-063 — Metronidazole · Alcohol · exam pearl · target: ethanol interaction · difficulty: 1
What is the traditional exam warning associated with metronidazole and alcohol?::Avoid alcohol because of the classic teaching about a disulfiram-like reaction; the notes also state that modern evidence for a clinically meaningful interaction is less convincing.

### AM-064 — Vaginal infections · BV versus trichomoniasis · diagnosis · target: microscopy/pH · difficulty: 1
Which microscopic finding is classically associated with bacterial vaginosis?::Clue cells with vaginal pH greater than 4.5.

### AM-065 — Trichomoniasis · Clinical finding · diagnosis · target: cervix · difficulty: 1
What classic cervical finding is associated with Trichomonas vaginalis infection?::A strawberry cervix.

### AM-066 — Fluoroquinolones · Mechanism · target: DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV · difficulty: 1
What enzymes are inhibited by fluoroquinolones?::Bacterial DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV.

### AM-067 — Fluoroquinolones · Bactericidal action · mechanism · target: DNA replication · difficulty: 1
How do fluoroquinolones kill bacteria?::They impair DNA replication and transcription through DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV inhibition and are bactericidal.

### AM-068 — Ciprofloxacin · CYP interaction · pharmacokinetics · target: CYP1A2 · difficulty: 1
Which important CYP interaction is characteristic of ciprofloxacin?::Ciprofloxacin inhibits CYP1A2 and can increase concentrations of substrates such as theophylline.

### AM-069 — Fluoroquinolones · QT prolongation · comparison · target: cardiac repolarization · difficulty: 1
Which fluoroquinolones in the notes are particularly associated with QT prolongation?::Moxifloxacin and sparfloxacin are emphasized as higher-risk examples.

### AM-070 — Fluoroquinolones · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: tendon/CNS/metabolic · difficulty: 1
What major adverse-effect cluster should you remember for fluoroquinolones?::QT prolongation, dysglycemia, CNS effects, photosensitivity, rash/SJS-TEN risk and tendinitis/tendon rupture.

### AM-071 — UTI · Fosfomycin · dose · target: MurA · difficulty: 1
What is the common current oral fosfomycin regimen for uncomplicated cystitis in adult women?::A single 3-g oral sachet of fosfomycin tromethamine.

### AM-072 — Fosfomycin · Mechanism · target: MurA/cell wall · difficulty: 1
Which enzyme is inhibited by fosfomycin?::MurA, an early step in peptidoglycan synthesis.

### AM-073 — Tetracyclines · Mechanism · target: 30S A site · difficulty: 1
How do tetracyclines inhibit bacterial protein synthesis?::They bind the 30S ribosomal subunit and prevent aminoacyl-tRNA from entering the A site.

### AM-074 — Tetracyclines · Pharmacodynamics · bacteriostasis · target: 30S · difficulty: 1
Are tetracyclines generally bacteriostatic or bactericidal?::Bacteriostatic.

### AM-075 — Doxycycline · Clinical associations · drug-use · target: atypical organisms · difficulty: 1
Which infections are particularly high-yield associations for doxycycline?::Rickettsial infections, cholera, chlamydial infections, leptospirosis, brucellosis and Lyme disease.

### AM-076 — Demeclocycline · SIADH · mechanism · target: ADH/V2 action · difficulty: 1
Why can demeclocycline be used in selected cases of SIADH?::It inhibits renal ADH action and can produce nephrogenic diabetes insipidus.

### AM-077 — Minocycline · Clinical use · drug-use · target: Cutibacterium/leprosy · difficulty: 1
Which two classic uses of minocycline are emphasized?::Acne and leprosy.

### AM-078 — Tetracycline · H. pylori · regimen · target: protein synthesis · difficulty: 1
In which common H. pylori regimen does tetracycline appear as a component?::Bismuth quadruple therapy.

### AM-079 — Tetracyclines · Drug interactions · absorption · target: divalent/trivalent cations · difficulty: 1
Which common substances reduce tetracycline absorption by chelation?::Calcium, iron, magnesium and other polyvalent cations.

### AM-080 — Tetracycline toxicity · Expired drug · classic association · target: renal tubules · difficulty: 1
What classic toxicity is associated with ingestion of degraded/expired tetracycline?::Fanconi syndrome.

### AM-081 — Tetracyclines · Pregnancy/children · safety · target: teeth/bone · difficulty: 1
What is the classic exam rule concerning tetracyclines in pregnancy and young children?::Avoid tetracyclines because of effects on developing teeth and bone; the notes use <8 years as the exam rule.

### AM-082 — Glycylcyclines · Tigecycline · classification · target: 30S · difficulty: 1
What is tigecycline, and which ribosomal subunit does it act on?::It is a glycylcycline related to tetracyclines and acts on the 30S ribosomal subunit.

### AM-083 — Macrolides · Mechanism · target: 50S translocation · difficulty: 1
How do macrolides inhibit bacterial protein synthesis?::They bind the 50S subunit and inhibit translocation.

### AM-084 — Macrolides · Atypical pneumonia · drug-use · target: atypical bacteria · difficulty: 1
Which three associations can be recalled for macrolides in community-acquired/atypical respiratory infection?::Legionella, Mycoplasma and pertussis.

### AM-085 — Macrolides · CYP inhibition · comparison · target: CYP3A4 · difficulty: 1
Which macrolide has the strongest CYP inhibition among the common agents listed?::Erythromycin; clarithromycin is intermediate, while azithromycin has the least CYP inhibition.

### AM-086 — Macrolides · Diphtheria · treatment · target: Corynebacterium toxin · difficulty: 1
What pharmacologic combination is used for diphtheria in the notes?::An appropriate macrolide such as erythromycin or azithromycin plus diphtheria antitoxin for respiratory disease.

### AM-087 — Gonorrhea · Current regimen · drug-use · target: Neisseria gonorrhoeae · difficulty: 2
What is the current ceftriaxone regimen for uncomplicated gonorrhea in adults weighing <150 kg?::Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM once.

### AM-088 — Gonorrhea · Chlamydia coverage · combination · target: Chlamydia trachomatis · difficulty: 1
What should be added to gonorrhea treatment if chlamydia has not been excluded?::Doxycycline 100 mg orally twice daily for 7 days.

### AM-089 — Linezolid · Mechanism · target: 23S rRNA/50S · difficulty: 1
How does linezolid inhibit bacterial protein synthesis?::It binds 23S rRNA on the 50S subunit and prevents initiation-complex formation.

### AM-090 — Linezolid · Adverse effects · mnemonic · target: marrow/CNS/mitochondria · difficulty: 1
What does the POST mnemonic remind you about linezolid toxicity?::Peripheral neuropathy, optic neuropathy/neuritis, serotonin syndrome risk and thrombocytopenia/bone-marrow suppression.

### AM-091 — Linezolid · Pharmacokinetics · oral versus IV · target: bioavailability · difficulty: 1
What is the important route advantage of linezolid?::Oral bioavailability is approximately 100%, giving oral and IV formulations comparable systemic exposure.

### AM-092 — Streptogramins · Composition · target: 50S · difficulty: 1
What is the composition ratio of quinupristin-dalfopristin?::Approximately 30% quinupristin and 70% dalfopristin.

### AM-093 — Aminoglycosides · Mechanism · target: 30S · difficulty: 1
How do aminoglycosides interfere with bacterial protein synthesis?::They bind the 30S ribosome, interfere with initiation and cause misreading of mRNA.

### AM-094 — Aminoglycosides · Killing pattern · PK/PD · target: concentration/MIC · difficulty: 1
What is the characteristic pharmacodynamic killing pattern of aminoglycosides?::Concentration-dependent killing with a clinically important post-antibiotic effect.

### AM-095 — Streptomycin · Clinical association · drug-use · target: Mycobacterium · difficulty: 1
Name important classic indications for streptomycin from the notes.::Tuberculosis, plague and tularemia.

### AM-096 — Amikacin · Clinical association · drug-use · target: resistant Gram-negatives · difficulty: 1
Which aminoglycoside is strongly associated with resistant Gram-negative infections, Pseudomonas and selected TB regimens?::Amikacin.

### AM-097 — Gentamicin · Synergy · clinical pharmacology · target: bacterial protein synthesis · difficulty: 1
What important pharmacologic role does gentamicin have in selected infections?::It can provide synergistic activity with other antimicrobials in selected infections.

### AM-098 — Aminoglycosides · Ototoxicity · adverse effect · target: CN VIII · difficulty: 1
What is the classic aminoglycoside ototoxicity pattern?::Irreversible cochlear or vestibular damage, with high-frequency hearing loss as a classic association.

### AM-099 — Aminoglycosides · Nephrotoxicity · adverse effect · target: proximal tubule · difficulty: 1
What is the classic renal toxicity of aminoglycosides?::Renal tubular injury with nephrotoxicity.

### AM-100 — Aminoglycosides · Neuromuscular blockade · adverse effect · target: neuromuscular junction · difficulty: 1
In which patient is aminoglycoside-associated neuromuscular blockade particularly concerning?::A patient with myasthenia gravis.

### AM-101 — Neomycin · Hepatic encephalopathy · route/clinical use · target: gut bacteria · difficulty: 1
Why can oral neomycin reduce ammonia production in the gut?::It is poorly absorbed and reduces intestinal bacterial load and ammonia production.

### AM-102 — Aminoglycosides · Intrinsic resistance · mechanism · target: oxygen-dependent uptake · difficulty: 1
Why are aminoglycosides ineffective against anaerobic bacteria?::They require oxygen-dependent transport into bacterial cells.

### AM-103 — Folate antagonists · Pathway · sequence · target: folate synthesis · difficulty: 1
What is the general folate pathway blocked by sulfonamides and trimethoprim?::PABA → dihydropteroate/folate synthesis → dihydrofolate → tetrahydrofolate → nucleotide synthesis.

### AM-104 — Sulfonamides · Mechanism · target: DHPS · difficulty: 1
Which enzyme is inhibited by sulfonamides?::Dihydropteroate synthase in the bacterial folate pathway.

### AM-105 — Trimethoprim · Mechanism · target: bacterial DHFR · difficulty: 1
Which enzyme is inhibited by trimethoprim?::Bacterial dihydrofolate reductase.

### AM-106 — Pyrimethamine · Mechanism · target: protozoal DHFR · difficulty: 1
Which enzyme is targeted by pyrimethamine?::Protozoal dihydrofolate reductase.

### AM-107 — Cotrimoxazole · Composition · ratio · target: sequential folate blockade · difficulty: 1
What is the composition and weight ratio of cotrimoxazole?::Trimethoprim plus sulfamethoxazole, with sulfamethoxazole:trimethoprim approximately 5:1 by weight.

### AM-108 — Cotrimoxazole · Mechanism · synergy · target: DHPS/DHFR · difficulty: 1
Why is cotrimoxazole described as producing sequential folate blockade?::Sulfamethoxazole inhibits dihydropteroate synthase and trimethoprim inhibits bacterial DHFR.

### AM-109 — Cotrimoxazole · Pneumocystis · drug of choice · target: P. jirovecii · difficulty: 1
What is the major drug-of-choice association for Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia?::Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole.

### AM-110 — Dapsone · Burns · topical antimicrobial · target: folate synthesis · difficulty: 1
Which topical sulfonamide is associated with burn-wound antimicrobial therapy in the notes?::Silver sulfadiazine.

### AM-111 — Pregnancy · Antibiotic safety · comparison · target: fetal toxicity · difficulty: 1
Which antibiotic groups are generally listed as comparatively safe in pregnancy in the notes?::Penicillins, cephalosporins and macrolides, particularly azithromycin/erythromycin.

### AM-112 — Pregnancy · Antibiotic avoidance · comparison · target: fetus/neonate · difficulty: 1
Which major antibiotic groups are listed as avoid/caution in pregnancy?::Fluoroquinolones, aminoglycosides, tetracyclines and sulfonamides, with indication-specific qualification.

### AM-113 — Renal failure · Antibiotics · pharmacokinetics · target: biliary elimination · difficulty: 1
Which antimicrobials are highlighted as relatively renal-safe because of biliary elimination?::Ceftriaxone, cefoperazone, erythromycin, azithromycin, linezolid, doxycycline and selected fluoroquinolones such as moxifloxacin.

### AM-114 — Renal failure · Dose adjustment · comparison · target: renal clearance · difficulty: 1
Which major antibiotic group commonly requires renal dose adjustment?::Aminoglycosides, and many penicillins/β-lactams.

### AM-115 — Antimicrobial resistance · Enzymatic degradation · mechanism · target: drug molecule · difficulty: 1
Which three antimicrobial groups have classic enzymatic inactivation associations in the notes?::Aminoglycosides, β-lactams and chloramphenicol.

### AM-116 — Antimicrobial resistance · Efflux · mechanism · target: drug transport · difficulty: 1
Which drugs are classic examples of resistance caused by drug efflux in the notes?::Tetracyclines, tigecycline and chloroquine.

### AM-117 — Antimicrobial resistance · Target modification · mechanism · target: PBP/ribosome/DNA enzymes · difficulty: 1
What are the classic target-modification resistance examples in the notes?::MRSA with altered PBP2a, macrolide resistance through ribosomal target modification and fluoroquinolone resistance through DNA gyrase/topoisomerase IV alterations.

### AM-118 — MIC · Definition · pharmacodynamics · target: antimicrobial susceptibility · difficulty: 1
What is MIC?::The minimum inhibitory concentration is the lowest antimicrobial concentration that inhibits visible bacterial growth.

### AM-119 — PK/PD · Concentration-dependent killing · comparison · target: Cmax/MIC · difficulty: 1
Which major antibiotic class is the classic example of concentration-dependent killing?::Aminoglycosides.

### AM-120 — PK/PD · Time-dependent killing · comparison · target: time above MIC · difficulty: 1
Which major antimicrobial class is the classic exam example of time-dependent killing?::β-lactams, with efficacy related to the time that free-drug concentrations remain above MIC.

### AM-121 — Vancomycin · PK/PD · exposure target · target: AUC/MIC · difficulty: 2
Which pharmacodynamic index is important for modern vancomycin exposure monitoring in serious MRSA infection?::AUC/MIC.

### AM-122 — Post-antibiotic effect · Definition · pharmacodynamics · target: bacterial growth · difficulty: 1
What is the post-antibiotic effect?::Continued suppression of bacterial growth after antibiotic concentrations fall below the MIC.

### AM-123 — TB · First-line drugs · classification · target: Mycobacterium tuberculosis · difficulty: 1
What four classic first-line drugs form HRZE in drug-susceptible TB?::Isoniazid, rifampicin, pyrazinamide and ethambutol.

### AM-124 — TB · Standard Indian regimen · regimen · target: drug-susceptible TB · difficulty: 1
What standard daily drug-susceptible TB regimen is emphasized in the Indian programme notes?::2 months of HRZE followed by 4 months of HRE.

### AM-125 — TB · MDR definition · resistance classification · target: INH/RIF · difficulty: 1

### AM-272 — Antimicrobials · High-yield integration · comparison · target: ribosome/cell wall/DNA · difficulty: 2
Match the classic exam targets: β-lactams, vancomycin, tetracyclines, aminoglycosides, macrolides, fluoroquinolones and metronidazole.::β-lactams = PBPs/transpeptidation; vancomycin = D-Ala-D-Ala; tetracyclines = 30S A-site tRNA entry; aminoglycosides = 30S misreading; macrolides = 50S translocation; fluoroquinolones = DNA gyrase/topo IV; metronidazole = DNA damage after reductive activation.

### AM-273 — Antimicrobials · Adverse effects · bone-marrow suppression · difficulty: 2
Which important antibiotics are associated with bone-marrow suppression/myelosuppression?::**Chloramphenicol** → classic **aplastic anaemia**; **linezolid** → thrombocytopenia ± anaemia/leukopenia, especially with prolonged therapy; **TMP-SMX/sulfonamides** → cytopenias, especially with folate deficiency; **piperacillin/tazobactam** → neutropenia with prolonged therapy; **vancomycin** → neutropenia, usually after prolonged therapy. **Exam core: chloramphenicol + linezolid.**
