### Types of hormone receptors

#### Hormones that bind to intracellular receptors

| Receptor | Hormones |
|---|---|
| Cytoplasmic receptors | Glucocorticoids, mineralocorticoids, androgens, vitamin D |
| Nuclear receptors | Thyroid hormones, vitamin A |

#### Hormones that bind to cell-surface receptors

| Pathway/receptor | Hormones |
|---|---|
| **Gs** — stimulation of second messenger cAMP | ACTH, CRH, TSH, GHRH, FSH, LH, hCG, vasopressin (V2 receptors), catecholamines (β receptors), glucagon, secretin, parathyroid hormone |
| **Gi** — inhibition of second messenger cAMP | Catecholamines (α2 receptors), acetylcholine (M2, M4), somatostatin |
| **Gq** — second messenger is phospholipase C or cGMP (as stated in source) | Catecholamines (α1 receptors), GnRH, GHRH, TRH, vasopressin (V1 receptor), gastrin, cholecystokinin, oxytocin, acetylcholine (M1, M3, M5) |
| **cGMP** second messenger | Nitric oxide, atrial natriuretic peptide |

### Receptors using tyrosine kinase
- **JAK-STAT:** non-receptor tyrosine kinase
- **MAP kinase:** receptor tyrosine kinase pathway

Associated hormones/factors in the source:
- Erythropoietin
- Growth hormone
- Prolactin
- Growth factors such as IGF, VEGF, FGF, PDGF
- Insulin

### Notes from the source
1. Although cytoplasmic receptors are present in the cytoplasm, once the hormone–receptor complex is formed, it moves to the nucleus to initiate its actions.
2. Vitamin D is a cytoplasmic receptor. All intracellular receptors are inherently nuclear receptors, as they bind and become activated in the nucleus; however, they have two primary sites of action: cytoplasm and nucleus.
3. Receptor tyrosine kinases have intrinsic tyrosine kinase activity, whereas non-receptor tyrosine kinases have an external tyrosine kinase component that interacts with the receptor as required.
