# Plateau Potential and Pacemaker Potential

## 1. Plateau Potential

### Where it occurs
- Ventricular muscle cells  
- Atrial muscle cells  
- Purkinje fibers  

These cells show a **prolonged depolarization phase called the plateau**.

---

# Phases of Ventricular Action Potential

The ventricular myocyte action potential has **5 phases (0–4)**.

---

## Phase 4 – Resting Membrane Potential

- Approximately **–90 mV**
- Maintained by:
  - High **K⁺ permeability**
  - **Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase**
  - **Inward rectifier K⁺ channels**

---

## Phase 0 – Rapid Depolarization

Triggered when neighboring cells depolarize.

### Mechanism
- **Fast voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open**
- Massive **Na⁺ influx**

### Membrane potential change
- –90 mV → **+20 mV**

---

## Phase 1 – Initial Repolarization

A short transitional phase.

### Mechanism
- **Na⁺ channels inactivate**
- **Transient outward K⁺ current**

Membrane potential decreases slightly.

---

## Phase 2 – Plateau Phase

Membrane potential remains around **0 mV for ~200–250 ms**.

### Mechanism: balance between two currents

#### 1. Ca²⁺ influx
- Through **L-type Ca²⁺ channels**
- Opens around **–40 mV**
- Ca²⁺ enters slowly and maintains depolarization
- **IN HYPERCALCEMIA:** Calcium enters cell very rapidly and thus rises intracellular calcium levels to rise very rapidly and to higher levels. The intracellular calcium binds to Calmodulin which then interacts with L- type calcium channels. This causes L-type Calcium channels to close and thus Plateau phase is shortened in hypercalcemia

#### 2. K⁺ efflux
- Through **delayed rectifier K⁺ channels**

The **balance between Ca²⁺ entry and K⁺ exit** produces the plateau.

---

## Phase 3 – Repolarization

### Mechanism
- **Ca²⁺ channels close**
- **K⁺ efflux increases**

Membrane potential returns to **–90 mV**.

---

# Importance of Plateau Potential

### 1. Prolongs cardiac contraction
Plateau lasts **200–300 ms**, producing a long contraction.

This prevents **tetany**.

Skeletal muscle:
- Action potential ≈ **2 ms**
- Tetany possible

Cardiac muscle:
- Action potential ≈ **250 ms**
- Tetany **not possible**

---

### 2. Produces a long refractory period

Absolute refractory period ≈ **200–250 ms**

Therefore the heart cannot be stimulated again until relaxation begins.

This ensures effective pumping.

---

## Why Plateau Occurs in Cardiac Muscle

1. **Slow L-type Ca²⁺ channels remain open longer**
2. **Reduced K⁺ permeability during early depolarization**
3. **Delayed opening of K⁺ channels**

These mechanisms maintain the plateau phase.
