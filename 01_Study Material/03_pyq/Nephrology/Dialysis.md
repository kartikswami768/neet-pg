## 1. Types of Dialysis
- **Hemodialysis (HD)**
- **Peritoneal Dialysis (PD)**
  - Continuous Ambulated PD (more commonly asked)
  - Automated PD

---

## 2. Indications for Dialysis (AEIOU)
- **A**cidosis – refractory metabolic acidosis  
- **E**lectrolyte imbalance – **Hyperkalemia** (most common exam answer)  
- **I**ntoxication – lithium, methanol, ethylene glycol  
- **O**verload – pulmonary edema not responding to diuretics  
- **U**remia – encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding  

---

## 3. Vascular Access (Very Important)
**Order of preference:**
1. **AV fistula** (BEST)
2. AV graft
3. Tunneled catheter
4. Non-tunneled catheter (emergency)

**AV fistula facts:**
- Most common site: **Radio-cephalic (wrist)**
- Maturation time: **6–8 weeks**
- Complication: **High-output cardiac failure**

---

## 4. Dialysis Clearance
- **Small molecules**: urea, creatinine → easily removed
- **Middle molecules**: β2-microglobulin → poor clearance  
  - Leads to **dialysis-related amyloidosis**

---

## 5. Complications of Hemodialysis
### Acute
- **Hypotension** (most common)
- Muscle cramps
- Disequilibrium syndrome

### Chronic
- Amyloidosis
- Anemia
- Secondary hyperparathyroidism
>Anemia is caused by CKD. But Blood and iron loss due to Dialysis worsens it.
>Secondary Hyperthyroidism has same story. It is caused by CKD. But Dialysis can’t remove phosphate adequately. So, PTH remains high despite Dialysis.

---

## 6. Dialysis Disequilibrium Syndrome
- Cause: rapid urea removal → **cerebral edema**
- Symptoms: headache, nausea, seizures
- Prevention: **slow dialysis**, mannitol

---

## 7. Peritoneal Dialysis (Continuous Ambulated PD)
**Advantages**
- Home-based
- Better BP control
- No vascular access

**Complication**
- **Peritonitis**
  - Most common organism: **Staph epidermidis**
  - Cloudy dialysate = diagnostic clue

---

## 8. Dialysate Composition
- **No / very low potassium** (so potassium diffuses out of blood)
- Contains:
  - Sodium
  - Calcium
  - Magnesium
  - Chloride
  - Bicarbonate / acetate (buffer)
  - Glucose (for ultrafiltration)

> These ions are not freely excreted and don’t need to be freely excreted. So they are mostly present at plasma levels.
> Water is removed using ultrafiltration. Pressure and Glucose are used for this purpose. Glucose creates an osmotic gradient to pull water out.
> Bicarbonate and acetate are buffer and added so that they diffuse into blood and helps in handling metabolic acidosis.
---

## 9. Contraindications
**Hemodialysis**
- Severe hemodynamic instability → prefer **CRRT**

**Peritoneal Dialysis**
- Extensive abdominal adhesions
- Recent abdominal surgery

---

## 10. CRRT (Continuous Renal Replacement Therapy)
- Used in **ICU / shock patients**
- Continuous, slow removal
- Less hypotension

---

## 11. Transplant vs Dialysis
- **Renal transplant** → best survival
- Dialysis → bridge or alternative
