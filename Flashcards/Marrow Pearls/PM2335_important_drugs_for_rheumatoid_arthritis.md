# Important drugs for rheumatoid arthritis

### 1) NSAIDs
Analgesic and anti-inflammatory; used as adjunctive therapy for management of uncontrollable symptoms and in acute flares.

### 2) Glucocorticoids
Used in acute flare for rapid control and as initial therapy before onset of action of DMARDs. Chronic administration of low doses is needed in patients with inadequate response to DMARDs.
- Prednisolone — oral
- Triamcinolone — intraarticular

### 3) DMARDs (disease-modifying anti-rheumatoid drugs)

#### Conventional DMARDs
- **Methotrexate (MTX):** antifolate drug; DMARD of choice and anchor drug for combination therapy. Regular LFT monitoring is required.
- **Hydroxychloroquine:** monotherapy for early and mild disease and in combination with other DMARDs for severe cases.
- **Sulfasalazine:** salicylate; reduces radiographic progression.
- **Leflunomide:** pyrimidine synthase inhibitor used as an alternative to MTX.

#### Biologicals
- **TNF-α antagonists:** used in combination with MTX; risk of serious bacterial infections, opportunistic fungal infection and reactivation of latent TB. Drugs listed: adalimumab, golimumab, etanercept, infliximab, certolizumab.
- **Abatacept:** T-cell costimulation inhibitor, used with MTX, leflunomide, etc.
- **Rituximab:** anti-CD20 antibody; used in refractory disease in combination with MTX.
- **Anakinra:** IL-1 receptor antagonist; should not be combined with anti-TNF alpha.
- **IL-6 receptor antagonists:** tocilizumab and sarilumab; used as monotherapy or combination therapy in moderate to severe disease.

#### Small molecules
- **Janus kinase inhibitors:** tofacitinib, baricitinib and upadacitinib; used as monotherapy or combination therapy.
