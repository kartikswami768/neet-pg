# Endocrine System — 100 Flashcards

These cards are derived from the cleaned endocrine pharmacology notes and emphasize recurrent exam patterns: mechanism, drug of choice, site of action, adverse effects, contraindications, comparisons, and clinical vignettes.

#flashcards/Pharmacology/Endocrine-System

### ENDO-001 — Diabetes · Type 1 DM · drug-use · target: insulin replacement · difficulty: 1
What is the essential pharmacologic replacement in type 1 diabetes mellitus?::Insulin; oral glucose-lowering drugs and pramlintide cannot replace the required insulin.

### ENDO-002 — Diabetes · Type 2 DM · first-line concept · target: hepatic glucose output · difficulty: 1
What is the classic exam answer for initial drug therapy of uncomplicated type 2 diabetes mellitus?::Metformin, unless contraindicated or a comorbidity-directed therapy is preferred.

### ENDO-003 — Diabetes · Severe hyperglycemia · indication · target: insulin · difficulty: 1
When should insulin be considered promptly in type 2 diabetes because of very high glycemia?::With symptoms of hyperglycemia or very high glycemia such as A1C >10% or blood glucose ≥300 mg/dL, or in hyperglycemic crisis.

### ENDO-004 — Diabetes · Cardiorenal disease · clinical use · target: GLP-1/SGLT2 · difficulty: 2
In type 2 diabetes with ASCVD, heart failure or CKD, what treatment principle is highly testable?::An SGLT2 inhibitor and/or a GLP-1 receptor agonist with demonstrated outcome benefit may be used irrespective of A1C and with or without metformin.

### ENDO-005 — Diabetes · Gestational diabetes · drug of choice · target: insulin · difficulty: 1
What is the preferred pharmacologic agent for gestational diabetes mellitus?::Insulin.

### ENDO-006 — Insulin · Rapid acting · classification · target: prandial glucose · difficulty: 1
Which insulin analogues are rapid/ultra-short acting?::Lispro, aspart and glulisine.

### ENDO-007 — Insulin · Regular insulin · emergency use · target: IV insulin · difficulty: 1
Which insulin is classically given IV in diabetic ketoacidosis and for potassium shifting?::Regular insulin.

### ENDO-008 — Insulin · Basal-bolus · comparison · target: hepatic glucose output · difficulty: 1
What is the basic distinction between bolus and basal insulin?::Bolus insulin controls meal-related/postprandial glucose, whereas basal insulin suppresses hepatic glucose output and fasting/intermeal hyperglycemia.

### ENDO-009 — Insulin · Long acting · classification · target: basal insulin · difficulty: 1
Which three long-acting insulin preparations are high-yield?::Glargine, detemir and degludec.

### ENDO-010 — Insulin · Peakless profile · comparison · target: pharmacokinetics · difficulty: 1
Which basal insulin preparations have a relatively flat/peakless profile compared with NPH?::Glargine and degludec are classic flat-profile basal insulins.

### ENDO-011 — Insulin · Afrezza · respiratory contraindication · target: lung · difficulty: 2
What is Afrezza, and which respiratory conditions contraindicate it?::Afrezza is inhaled human insulin powder; it is contraindicated in chronic lung disease such as asthma or COPD because of bronchospasm risk.

### ENDO-012 — Insulin · Glargine · formulation · target: pH · difficulty: 1
Which insulin is formulated as an acidic solution and forms a prolonged depot after subcutaneous injection?::Insulin glargine.

### ENDO-013 — Insulin · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: glucose · difficulty: 1
What is the most important acute adverse effect of insulin therapy?::Hypoglycemia.

### ENDO-014 — Insulin · Injection site · adverse effect · target: adipose tissue · difficulty: 1
What local complication can result from repeatedly injecting insulin at the same site, and how is it prevented?::Lipohypertrophy/lipoatrophy; prevent it by rotating injection sites.

### ENDO-015 — Sulfonylureas · Mechanism · target: KATP channel · difficulty: 1
How do sulfonylureas stimulate insulin secretion?::They close ATP-sensitive K+ channels, causing β-cell depolarization, opening voltage-gated Ca2+ channels and increasing insulin exocytosis.

### ENDO-016 — Sulfonylureas · Adverse effects · safety · target: insulin secretion · difficulty: 1
What are the classic adverse effects of sulfonylureas?::Hypoglycemia and weight gain.

### ENDO-017 — Sulfonylureas · Chlorpropamide · classic association · target: ADH/alcohol metabolism · difficulty: 1
Which sulfonylurea is classically associated with SIADH/hyponatremia and a disulfiram-like reaction?::Chlorpropamide.

### ENDO-018 — Meglitinides · Comparison · mealtime control · target: KATP channel · difficulty: 1
Which meglitinides are important, and what is their practical advantage?::Repaglinide and nateglinide; they are short-acting secretagogues suited to mealtime glucose control.

### ENDO-019 — GLP-1 receptor agonists · Mechanism · target: GLP-1 receptor · difficulty: 1
What are the major glucose-lowering and weight effects of GLP-1 receptor agonists?::They increase glucose-dependent insulin, decrease glucagon, slow gastric emptying and reduce appetite, leading to weight loss.

### ENDO-020 — GLP-1 receptor agonists · Drugs · classification · target: GLP-1 receptor · difficulty: 1
Name four important GLP-1 receptor agonists.::Exenatide, liraglutide, dulaglutide and semaglutide.

### ENDO-021 — Incretin therapies · Combination · comparison · target: GLP-1/DPP-4 · difficulty: 1
Should a GLP-1 receptor agonist routinely be combined with a DPP-4 inhibitor?::No. The combination is not recommended because it provides little or no meaningful additional glucose-lowering benefit.

### ENDO-022 — DPP-4 inhibitors · Mechanism · target: DPP-4 enzyme · difficulty: 1
How do DPP-4 inhibitors lower glucose?::They inhibit incretin degradation, increasing endogenous GLP-1 activity and thereby increasing glucose-dependent insulin and decreasing glucagon.

### ENDO-023 — DPP-4 inhibitors · Renal dosing · comparison · target: kidney · difficulty: 1
Which DPP-4 inhibitor is classically remembered as requiring no renal dose adjustment?::Linagliptin.

### ENDO-024 — DPP-4 inhibitors · Heart failure · safety · target: cardiac risk · difficulty: 2
Which DPP-4 inhibitors have particular heart-failure safety concerns?::Saxagliptin and alogliptin.

### ENDO-025 — Metformin · Mechanism · target: liver/AMPK · difficulty: 1
What is the main glucose-lowering action of metformin?::It reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis and hepatic glucose output, with effects related to AMPK signaling and improved insulin sensitivity.

### ENDO-026 — Metformin · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: GI/B12/lactate · difficulty: 1
What are the classic adverse-effect associations of metformin?::GI intolerance/diarrhea, vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use and rare lactic acidosis.

### ENDO-027 — Metformin · Renal impairment · contraindication · target: eGFR · difficulty: 1
At what eGFR is metformin contraindicated?::eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m².

### ENDO-028 — Pioglitazone · Mechanism · target: PPAR-γ · difficulty: 1
Which receptor does pioglitazone activate?::PPAR-γ, increasing insulin sensitivity in adipose and other insulin-sensitive tissues.

### ENDO-029 — Pioglitazone · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: fluid balance · difficulty: 1
What major adverse effects make pioglitazone an exam favorite?::Fluid retention/edema, weight gain and potential worsening of heart failure; bladder-cancer risk is also a labeling concern.

### ENDO-030 — Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors · Hypoglycemia treatment · target: intestinal enzymes · difficulty: 1
Why should hypoglycemia in a patient taking acarbose be treated with glucose/dextrose rather than sucrose?::Acarbose inhibits sucrose breakdown, so glucose/dextrose bypasses the inhibited digestive step.

### ENDO-031 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Site/mechanism · target: PCT/SGLT2 · difficulty: 1
Where do SGLT2 inhibitors act and what happens to urinary glucose?::They act in the proximal convoluted tubule, inhibit sodium-glucose reabsorption and increase glycosuria.

### ENDO-032 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: GU/ketones/volume · difficulty: 1
What adverse effects are classically associated with SGLT2 inhibitors?::Genital mycotic infection, GU infection, volume depletion, euglycemic/atypical DKA and rare Fournier gangrene.

### ENDO-033 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Cardiorenal use · clinical benefit · target: heart/kidney · difficulty: 1
Why are SGLT2 inhibitors important beyond glucose lowering?::They reduce cardiovascular and kidney outcomes in appropriate patients with heart failure and/or CKD.

### ENDO-034 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Perioperative safety · mechanism · target: ketogenesis · difficulty: 2
Why are SGLT2 inhibitors held around surgery and during prolonged fasting/severe illness?::Because these settings increase the risk of euglycemic or atypical diabetic ketoacidosis.

### ENDO-035 — Pramlintide · Mechanism · amylin analogue · target: glucagon/gastric emptying · difficulty: 1
What are the major actions of pramlintide?::It decreases glucagon, slows gastric emptying and increases satiety; it is an adjunct to mealtime insulin.

### ENDO-036 — Anti-obesity drugs · Tirzepatide · mechanism · target: GIP/GLP-1 receptors · difficulty: 1
Which drug in the notes is a dual GIP + GLP-1 receptor agonist?::Tirzepatide.

### ENDO-037 — Diabetic complications · Gastroparesis · drug-use · target: motilin receptor · difficulty: 1
Which drug can stimulate gastric motility through the motilin receptor in diabetic gastroparesis?::Erythromycin.

### ENDO-038 — Diabetic neuropathy · Epalrestat · mechanism · target: aldose reductase · difficulty: 1
What enzyme does epalrestat inhibit?::Aldose reductase, reducing glucose conversion to sorbitol.

### ENDO-039 — Hyperkalemia · Cardiac protection · drug-use · target: cardiac membrane · difficulty: 1
What is the role of calcium gluconate in hyperkalemia?::It stabilizes the cardiac membrane; it does not lower the serum potassium concentration.

### ENDO-040 — Hyperkalemia · Intracellular shift · mechanism · target: Na+/K+-ATPase · difficulty: 1
How does insulin lower serum potassium in hyperkalemia?::It shifts potassium into cells, with glucose given as needed to prevent hypoglycemia.

### ENDO-041 — Thyroid drugs · NIS inhibitors · mechanism · target: sodium-iodide symporter · difficulty: 1
Which drugs are classic inhibitors of iodide transport through the sodium-iodide symporter?::Perchlorate and thiocyanate; pertechnetate is an NIS-handling analogue used in nuclear medicine.

### ENDO-042 — Thionamides · Mechanism · target: TPO · difficulty: 1
Which thyroid-hormone synthesis steps are inhibited by thionamides?::Iodide oxidation, organification and coupling through inhibition of thyroid peroxidase.

### ENDO-043 — Carbimazole · Prodrug · comparison · target: methimazole · difficulty: 1
Which common antithyroid drug is a prodrug of methimazole?::Carbimazole.

### ENDO-044 — PTU · Peripheral conversion · mechanism · target: T4→T3 · difficulty: 1
Which thionamide also inhibits peripheral conversion of T4 to T3?::Propylthiouracil (PTU).

### ENDO-045 — Thionamides · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: marrow/liver · difficulty: 1
What two serious adverse effects are classically associated with thionamides?::Agranulocytosis and hepatotoxicity, with severe hepatotoxicity particularly important for PTU.

### ENDO-046 — Hyperthyroidism in pregnancy · First trimester · drug of choice · target: thyroid synthesis · difficulty: 1
Which thionamide is generally preferred in early pregnancy when treatment is required?::PTU, commonly through about week 16.

### ENDO-047 — Iodides · Acute thyroid effect · mechanism · target: hormone release · difficulty: 1
What is the rapid effect of high-dose iodide on the thyroid?::It acutely suppresses thyroid hormone release and organification through the Wolff–Chaikoff effect.

### ENDO-048 — Thyroid storm · Sequence · emergency treatment · target: adrenergic/synthesis/release · difficulty: 2
What is the high-yield pharmacologic sequence for thyroid storm?::β-blocker → thionamide → iodide after the thionamide → glucocorticoid.

### ENDO-049 — Radioiodine · I-131 · mechanism · target: thyroid tissue · difficulty: 1
How does I-131 treat hyperthyroidism?::It is concentrated by thyroid tissue and emits radiation that destroys thyroid follicular cells.

### ENDO-050 — Radioiodine · Contraindication · safety · target: pregnancy · difficulty: 1
What is the key reproductive contraindication to radioactive iodine therapy?::Pregnancy; breastfeeding also requires avoidance/appropriate interruption according to nuclear-medicine guidance.

### ENDO-051 — Thyroid hormones · Levothyroxine · drug-use · target: T4 replacement · difficulty: 1
What is the standard chronic thyroid-hormone replacement drug?::Levothyroxine, synthetic T4.

### ENDO-052 — Myxedema coma · Hormone replacement · emergency · target: T4/T3 · difficulty: 1
Which thyroid hormone preparations can be used IV in myxedema coma?::IV levothyroxine is standard; liothyronine can be added in selected protocols.

### ENDO-053 — Thyroid eye disease · Teprotumumab · mechanism · target: IGF-1R · difficulty: 1
What is the target and indication of teprotumumab?::It inhibits IGF-1 receptor signaling and is used for thyroid eye disease.

### ENDO-054 — Medullary thyroid carcinoma · Vandetanib · mechanism · target: tyrosine kinases/RET · difficulty: 1
Which drug is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor used for advanced medullary thyroid carcinoma?::Vandetanib.

### ENDO-055 — Bisphosphonates · Mechanism · target: FPPS/mevalonate pathway · difficulty: 1
How do nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates inhibit osteoclasts?::They inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase in the mevalonate pathway, impairing osteoclast function and promoting osteoclast death.

### ENDO-056 — Bisphosphonates · Uses · mnemonic · target: bone resorption · difficulty: 1
What three major uses can be recalled by HOP?::Hypercalcemia, osteoporosis and Paget disease.

### ENDO-057 — Bisphosphonates · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: bone/kidney/esophagus · difficulty: 1
What are the classic serious adverse effects of bisphosphonates?::Renal toxicity, osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical/subtrochanteric femoral fracture and esophagitis.

### ENDO-058 — Zoledronic acid · Route · comparison · target: IV infusion · difficulty: 1
What is the correct route for zoledronic acid in osteoporosis therapy?::Intravenous infusion, not intramuscular injection.

### ENDO-059 — Teriparatide · Mechanism · target: PTH receptor/osteoblast · difficulty: 1
Why does intermittent teriparatide stimulate bone formation?::Intermittent PTH receptor stimulation preferentially increases osteoblast activity and bone formation; sustained PTH excess favors bone resorption.

### ENDO-060 — Teriparatide · Clinical role · safety · target: bone formation · difficulty: 2
For which osteoporosis patients is teriparatide especially useful?::Patients at very high fracture risk; avoid/caution in conditions with increased baseline osteosarcoma risk or hypercalcemia.

### ENDO-061 — Denosumab · Mechanism · target: RANKL · difficulty: 1
What is the molecular target of denosumab?::RANK ligand (RANKL).

### ENDO-062 — Denosumab · CKD · safety · target: calcium homeostasis · difficulty: 2
Why can denosumab be used when renal function limits bisphosphonate choices, yet still requires caution in advanced CKD?::It is not renally cleared like bisphosphonates, but advanced CKD increases the risk of severe hypocalcemia.

### ENDO-063 — Denosumab · Discontinuation · rebound · target: bone turnover · difficulty: 2
What can happen if denosumab is stopped or delayed without follow-up antiresorptive therapy?::Rebound bone turnover can cause rapid bone loss and increased vertebral-fracture risk.

### ENDO-064 — Cinacalcet · Mechanism · target: CaSR · difficulty: 1
How does cinacalcet reduce PTH secretion?::It activates the calcium-sensing receptor on parathyroid cells, increasing calcium sensitivity and reducing PTH secretion.

### ENDO-065 — Romosozumab · Mechanism · target: sclerostin · difficulty: 1
What is the molecular target of romosozumab?::Sclerostin.

### ENDO-066 — Burosumab · Mechanism/indication · target: FGF23 · difficulty: 1
What is the classic disease association of burosumab?::X-linked hypophosphatemia and related approved FGF23-mediated hypophosphatemic disorders, not routine osteoporosis.

### ENDO-067 — Hypercalcemia · Initial treatment · target: volume status · difficulty: 1
What is the core initial pharmacologic intervention in significant hypercalcemia?::Isotonic saline volume repletion.

### ENDO-068 — Hypercalcemia · Diuretics · comparison · target: renal calcium · difficulty: 1
Why are thiazide diuretics generally avoided in hypercalcemia?::They increase renal calcium reabsorption and can worsen hypercalcemia.

### ENDO-069 — Hypercalcemia of malignancy · Rapid effect · target: calcitonin · difficulty: 2
Which drug can rapidly lower calcium for short-term control in severe hypercalcemia?::Calcitonin; its use is limited by tachyphylaxis.

### ENDO-070 — Hyperphosphatemia · Drug of choice · target: intestinal phosphate · difficulty: 1
What is the classic drug of choice for hyperphosphatemia in the notes?::Sevelamer.

### ENDO-071 — GH axis · Physiology · sequence · target: GHRH/GH/IGF-1 · difficulty: 1
What is the classic GH–IGF-1 sequence?::Hypothalamic GHRH → anterior pituitary GH → liver IGF-1 → growth-promoting effects.

### ENDO-072 — Somatropin · Indication · timing · target: GH receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the classic indication for somatropin in a child, and when is it effective for increasing linear height?::Growth hormone deficiency; it must be given before epiphyseal closure for linear growth.

### ENDO-073 — Somatropin · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: GH/IGF-1 · difficulty: 1
What adverse effects can occur with pharmacologic growth hormone?::Edema/arthralgia, carpal tunnel syndrome, hyperglycemia and, in children, growth-plate-related effects.

### ENDO-074 — Mecasermin · Mechanism/indication · target: IGF-1 · difficulty: 1
What is mecasermin used for?::Severe primary IGF-1 deficiency; it provides recombinant IGF-1 downstream of the GH receptor.

### ENDO-075 — Pituitary inhibitory hormones · Comparison · target: GH/prolactin · difficulty: 1
Which hypothalamic hormones inhibit GH and prolactin, respectively?::Somatostatin inhibits GH; dopamine inhibits prolactin.

### ENDO-076 — Acromegaly · Treatment · clinical use · target: pituitary adenoma/GH · difficulty: 1
What is an important first-line treatment for a surgically resectable GH-secreting pituitary adenoma?::Transsphenoidal surgery.

### ENDO-077 — Acromegaly · Somatostatin analogues · drug-use · target: SSTR · difficulty: 1
Which somatostatin analogues are major pharmacologic treatments for acromegaly?::Octreotide and lanreotide; pasireotide is used in selected patients.

### ENDO-078 — Acromegaly · Pegvisomant · mechanism · target: GH receptor · difficulty: 1
How does pegvisomant work in acromegaly?::It antagonizes the GH receptor and reduces IGF-1 production.

### ENDO-079 — Prolactinoma · Drug of choice · target: D2 receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the major drug of choice for prolactinoma?::Cabergoline.

### ENDO-080 — Prolactinoma · Pregnancy · comparison · target: D2 receptor · difficulty: 1
Which dopamine agonist has the classic pregnancy-associated role in prolactinoma?::Bromocriptine.

### ENDO-081 — Dopamine agonists · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: vascular/valvular system · difficulty: 2
What adverse effects are classically associated with dopamine agonists used for prolactinoma?::Nausea, orthostatic hypotension, headache and neuropsychiatric effects; high cumulative cabergoline exposure is associated with valvular heart-disease concern.

### ENDO-082 — GnRH · Physiology · comparison · target: pituitary GnRH receptor · difficulty: 1
What happens with pulsatile versus continuous GnRH exposure?::Pulsatile GnRH stimulates FSH/LH release; continuous GnRH initially stimulates but then causes receptor downregulation and suppresses FSH/LH.

### ENDO-083 — GnRH agonists · Flare phenomenon · mechanism · target: GnRH receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the key difference between continuous GnRH agonist administration and GnRH antagonist administration?::Continuous agonists cause an initial LH/FSH and sex-steroid flare followed by suppression; antagonists suppress gonadotropins immediately without an initial flare.

### ENDO-084 — GnRH agonists · Clinical uses · drug-use · target: gonadotropins · difficulty: 1
Name important clinical uses of continuous GnRH agonists.::Prostate cancer, selected hormone-responsive breast cancer, endometriosis and central precocious puberty.

### ENDO-085 — GnRH antagonists · Assisted reproduction · drug-use · target: GnRH receptor · difficulty: 1
Which GnRH antagonists are used in assisted-reproduction protocols to prevent premature LH surges?::Cetrorelix and ganirelix.

### ENDO-086 — GnRH antagonists · Prostate cancer · comparison · target: androgen deprivation · difficulty: 1
Which GnRH antagonist is a high-yield drug for androgen-deprivation therapy in prostate cancer?::Degarelix.

### ENDO-087 — Contraception · Combined OCP · mechanism · target: FSH/LH · difficulty: 1
How do combined oral contraceptives primarily prevent pregnancy?::They suppress hypothalamic-pituitary gonadotropin signaling, preventing the LH surge and ovulation; they also thicken cervical mucus.

### ENDO-088 — Emergency contraception · Ulipristal · dose/timing · target: progesterone receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the dose and time window for ulipristal as emergency contraception?::30 mg within 120 hours (5 days) after unprotected intercourse.

### ENDO-089 — Emergency contraception · Mechanism · comparison · target: ovulation · difficulty: 1
What is the primary modern mechanism of oral emergency contraceptive pills?::They delay or prevent ovulation; preventing embryo implantation is not the primary mechanism used in current teaching.

### ENDO-090 — Contraceptive adverse effects · Estrogen-containing OCPs · safety · target: coagulation/cardio · difficulty: 1
What is the major serious adverse-effect concern with estrogen-containing combined hormonal contraception?::Venous thromboembolism, with arterial events such as stroke/MI becoming important in selected high-risk patients.

### ENDO-091 — Corticosteroids · Classification · comparison · target: glucocorticoid/mineralocorticoid activity · difficulty: 1
Which glucocorticoid is the classic short-acting agent with appreciable mineralocorticoid activity, and which are long-acting with minimal mineralocorticoid effect?::Hydrocortisone has substantial mineralocorticoid activity; dexamethasone and betamethasone are long-acting with minimal clinically significant mineralocorticoid effect.

### ENDO-092 — Adrenal insufficiency · Replacement · comparison · target: glucocorticoid/mineralocorticoid · difficulty: 1
Why is hydrocortisone useful in primary adrenal insufficiency, and what additional drug may be required chronically?::Hydrocortisone provides both glucocorticoid and some mineralocorticoid activity; fludrocortisone is often added for mineralocorticoid replacement.

### ENDO-093 — Fetal lung maturation · Corticosteroids · drug-use · target: surfactant · difficulty: 1
Which glucocorticoids are classically used to accelerate fetal lung maturation?::Betamethasone or dexamethasone given IM to patients at risk of preterm birth.

### ENDO-094 — Glucocorticoid adverse effects · Ocular · target: lens/IOP · difficulty: 1
Which cataract is classically associated with chronic systemic glucocorticoid therapy?::Posterior subcapsular cataract.

### ENDO-095 — Cushing syndrome · Metyrapone · mechanism · target: 11β-hydroxylase · difficulty: 1
Which steroidogenic enzyme is classically inhibited by metyrapone?::11β-hydroxylase.

### ENDO-096 — Cushing syndrome · Mitotane · mechanism · target: adrenal cortex · difficulty: 1
Why is mitotane sometimes described as producing a “medical adrenalectomy”?::It is adrenolytic and suppresses adrenal steroidogenesis, causing functional destruction/inhibition of adrenal cortical tissue.

### ENDO-097 — Cushing syndrome · Mifepristone · mechanism · target: glucocorticoid receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the key pharmacologic action of mifepristone in Cushing syndrome?::It blocks the glucocorticoid receptor, making it especially useful when hyperglycemia/glucose intolerance is a major clinical problem.

### ENDO-098 — Cushing syndrome · Steroidogenesis inhibitors · comparison · target: adrenal CYP enzymes · difficulty: 2
Which drugs from the notes inhibit adrenal steroidogenesis and may be used in severe hypercortisolism?::Metyrapone, ketoconazole, etomidate and mitotane; osilodrostat is another modern steroidogenesis inhibitor.

### ENDO-099 — Reproductive endocrinology · Tamoxifen/raloxifene · comparison · target: estrogen receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the classic pharmacologic distinction between tamoxifen and raloxifene?::Tamoxifen is anti-estrogenic in breast but has estrogenic effects in bone and endometrium; raloxifene is anti-estrogenic in breast and endometrium and is used in postmenopausal osteoporosis.

### ENDO-100 — Reproductive endocrinology · Integrated comparison · target: ER/aromatase/androgen pathway · difficulty: 2
How do fulvestrant, aromatase inhibitors, finasteride/dutasteride and androgen-receptor antagonists differ mechanistically?::Fulvestrant degrades/downregulates the estrogen receptor; aromatase inhibitors reduce androgen-to-estrogen conversion; finasteride/dutasteride inhibit 5α-reductase and lower DHT; flutamide/bicalutamide/enzalutamide directly antagonize androgen-receptor signaling.
