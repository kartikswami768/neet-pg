# Renal — Embryology

## Kidney embryology

### Pronephros
- **Week 4** of development; then degenerates.

### Mesonephros
- **Week 4** of development.
- Functions as an interim kidney for the **1st trimester** in both sexes.
- Mesonephric ducts persist in the male genital system as the **Wolffian duct**, forming the ductus deferens and epididymis.
- Mesonephric ducts degenerate in females.

### Metanephros
- **Permanent kidney**.
- First appears in **week 5** of development.
- Nephrogenesis is normally completed by **week 36 of gestation**.

#### Ureteric bud (metanephric diverticulum)
- Fully canalized by **week 10** of development.
- Derived from the **mesonephric duct**.
- Forms the **ureters, renal pelvises, calyces, and collecting ducts**.

#### Metanephric mesenchyme (metanephric blastema)
- Ureteric bud interacts with this tissue to induce differentiation.
- Forms the **glomerulus through distal convoluted tubule (DCT)**.

> **Developmental principle:** Aberrant interaction between the ureteric bud and metanephric mesenchyme can result in congenital renal malformations such as **renal agenesis** and **multicystic dysplastic kidney**.

### Ureteropelvic junction
The last part of the ureter to canalize. Failure of complete canalization can produce congenital obstruction, which may be unilateral or bilateral.

- Most common pathologic cause of **prenatal hydronephrosis**.
- Detected by **prenatal ultrasound**.

### Potter sequence
**Oligohydramnios → compression of the developing fetus → characteristic fetal deformities.**

- **Limb deformities:** eg, club feet
- **Facial anomalies:** eg, low-set ears, retrognathia, flattened nose
- Compression of the chest and lack of amniotic-fluid aspiration into fetal lungs → **pulmonary hypoplasia**, the major cause of death

**Causes include:**
- Chronic placental insufficiency
- Reduced fetal urine output, including:
 - Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD)
 - Obstructive uropathy (eg, posterior urethral valves)
 - Bilateral renal agenesis
- Preterm premature rupture of membranes
- Maternal ACE inhibitor use

> **Mnemonic:** Babies who can’t “**Pee**” in utero develop Potter sequence.

### POTTER sequence associated with
- **P**ulmonary hypoplasia
- **O**ligohydramnios (trigger)
- **T**wisted face
- **T**wisted skin
- **E**xtremity defects
- **R**enal failure (in utero)

## Congenital renal anomalies

### Horseshoe kidney
Inferior poles of both kidneys fuse abnormally. During ascent from the pelvis, the fused kidney is trapped beneath the **inferior mesenteric artery** and remains low in the abdomen.

- Kidneys can function normally.
- Associated with **hydronephrosis** (eg, ureteropelvic junction obstruction), renal stones, infection, and increased risk of renal cancer.
- Higher incidence in chromosomal aneuploidy, including **Turner syndrome** and **trisomies 13, 18, and 21**.

### Congenital solitary functioning kidney
Born with only one functioning kidney.

- Majority are asymptomatic because of **compensatory hypertrophy** of the contralateral kidney.
- Anomalies in the contralateral kidney are common.
- Often diagnosed prenatally by ultrasound; bilateral agenesis or dysplasia can lead to **Potter sequence**.

### Unilateral renal agenesis
- Ureteric bud fails to develop and induce differentiation of metanephric mesenchyme.
- Results in **complete absence of kidney and ureter**.

### Multicystic dysplastic kidney
- Ureteric bud develops, but fails to induce differentiation of metanephric mesenchyme.
- Produces a **nonfunctional kidney composed of cysts and connective tissue**.
- Nongenetic inheritance, although the condition tends to run in families.
- Usually unilateral.

### Duplex collecting system
- Bifurcation of the ureteric bud before it enters the metanephric blastema creates a **Y-shaped bifid ureter**.
- Alternatively, two ureteric buds may reach and interact with the metanephric blastema.
- Strongly associated with **vesicoureteral reflux and/or ureteral obstruction**.
- Increased risk for **UTIs**.
- Frequently presents with **hydronephrosis**.

### Posterior urethral valves
- Membrane remnant in the posterior (prostatic) urethra in males.
- Persistence can produce **urethral obstruction**.
- Prenatal ultrasound may show **bilateral hydronephrosis** and a dilated or thick-walled bladder.
- Severe fetal obstruction is associated with **oligohydramnios**.
- Most common cause of **bladder outlet obstruction in male infants**.

> **Figure:** Obstruction by posterior urethral valves is illustrated with hydronephrosis, hydroureter, urinary reflux, a distended bladder, hypertrophied bladder wall, and an obstructed posterior urethral membrane.

## Vesicoureteral reflux

**Vesicoureteral reflux (VUR)** is retrograde flow of urine from the bladder toward the upper urinary tract.

- **Primary:** abnormal or insufficient insertion of the ureter within the vesical wall at the ureterovesical junction (UVJ).
- **Secondary:** abnormally high bladder pressure results in retrograde flow through the UVJ.
- Increases the risk of **recurrent UTIs**.
