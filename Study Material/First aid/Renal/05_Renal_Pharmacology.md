# Renal — Pharmacology

## Diuretics — site of action

| Nephron site                       | Drug(s) / class shown           |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Glomerulus / filtration**        | Mannitol                        |
| **Proximal convoluted tubule**     | Acetazolamide; SGLT2 inhibitors |
| **Thick ascending limb**           | Loop diuretics                  |
| **Early distal convoluted tubule** | Thiazide diuretics              |
| **Collecting tubule/duct**         | K⁺-sparing diuretics            |

![[Images/625_diuretics_site_of_action.svg]]

### Effects on electrolyte excretion
| Drug class | Na⁺ | HCO₃⁻ | K⁺ | Cl⁻ | Ca²⁺ | Mg²⁺ | H⁺ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors | ↑ | ↑↑ | ↑↑ | —/↑ | — | — | ↓ |
| Loop diuretics | ↑↑ | ↓ | ↑↑ | ↑↑ | ↑↑ | ↑↑ | ↑ |
| Thiazide diuretics | ↑ | ↑ | ↑↑ | ↑ | ↓ | ↓↓ | ↑ |
| K⁺-sparing diuretics | ↑ | — | ↓ | ↑ | ↓/— | ↓/— | ↓ |

### Effects on acid–base status
**Acidemia with carbonic anhydrase inhibition:** ↓ HCO₃⁻ reabsorption.

**K⁺-sparing diuretics:** aldosterone blockade prevents K⁺ and H⁺ secretion; hyperkalemia also drives K⁺ into cells in exchange for H⁺ leaving cells.

**Alkalemia with loops/thiazides:**
- Volume contraction → ↑ angiotensin II → ↑ Na⁺/H⁺ exchange in PCT → ↑ HCO₃⁻ reabsorption (“contraction alkalosis”)
- K⁺ loss → K⁺ leaves cells in exchange for H⁺ entering
- In low-K⁺ states, H⁺ rather than K⁺ is exchanged for Na⁺ in the cortical collecting tubule → alkalosis + **paradoxical aciduria**

## Mannitol

**Mechanism** 
Osmotic diuretic. ↑ serum osmolality → fluid shift from interstitium to intravascular space → ↑ urine flow and ↓ intracranial/intraocular pressure.

**Clinical use**
- Drug overdose
- Elevated intracranial pressure
- Elevated intraocular pressure
- Augmenting diuresis

**Adverse effects**
- Dehydration
- Hypo- or hypernatremia
- Pulmonary edema
- Contraindicated in **anuria** and **heart failure**

## Acetazolamide

**Mechanism** 
Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor. Causes self-limited NaHCO₃ diuresis, decreases total-body HCO₃⁻ stores, and alkalinizes urine.

**Clinical use**
- Glaucoma
- Metabolic alkalosis
- Altitude sickness (by offsetting respiratory alkalosis)
- Idiopathic intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor cerebri)

**Adverse effects**
- Proximal renal tubular acidosis (**type 2 RTA**)
- Paresthesias
- NH₃ toxicity
- Sulfa allergy
- Hypokalemia
- Promotes calcium-phosphate stones because they are less soluble at high urine pH

> **Mnemonic:** “Acid”azolamide causes acidosis.

## Loop diuretics

**Examples:** furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide.

**Mechanism** 
Sulfonamide loop diuretics inhibit the **Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter** in the thick ascending limb. This abolishes medullary hypertonicity and impairs urine concentration.

- Associated with increased prostaglandin activity and afferent arteriolar vasodilation; NSAIDs blunt this effect.
- **Increase Ca²⁺ excretion** — “loops lose calcium.”

**Clinical use**
- Edematous states: HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, pulmonary edema
- Hypertension
- Hypercalcemia

**Adverse effects**
- Ototoxicity
- Hypokalemia
- Hypomagnesemia
- Dehydration
- Sulfa allergy
- Metabolic alkalosis
- Interstitial nephritis
- Gout

> **Mnemonic:** **OHH DAANG**.

### Ethacrynic acid
**Mechanism:** Nonsulfonamide inhibitor of the Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter in the thick ascending limb.

**Clinical use:** Diuresis in patients allergic to sulfa drugs.

**Adverse effect:** Similar to furosemide, but **more ototoxic**.

> **Mnemonic:** “Loop earrings hurt your ears.”

![[Images/626_loop_diuretics_site_of_action.svg]]

## Thiazide diuretics

**Examples:** hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, metolazone.

**Mechanism** 
Inhibit NaCl reabsorption in the **early DCT** → ↓ diluting capacity of the nephron; **↓ Ca²⁺ excretion**.

**Clinical use**
- Hypertension
- Heart failure
- Idiopathic hypercalciuria
- Nephrogenic diabetes insipidus
- Osteoporosis
- Potentiation of loop diuretics in refractory volume overload

**Adverse effects**
- Hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis
- Hyponatremia
- Hyperglycemia
- Hyperlipidemia
- Hyperuricemia
- Hypercalcemia
- Sulfa allergy

> **Mnemonic:** “Hypergluc.”

![[Images/627_thiazide_diuretics_site_of_action.svg]]

## Potassium-sparing diuretics

**Examples:** spironolactone, eplerenone, amiloride, triamterene.

> **Mnemonic:** **SEAT**.

![[Images/627_potassium_sparing_diuretics_site_of_action.svg]]

**Mechanism**
- **Spironolactone, eplerenone:** competitive aldosterone-receptor antagonists in the cortical collecting tubule.
- **Triamterene, amiloride:** block Na⁺ channels (ENaC) at the same site.

**Clinical use**
- Hyperaldosteronism
- Heart failure
- Hepatic ascites (**spironolactone**)
- Nephrogenic DI (**amiloride**)
- Antiandrogen effect (**spironolactone**)

**Adverse effects**
- Hyperkalemia → arrhythmias
- Endocrine effects with spironolactone (eg, gynecomastia, antiandrogen effects)
- Metabolic acidosis

## ACE inhibitors

**Examples:** captopril, enalapril, lisinopril, ramipril.

**Mechanism** 
Inhibit ACE → ↓ angiotensin II → ↓ GFR by preventing efferent arteriolar constriction. Renin rises because negative feedback is lost. ACE inhibition also prevents inactivation of **bradykinin**, a potent vasodilator.

**Clinical use**
- Hypertension
- Heart failure (↓ mortality)
- Proteinuria
- Diabetic nephropathy
- Prevention of unfavorable cardiac remodeling from chronic hypertension
- In CKD (eg, diabetic nephropathy), ↓ intraglomerular pressure and slows GBM thickening

**Adverse effects**
- **Cough** (dry, nonproductive)
- **Angioedema**
- Teratogenicity / fetal renal malformations
- ↑ creatinine due to ↓ GFR
- Hyperkalemia
- Hypotension
- Caution in **bilateral renal artery stenosis** because further ↓ GFR can precipitate renal failure

> **Mnemonic:** Captopril’s **CATCHH**.

## Angiotensin II receptor blockers (ARBs)

**Examples:** losartan, candesartan, valsartan.

**Mechanism** 
Selectively block binding of angiotensin II to the **AT₁ receptor**. Effects are similar to ACE inhibitors, but ARBs **do not increase bradykinin**.

**Clinical use**
- Hypertension
- Heart failure
- Proteinuria
- CKD (eg, diabetic nephropathy) when ACE inhibitors are not tolerated (eg, cough, angioedema)

**Adverse effects**
- Hyperkalemia
- ↓ GFR
- Hypotension
- Teratogenicity

## Aliskiren

**Mechanism** 
Direct renin inhibitor; blocks conversion of angiotensinogen to angiotensin I.

> **Mnemonic:** “Aliskiren kills renin.”

**Clinical use:** Hypertension.

**Adverse effects**
- Hyperkalemia
- ↓ GFR
- Hypotension
- Angioedema
- Relatively contraindicated with ACE inhibitors or ARBs
- Contraindicated in pregnancy
