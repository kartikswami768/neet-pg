# Renal — Pathology

## Urinary casts

Presence of casts indicates that hematuria or pyuria is of **glomerular or renal tubular origin**.

- Bladder cancer or kidney stones → hematuria **without casts**.
- Acute cystitis → pyuria **without casts**.
- All casts contain a matrix composed primarily of **Tamm–Horsfall mucoprotein (uromodulin)**, secreted by renal tubular cells.

| Cast | Association |
|---|---|
| **RBC casts** | Glomerulonephritis; hypertensive emergency |
| **WBC casts** | Tubulointerstitial inflammation, acute pyelonephritis, transplant rejection |
| **Granular casts** | Acute tubular necrosis; may be “muddy brown” |
| **Fatty casts / oval fat bodies** | Nephrotic syndrome; “Maltese cross” sign |
| **Waxy casts** | End-stage renal disease / chronic kidney disease |
| **Hyaline casts** | Nonspecific; can occur with dehydration, exercise, or diuretic therapy |

## Nomenclature of glomerular disorders

| Type | Characteristic | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Focal** | <50% of glomeruli involved | Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis |
| **Diffuse** | >50% of glomeruli involved | Diffuse proliferative glomerulonephritis |
| **Proliferative** | Hypercellular glomeruli | Membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis |
| **Membranous** | Thickening of GBM | Membranous nephropathy |
| **Primary glomerular disease** | Primary kidney disease specifically impacting the glomeruli | Minimal change disease |
| **Secondary glomerular disease** | Systemic disease or disease of another organ system that also impacts glomeruli | SLE, diabetic nephropathy |

## Glomerular syndromes

| Syndrome | Etiology / mechanism | Clinical presentation | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nephritic syndrome** | Glomerular inflammation → GBM damage → RBCs enter urine → dysmorphic RBCs and hematuria | Hematuria, RBC casts, ↓ GFR, oliguria, azotemia, ↑ renin, HTN; proteinuria usually <3.5 g/day but can reach nephrotic range | Infection-associated GN, Goodpasture syndrome, IgA nephropathy, Alport syndrome, MPGN |
| **Nephrotic syndrome** | Podocyte damage → impaired charge barrier → proteinuria | Massive proteinuria >3.5 g/day, edema, hypoalbuminemia, hypercholesterolemia; fatty casts; hypercoagulability and infection risk | FSGS, minimal change disease, membranous nephropathy, amyloidosis, diabetic glomerulonephropathy |
| **Nephritic–nephrotic syndrome** | Severe GBM damage + impaired charge barrier | Nephrotic-range proteinuria with concomitant nephritic features | Diffuse proliferative GN, MPGN |

## Nephritic syndrome

| Disorder | Mechanism / setting | Light microscopy | Immunofluorescence | Electron microscopy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Infection-related GN** | Type III HSR with consumptive hypocomplementemia; children ~2–4 weeks after GAS pharyngitis/skin infection; Staphylococcus also in adults | Enlarged, hypercellular glomeruli | Granular “starry sky / lumpy-bumpy” IgG, IgM, C3 along GBM and mesangium | **Subepithelial immune-complex humps** |
| **IgA nephropathy (Berger disease)** | Occurs concurrently with respiratory/GI infection | Mesangial proliferation | IgA-based immune-complex deposits in mesangium | Mesangial immune-complex deposition |
| **Rapidly progressive (crescentic) GN** | Poor prognosis; multiple causes | Crescent formation | Goodpasture: linear IF; pauci-immune vasculitis: negative IF; PSGN/DPGN: granular IF | Depends on underlying cause; Goodpasture shows GBM breaks/necrosis/crescents |
| **Diffuse proliferative GN** | Often due to SLE (“wire lupus”) | “Wire-loop” capillaries | Granular | Subendothelial ± subepithelial/intramembranous IgG-based immune complexes, often with C3 |

### RPGN patterns shown
- **Goodpasture syndrome:** antibodies against GBM and alveolar basement membrane → hematuria + hemoptysis; **linear IF**.
- **Pauci-immune:** usually no deposits; associated with granulomatosis with polyangiitis (PR3-ANCA/c-ANCA), eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis, or microscopic polyangiitis (MPO-ANCA/p-ANCA).
- **PSGN / DPGN:** **granular IF**.

### Alport syndrome
- Type IV collagen mutation → GBM abnormalities; notes mostly **X-linked dominant**.
- Eye problems (retinopathy, anterior lenticonus), glomerulonephritis, sensorineural hearing loss.
- “Can’t see, can’t pee, can’t hear a bee.”
- Irregular thinning, thickening, and splitting of GBM.
- Initially negative / nonspecific immunofluorescence staining.
- **Basket-weave** appearance on EM due to irregular thickening and longitudinal splitting of GBM.

### Membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis (MPGN)
- **Type I:** may be secondary to HBV/HCV infection.
- **Type II:** associated with C3 nephritic factor, an IgG autoantibody that stabilizes C3 convertase → persistent complement activation → ↓ C3.
- Mesangial ingrowth → GBM splitting → **tram-track** appearance.
- Type I: subendothelial immune-complex deposits.
- Type II: intramembranous deposits (**dense deposit disease**).

## Nephrotic syndrome

| Disorder | Mechanism / associations | Light microscopy | Immunofluorescence | Electron microscopy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Minimal change disease** | Usually primary/idiopathic; may follow infection, immunization, or another immune stimulus; rarely secondary to lymphoma; more common in children | Essentially normal glomeruli | — | **Effacement of podocyte foot processes** |
| **Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS)** | Primary or secondary (eg, HIV, sickle cell disease, obesity, congenital malformations); may progress to CKD | Segmental sclerosis and hyalinosis | Often negative; may show nonspecific focal IgM, C3, C1 | Foot-process effacement |
| **Membranous nephropathy** | Primary: anti-PLA₂ receptor antibodies; secondary to NSAIDs, penicillamine, gold, HBV, HCV, syphilis, SLE, solid tumors; ↑ thromboembolism risk | Diffuse capillary and GBM thickening | Granular immune-complex deposition | **“Spike and dome”** subepithelial deposits |
| **Amyloidosis** | AL amyloid, AA amyloid, prolonged dialysis, chronic conditions | Congo red positive with **apple-green birefringence** | AL may show light chains; AA may show AA protein | Mesangial expansion by amyloid fibrils |
| **Diabetic glomerulonephropathy** | Hyperglycemia → nonenzymatic glycation → mesangial expansion → GBM thickening and ↑ permeability; hyperfiltration contributes to glomerulosclerosis | Mesangial expansion, GBM thickening, eosinophilic nodular glomerulosclerosis (**Kimmelstiel–Wilson lesions**) | Usually nonspecific / negative | Prominent GBM thickening, expanded mesangium, increased mesangial matrix, segmental podocyte effacement |

## Kidney stones

Kidney stones can lead to **hydronephrosis, pyelonephritis, and acute kidney injury**.

An obstructing stone typically produces unilateral flank tenderness, colicky pain radiating to the groin, and hematuria. Recommended encouraging fluid intake for treatment/prevention.

> **Radiolucent stones:** cystine and uric acid (“I can’t C U”).

| Stone | Precipitates with | X-ray | CT | Urine crystal | Key notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Calcium oxalate** | Hypocitraturia (usually with ↓ urine pH) | Radiopaque | Hyperdense | Envelope or dumbbell | ~80% of stones; most common stone type. Risks include ethylene glycol, vitamin C excess, hypocitraturia, malabsorption/Crohn disease. Treatment: thiazides, citrate, low-sodium diet. |
| **Calcium phosphate** | ↑ pH | Radiopaque | Hyperdense | Wedge-shaped prism | Treatment: low-sodium diet, thiazides. |
| **Struvite (ammonium magnesium phosphate)** | ↑ pH | Radiopaque | Hyperdense | Coffin-lid / sarcophagus | Urease-positive organisms (eg, Proteus, Staphylococcus saprophyticus, Klebsiella) alkalinize urine. Can form staghorn calculi. Treatment: eradicate infection + surgical removal. |
| **Uric acid** | ↓ pH | Radiolucent | Visible | Rhomboid / rosettes | ~5% of stones. Associated with hyperuricemia and high-cell-turnover states. Treatment: urine alkalinization, allopurinol. |
| **Cystine** | ↓ pH | Faintly radiopaque / moderately radiodense | — | Hexagonal | Autosomal-recessive cystinuria with defective PCT reabsorption of cystine and COLA amino acids. Sodium nitroprusside positive. Treatment: low-sodium diet, urine alkalinization, chelation if refractory. |

## Hydronephrosis and urinary incontinence

### Hydronephrosis
Dilation/distention of the renal pelvis and/or calyces.

- Usually caused by urinary tract obstruction (stones, severe BPH, congenital obstruction, locally advanced cervical cancer, ureteral injury).
- Other causes include retroperitoneal fibrosis and VUR.

![[Images/618_hydronephrosis_ultrasound.jpg]]

- Dilation occurs **proximal** to the obstruction.
- Serum creatinine rises if obstruction is bilateral or involves a solitary functioning kidney.
- Can compress and atrophy the renal cortex and medulla.

### Urinary incontinence
| Type | Mechanism | Associations | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stress** | Outlet incompetence → leakage with ↑ intra-abdominal pressure | Obesity, pregnancy, vaginal delivery, prostate surgery | Pelvic-floor strengthening/Kegel exercises, weight loss, pessaries |
| **Urgency** | Detrusor overactivity → urge to void immediately | UTI, stones, tumors, pelvic radiation | Pelvic-floor PT, bladder training, antimuscarinics (eg, oxybutynin), mirabegron/vibegron |

![[Images/618_stress_incontinence_diagram.svg]]
![[Images/618_urgency_incontinence_diagram.svg]]
![[Images/618_overflow_incontinence_diagram.svg]]

| **Overflow** | Incomplete emptying from detrusor underactivity or outlet obstruction | Urinary retention, polyuria (eg, diabetes), BPH, spinal-cord injury | Catheterization; relieve obstruction (eg, α-blockers for BPH) |

**Mixed incontinence** contains features of both stress and urgency incontinence.
## Urinary tract infection and pyelonephritis

### Acute cystitis
Inflammation of the urinary bladder.

**Presentation:** suprapubic pain, dysuria, frequency, urgency; systemic features such as high fever/chills are usually absent.

**Risk factors:** female sex, sexual intercourse, indwelling catheter, diabetes mellitus, impaired bladder emptying.

**Causes:**
- **E. coli** — most common
- **Staphylococcus saprophyticus** — sexually active young women
- Klebsiella
- Proteus mirabilis — “ammonia scent”
- Enterococcus spp.

**Labs:** leukocyte esterase positive; nitrites suggest Enterobacteriaceae. Sterile pyuria can suggest urethritis from *N. gonorrhoeae* or *C. trachomatis*.

**Treatment:** antibiotics such as TMP-SMX or nitrofurantoin.

### Acute pyelonephritis
Neutrophils infiltrate the renal interstitium, with relative sparing of glomeruli and vessels.

- Fever, flank pain / costovertebral-angle tenderness, nausea/vomiting, chills
- Most often ascending UTI; **E. coli** is most common
- May occur by hematogenous spread
- Urine contains WBCs ± **WBC casts**
- CT can show **striated parenchymal enhancement**
- Risk factors: catheter, urinary obstruction, VUR, diabetes, pregnancy
- Complications: chronic pyelonephritis, papillary necrosis, perinephric abscess, urosepsis
- Treatment: antibiotics

![[Images/619_acute_pyelonephritis_micrograph_A.jpg]]

### Chronic pyelonephritis
Usually follows recurrent or inadequately treated acute pyelonephritis in the setting of a predisposition such as VUR or chronically obstructing stones.

- **Coarse, asymmetric corticomedullary scars**
- **Blunted calyces**
- Tubules may contain eosinophilic casts resembling thyroid tissue (**thyroidization**)

![[Images/619_chronic_pyelonephritis_micrograph_B.jpg]]

## Acute kidney injury

| Feature | Prerenal azotemia | Intrinsic renal failure | Postrenal azotemia |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Etiology** | Hypovolemia; ↓ cardiac output; ↓ effective circulating volume (HF, liver failure) | Tubular/interstitial: ATN, AIN; glomerular: acute GN; vascular: vasculitis, hypertensive emergency, TTP-HUS | Stones, BPH, neoplasm, congenital anomalies |
| **Pathophysiology** | ↓ RBF → ↓ GFR → ↑ Na⁺/H₂O and urea reabsorption | In ATN, necrosis → tubular debris and backflow → ↓ GFR | Bilateral outflow obstruction (or obstruction of a solitary kidney) |
| **Urine osmolality (mOsm/kg)** | >500 | <350 | Variable |
| **Urine Na⁺ (mEq/L)** | <20 | >40 | Variable |
| **FENa** | <1% | >2% | Variable |
| **BUN/Cr** | >20 | <15 | Variable |

### Acute interstitial nephritis (AIN)
Acute tubulointerstitial inflammation, often occurring after drug exposure acting as a hapten and triggering hypersensitivity.

**Common causes:** diuretics, NSAIDs, penicillin derivatives, proton-pump inhibitors, rifampin, quinolones, sulfonamides.

Less commonly due to systemic infection or inflammatory disease (eg, Mycoplasma, Sjögren syndrome, SLE, sarcoidosis).

**Features:** pyuria (classically eosinophils), azotemia, fever, rash, hematuria, and costovertebral-angle tenderness; may be asymptomatic.

> **Mnemonic:** **DRAINS** = Diuretics, Rifampin, Antibiotics, Proton-pump inhibitors, NSAIDs, Sulfa drugs.

## Acute tubular necrosis and renal failure

### Acute tubular necrosis (ATN)
Most common cause of intrinsic AKI in hospitalized patients; many cases resolve spontaneously.

**Key finding:** granular (“muddy brown”) casts and increased FENa.

![[Images/621_acute_tubular_necrosis_granular_cast_A.jpg]]
![[Images/621_acute_tubular_necrosis_tubular_injury_micrograph_B.jpg]]

#### Three stages
1. **Inciting event**
2. **Maintenance phase:** oliguric, 1–3 weeks; risk of hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, uremia
3. **Recovery phase:** polyuric; BUN/creatinine fall; risk of hypokalemia and wasting of other electrolytes/minerals

#### Ischemic ATN
Often secondary to reduced renal blood flow. PT and thick ascending limb are particularly susceptible.

#### Nephrotoxic ATN
Causes include aminoglycosides, radiocontrast, lead, cisplatin, ethylene glycol, uric acid in tumor lysis, myoglobinuria, and hemoglobinuria. PCTs are particularly susceptible.

### Diffuse cortical necrosis
Acute generalized cortical infarction of both kidneys, likely related to vasospasm + DIC.

- Associated with obstetric catastrophes such as placental abruption
- Also associated with septic shock

### Renal papillary necrosis
Sloughing of necrotic renal papillae → gross hematuria.

Associated with:
- **S**ickle cell disease/trait
- **A**cute pyelonephritis
- **A**nalgesics (eg, NSAIDs)
- **D**iabetes mellitus

> **Mnemonic:** **SAAD**.

![[Images/621_renal_papillary_necrosis.jpg]]

### Consequences of renal failure — MAD HUNGER
- **M**etabolic acidosis
- **D**yslipidemia (especially ↑ triglycerides)
- **H**igh potassium
- **U**remia
- **N**a⁺/H₂O retention → HF, pulmonary edema, hypertension
- **G**rowth retardation / developmental delay
- **E**rythropoietin deficiency → anemia
- **R**enal osteodystrophy

Two broad forms: **acute** (eg, ATN) and **chronic** (eg, hypertension, diabetes, congenital anomalies).

> CKD stages are defined by incremental reductions in GFR. Early CKD can maintain near-normal phosphate levels partly through increased **FGF23**, which promotes renal phosphate excretion.

**Uremia** can present with pericarditis, encephalopathy (asterixis), anorexia, and nausea.

## Renal osteodystrophy and cystic disease

### Renal osteodystrophy
CKD is associated with:
- Hypocalcemia
- Hyperphosphatemia
- Failure of vitamin D hydroxylation
- Secondary hyperparathyroidism → potentially tertiary hyperparathyroidism if poorly managed

High serum phosphate binds Ca²⁺ → tissue deposition → ↓ serum Ca²⁺.

↓ 1,25-(OH)₂D₃ → ↓ intestinal Ca²⁺ absorption.

Can cause **subperiosteal thinning of bones**.

### Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD)
- Numerous cysts in cortex and medulla → bilateral enlarged kidneys → progressive destruction of renal parenchyma
- Flank pain, hematuria, hypertension, UTI, progressive renal failure
- ~50% eventually develop progressive renal failure 
- **PKD1** (85%; chromosome 16) and **PKD2** (15%; chromosome 4)

![[Images/622_autosomal_dominant_polycystic_kidney_gross.jpg]]

- Associated with **berry aneurysms, mitral valve prolapse, benign hepatic cysts, diverticulosis**
- Hypertension/proteinuria: ACE inhibitors or ARBs

### Autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD)
- **PKHD1** mutation affecting fibrocystin
- Cystic dilation of **collecting ducts**
- Often presents in infancy / prenatal ultrasound
- Associated with **congenital hepatic fibrosis**
- Severe fetal oliguria can lead to Potter sequence
- Later concerns: systemic hypertension, progressive renal insufficiency, portal hypertension

![[Images/622_autosomal_recessive_polycystic_kidney_CT.jpg]]

### Autosomal dominant tubulointerstitial kidney disease
Also called medullary cystic kidney disease.

- Tubulointerstitial fibrosis and progressive renal insufficiency
- Inability to concentrate urine
- Medullary cysts may not be visualized
- Smaller kidneys on ultrasound
- Poor prognosis

### Simple vs complex renal cysts
- **Simple cysts:** ultrafiltrate-filled, anechoic on ultrasound, very common, usually incidental/asymptomatic.
- **Complex cysts:** septations, enhancement, or solid components; require follow-up or removal because of possible renal cell carcinoma.

## Renovascular disease and renal cell carcinoma

### Renal artery stenosis (RAS)
Unilateral or bilateral RAS → ↓ renal perfusion → ↑ renin → ↑ angiotensin II → hypertension.

**Common causes**
- **Atherosclerosis:** proximal 1/3 of renal artery; typical older males/smokers
- **Fibromuscular dysplasia:** distal 2/3 or segmental branches; typical younger/middle-aged females

**Unilateral RAS:** affected kidney can atrophy, producing asymmetric kidney size; affected renal vein has increased renin.

**Bilateral RAS:** creatinine can rise sharply after ACE inhibitor, ARB, or renin-inhibitor therapy because RAAS-mediated renal perfusion is reduced.

Clinical clues include severe/refractory hypertension, flash pulmonary edema, and epigastric/flank bruit. Other large-vessel stenoses may coexist.

![[Images/623_renovascular_disease_flowchart.svg]]

### Renal cell carcinoma (RCC)
Most common primary renal malignancy.

- **Clear-cell RCC** is the most common subtype.
- Arises from the **PCT**.
- Clear polygonal cells contain accumulated lipid/carbohydrate; gross lesion may be golden-yellow.
- May invade the renal vein → IVC and hematogenous metastasis to **lung, bone, liver**.
- Classic triad: flank pain, palpable mass, hematuria.
- Other findings: anemia, less commonly secondary polycythemia, fever, weight loss.
- Associated with paraneoplastic syndromes including PTHrP, ectopic EPO, ACTH, and renin.
- Clear-cell RCC is associated with chromosome **3p/VHL** changes.
- Localized disease: surgery/ablation; metastatic disease: immunotherapy/targeted therapy as described.
- Resistant to radiation and chemotherapy.

## Other renal and urinary tract tumors

### Renal oncocytoma
Benign epithelial tumor arising from collecting ducts.

- Well-circumscribed mass
- Large eosinophilic cells with abundant mitochondria
- Central scar can be present
- Lacks the perinuclear clearing of chromophobe RCC
- May present with painless hematuria, flank pain, or abdominal mass
- Often resected to exclude malignancy

![[Images/624_renal_oncocytoma_histology_C.jpg]]
![[Images/624_renal_oncocytoma_gross_B.jpg]]
![[Images/624_renal_oncocytoma_CT_A.jpg]]

### Nephroblastoma (Wilms tumor)
- Most common renal malignancy of **early childhood (ages 2–4)**.
- Contains embryonic glomerular structures.
- Usually a large unilateral palpable flank mass ± hematuria and hypertension.
- Associated with loss-of-function mutations of **WT1 or WT2 on chromosome 11**.

![[Images/624_nephroblastoma_Wilms_tumor_CT_A.jpg]]

#### Syndromes
- **WAGR:** Wilms tumor, aniridia, genitourinary malformations, developmental delay
- **Denys–Drash:** Wilms tumor, diffuse mesangial sclerosis, gonadal dysgenesis / male pseudohermaphroditism
- **Beckwith–Wiedemann:** Wilms tumor, organomegaly, macroglossia, hemihyperplasia, omphalocele

### Urothelial carcinoma of the bladder
Also called transitional-cell carcinoma.

- Most common tumor of the urinary tract system; can occur in renal calyces, renal pelvis, ureters, and bladder.
- Suggested by **painless hematuria without casts**.
- Risk factors: tobacco smoking, aromatic amines (dyes), cyclophosphamide.

> **Mnemonic:** **Pee SAC** — Smoking, Aromatic amines, Cyclophosphamide.

![[Images/624_urothelial_carcinoma_bladder_cystoscopy_A.jpg]]
![[Images/624_urothelial_carcinoma_bladder_histology_B.jpg]]

### Squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder
Chronic irritation → squamous metaplasia → dysplasia → squamous-cell carcinoma.

**Risk factors (“4 S’s”):**
- *Schistosoma haematobium*
- chronic cystitis (“systitis”)
- smoking
- chronic nephrolithiasis (stones)

Presents with **painless hematuria without casts**.
