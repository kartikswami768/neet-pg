# Renal — Physiology

## Fluid compartments

### 60–40–20 rule
- **60%** of body weight = total body water
- **40%** = intracellular fluid (ICF)
- **20%** = extracellular fluid (ECF)

ICF is mainly composed of **K⁺, Mg²⁺, and organic phosphates** (eg, ATP).

ECF is mainly composed of **Na⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻, and albumin**.

> **Mnemonic:** **HIKIN** = “HI K⁺ INtracellularly.”

Other relationships shown:
- 55–60% total body water; 40–45% nonwater mass
- ICF ≈ 2/3 of total body water; ECF ≈ 1/3
- ECF ≈ 75% interstitial fluid
- Blood volume ≈ plasma + RBC volume (**~6 L**)
- Blood volume ≈ 25% plasma and 10% RBC volume in the diagram's body-mass scheme

### Measurement
- Plasma volume can be measured by **radiolabeled albumin**.
- Extracellular volume can be measured by **inulin or mannitol**.
- Serum osmolality = **275–295 mOsm/kg H₂O**.
- Plasma volume = **TBV × (1 − Hct)**.

## Glomerular filtration barrier

The barrier filters plasma according to **size and charge selectivity**.

### Components
1. Fenestrated capillary endothelium
2. **Glomerular basement membrane (GBM)** containing type IV collagen chains and heparan sulfate
3. Visceral epithelial layer formed by **podocyte foot processes**

### Charge barrier
The glomerular filtration barrier contains negatively charged glycoproteins that restrict entry of negatively charged molecules such as **albumin**.

### Size barrier
- Fenestrated capillary endothelium prevents entry of very large particles/blood cells (>100 nm in the figure).
- The slit diaphragm prevents entry of molecules >40–50 nm.
- Podocyte foot processes participate in both charge and size selectivity.

## Renal clearance, GFR, and renal plasma flow

### Renal clearance
$$C_x = rac{U_x 	imes V}{P_x}$$

**Cₓ** = volume of plasma from which substance X is completely cleared in urine per unit time.

- **Cₓ < GFR:** net tubular reabsorption and/or substance is not freely filtered
- **Cₓ > GFR:** net tubular secretion of X
- **Cₓ = GFR:** no net secretion or reabsorption

Where:
- **Uₓ** = urine concentration of X
- **Pₓ** = plasma concentration of X
- **V** = urine flow rate

### Glomerular filtration rate
Inulin clearance estimates GFR because inulin is **freely filtered and neither reabsorbed nor secreted**.

$$C_{inulin}=GFR=rac{U_{inulin}	imes V}{P_{inulin}}$$

$$GFR=K_f[(P_{GC}-P_{BS})-(\pi_{GC}-\pi_{BS})]$$

- PGC = glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure
- PBS = Bowman-space hydrostatic pressure
- πGC = glomerular capillary oncotic pressure
- πBS = Bowman-space oncotic pressure (normally ≈ 0)
- Kf = filtration coefficient

**Normal GFR ≈ 100 mL/min.**

**Creatinine clearance** approximates GFR but slightly **overestimates** it because creatinine is moderately secreted by proximal renal tubules.

![[Images/600_creatinine_clearance_gfr_graph.svg]]

### Effective renal plasma flow
Effective renal plasma flow (eRPF) can be estimated by **para-aminohippuric acid (PAH) clearance** because nearly all PAH entering the kidney is excreted by filtration plus secretion.

$$eRPF=C_{PAH}=rac{U_{PAH}	imes V}{P_{PAH}}$$

$$RBF=rac{RPF}{1-Hct}$$

- Renal blood flow is usually **20–25% of cardiac output**.
- eRPF slightly underestimates true RPF.

## Renal blood-flow autoregulation

Autoregulatory mechanisms maintain relatively constant **renal blood flow (RBF) and GFR** despite rapid fluctuations in renal perfusion pressure.

### Myogenic mechanism
↑ arterial pressure → stretch of the afferent arteriole → vascular smooth-muscle activation → **afferent arteriolar vasoconstriction** → ↓ RBF.

### Tubuloglomerular feedback
↑ NaCl delivery sensed by the macula densa → paracrine vasoconstriction of the afferent arteriole → ↓ RBF. A fall in NaCl delivery can produce the opposite response.

### Filtration fraction
$$FF=rac{GFR}{RPF}$$

**Normal FF ≈ 20%.**

### Key arteriolar effects
- **Prostaglandins** dilate the afferent arteriole.
- **Angiotensin II** preferentially constricts the efferent arteriole.
- **NSAIDs + ACE inhibitors together** can constrict both afferent and efferent arterioles and impair renal function.

### Changes in glomerular dynamics
| Change | GFR | RPF | FF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Afferent arteriole constriction | ↓ | ↓ | — |

| ↑ Plasma protein concentration | ↓ | — | ↓ |

| Ureteral constriction | ↓ | ↓ | — |

## Reabsorption and secretion

### Calculation
- **Filtered load** = GFR × Pₓ
- **Excretion rate** = V × Uₓ
- **Reabsorption rate** = filtered − excreted
- **Secretion rate** = excreted − filtered

### Fractional excretion of sodium (FENa)

### Glucose handling
At a normal plasma glucose level (**60–120 mg/dL**), glucose is completely reabsorbed in the **PCT** by Na⁺/glucose cotransport.

- **Renal threshold:** ~**200 mg/dL** in adults, at which glucosuria begins.
- **Tm:** ~**375 mg/min**, at which transporters are saturated.
- Pregnancy increases GFR; therefore glucosuria can occur at lower plasma glucose concentrations.
- **SGLT2 inhibitors** (eg, “-flozins”) produce glucosuria.
- Glucosuria is an important clinical clue to **diabetes mellitus**.
- **Splay phenomenon:** the transition to maximal transport is gradual because nephrons have heterogeneous Tm values.

## Nephron transport physiology

### Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)
- Early PCT contains a **brush border**.
- Reabsorbs all glucose and amino acids and most **HCO₃⁻, Na⁺, Cl⁻, PO₄³⁻, K⁺, H₂O, and uric acid**.
- Absorption is isotonic.
- Generates/secretes **NH₃**, permitting greater renal H⁺ excretion.
- **PTH** inhibits Na⁺/PO₄³⁻ cotransport → ↑ PO₄³⁻ excretion.
- **Angiotensin II** stimulates Na⁺/H⁺ exchange → ↑ Na⁺, H₂O, and HCO₃⁻ reabsorption.
- **65–80% of Na⁺ and H₂O** is reabsorbed.

![[Images/603_proximal_convoluted_tubule_transport.svg]]

### Thin descending limb of Henle
- Passively reabsorbs **H₂O** through the hypertonic medulla.
- Impermeable to Na⁺.
- Concentrating segment; makes tubular fluid **hypertonic**.

### Thick ascending limb of Henle
- Reabsorbs **Na⁺, K⁺, and Cl⁻** via the Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter.
- K⁺ back-leak generates a positive lumen potential → paracellular reabsorption of **Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺**.
- Impermeable to H₂O.
- Makes urine less concentrated as fluid ascends.
- **10–20% of Na⁺** is reabsorbed here.

![[Images/603_thick_ascending_limb_transport.svg]]

### Early distal convoluted tubule
- Reabsorbs **Na⁺ and Cl⁻**.
- Impermeable to H₂O.
- Makes urine fully dilute (hypotonic).
- **PTH** increases Ca²⁺ reabsorption via the Ca²⁺/Na⁺ exchanger.
- **5–10% of Na⁺** is reabsorbed.

![[Images/603_distal_convoluted_tubule_transport.svg]]

### Collecting tubule
- Reabsorbs Na⁺ in exchange for secretion of **K⁺ and H⁺**, regulated by aldosterone.
- **Aldosterone** acts at the mineralocorticoid receptor → mRNA → protein synthesis.
- In principal cells: ↑ apical K⁺ conductance, ↑ Na⁺/K⁺ pump, ↑ ENaC activity → lumen negativity → ↑ K⁺ secretion.
- In α-intercalated cells: ↑ H⁺ ATPase activity → ↑ H⁺ secretion.
- **ADH** acts at the V2 receptor → insertion of aquaporin-2 water channels on the apical membrane.
- **3–5% of Na⁺** is reabsorbed in this segment.
- Urea reabsorption occurs in the **medullary** collecting duct.

![[Images/603_collecting_tubule_transport.svg]]

> **Key:** ↑ / ↓ denote important differentiating features.

### Tubular-fluid / plasma concentration ([TF/P])
- **[TF/P] > 1:** solute is reabsorbed more slowly than water or is secreted.
- **[TF/P] = 1:** solute and water are reabsorbed at the same rate.
- **[TF/P] < 1:** solute is reabsorbed faster than water.

Inulin concentration increases along the PCT because water is reabsorbed while inulin is not; its amount does not change.

Chloride is reabsorbed more slowly than sodium in early PCT, so its relative concentration rises before reaching a plateau more distally.

## Renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system (RAAS)

### RAAS sequence

![[Images/606_renin_angiotensin_aldosterone_system.svg]]

**↓ renal perfusion pressure / ↑ sympathetic tone (β₁) / ↓ NaCl delivery to macula densa → renin → angiotensin I → ACE → angiotensin II.**

**Angiotensin II**:
- Vasoconstriction
- Preferential **efferent arteriolar constriction** → preserves GFR when RBF falls
- ↑ proximal Na⁺/H⁺ exchange → ↑ Na⁺, HCO₃⁻, and H₂O reabsorption
- ↑ H⁺ secretion
- ↑ aldosterone secretion
- ↑ thirst and ADH effects

**Aldosterone**:
- ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption
- ↑ K⁺ secretion
- ↑ H⁺ secretion

**ANP/BNP**:
- Released from atria/ventricles in response to ↑ volume
- Relax vascular smooth muscle via **cGMP**
- ↑ GFR
- ↓ renin → inhibits the angiotensin–aldosterone system
- Dilates afferent arterioles
- Promotes natriuresis

**ADH (vasopressin)**:
- Primarily regulates serum osmolality; also responds to low-volume states.
- Stimulates water reabsorption in collecting ducts.
- Stimulates urea reabsorption in the medullary collecting duct to maximize the corticopapillary osmotic gradient.

## Juxtaglomerular apparatus and renal endocrine/paracrine functions

### Juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA)
Consists of:
- **Mesangial cells**
- **Juxtaglomerular (JG) cells** — modified smooth muscle cells of the afferent arteriole
- **Macula densa** — NaCl sensor in the DCT

JG cells secrete renin in response to ↓ renal blood pressure and ↑ sympathetic tone via β₁ receptors.

Macula densa cells sense ↓ NaCl delivery → ↑ renin release → efferent arteriolar vasoconstriction → ↑ GFR.

Communication between JG cells and macula densa occurs through **gap junctions**.

The JGA helps prevent short-term changes in GFR through autoregulation and maintains GFR longer-term through the RAAS.

> **β-blockers:** ↓ BP by ↓ CO and inhibition of β₁ receptors at the JGA → ↓ renin release.

### Kidney endocrine/paracrine functions
| Substance | Trigger | Major effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Erythropoietin** | Peritubular interstitial cells in response to hypoxia | Stimulates RBC proliferation in bone marrow; used for anemia secondary to CKD |
| **Calcitriol** | PCT conversion of 25-OH vitamin D₃ → 1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D₃ via 1α-hydroxylase; stimulated by PTH | ↑ calcium absorption from small bowel |

![[Images/607_vitamin_d_activation_pathway.svg]]

| **Prostaglandins** | Renal paracrine secretion | Afferent arteriolar vasodilation → ↑ RBF; NSAIDs block renal-protective prostaglandin synthesis and can reduce GFR in low-flow states |
| **Dopamine** | Secreted by PCT cells | Promotes natriuresis; at low doses dilates renal arteries/arterioles → ↑ RBF with little change in GFR; at higher doses acts as a vasoconstrictor |

## Hormones acting on the kidney

| Hormone | Major renal effects |
|---|---|
| **Angiotensin II** | Efferent arteriolar constriction → ↑ GFR and ↑ FF, with simultaneous proximal/distal Na⁺ reabsorption to preserve circulating volume |
| **Atrial natriuretic peptide** | Indirect afferent dilation via inhibition of norepinephrine → ↑ GFR and ↑ Na⁺ filtration without compensatory distal Na⁺ reabsorption → Na⁺ and volume loss |
| **PTH** | ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption in DCT; ↓ PO₄³⁻ reabsorption in PCT; ↑ 1,25-(OH)₂D₃ production |
| **Aldosterone** | ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption, ↑ K⁺ secretion, ↑ H⁺ secretion |
| **ADH (vasopressin)** | ↑ aquaporins in principal cells → ↑ H₂O reabsorption; ↑ urea reabsorption in medullary collecting ducts |

### Potassium shifts
**K⁺ shifts into cells:** insulin, β-agonists, alkalosis.

**K⁺ shifts out of cells:** acidosis, hypoinsulinemia, β-blockade, digoxin, succinylcholine, cell lysis.

## Electrolyte disturbances and acid–base physiology

### Electrolyte disturbances
| Electrolyte | Low serum concentration | High serum concentration |
|---|---|---|
| **Na⁺** | Nausea, malaise, stupor, coma, seizures | Irritability, stupor, coma |
| **K⁺** | U waves, flattened T waves, arrhythmias, cramps, weakness | Wide QRS, peaked T waves, arrhythmias, weakness |
| **Ca²⁺** | Tetany, seizures, QT prolongation, Chvostek/Trousseau signs | “Stones, bones, groans, thrones, psychiatric overtones” |
| **Mg²⁺** | Tetany, torsades de pointes, hypokalemia, hypocalcemia | ↓ DTRs, lethargy, bradycardia, hypotension, cardiac arrest, hypocalcemia |
| **PO₄³⁻** | Bone loss, osteomalacia, rickets | Renal stones, metastatic calcifications, hypocalcemia |

### Acid–base physiology
Metabolic disorders primarily alter **HCO₃⁻**; respiratory disorders primarily alter **PCO₂**.

| Disorder | pH | PCO₂ | HCO₃⁻ | Compensation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Metabolic acidosis | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ | Hyperventilation (immediate) |
| Metabolic alkalosis | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ | Hypoventilation (immediate) |
| Respiratory acidosis | ↓ | ↑ | ↑ | ↑ renal HCO₃⁻ reabsorption (delayed) |
| Respiratory alkalosis | ↑ | ↓ | ↓ | ↓ renal HCO₃⁻ reabsorption (delayed) |

### Winters formula
For simple metabolic acidosis:
$$PCO_2 = 1.5[HCO_3^-] + 8 \pm 2$$

- Measured PCO₂ **above** predicted → concomitant respiratory acidosis.
- Measured PCO₂ **below** predicted → concomitant respiratory alkalosis.

## Acidosis and alkalosis approach

### Initial classification
- **Acidemia:** pH < 7.35
- **Alkalemia:** pH > 7.45
- Respiratory acidosis: PCO₂ >44 mm Hg
- Respiratory alkalosis: PCO₂ <36 mm Hg
- Metabolic acidosis: HCO₃⁻ <20 mEq/L
- Metabolic alkalosis: HCO₃⁻ >28 mEq/L

### Anion gap
$$AG=Na^+-(Cl^-+HCO_3^-)$$

The list includes **GOLDMARK** causes of high-anion-gap metabolic acidosis:
- **G**lycols (ethylene glycol, propylene glycol)
- **O**xoproline (chronic acetaminophen use)
- **L**-lactate
- **D**-lactate
- **M**ethanol and other alcohols
- **A**spirin (late effect)
- **R**enal failure
- **K**etones (diabetic, alcoholic, starvation)

### Normal-anion-gap metabolic acidosis — SHARDS
- **S**pironolactone
- **H**yperchloremia / hyperalimentation
- **A**ddison disease / acetazolamide
- **R**enal tubular acidosis
- **D**iarrhea
- **S**aline infusion

### Metabolic alkalosis
**Saline-resistant:** hyperaldosteronism, Bartter syndrome, Gitelman syndrome, current loop/thiazide diuretics.

**Saline-responsive:** vomiting, recent loop/thiazide diuretics, antacids.

The approach uses urine chloride **>20 vs <20 mEq/L** to separate these pathways.
