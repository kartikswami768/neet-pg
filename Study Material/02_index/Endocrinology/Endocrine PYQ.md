# CORE EXAM THEMES (High-Yield Patterns Seen in NEET-PG & INI-CET)

These represent **concept clusters repeatedly targeted by examiners**.

## 1. Thyroid Clinical Medicine
Most frequently tested endocrine gland.

Key focus areas:
- Thyroid nodules (hot vs cold)
- FNAC limitations (follicular adenoma vs carcinoma)
- Graves disease investigations
- Thyroid surgery complications
- Thyroid storm
- Neck dissection
- Thyroid cancer management

---

## 2. Diabetes Pharmacology
One of the **highest-yield pharmacology areas**.

Common exam patterns:
- Insulin preparations
- C-peptide physiology
- Mechanism of sulfonylureas
- Metformin contraindications
- DPP-4 inhibitors (linagliptin renal safety)
- Incretin physiology
- GLP-1 drug adverse effects

---

## 3. Calcium–Bone Axis
Very frequent integrated physiology/pharmacology questions.

Key themes:
- PTH physiology
- Hyperparathyroidism vs pseudohypoparathyroidism
- Vitamin D metabolism
- Osteoporosis drugs
- Teriparatide mechanism
- Bisphosphonate precautions
- Hypercalcemia of malignancy

---

## 4. Adrenal Disorders
Examiners prefer **clinical-physiology integration**.

Repeated themes:
- Addison disease causes
- Chronic adrenal insufficiency replacement
- Addisonian crisis
- Steroidogenesis pathway
- Aldosterone regulation
- Pheochromocytoma

---

## 5. Pituitary Pathology
Usually asked via **clinical vignettes**.

Key topics:
- Prolactinoma
- Pituitary apoplexy
- Sheehan syndrome
- TSH-secreting adenoma
- Hypopituitarism hormone patterns

---

## 6. Hormone Receptor Mechanisms
Classic **concept-based physiology/pharmacology questions**.

High-yield receptors:
- Intracellular steroid receptors
- GPCR hormones
- Tyrosine kinase receptors

---

## 7. Reproductive Endocrine Pharmacology
Usually tested through **drugs rather than physiology**.

Important topics:
- Combined oral contraceptives
- Emergency contraception
- Tamoxifen adverse effects
- Fulvestrant
- PCOS ovulation induction

---

## 8. Electrolyte and Hyponatremia Differentiation
INI-CET particularly likes **diagnostic interpretation questions**.

Important comparisons:
- SIADH vs cerebral salt wasting
- Pseudohyponatremia
- Urine sodium/osmolality patterns
