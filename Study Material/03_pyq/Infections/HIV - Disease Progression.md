## HIV Disease Progression (Exam-Oriented: NEET PG / FMGE / INICET)

HIV infection progresses through **four clinically recognized stages** (including older but exam-relevant terminology):

1. Acute (Primary) HIV infection  
2. Asymptomatic HIV infection ± Persistent Generalized Lymphadenopathy  
3. AIDS Related Complex (ARC)  
4. AIDS (Advanced HIV disease)

---

## 1. Acute / Primary HIV Infection

**Time period**
- 2–6 weeks after exposure

**Pathophysiology**
- Massive viral replication  
- Very high viral load  
- Transient fall in CD4 count

**Clinical features (Acute Retroviral Syndrome)**
- Occurs in 50–70% patients  
- Fever (most common)  
- Sore throat  
- Generalized lymphadenopathy  
- Maculopapular rash (trunk)  
- Myalgia, arthralgia  
- Diarrhea  
- Oral ulcers  

**Exam pearl**
- Mimics infectious mononucleosis  
- HIV → generalized lymphadenopathy  

**Laboratory findings (high-yield)**
- HIV antibodies: negative (window period)  
- p24 antigen: positive  
- HIV RNA: positive  

**Best test in suspected acute HIV**
- HIV RNA / p24 antigen

---

## 2. Asymptomatic HIV Infection / Clinical Latency

**Duration**
- 5–10 years without ART  
- Much longer with ART

**Pathophysiology**
- Ongoing viral replication in lymphoid tissue  
- Gradual CD4 decline

**Clinical features**
- Asymptomatic OR  
- Persistent Generalized Lymphadenopathy (PGL)

**PGL definition (PYQ favorite)**
- Lymph nodes >1 cm  
- ≥2 non-inguinal sites  
- >3 months  
- No other cause

**CD4 count**
- Usually >500 initially, then declines toward 200–500

---

## 3. AIDS Related Complex (ARC)

**Definition**
- Symptomatic HIV infection  
- Does NOT fulfill criteria for AIDS  
- Older term but still asked in exams

**Clinical features (>1 month, unexplained)**
- Fever  
- Chronic diarrhea  
- Weight loss (<10%)  
- Night sweats  
- Fatigue  
- Oral candidiasis  
- Oral hairy leukoplakia  
- Recurrent herpes zoster  

**CD4 count**
- Usually 200–500 cells/mm³

**Important distinction**
- No AIDS-defining illness  
- Presence of PCP, toxoplasmosis, CMV, Kaposi sarcoma → AIDS, not ARC

---

## 4. AIDS (Advanced HIV Disease)

**Definition (CDC / WHO)**
- CD4 <200 cells/mm³  
  OR  
- Any AIDS-defining illness (at any CD4)

**High-yield CD4 cutoffs and infections**

**CD4 <500**
- Oral candidiasis  
- Herpes zoster  
- Tuberculosis (can occur at any CD4)

**CD4 <200**
- Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP)  
	- Most common AIDS-defining illness

**CD4 <100**
- Toxoplasma gondii encephalitis  
- Cryptococcal meningitis

**CD4 <50**
- CMV retinitis (most common cause of blindness in AIDS)  
- Mycobacterium avium complex (MAC)

**Malignancies**
- Kaposi sarcoma  
- Non-Hodgkin lymphoma  
- Invasive cervical cancer

---

## Frequently Asked Exam Concepts

- Most infectious stage: Acute HIV  
- Most common opportunistic infection overall: Tuberculosis  
- Best test in window period: HIV RNA / p24 antigen  
- ARC: symptomatic HIV with CD4 >200 and no defining illness  
- AIDS: CD4 <200 or defining illness  
- ART halts progression but does not eradicate virus  

---

## One-Line PYQ Takeaways

- ARC lies between asymptomatic HIV and AIDS  
- Oral candidiasis does NOT always mean AIDS  
- CMV retinitis occurs at CD4 <50  
- Ring-enhancing lesion + CD4 <100 → Toxoplasma
