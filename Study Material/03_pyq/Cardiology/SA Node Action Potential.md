# Pacemaker Potential

This is different from ventricular action potentials.

### Occurs in
- **SA node**
- **AV node**
- Some **Purkinje fibers**

These cells **do not have a stable resting membrane potential**.  
Instead they **slowly depolarize automatically**.

---

# Pacemaker Cell Action Potential

Pacemaker cells have **3 phases instead of 5**.

They **lack phases 1 and 2**.

The phases are:

- **Phase 4 – Pacemaker potential**
- **Phase 0 – Depolarization**
- **Phase 3 – Repolarization**

---

## Phase 4 – Pacemaker Potential

This is the key feature responsible for **automaticity**.

Membrane potential slowly rises from about **–60 mV to –40 mV**.

### Mechanisms responsible
### 1. *Funny Current (If)*

Called **funny current** because it behaves unusually.

Channel:
- **HCN channel**
Activated by:
- **Hyperpolarization**
Ion movement:
- Mainly **Na⁺ influx**

This produces **slow spontaneous depolarization**.

### 2. Reduced K⁺ Efflux
K⁺ channels gradually close.
Less K⁺ leaving the cell makes the membrane potential **more positive**.

### 3. T-type Ca²⁺ Channels

Open at about **–50 mV**.
They allow **small Ca²⁺ influx**, further depolarizing the membrane.
Once membrane potential reaches **threshold (~ –40 mV)**:

---

## Phase 0 – Depolarization

In pacemaker cells the upstroke is produced by ***Ca²⁺ influx**, not Na⁺.*
### Channels involved
- **L-type Ca²⁺ channels**

Because Ca²⁺ channels open more slowly than Na⁺ channels, the **upstroke is slower** than in ventricular cells.

---

## Phase 3 – Repolarization

### Mechanism
- **Ca²⁺ channels close**
- **Voltage-gated K⁺ channels open**
- **K⁺ efflux**

Membrane potential returns to about **–60 mV**.

The cycle then repeats automatically.


---

# Why SA Node is the Primary Pacemaker

Because it has the **fastest phase-4 depolarization**.

Intrinsic rates:

- **SA node:** 60–100 bpm  
- **AV node:** 40–60 bpm  
- **Purkinje fibers:** 20–40 bpm  

The fastest pacemaker suppresses the others (**overdrive suppression**).

---

# Regulation of Pacemaker Potential

## Sympathetic stimulation

Neurotransmitter: **Noradrenaline**

Effects:
- Increases **funny current**
- Increases **Ca²⁺ influx**

Result:
- Steeper phase-4 slope
- **Increased heart rate**

---

## Parasympathetic stimulation

Neurotransmitter: **Acetylcholine**

Effects:
- Opens **K⁺ channels**
- Reduces funny current

Result:
- Hyperpolarization
- Slower phase-4 depolarization
- **Decreased heart rate**

---

# Key Differences

| Feature | Plateau Potential | Pacemaker Potential |
|---|---|---|
| Cells | Ventricular/atrial muscle | SA node & AV node |
| Resting potential | Stable (–90 mV) | Unstable |
| Main depolarizing ion | Na⁺ (phase 0) | Ca²⁺ (phase 0) |
| Plateau phase | Present | Absent |
| Automaticity | No | Yes |
| Function | Sustains contraction | Generates rhythm |

---

## Simple Conceptual Summary

**Plateau potential:**  
Maintains prolonged depolarization so the heart contracts long enough to pump blood effectively.

**Pacemaker potential:**  
Produces spontaneous depolarization that allows the heart to generate its own rhythmic impulses.
