## Definition
Parkinson’s disease is a chronic, progressive neurodegenerative disorder caused by degeneration of dopaminergic neurons in the **substantia nigra pars compacta**, leading to dopamine deficiency in the striatum.

---

## Pathophysiology
- Dopamine ↓ and Acetylcholine ↑ in basal ganglia  
- Inhibition to thalamus → hypokinesia  
- **Lewy bodies**: intracytoplasmic eosinophilic inclusions made of **alpha-synuclein and ubiquitin**

**Exam pearl:**  
Parkinson’s = Dopamine ↓ + Acetylcholine ↑

---

## Etiology
- **Idiopathic** – most common  
- Secondary causes:
  - Drugs: antipsychotics, metoclopramide
  - Toxins: MPTP
  - Post-encephalitic
  - Vascular
  - Trauma

---

## Cardinal Clinical Features (TRAP)
- **T** – Tremor (resting, pill-rolling, disappears on action)
- **R** – Rigidity (lead pipe, cogwheel)
- **A** – Akinesia / Bradykinesia
- **P** – Postural instability (late feature)

### Important Exam Points
- Resting tremor frequency: **4–6 Hz**
- Rigidity present throughout range of motion
- Cogwheel rigidity = rigidity + tremor
- Postural instability occurs late

### What doesn’t happen here?
1. Symmetrical onset of motor features. It starts asymmetrically as degeneration happen on 1 side first.
2. **Pyramidal Signs:** Spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski
3. **Cerebellar Signs:** Ataxia, Intentional tremor 
	- Ataxia is lack of coordination, NOT weakness or rigidity
	- Similarly intentional tremor is also about coordination
4. **Early** Postural instability, dementia, dysphagia, autonomic failure, hallucination. *All these things happen late in the disease.*

---

### Other Motor Features
- Mask-like facies
- Stooped posture
- Shuffling gait
- Festinating gait
- Micrographia
- Reduced arm swing
- Hypophonia

---

### Non-Motor Features
- Autonomic: constipation, orthostatic hypotension, urinary retention
- Neuropsychiatric: depression, **dementia, hallucinations**
- Sleep: REM sleep behavior disorder
- Seborrheic dermatitis
- **Anosmia** – early feature

---

## Parkinson’s vs Essential Tremor
| Feature | Parkinson’s | Essential Tremor |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| Type of tremor | Resting | Action |
| Symmetry | Asymmetric | Symmetric |
| Effect of movement | Improves | Worsens |
| Alcohol | No benefit | Improves |

---

## Diagnosis
- Clinical diagnosis
- Good response to **levodopa** supports diagnosis
- MRI brain: usually normal
- DAT scan: reduced dopamine uptake

---

## Treatment Goals
- Increase dopamine
- Decrease acetylcholine
- Protect neurons
- Improve quality of life

---

## Drugs Used (Very High Yield)

### Levodopa + Carbidopa
- Most effective drug
- Levodopa → dopamine in brain
- Carbidopa blocks peripheral conversion
- Side effects: nausea, vomiting, hypotension, dyskinesia, hallucinations
- Long-term: wearing-off, on-off phenomenon

### Dopamine Agonists
- Bromocriptine, Pramipexole, Ropinirole, Rotigotine
- Preferred in younger patients
- Side effects: impulse control disorder, hallucinations

### MAO-B Inhibitors
- Selegiline, Rasagiline
- Prevent dopamine breakdown
- Selegiline → insomnia (amphetamine metabolites)

### COMT Inhibitors
- Entacapone, Tolcapone
- Increase levodopa duration
- **Tolcapone → hepatotoxicity**

### Anticholinergics
- Trihexyphenidyl, Benztropine
- Best for tremor
- Avoid in elderly (confusion, urinary retention)

### Amantadine
- Increases dopamine release
- Useful for dyskinesia
- Side effect: livedo reticularis

---

## Surgical Treatment
**Deep Brain Stimulation**
- Target: Subthalamic nucleus or Globus pallidus internus
- Indication: advanced disease with motor fluctuations

---

## Parkinson Plus Syndromes (Poor Levodopa Response)
- **Progressive supranuclear palsy** – vertical gaze palsy, early falls
- **Multiple system atrophy** – autonomic failure
- **Corticobasal degeneration** – alien limb
- **Lewy body dementia** – dementia before or within 1 year of motor symptoms

---

## Drug-Induced Parkinsonism
- Caused by antipsychotics
- Symmetric involvement
- Usually no resting tremor
- Reversible

---

## One-Liners for NEET PG
- Most common cause: idiopathic
- Pathological hallmark: Lewy bodies
- Most effective drug: Levodopa
- Best drug for tremor: Anticholinergics
- Early postural instability: Parkinson plus
- Early anosmia: Parkinson’s
- Dementia late: Parkinson’s
- Dementia early: Lewy body dementia
