## HSV Encephalitis

HSV encephalitis is the **most common cause of sporadic, fatal viral encephalitis in adults** and is a very high-yield topic for **NEET PG and FMGE**.

### Cause and Epidemiology
- Most commonly caused by **Herpes simplex virus type 1 (HSV-1)** in adults and older children  
- **HSV-2** is more common in **neonates**
- Virus usually reaches the brain by **reactivation from latent infection in the trigeminal ganglion**

### Pathophysiology
- HSV has a predilection for the **temporal and frontal lobes**
- Causes **hemorrhagic necrosis** of brain tissue

### Clinical Features
- Acute onset **fever**, **headache**, and **altered sensorium**
- **Focal neurological deficits** are common
- Temporal lobe involvement leads to:
  - Personality changes
  - Memory loss
  - Hallucinations
  - Seizures
- **Aphasia** if the dominant temporal lobe is involved

### CSF Findings
- **Lymphocytic pleocytosis**
- **Mildly elevated protein**
- **Normal glucose**
- **Red blood cells present** due to hemorrhagic necrosis
- **Gold standard** diagnostic test: **HSV DNA PCR in CSF**

### Imaging
- **MRI** is the imaging modality of choice
- Shows **temporal lobe hyperintensity** on **T2 and FLAIR**
- **CT scan** may be normal early, later shows hypodense temporal lobe lesions

### EEG Findings
- **Periodic lateralized epileptiform discharges (PLEDs)** over the temporal region

### Treatment
**Immediate** treatment with **intravenous acyclovir** is crucial and should be started as soon as HSV encephalitis is suspected, **even before confirmation**. Delay in treatment significantly increases mortality.
### Prognosis
- Untreated disease has **very high mortality**
- Early acyclovir therapy markedly improves survival
- Residual neurological deficits may persist

### High-Yield Exam Pearls
- Most common sporadic encephalitis in adults
- Temporal lobe involvement with hemorrhagic CSF
- Diagnosis confirmed by **CSF HSV PCR**
- Treatment is **IV acyclovir**
