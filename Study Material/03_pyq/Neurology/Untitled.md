# Clinical Case Scenario: Tabes Dorsalis

## Patient History
A 52-year-old male presents to the outpatient neurology clinic complaining of a **6-month history of worsening balance and unsteady walking**, particularly at night or when closing his eyes while washing his face. Over the past year, he has also experienced recurrent, sudden, shooting, "lightning-like" pains that start in his abdomen and radiate down both legs. 

He reports occasional severe, sharp epigastric pain accompanied by nausea and vomiting, which his primary care physician previously evaluated as acid reflux without relief (visceral crisis). On further questioning, he reveals a history of an untreated genital ulcer in his early twenties that resolved spontaneously.

---

## Physical Examination

### Neurological Examination
* **Gait & Coordination:** 
  * **Sensory Ataxia:** High-stepping, wide-based "stamping" gait (slapping feet onto the ground to augment tactile feedback).
  * **Romberg Sign:** Positive (patient stands steady with eyes open, but sways significantly and loses balance immediately upon closing his eyes).
* **Sensory Assessment:** 
  * Bilaterally impaired **vibration sense** (using a 128 Hz tuning fork) and loss of **proprioception** (joint position sense) at the toes and ankles.
  * Preserved pain and temperature perception in distal limbs, but hyperalgesic sensory loss over the central face and lower trunk.
* **Reflexes:** 
  * Absent deep tendon reflexes in the lower extremities (**areflexia** at knees and ankles — *Westphal's sign*).
* **Cranial Nerves:** 
  * **Argyll Robertson Pupils:** Bilaterally small, irregular pupils that **accommodate to near targets but do not react to direct or consensual light** ("Prostitute's pupil" — accommodates but doesn't react).

### Musculoskeletal Examination
* Mild swelling, hypermobility, and instability of the right knee joint without warmth or significant tenderness, characteristic of an early **Charcot joint** (neuropathic arthropathy due to loss of deep pain and proprioceptive sensation).
* Urinary retention with overflow incontinence on history and post-void residual ultrasound (neurogenic bladder / hypotonic detrusor).

## Diagnostic Findings

| Investigation | Result | Clinical Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Serum VDRL / RPR** | Non-reactive or Low Titre | May be negative in up to 25–30% of late tertiary syphilis cases. |
| **Serum FTA-ABS / TPA** | **Positive** | Confirms past exposure to *Treponema pallidum* (treponemal test remains positive for life). |
| **CSF Analysis** | Lymphocytic pleocytosis (>10 cells/µL), elevated protein (>45 mg/dL). | Indicates active central nervous system neuroinflammation. |
| **CSF VDRL** | **Positive** | High specificity; diagnostic gold standard for neurosyphilis. |
| **Spinal MRI** | Hyperintensity/atrophy restricted to the posterior columns (fasciculus gracilis and cuneatus) on T2-weighted sequences. | Confirms localization of dorsal column demyelination. |
