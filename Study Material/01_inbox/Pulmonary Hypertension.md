# Pulmonary Hypertension (PH) & Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of Newborn (PPHN)

## 1. Pulmonary Hypertension

### Definition
- **PH = mPAP >20 mmHg at rest** on right-heart catheterization.
- **Pre-capillary PH/PAH:**
  - mPAP >20 mmHg
  - PAWP ≤15 mmHg
  - PVR >2 Wood units

### Pathology
Normal:
> ↓ PVR after birth/adulthood → adequate pulmonary blood flow

PH:
> Pulmonary vasoconstriction + vascular remodeling → ↑ PVR → RV pressure overload → RV hypertrophy → RV failure

### Key vascular abnormalities
- ↓ NO → ↓ cGMP
- ↓ Prostacyclin (PGI₂) → ↓ cAMP
- ↑ Endothelin-1
- Smooth-muscle proliferation
- Intimal fibrosis
- Advanced PAH → **plexiform lesions**

### Major WHO groups

| Group | Major cause |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) |
| 2 | Left-heart disease |
| 3 | Lung disease/hypoxia |
| 4 | Chronic thromboembolic PH |
| 5 | Multifactorial/unclear |

---

## 2. Management of PH

### General principles
- Treat underlying cause
- Oxygen if hypoxemic
- Diuretics → RV congestion
- Treat arrhythmias/infections/anemia etc.
- Anticoagulation → selected patients
- Refer severe disease to PH centre

### PAH-specific drugs

| Pathway | Drugs | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **NO → cGMP** | Sildenafil, tadalafil | PDE-5 inhibition → ↑ cGMP |
| | Riociguat | Soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator → ↑ cGMP |
| **Endothelin** | Bosentan, ambrisentan, macitentan | Endothelin receptor blockade |
| **Prostacyclin → cAMP** | Epoprostenol, treprostinil, iloprost | ↑ cAMP → vasodilation + antiproliferative |
| | Selexipag | Oral IP prostacyclin-receptor agonist |
| **NEW: Activin pathway** | **Sotatercept** | Activin signaling inhibitor → reverses pulmonary vascular remodeling |

### New major drug: SOTATERCEPT

**Sotatercept = first major PAH drug targeting the activin/TGF-β superfamily pathway.**

- Activin signaling contributes to abnormal pulmonary vascular proliferation/remodeling.
- Sotatercept acts as an **activin receptor type IIA-Fc ligand trap**.
- Produces improvement in:
  - Pulmonary vascular resistance
  - Exercise capacity
  - WHO functional class
  - Risk of clinical worsening

**Current role:**
- Add-on therapy to background PAH treatment.
- 2026 ERS guideline supports addition particularly in patients remaining at **intermediate-low, intermediate-high or high risk** despite PAH therapy.

**Dose:**
- 0.3 mg/kg SC initially
- → **0.7 mg/kg SC every 3 weeks**

**Important adverse effects:**
- ↑ Hemoglobin / erythrocytosis
- ↓ Platelets
- Bleeding
- Telangiectasia
- Hypertension
- Hypersensitivity

> **Exam pearl: Sotatercept = PAH + Activin pathway + vascular remodeling.**

## 3. Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension of the Newborn (PPHN)

### Definition
Failure of normal postnatal fall in pulmonary vascular resistance:

> **Persistently ↑ PVR → ↓ pulmonary blood flow → right-to-left shunting → severe hypoxemia**

Shunting occurs through:
- **PDA**
- **PFO**

### Pathology

Normally:

> Birth → ↑ O₂ → pulmonary vasodilation → ↓ PVR → ↑ pulmonary blood flow

PPHN:

> ↑ PVR persists → pulmonary arterial pressure remains high → R→L shunt → hypoxemia

### Common causes

- Meconium aspiration syndrome
- Perinatal asphyxia
- Pneumonia/sepsis
- RDS
- Congenital diaphragmatic hernia
- Pulmonary hypoplasia

---

# 4. Management of PPHN

## Step 1 — Correct the cause

- Adequate oxygenation
- Lung recruitment
- Mechanical ventilation when required
- Surfactant if indicated
- Treat sepsis/pneumonia
- Maintain temperature and glucose
- Correct acidosis
- Maintain adequate systemic BP and cardiac output

**Avoid:**
- Severe hypoxemia
- Acidosis
- Hypothermia
- Excessive lung inflation

---

## Step 2 — Pulmonary vasodilation

### First-line specific drug: INHALED NITRIC OXIDE (iNO)

**NO → ↑ cGMP → pulmonary smooth-muscle relaxation**

Advantages:
- Selective pulmonary vasodilator
- Improves oxygenation
- ↓ right-to-left shunting
- ↓ need for ECMO

**Typical starting dose: 20 ppm**

Usually used in late-preterm/term infants with hypoxemic respiratory failure despite optimized ventilation.

> **PPHN → iNO = key drug**

---

## 5. Drugs for iNO-refractory PPHN

| Drug | Mechanism | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Sildenafil** | PDE-5 inhibitor → ↑ cGMP | iNO-refractory PPHN / where iNO unavailable |
| **Milrinone** | PDE-3 inhibitor → ↑ cAMP | Particularly useful with LV dysfunction / low cardiac output |
| **Iloprost** | Prostacyclin analogue → ↑ cAMP | Refractory PPHN |
| **Epoprostenol / treprostinil** | Prostacyclin pathway | Severe refractory cases |
| **PGE₁ (alprostadil)** | Maintains ductal patency | Selected severe RV failure / ductal-dependent physiology |
| **Vasopressors/inotropes** | ↑ systemic vascular pressure | Support systemic perfusion and reduce R→L shunting |

### Important distinction

**Sildenafil in PPHN ≠ first-line.**

> **First-line → iNO**

> **iNO-refractory → sildenafil ± other pulmonary vasodilators**

Neonatal evidence for sildenafil/prostacyclin/milrinone remains considerably less robust than for iNO. iNO remains the only FDA-approved pulmonary vasodilator specifically for neonatal PPHN. 

---

# 6. Refractory PPHN

If severe hypoxemia persists despite:

> Optimal ventilation + lung recruitment + iNO + cardiovascular support

→ **ECMO**

---

# 7. Ultra-high-yield comparison

| Feature | Adult PAH | PPHN |
|---|---|---|
| Basic problem | Chronic pulmonary vascular disease | Failure of normal neonatal PVR fall |
| Main pathology | Vascular remodeling | Persistent fetal pulmonary circulation |
| Major pathways | ↓NO, ↓PGI₂, ↑ET-1, ↑activin signaling | ↑PVR with abnormal vasoconstriction |
| Key drug | **Combination PAH therapy** | **iNO** |
| NO pathway | Sildenafil/tadalafil, riociguat | **iNO** |
| Prostacyclin | Epoprostenol, treprostinil, iloprost, selexipag | Refractory cases |
| Endothelin blockade | Bosentan/ambrisentan/macitentan | Not routine |
| New drug | **Sotatercept** | No comparable newly established neonatal drug |
| Severe refractory disease | Advanced PAH therapy/transplant | **ECMO** |

## One-line memory

**PAH:**
> **ET-1 ↑ + NO ↓ + PGI₂ ↓ + Activin signaling ↑ → endothelin blocker + PDE5/sGC + prostacyclin + sotatercept**

**PPHN:**
> **PVR fails to fall → R→L shunt → hypoxemia → O₂ + lung recruitment + iNO → sildenafil/other agents if refractory → ECMO**
