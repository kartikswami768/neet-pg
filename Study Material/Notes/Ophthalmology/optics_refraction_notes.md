# Optics and Refraction

## 1. Optics of the Eye

### Refractive power and optical media

The eye behaves as a converging optical system. The **cornea contributes maximally to total ocular refraction** because the largest refractive-index change occurs at the **air–cornea interface**.

| Medium          | Refractive index |
| --------------- | ---------------: |
| Air             |              1.0 |
| Cornea          |            1.376 |
| Aqueous humour  |            1.336 |
| Lens — average  |             1.39 |
| Lens cortex     |             1.38 |
| Lens nucleus    |             1.40 |
| Vitreous humour |            1.337 |

Corneal power is approximately **+43 to +44 D**. In the Gullstrand schematic representation, the anterior corneal surface contributes **+48.83 D**, the posterior corneal surface **−5.88 D**, and the lens contributes approximately **+16 to +19 D** depending on the representation.

**Purkinje images** arise from reflections at four refracting surfaces:
1. Anterior surface of cornea
2. Posterior surface of cornea
3. Anterior surface of lens
4. Posterior surface of lens — inverted image

|---|---|---:|

| II | Posterior cornea | −5.88 D |

**Listing’s reduced eye:**
- Total ocular power = **58.6 D**.
- All elements are measured from the anterior corneal surface.
- Major determinants of ocular power are the **curvature of the anterior corneal surface** and **axial length of the eyeball**.
- Principal point = **1.35 mm** from the anterior corneal surface.
- Nodal point = **7.08 mm** from the anterior corneal surface.
- Focal point = **24.13 mm** from the anterior corneal surface.

### Axes of the eye
- **Anatomical/optical axis:** anatomical axis of the eye.
- **Visual axis:** line joining the fixation point to the fovea through the nodal point.
- **Pupillary axis:** axis through the centre of the pupil.
- The visual axis bisects the fovea.
**Angles:**
- **Angle α (alpha):** between the anatomical/optical axis and visual axis at the nodal point.
- **Angle κ (kappa):** between the pupillary axis/pupillary line and the visual axis at the cornea.
- The fovea lies slightly temporal to the optic disc, producing these angular relationships.
- A large positive kappa is associated with pseudoexotropia and is seen in hypermetropia.
- A large negative kappa is associated with pseudoesotropia and is seen in myopia.

## 2. Refractive Error: Terminology and Classification

### Basic terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Emmetropia** | Absence of refractive error |
| **Ametropia** | Presence of refractive error: myopia, hypermetropia or astigmatism |
| **Anisometropia** | Difference in refractive error between the two eyes |
| **Aniseikonia** | Difference in image size between the two eyes |
| **Anisocoria** | Difference in pupil size between the two eyes |
| **Astigmatism** | Difference in refractive error in different axes of the same eye |

Clinically significant anisometropia is a difference of **>2.5 D** between the two eyes. A **2.5 D** difference corresponds to approximately **5% difference in image size (aniseikonia)**; fusion is unable to compensate beyond this level.

### Causes of refractive errors

| Cause | Optical/structural change | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Axial** | Change in eyeball length; longer eye → myopia, shorter eye → hypermetropia | Most common |
| **Curvatural** | Change in corneal shape/curvature | Second most common |
| **Index** | Change in lens refractive index; classically nuclear cataract | Third |
| **Positional** | Change in lens position | Most rare |

**Second sight:** a previously presbyopic patient becomes able to read near objects without glasses. Nuclear cataract increases lens refractive index and produces **index myopia**, producing this apparent improvement in near vision.

## 3. Myopia

### Optical basis

- The eyeball is longer than normal, so parallel rays focus **in front of the retina**.
- Corrected with a **concave/negative (diverging) lens**.
- **Axial myopia** is the most common type.
- **1 mm increase in axial length ≈ 3 D myopia.**

| Feature | Myopia | Hypermetropia |
|---|---|---|
| Ocular power | More | Less |
| Image | In front of retina | Behind retina |
| Corrective lens | − / concave / diverging | + / convex / converging |
| Axial length | Increased | Decreased |
| Corneal curvature | Increased | Decreased |
| Lens refractive index | Increased in index myopia | Decreased in the comparative scheme |
| Lens position | Anterior | Posterior |

Anterior lens displacement producing myopia is associated with **Weill–Marchesani syndrome**. Increased lens refractive index and index myopia are classically associated with **nuclear cataract**.

## 4. Hypermetropia

- The eyeball is shorter than normal.
- Parallel rays focus **behind the retina**.
- Corrected with a **convex/positive (converging) lens**.
- Hypermetropia is the **most common refractive error at birth**.
- Hypermetropia at birth is approximately **+2.5 to +3 D**.
- At birth, axial length is approximately **17 mm**, with **2–3 D hypermetropia**; the eye becomes emmetropic by approximately **5–6 years**.
- Small eyes have shallow anterior chambers and are predisposed to **angle-closure glaucoma**.
- Fundus: **silk-shot retina**.
- Optic disc may appear as **pseudopapillitis/pseudopapilledema**.
- Large positive kappa may produce pseudoexotropia.

## 5. Astigmatism

Astigmatism is a refractive error in which optical power differs between meridians, producing two principal focal lines/points rather than a single point focus.

The cornea is normally spherical at birth and becomes more oval with normal lid pressure. Astigmatism is corrected with **cylindrical lenses**, which may be positive or negative, or with toric contact lenses/toric IOLs.

### Sturm’s conoid

When light passes through an astigmatic/toric surface, the two principal meridians have different powers. The rays form two focal lines at different locations, with a **circle of least diffusion** between them. The refractive state changes progressively between the two focal lines.

When the vertical meridian is more strongly curved, it has greater power (**Vc > Hc; Vp > Hp**); vertical rays reach focus first and horizontal rays later.

> **Diagram omitted:** Astigmatism and Sturm’s conoid.
### Regular and irregular astigmatism

| Type | Principal meridians / foci | Typical cause |
|---|---|---|
| **Regular** | Two principal meridians are perpendicular; two primary focal lines are present | Normal lid pressure/corneal shape |
| **Irregular** | Principal meridians are not perpendicular; multiple/non-primary foci | Corneal scar, keratoconus; corneal/retinal scarring |

### With-the-rule, against-the-rule and oblique astigmatism

| Type | Steeper meridian | Flatter meridian | Cylindrical notation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **With-the-rule (WTR)** | Vertical, 90° | Horizontal, 180° | Minus cylinder axis 180° / plus cylinder axis 90° |
| **Against-the-rule (ATR)** | Horizontal, 180° | Vertical, 90° | Minus cylinder axis 90° / plus cylinder axis 180° |
| **Oblique** | Principal meridians are neither horizontal nor vertical | — | Oblique axes |

Mnemonic: **“With CV, against VC.”**

### Classification by focal position

| Type | Focal point 1 | Focal point 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Simple myopic** | On retina | In front of retina |
| **Simple hypermetropic** | On retina | Behind retina |
| **Compound myopic** | In front of retina | In front of retina, at a different point |
| **Compound hypermetropic** | Behind retina | Behind retina, at a different point |
| **Mixed** | In front of retina | Behind retina |

**High-yield rule:**
- Simple = one focus on the retina, the other off the retina.
- Compound = both foci on the same side of the retina.
- Mixed = one focus in front and one behind the retina.

### Identifying the type from a spectacle prescription

| Prescription pattern | Type |
|---|---|
| 0.00 D sphere / −1.50 D cylinder | Simple myopic astigmatism |
| −1.00 D sphere / −0.50 D cylinder | Compound myopic astigmatism |
| 0.00 D sphere / +2.00 D cylinder | Simple hypermetropic astigmatism |
| +1.50 D sphere / +3.50 D cylinder | Compound hypermetropic astigmatism |
| +2.00 D sphere / −3.00 D cylinder | Mixed astigmatism |

A numerical rule given for mixed astigmatism is: **sphere and cylinder have opposite signs AND the absolute cylinder value is greater than the absolute spherical value**.

Example:
- −3.00 D sphere / +1.00 D cylinder → not mixed because |cylinder| < |sphere|.
- −1.00 D sphere / +3.00 D cylinder → mixed because signs are opposite and |cylinder| > |sphere|.

### Worked astigmatism example

For **−3 D sphere +2 D cylinder at 180°**:
1. Convert the prescription into the two principal meridians.
2. The −3 D spherical component applies in both meridians.
3. A +2 D cylinder at 180° acts in the meridian 90° away, giving powers of **−1 D and −3 D** in the two principal meridians.
4. Both focal positions are therefore in front of the retina at different locations.
5. The error is **compound myopic astigmatism**.

# 7. Accommodation

Accommodation increases ocular power for near vision.

| Parameter | Far vision | Near vision |
|---|---|---|
| Ciliary muscle | Relaxes | Contracts |
| Ciliary ring | Expands | Constricts |
| Zonules | Taut | Lax |
| Lens | Thin/flat, lower power | Thick/rounder, more convex, higher power |
| Pupil | Dilates | Constricts |

Mechanism for near vision: **ciliary muscle contracts → ciliary ring narrows → zonules relax → lens becomes thicker/more convex → refractive power increases**.

> **Diagram omitted:** Accommodation: far and near.
## Amplitude of accommodation

The amplitude of accommodation is the difference between the accommodative/dioptric value at the near point (**proximum, P**) and that at the far point (**remotum, R**):

**A = P − R**

For a near point of 20 cm, the dioptric value is calculated from the reciprocal of the focal distance: For a near point of **20 cm**:

**P = 100 / 20 = 5 D**

For a hypermetrope of **+4 D**, the far point lies behind the retina and is represented with a negative sign:

**R = −4 D**

Therefore:

**A = 5 − (−4) = 9 D**

> **Diagram omitted:** Accommodation amplitude and retinoscopy calculation.
## Presbyopia

- Physiological insufficiency of accommodation causing difficulty with near vision.
- Typically becomes clinically relevant after **40 years**.
- Corrected with **convex/positive near addition or reading spectacles**.
- A near add of approximately **+1 D around 40–45 years** is given; one age-based scheme increases the add by **0.5 D every 5 years**.

## Spasm of accommodation / pseudomyopia

- Also called **pseudomyopia**.
- Persistent ciliary-muscle spasm produces excessive accommodation and difficulty with distance vision.
- Cycloplegics, particularly **atropine**, relax the ciliary muscle.

## 8. Visual Acuity and Its Optical Relationship to Refraction

### Snellen visual acuity

- Standard examination distance = **6 m**.
- Normal acuity is given as **6/5 or 6/6**.
- Least recordable vision is **1/60**; poorer vision is recorded as finger counting, hand movements and perception of light.
- Each Snellen optotype subtends **5 minutes of arc** at the nodal point at its designated viewing distance.
- Each component stroke/gap of the optotype subtends approximately **1 minute of arc**.
- A 6/60 line at 6 m subtends **50 minutes of arc** when the 5-minute optotype angle is scaled by 60/6.
- A 6/18 line at 6 m subtends **15 minutes of arc**.

**LogMAR chart:** minimum-angle-of-resolution based; 0 represents good vision, while higher values represent poorer vision; five letters are used per row.

### Near visual acuity

- Examination distance = approximately **30–35 cm**.
- Snellen near chart: **N6 to N36**; N6 is best and N36 is poorest.
- Jaeger chart: **J1–J7**.

### Infant and preschool visual assessment

- Optokinetic nystagmus drum.
- Preferential-looking tests such as **Teller acuity cards** and **Keeler gratings**.
- Cardiff acuity test for preschool children.
- Snellen E chart and Landolt C ring when letter recognition is difficult.
- Menace reflex develops at approximately **4–6 months**.

### Pinhole test

The pinhole eliminates peripheral rays and allows more central rays to reach the eye.

- Pinhole size is approximately **1 mm** for refractive testing; a general pinhole is approximately **1–2.5 mm**.
- Improvement in visual acuity with pinhole suggests **residual refractive error**.
- Failure to improve suggests that reduced vision is not primarily due to uncorrected refractive error and raises the possibility of ocular/macular pathology.

## 9. Optical Axes and Ophthalmoscopic Optics

### Indirect ophthalmoscopy

Indirect ophthalmoscopy produces a **real, inverted image**, with magnification around **3–5×**. The field is approximately **8 disc diameters (8 DD)**; 1 DD is approximately 1.5 mm.

Magnification is given by:

**Magnification = Power of eye / Power of condensing lens**

### Direct ophthalmoscopy

- Used to examine the **central retina**.
- Magnification is given as approximately **Power / 4**.
- Greater magnification produces a smaller field, approximately **2 DD**.
- A “distance direct ophthalmoscope” examination is performed at approximately **25 cm**.
- Posterior subcapsular cataract and oil-droplet reflex are specifically mentioned as findings detectable with the direct ophthalmoscope.

## 10. Keratometry and Corneal Optical Assessment

- **Keratometry** measures corneal curvature.
- Corneal curvature is directly proportional to corneal power.
- Astigmatism is present when the principal corneal curvatures differ; a simple description is **vertical curvature ≠ horizontal curvature**.
- Keratometry is used in **IOL power calculation** along with axial-length measurement.
- Keratometry is also increased in keratoconus because the cornea becomes steeper.
- Corneal topography evaluates the corneal surface/shape using Placido-disc methods.

For cataract biometry:
- Keratometry → corneal curvature.
- A-scan ultrasonography → axial length; normal axial length approximately **24 mm**.
- Formulae listed: **SRK-T, Hoffer Q, Holladay, Haigis-L**; Hoffer Q is associated with AL <22 mm, Holladay with AL >24.5 mm, and Haigis-L with post-refractive-surgery cases.

### 11. Objective Refraction: Retinoscopy / Skiascopy

**Retinoscopy** is an objective method of determining refractive error. It is also called **skiascopy** or the shadow test.

#### Technique

- A retinoscope is placed approximately **1 m** from the patient; **2/3 m** is also used.
- The examiner observes the red reflex and compares its movement with movement of the retinoscope/light.
- A trial lens is introduced to neutralize the reflex movement.
- The retinoscopic reading is the lens power that neutralizes the observed movement.

#### Reflex movement

| Reflex | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Against motion** | Myopia >1 D at 1 m working distance |
| **No movement / neutral** | Approximately 1 D myopia at 1 m |
| **With motion** | Myopia <1 D, emmetropia or hypermetropia |

At **1 m**, neutralization corresponds to approximately **−1 D** because of the working-distance effect.

> **Diagram omitted:** Retinoscopy reflex movement.
#### Retinoscopy correction factors

The corrected retinoscopic value is given as:

**Corrected retinoscopic value (RF) = RF − C₁ − C₂**

Where:
- **C₁ = 1/d**, the working-distance correction factor.
- **d** = examination distance in metres.
- **C₂** = tonus allowance/cycloplegic correction used to account for residual accommodative tone.

| Cycloplegic | C₂ |
|---|---:|
| Atropine | 1.0 D |
| Cyclopentolate | 0.75 D |
| Homatropine | 0.50 D |
| Tropicamide | No tonus allowance |

Examples of distance correction:
- 1 m → C₁ = 1 D.
- 2/3 m → C₁ ≈ 1.5 D.

#### Worked retinoscopy correction and transposition

If retinoscopy gives **−5 D and −4 D** at a working distance of 2/3 m and a total correction factor of **2 D** is used:

- Corrected principal meridians = −5 − 2 = **−7 D** and −4 − 2 = **−6 D**.
- Write the prescription as **−6 D sphere / −1 D cylinder at 180°**.
- Transpose it by adding sphere and cylinder: −6 + (−1) = **−7 D**.
- Change the cylinder sign: −1 D → **+1 D**.
- Change the axis by 90°: 180° → **90°**.
- Final transposed form = **−7 D sphere / +1 D cylinder at 90°**.

> **Diagram omitted:** Retinoscopy correction and transposition.
#### Retinoscopy instruments

- Reflecting-mirror retinoscope.
- Priestley–Smith retinoscope.
- Self-illuminated **streak retinoscope** — most commonly used.
- Automated refractometers provide automated objective refractive assessment.

The streak retinoscope produces vertical/horizontal light beams; examination distance is approximately **1 m**.

### 12. Subjective Refraction

Subjective refraction depends on the patient's visual response and follows objective refraction.

#### Basic method

1. **Trial-and-error method** using trial lenses/trial frame.
2. Refine the **cylinder**.
3. Refine the **sphere**.

> **Diagram omitted:** Refraction workflow and spectacle prescriptions.
#### Spectacle lens patterns

| Prescription | Refractive error |
|---|---|
| − spherical | Myopia |
| + spherical | Hypermetropia |
| − cylinder | Simple myopic astigmatism |
| + cylinder | Simple hypermetropic astigmatism |
| − sphere / − cylinder | Compound myopic astigmatism |
| + sphere / + cylinder | Compound hypermetropic astigmatism |
| − sphere / + cylinder **or** + sphere / − cylinder | Mixed astigmatism |

#### Refinement of cylinder

##### Jackson cross-cylinder (JCC)

The combinations include:
- **−0.50 D sphere / +1.00 D cylinder**.
- **−0.25 D sphere / +0.50 D cylinder**.

JCC is used to refine cylinder power and axis.

##### Astigmatic fan test

The cylinder is refined until the patient can see the fan axes clearly/equally.

> **Diagram omitted:** JCC and astigmatic fan.
#### Refinement of sphere: duochrome test

The duochrome test uses red and green halves to refine the spherical component.

- **Green better → residual hypermetropia**.
- **Red better → residual myopia**.

The test relies on chromatic dispersion and differential focus of the two colours.

#### Pinhole during subjective assessment

A **1-mm pinhole** is used for refraction refinement.
- VA improves → residual refractive error.
- VA decreases/does not improve → macular/ocular pathology is more likely.

### 13. Prism and Prismatic Clinical Optics


#### Prism dioptre relationships used clinically

- **1 prism dioptre ≈ 0.5°** of deviation.
- **1 mm** displacement of the corneal light reflex corresponds to approximately **7° squint / 14 prism dioptres**.
- In Hirschberg assessment:
  - Reflex at pupillary margin → **15° ≈ 30 PD**.
  - Reflex at mid-iris → **30° ≈ 60 PD**.
  - Reflex at limbus → **45° ≈ 90 PD**.
- Degrees can therefore be converted to prism dioptres by multiplying by **2** in this clinical scheme.

#### Prism bar cover test

- Used to measure ocular deviation/tropia; it is the most accurate test for degree of tropia.
- **Base of prism is placed opposite to the direction of deviation.**
- **Degree of tropia = 2 × prism power at which the deviated eye becomes straight.**
- The mnemonic **DOOB: Deviation Opposite Of Base** is used to remember prism orientation.

#### Four-prism base-out test

With the patient fixating straight ahead, a base-out prism causes a conjugate movement of the eyes; the recovery movement is used to assess binocular function.

> **Diagram omitted:** Prism base-out test.
Prisms are also listed as an optical treatment step in squint after refractive correction, occlusion/orthoptic measures and before surgical correction.

# 14. Optical Aberrations and Distortion Mentioned

## Spherical aberration

A **pin-cushion effect** is attributed to **high spherical aberration**.

## Chromatic aberration

Thick high-plus aphakic spectacle lenses can produce **chromatic aberration**, with altered perception of colours.

## Prismatic dispersion

Corneal oedema can produce **halos around lights** from prismatic dispersion.

## Aphakic spectacle distortion

The peripheral prismatic effect of a thick high-plus lens can produce ring scotoma and the jack-in-the-box phenomenon.

## 15. Refractive Changes Associated with the Lens

### Index myopia

Nuclear cataract increases the refractive index of the lens and can produce **index myopia**. This may create the phenomenon of second sight in a previously presbyopic patient.

### Lens position

- Anterior displacement → myopic shift; **Weill–Marchesani syndrome** is associated with anterior lens displacement.
- Posterior displacement → hypermetropic shift.

### Pseudophakia

After crystalline-lens removal, a posterior chamber IOL restores the missing optical element while avoiding the large image magnification and peripheral prism effects of aphakic spectacles. A posterior chamber, in-the-bag IOL is the preferred correction.

## 16. Refractive Surgery and Myopia Control

### Refractive surgery eligibility

Criteria include:
- Age **>18 years**.
- Stable refraction for at least **1 year**.
- No major ocular comorbidity such as keratoconus, herpetic keratitis, corneal dystrophy/degeneration, cataract, glaucoma, uveitis, lagophthalmos, dry eye or blepharitis.
- No major systemic contraindication such as diabetes mellitus, keloid tendency, pregnancy/lactation, autoimmune disease or immunosuppression.
- **Keratoconus** is a strict contraindication.
- Diabetes is contraindicated because of delayed healing.

### Refractive-surgery categories

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Cornea-based, incision | Radial keratotomy |
| Cornea-based, excimer laser | PRK, LASIK |
| Cornea-based, femtosecond laser | SMILE, SILK |
| Lenticular-based | Phakic IOL/ICL, clear-lens extraction |
| Combined | Bioptics |

### FDA limits for cornea-based surgery given here

| Refractive error | Upper limit |
|---|---:|
| Myopia | < −12 D |
| Hypermetropia | < +6 D |
| Astigmatism | < 6 D |

### Lasers

| Laser | Type | Wavelength |
|---|---|---:|
| Excimer | Argon fluoride (ArF), excited dimer | 193 nm |
| Femtosecond | Neodymium:glass (Nd:Glass) | 1053 nm |

### Major procedures

**Radial keratotomy:** peripheral radial corneal incisions flatten the cornea and reduce converging power; now obsolete and contraindicated in boxers because of traumatic rupture risk.

**PRK:** epithelium is removed and excimer laser ablates corneal stroma directly. Vision improves as epithelium heals over approximately **7–10 days**. Healing discomfort is a disadvantage. PRK is preferred in defence personnel because there is no flap that can later be dislodged.

**LASIK:** a corneal flap is created with a microkeratome or femtosecond laser; excimer laser ablation is performed in the stromal bed; the flap is repositioned. It is the most popular refractive surgery.

**SMILE:** femtosecond laser creates two stromal planes and a lens-shaped biconvex lenticule; the lenticule is removed through a small incision without creating a flap, preserving greater postoperative corneal strength.

**ICL:** placed behind the iris and over the natural lens; useful for high refractive errors or when corneal laser surgery is unsuitable. Minimum anterior chamber depth given = **2.8 mm**.

**INTACS:** intracorneal stromal ring segments.

### Myopia control

Used for progressive myopia in children/young patients:

| Method | Mechanism / key point |
|---|---|
| Reduced near work/screen time | Lifestyle modification; first/basic measure |
| Low-dose atropine | Relaxation of ciliary muscle and prevention of axial elongation; **0.01–0.05%**, once nightly |
| Myopia-control spectacle lenses | Peripheral defocus; concentric rings focus centrally while altering peripheral focus |
| Orthokeratology | Hard lenses worn overnight reshape the cornea temporarily and reduce axial elongation |

#### 19. Examination and Revision Pearls

- **Cornea provides the largest refractive contribution** because the air–cornea interface has the largest refractive-index change.
- **Total ocular power in the Listing reduced eye = 58.6 D.**
- **Purkinje images = four reflections** from the anterior/posterior cornea and anterior/posterior lens.
- **Myopia:** image in front of retina → minus/concave lens.
- **Hypermetropia:** image behind retina → plus/convex lens.
- **1 mm axial elongation ≈ 3 D myopia.**
- **WTR:** vertical steep; minus-cylinder axis 180°.
- **ATR:** horizontal steep; minus-cylinder axis 90°.
- **Simple:** one focus on retina; **compound:** both on same side; **mixed:** one on each side.
- **Sturm’s conoid** describes the two focal lines of astigmatism and the circle of least diffusion between them.
- **Aphakic spectacles** cause high magnification, peripheral prism, ring scotoma and jack-in-the-box phenomenon.
- **Retinoscopy:** against movement → >1 D myopia at 1 m; neutral → 1 D myopia; with movement → <1 D myopia/emmetropia/hypermetropia.
- **Duochrome:** green better → residual hypermetropia; red better → residual myopia.
- **JCC** refines cylinder; **astigmatic fan** refines cylindrical correction.
- **Pinhole improvement** supports residual refractive error.
- **Accommodation:** ciliary contraction → zonular relaxation → thicker/more convex lens → increased power.
- **Presbyopia:** age-related accommodative insufficiency → plus near correction.
- **Index myopia** is classically associated with nuclear cataract and may produce second sight.
- **Keratometry** measures corneal curvature and contributes to IOL power calculation.
- **Prism bar cover test** is used to quantify ocular deviation; prism base is placed opposite the direction of deviation.
