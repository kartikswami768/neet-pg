# Glaucoma

## 1. Definition and Core Concept

**Glaucoma = progressive glaucomatous optic neuropathy + characteristic irreversible visual-field loss.**

- **IOP is the major modifiable risk factor**, but glaucoma can occur with normal IOP → **normal-tension glaucoma (NTG)**.
- Normal IOP: **10–21 mmHg** (also quoted as 11–21 mmHg).
- Diurnal variation: up to **5 mmHg**, usually higher in the morning.
- Glaucoma assessment rests on:
  1. **IOP**
  2. **Optic nerve/optic disc structural damage**
  3. **Visual-field defects**
- Established structural and functional damage is **irreversible**.
- Treatment aims to maintain a **target IOP** that prevents progression without compromising quality of life.

---

## 2. Classification

### 2.1 Broad Classification

| Group                        | Types                           |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Congenital/developmental** | Congenital, infantile, juvenile |
| **Acquired**                 | Primary or secondary            |
| **Primary**                  | Open-angle or angle-closure     |
| **Secondary**                | Open-angle or angle-closure     |

### 2.2 Congenital, Infantile and Juvenile Glaucoma

- **True congenital:** present at birth.
- **Infantile:** ~3 months–3 years.
- **Juvenile:** ~3 years–adolescence.
- **Buphthalmos** occurs because the elastic infantile sclera stretches in response to raised IOP, especially up to ~3 years of age.

### 2.3 Open-Angle vs Angle-Closure Glaucoma

| Feature          | Open-angle glaucoma            | Angle-closure glaucoma |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Angle            | Open                           | Narrow/closed          |
| Main pathology   | Trabecular outflow dysfunction | Iris obstructs angle   |
| Anterior chamber | Deep                           | Shallow                |
| Laser principle  | Trabeculoplasty                | Iridotomy/iridoplasty  |

### 2.4 Ocular Hypertension vs POAG vs NTG

| Feature             | Ocular hypertension | POAG     | Normal-tension glaucoma |
| ------------------- | ------------------- | -------- | ----------------------- |
| IOP                 | >21 mmHg            | Elevated | Persistently <21 mmHg   |
| Optic disc damage   | Absent              | Present  | Present                 |
| Visual-field defect | Absent              | Present  | Present                 |

> **Key concept:** High IOP alone does not equal glaucoma, and normal IOP does not exclude glaucoma.

## 3. Risk Factors

### 3.1 General Risk Factors

- Raised IOP
- Age >40 years
- Positive family history
- High myopia

### 3.2 Risk Factors for Angle Closure

- Hypermetropia / small eye
- Shallow anterior chamber
- Narrow angle
- Female sex
- Anteriorly placed lens–iris diaphragm

#### Why Does Hypermetropia Predispose to Angle Closure?

**Small globe → shallow anterior chamber → narrow angle → restricted access of aqueous to trabecular meshwork.**

## 4. Aqueous Humour

### 4.1 Production

Aqueous humour is produced mainly by the **non-pigmented ciliary epithelium of the ciliary processes**.

| Mechanism | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Active secretion** | Major |
| Ultrafiltration | Minor |
| Diffusion | Minor |

- Active secretion involves ATP-dependent ion transport, including Na⁺/K⁺ transport.
- Production rate: approximately **2–3 µL/min**.

### 4.2 Circulation

**Ciliary processes → posterior chamber → pupil → anterior chamber → outflow pathways**

### 4.3 Outflow Pathways

| Pathway | Contribution | Route | IOP dependence | Important drugs/procedures |
|---|---:|---|---|---|
| **Trabecular/conventional** | ~80–90% | TM → Schlemm canal → collector/aqueous veins → episcleral veins | IOP-dependent | Pilocarpine, ROCK inhibitors, trabeculoplasty |
| **Uveoscleral** | ~10–20% | Ciliary body/supraciliary space → sclera | IOP-independent | Prostaglandin analogues |

#### Trabecular Pathway

**Trabecular meshwork → Schlemm canal → collector/aqueous veins → episcleral veins**

The **iridocorneal angle** is the key anatomical site for conventional aqueous outflow.

## 5. Anterior Chamber Angle and Gonioscopy

### 5.1 Angle Structures

From posterior/deep to anterior/superficial:

1. **Iris root**
2. **Ciliary body band**
3. **Scleral spur**
4. **Trabecular meshwork**
5. **Schwalbe line**

**Schwalbe line = peripheral termination of Descemet membrane.**

### 5.2 Gonioscopy

**Gonioscopy is the best method for direct examination of the anterior chamber angle.**

#### Principle

- The normal cornea causes **total internal reflection**, preventing direct visualization of the angle.
- Gonioscopy changes the optical interface and allows visualization.
- Critical angle of cornea: approximately **46°**.
- Gonioscopy does **not require pupil dilation**.
- Dilated pupil is listed as a contraindication in the source material.

#### Types of Gonioscopy

| Type | View | Setting | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | Direct | Supine/surgery | Koeppe, Barkan, Richardson-Shaffer, Swan-Jacob |
| **Indirect** | Mirrored/inverted | Slit lamp | Goldmann, Zeiss, Posner, Sussman |

### 5.3 Other Methods of Angle Assessment

#### Van Herick Method

Compares **peripheral anterior chamber depth (PACD)** with **corneal thickness (CT)**.

- **PACD = CT → open angle**
- **PACD <¼ CT → shallow/closing angle**

#### Oblique Flashlight Test

Torch is shone from temporal side:

- Nasal iris illuminated → **open angle**
- Nasal iris eclipse/shadow → **closed/narrow angle**

#### Imaging

- **UBM:** angle and ciliary-body anatomy; useful for plateau iris.
- **AS-OCT:** structural imaging of the anterior segment and angle.

## 6. Intraocular Pressure and Tonometry

### 6.1 Tonometry Overview

| Instrument | Key Point |
|---|---|
| **Schiotz** | Indentation; affected by scleral rigidity; less preferred |
| **Goldmann** | Gold standard; applanation; Imbert-Fick; 3.06-mm diameter |
| **Perkins** | Hand-held applanation; children/OT/anaesthesia |
| **Tonopen** | Portable; irregular/scarred cornea; children |
| **Mackay-Marg** | Irregular/oedematous cornea |
| **Pascal** | Dynamic contour; relatively CCT-independent; useful post-LASIK |
| **Rebound** | Useful for self-monitoring |
| **Non-contact** | Air-puff; screening |
| **Transpalpebral** | Through eyelid; uncommon |
| **Ocular Response Analyzer** | Based on corneal hysteresis |

### 6.2 Goldmann Applanation Tonometry

**Gold standard for IOP measurement.**

#### Principle

Based on the **Imbert-Fick law**:

**Pressure = Force / Area applanated**

#### Important Facts

- Uses **fluorescein + cobalt-blue illumination**.
- Fixed applanation diameter: **3.06 mm**.
- **Thin cornea → IOP underestimated.**
- **Thick cornea → IOP overestimated.**

## 7. Glaucomatous Optic Neuropathy

### 7.1 Examination of the Optic Disc

- **Direct ophthalmoscopy:** ~15× magnification; no binocular depth perception.
- Slit-lamp biomicroscopy with **+90 D lens**.
- Confocal scanning laser tomography.
- Spectral-domain OCT.

### 7.2 Glaucomatous Cupping

| Finding | Key Point |
|---|---|
| **C/D ratio** | Normal ~0.3; suspicious >0.5 |
| **Inter-eye C/D asymmetry** | >0.2 suspicious |
| Neuroretinal rim loss | Inferior rim classically emphasized |
| Vertical cupping | Due to early arcuate fibre involvement |
| Deep cup | Neural tissue loss |
| Laminar dot sign | Visible lamina cribrosa pores |
| Nasal vessel shifting | Optic nerve/laminar remodelling |
| Bayonetting | Sharp vessel angulation/kinking |
| Peripapillary atrophy | Alpha/beta zones |
| RNFL striation loss | Retinal nerve fibre loss |
| Splinter haemorrhage | Disc-margin haemorrhage |

#### Lamina Cribrosa

Remodelling contributes to:

- Deepening of the cup.
- Nasal displacement of retinal vessels.
- Visible laminar pores.

> **Core progression:** Neural tissue loss → rim thinning → vertical cupping → laminar/vessel changes → characteristic field loss.

---

## 8. Visual Field Defects

### 8.1 Normal Visual Field

| Direction | Approximate Extent |
|---|---:|
| Temporal | **100°** |
| Inferior | **70°** |
| Nasal | **60°** |
| Superior | **50°** |

- Normal field: horizontally oval with inferonasal notch.
- Blind spot: approximately **10–20°** from fixation.
- Fovea → blind spot: approximately **2 disc diameters / 3 mm**.

### 8.2 Perimetry

#### Kinetic Perimetry

- Moving target.
- Classic example: **Goldmann perimeter**.

#### Static Perimetry

- Stationary target with threshold testing.
- Examples:
  - **Humphrey Field Analyzer**
  - Octopus
  - Dicon

**Humphrey automated perimetry** is emphasized as the clinical gold standard.

An **isopter** is a contour line connecting areas of equal visual-field sensitivity.

### 8.3 Glaucomatous Field Progression

**Isopter contraction → peripheral field loss → baring of blind spot → paracentral scotoma → Seidel scotoma → Bjerrum/arcuate scotoma → double arcuate/ring scotoma → Roenne nasal step → central visual loss → temporal island**

| Defect | Characteristic |
|---|---|
| **Paracentral scotoma** | Early defect in Bjerrum area |
| **Seidel scotoma** | Comma/sickle-shaped; joins blind spot |
| **Bjerrum/arcuate scotoma** | Arc-shaped; follows arcuate fibres |
| **Double arcuate/ring scotoma** | Superior + inferior arcuate defects |
| **Roenne nasal step** | Step between superior and inferior hemifields |
| **Temporal island** | Last remaining field |

**Bjerrum area:** central approximately **20–30°**.

## 9. Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma

POAG is a **chronic glaucoma with an open anterior chamber angle despite defective trabecular outflow**.

### 9.1 Typical Profile

- Usually >50 years.
- Positive family history may occur.
- Genes:
  - **MYOC / myocilin**
  - Optineurin
  - WDR36

### 9.2 Symptoms Described

- Headache
- Delayed dark adaptation
- Frequent change in presbyopic spectacles

### 9.3 IOP

- IOP >21 mmHg, or
- Inter-eye difference >5 mmHg highlighted in the source.

### 9.4 Optic Disc

- Inferior rim loss
- Vertical cupping
- C/D >0.5
- Bayonetting
- Laminar dot sign
- Peripapillary atrophy

### 9.5 Visual Field

Follows the characteristic glaucomatous sequence:

**Paracentral → Seidel → arcuate/Bjerrum → double arcuate → nasal step → central loss → temporal island**

---

## 10. Normal-Tension Glaucoma

- IOP **persistently <21 mmHg**.
- Glaucomatous optic-disc changes present.
- Characteristic visual-field defects present.
- **Perfusion factors other than IOP** are important.

> Glaucomatous structural and functional damage despite normal-range measured IOP.

---

## 11. Ocular Hypertension

- IOP **>21 mmHg**.
- Normal optic disc.
- No visual-field defect.

> **Ocular hypertension = raised IOP without established glaucomatous damage.**

---

## 12. Primary Angle-Closure Disease

### 12.1 Pupillary-Block Mechanism

**Lens–iris contact → impaired posterior-to-anterior aqueous flow → posterior iris pressure ↑ → iris bombe → iridotrabecular contact → PAS**

#### Peripheral Anterior Synechiae

Persistent contact between the peripheral iris and angle structures can produce **PAS**.

### 12.2 Plateau Iris

- Anteriorly positioned/rotated ciliary body.
- Causes persistent angle crowding.
- Angle closure can persist even after relief of pupillary block.

### 12.3 Primary Angle-Closure Spectrum

| Condition | Findings |
|---|---|
| **PACS** | Occludable angle ≥2 quadrants; no PAS; normal IOP |
| **PAC** | Occludable/narrow angle; PAS may be present |
| **PACG** | Occludable angle + PAS + high IOP + optic neuropathy |

---

## 13. Acute Primary Angle Closure

### 13.1 Predisposing Factors

- Hypermetropia/small eye
- Female sex
- Anteriorly placed lens–iris diaphragm
- Shallow anterior chamber
- Narrow angle

### 13.2 Precipitating Factors

- Dark environment
- Prone position
- Mydriasis
- Emotional stress
- **Topiramate**
- Sympathomimetics

### 13.3 IOP

May rapidly reach **40–60 mmHg**.

### 13.4 Symptoms

- Severe headache
- Severe eye pain
- Painful red eye
- Photophobia
- Blepharospasm
- Vomiting
- Hazy/distorted vision
- Halos around lights

### 13.5 Signs

- Marked congestion
- **Corneal oedema**
- Shallow anterior chamber
- Closed angle
- **Fixed, mid-dilated, vertically oval pupil**
- **Rock-hard eye**
- Nasal eclipse on flashlight examination
- ± PAS
- Cupping may or may not yet be present

### 13.6 Emergency Treatment

1. **Supine position**
2. **IV mannitol**
3. **Systemic acetazolamide** as indicated
4. **Pilocarpine after IOP has fallen**
5. Topical corticosteroids
6. Other osmotic/supportive measures as indicated

> **Mannitol = emergency osmotic drug emphasized.**

#### Why Is Pilocarpine Given After IOP Falls?

At very high IOP, iris sphincter is relatively ischaemic and poorly responsive. After pressure falls, pilocarpine can contract the sphincter, pull peripheral iris centrally and help reopen the angle.

### 13.7 Definitive Treatment

**Laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI)**

- Usually **Nd:YAG laser**.
- Peripheral iris opening.
- Typically placed around **11–1 o'clock**.
- Bypasses pupillary block.
- Equalizes posterior and anterior chamber pressure.
- Prophylactic LPI may be performed in the fellow eye.

### 13.8 Vogt Triad After Acute Angle Closure

1. Fixed/mid-dilated pupil with sphincter damage.
2. Iris atrophy.
3. **Glaukomflecken** — anterior subcapsular lens opacities.

---

## 14. Chronic Angle-Closure Glaucoma

- Can clinically resemble POAG.
- Gonioscopy demonstrates progressive/creeping angle closure.

### 14.1 Dark Room Prone Provocative Test

- Patient placed in a dark room/prone setting.
- IOP response assessed.
- Particularly useful after peripheral iridotomy.
- IOP rise may support **plateau iris**.

### 14.2 Absolute Glaucoma

Advanced/end-stage glaucoma with severe pressure-related disease.

Cyclodestructive procedures may be used when IOP must be reduced by decreasing aqueous production.

---

## 15. Secondary Glaucomas

### 15.1 Pigmentary Glaucoma

A **secondary open-angle glaucoma**.

#### Mechanism

**Iris pigment dispersion → pigment deposition in trabecular meshwork → trabecular obstruction**

#### Features

- Young myopes
- Exercise may increase pigment dispersion
- **Krukenberg spindle**
- **Sampaolesi line**

---

### 15.2 Pseudoexfoliation Glaucoma

Described as the **most common cause of secondary open-angle glaucoma**.

#### Mechanism

Pseudoexfoliative material → deposition in anterior segment → trabecular obstruction → raised IOP.

#### Signs

- **Target sign** → anterior lens capsule.
- **Fnocks** → pupillary-margin deposits.
- Poor mydriasis may occur.
- **Sampaolesi line** may occur.

> **Memory hook:** Pseudoexfoliation → **Target + Fnocks**

---

### 15.3 Neovascular Glaucoma

#### Causes

Major causes:

- **Proliferative diabetic retinopathy**
- **Ischaemic CRVO**

#### Pathogenesis

**Retinal ischaemia → VEGF ↑ → rubeosis iridis → angle neovascularization → impaired trabecular drainage → fibrovascular contraction → synechial angle closure**

Early disease may initially behave as an **open-angle glaucoma**.

#### Management

- **Anti-VEGF**
- **Pan-retinal photocoagulation (PRP)** for underlying retinal ischaemia

---

### 15.4 Lens-Induced Glaucoma

#### Phacomorphic vs Phacolytic

| Feature | Phacomorphic | Phacolytic |
|---|---|---|
| Lens | **Swollen/intumescent** | **Shrunken/hypermature** |
| AC | **Shallow** | **Deep** |
| Cataract | Intumescent | Morgagnian/hypermature |
| Mechanism | Lens pushes iris forward → angle crowding | Lens proteins → macrophages → TM obstruction |
| Capsule | Intact | Intact |

##### Phacomorphic

**Big/swollen lens → iris pushed forward → shallow AC → angle closure**

##### Phacolytic

**Hypermature lens → lens protein leakage → macrophage uptake → macrophages obstruct TM**

> **Memory:**  
> **Phacomorphic = swollen lens + shallow AC**  
> **Phacolytic = protein + macrophages + deep AC**

---

### 15.5 Malignant Glaucoma

Also called **ciliary block glaucoma**.

#### Typical Setting

Usually follows **intraocular surgery**.

#### Mechanism

**Aqueous misdirection posteriorly → vitreous expansion → lens–iris diaphragm pushed forward → very shallow anterior chamber**

#### Treatment

1. **Atropine**
2. Nd:YAG opening of anterior hyaloid if inadequate
3. **Pars plana vitrectomy** if refractory

> **High-yield:** Malignant glaucoma is the glaucoma in which **atropine is specifically used**.

---

### 15.6 Posner-Schlossman Syndrome

Also called **glaucomatocyclitic crisis**.

#### Features

- Mild anterior uveitis
- Markedly raised IOP
- **Open angle**
- Glaucoma component predominates

#### Associations

- Young males
- CMV
- H. pylori
- HLA association

#### Treatment

- Control IOP.
- **Beta blocker** described as drug of choice for IOP control.
- Topical steroids under antiglaucoma cover.
- Prostaglandins and pilocarpine are listed as contraindicated because of inflammatory concerns.

---

## 16. Congenital Glaucoma

Congenital glaucoma is a **developmental open-angle glaucoma caused by abnormal trabecular development**.

### 16.1 Pathogenesis

**Trabeculodysgenesis → Barkan membrane → impaired aqueous outflow → raised IOP → globe enlargement**

Consequences:

- Buphthalmos
- Megalocornea
- Corneal oedema
- Increased axial length
- Descemet tears → Haab striae

### 16.2 Clinical Features

- **Buphthalmos**
- Enlarged cornea
- Deep anterior chamber
- Increased axial length → myopia
- Blue sclera
- **Corneal oedema/hazy cornea = earliest sign**
- **Haab striae = horizontal Descemet tears**
- Blepharospasm
- Photophobia
- Lacrimation

#### Corneal Diameter

- Megalocornea: >**11.5 mm** in one description.
- >**13 mm** used elsewhere for enlarged cornea in buphthalmos.

### 16.3 Management

Congenital glaucoma is fundamentally **surgical**.

#### Procedures

- **Trabeculotomy + trabeculectomy**
- **Goniotomy** when the cornea is clear
- Goniotomy performed with a goniotomy knife

A functioning **bleb** indicates a functioning filtration pathway.

---

## 17. Medical Treatment of Glaucoma

### 17.1 Treatment Target

**Target IOP = IOP at which visual-field progression is prevented without compromising quality of life.**

---

### 17.2 Drugs Decreasing Aqueous Production

#### Beta Blockers

Examples:

- **Timolol** — non-selective
- **Betaxolol** — selective
- Levobunolol

##### Mechanism

**↓ Aqueous humour production**

##### Adverse Effects / Contraindications

- Asthma
- COPD
- Heart block
- Mask hypoglycaemia
- Corneal anaesthesia
- Nasolacrimal duct obstruction
- Blepharoconjunctivitis

---

#### Alpha-Adrenergic Agonists

Examples:

- **Apraclonidine**
- **Brimonidine**

##### Mechanism

- Apraclonidine → **↓ aqueous production + ↑ trabecular outflow**
- Brimonidine → predominantly **↓ aqueous production**

##### Adverse Effects

**Apraclonidine:**
- Eyelid retraction
- Mydriasis
- Follicular conjunctivitis

**Brimonidine:**
- Crosses BBB
- Drowsiness
- Depression
- Apnoea

Alpha agonists are listed as contraindicated in children because of risk of **CNS depression, apnoea and cardiac suppression**.

---

#### Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors

Examples:

- **Acetazolamide** — systemic
- **Dorzolamide** — topical
- **Brinzolamide** — topical

##### Mechanism

**↓ Aqueous humour production**

##### Caution

- Sulphonamide allergy

---

### 17.3 Drugs Increasing Aqueous Outflow

#### Prostaglandin Analogues

Examples:

- **Latanoprost**
- Travoprost
- Bimatoprost
- Tafluprost
- Unoprostone

##### Mechanism

**↑ Uveoscleral outflow**

##### Advantages

- Principal first-line class emphasized.
- Approximately **25–30% IOP reduction**.
- Once-daily dosing.
- Better compliance.
- Flatten diurnal IOP fluctuations.
- Few systemic adverse effects.
- Particularly emphasized in **normal-tension glaucoma**.

##### Adverse Effects

- **Eyelash lengthening / hypertrichosis**
- Iris/periocular pigmentation
- Ocular hyperaemia
- Cystoid macular oedema
- Uveitis

##### Cautions

- Uveitis
- Postoperative states
- CME
- Herpes simplex reactivation risk

---

#### Miotics

##### Pilocarpine

**Mechanism:** ↑ trabecular outflow.

Adverse effects/cautions:

- Brow ache
- Headache
- Myopia → retinal-detachment concern
- Uveitis

In acute angle closure:

**Pilocarpine is given only after IOP has fallen sufficiently.**

---

#### Rho-Kinase Inhibitors

Examples:

- **Netarsudil**
- **Ripasudil**

##### Mechanism

**↑ Trabecular outflow**

##### Important Adverse Effect

**Vortex keratopathy / corneal verticillata**

Other causes of vortex keratopathy:

- Chloroquine
- Amiodarone
- Tamoxifen
- Indomethacin
- Fabry disease

---

### 17.4 Hyperosmotic Agents

| Drug | Route |
|---|---|
| **Mannitol** | IV |
| **Glycerol** | Oral |
| Urea | — |
| Isosorbide | — |

#### Mechanism

**Vitreous dehydration → fluid movement away from eye → rapid IOP reduction**

#### Cautions

- Renal disease
- Compromised cardiac function

---

## 18. Laser Treatment

### 18.1 Core Principle

**Closed angle → treat iris / bypass pupillary block**

**Open angle → treat trabecular meshwork**

---

### 18.2 Laser Peripheral Iridotomy

- Usually **Nd:YAG laser**.
- Creates a peripheral iris opening.
- Bypasses pupillary block.
- Allows aqueous to move directly from posterior → anterior chamber.
- Indicated in **angle-closure glaucoma**.
- Usually placed around **11–1 o'clock**.
- May be performed prophylactically in the fellow eye.

---

### 18.3 Laser Iridoplasty

Used for:

- **Plateau iris**
- Persistent angle crowding

---

### 18.4 Laser Trabeculoplasty

Indicated in **open-angle glaucoma**.

#### Types

- **ALT** — argon laser trabeculoplasty
- **SLT** — selective laser trabeculoplasty using frequency-doubled Nd:YAG
- Diode laser techniques also described

#### Mechanism

Trabecular treatment → improved **trabecular aqueous outflow**.

---

### 18.5 Cyclodestructive Procedures

Used particularly for:

- Refractory glaucoma
- Absolute glaucoma

#### Mechanism

**Damage ciliary processes → ↓ aqueous production**

Methods:

- Cyclophotocoagulation
- Transscleral
- Endoscopic
- Cryotherapy
- Diode laser photocoagulation

---

## 19. Surgical Treatment

### 19.1 Trabeculectomy

Major filtration surgery.

#### Mechanism

Creates a controlled drainage pathway into the **subconjunctival space**.

#### Antimetabolites

- **Mitomycin C**
- **5-fluorouracil**

A functioning **bleb** indicates a functioning filtration pathway.

---

### 19.2 Non-Penetrating Surgery

- **Deep sclerectomy**
- **Viscocanalostomy**

---

### 19.3 Glaucoma Drainage Devices

Used particularly in **resistant glaucoma**.

Examples:

- Express mini-shunt
- Molteno device
- **Ahmed glaucoma valve**

---

### 19.4 MIGS

**Minimally invasive glaucoma surgery**

Example:

- **iStent**

---

## 20. Important Clinical Comparisons

### 20.1 Ocular Hypertension vs POAG vs NTG

| Feature | Ocular Hypertension | POAG | NTG |
|---|---|---|---|
| IOP | >21 mmHg | Elevated | <21 mmHg |
| Disc damage | No | Yes | Yes |
| Field defect | No | Yes | Yes |
| Core concept | Pressure without damage | Pressure-associated glaucoma | Glaucoma despite normal-range IOP |

### 20.2 Open-Angle vs Angle-Closure

| Feature | Open-Angle | Angle-Closure |
|---|---|---|
| Angle | Open | Narrow/closed |
| Obstruction | Trabecular dysfunction | Iris obstruction |
| AC | Deep | Shallow |
| Laser | Trabeculoplasty | Iridotomy/iridoplasty |

### 20.3 Phacomorphic vs Phacolytic

| Feature | Phacomorphic | Phacolytic |
|---|---|---|
| Lens | Swollen | Shrunken/hypermature |
| AC | Shallow | Deep |
| Mechanism | Forward iris displacement | Lens proteins + macrophages obstruct TM |
| Typical cataract | Intumescent | Morgagnian/hypermature |

### 20.4 Pseudoexfoliation vs Pigmentary

| Feature | Pseudoexfoliation | Pigmentary |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Exfoliative material obstructs TM | Pigment obstructs TM |
| Key sign | Target sign + Fnocks | Krukenberg spindle |
| Angle sign | Sampaolesi line | Sampaolesi line |
| Typical clue | Secondary OAG | Young myope |

### 20.5 Acute Angle Closure vs Malignant Glaucoma

| Feature | Acute Angle Closure | Malignant Glaucoma |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Pupillary block → iris bombe | Aqueous misdirection |
| AC | Shallow | Very shallow |
| Typical setting | Anatomically predisposed eye | Often after intraocular surgery |
| Key treatment | Mannitol/acetazolamide → pilocarpine → LPI | **Atropine → hyaloidotomy → PPV** |

## 21. High-Yield Examination Facts

### 21.1 Important Numbers

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---:|
| Normal IOP | **10–21 mmHg** |
| Ocular hypertension threshold | **>21 mmHg** |
| Acute angle-closure IOP | **40–60 mmHg** |
| Normal C/D ratio | **~0.3** |
| Suspicious C/D ratio | **>0.5** |
| Suspicious inter-eye C/D difference | **>0.2** |
| Goldmann applanation diameter | **3.06 mm** |
| Corneal critical angle | **~46°** |
| Aqueous production | **2–3 µL/min** |
| Trabecular outflow | **~80–90%** |
| Uveoscleral outflow | **~10–20%** |
| Bjerrum area | **20–30°** |
| Temporal visual field | **100°** |
| Inferior visual field | **70°** |
| Nasal visual field | **60°** |
| Superior visual field | **50°** |

### 21.2 Diagnostic Pearls

- **Normal IOP does not exclude glaucoma.**
- **Ocular hypertension = high IOP without disc/field damage.**
- **Goldmann = gold-standard tonometry.**
- Thin cornea → falsely low Goldmann reading.
- Thick cornea → falsely high Goldmann reading.
- **Gonioscopy = best angle examination.**
- Van Herick:
  - PACD = CT → open.
  - PACD <¼ CT → closed/closing.
- Nasal eclipse on flashlight → narrow/closed angle.

### 21.3 Treatment Pearls

- **Open angle → trabeculoplasty.**
- **Closed angle → peripheral iridotomy.**
- **Plateau iris → iridoplasty.**
- Acute angle closure:
  - **IV mannitol**
  - Acetazolamide
  - **Pilocarpine after IOP falls**
  - Definitive **LPI**
- Malignant glaucoma → **atropine**.
- Congenital glaucoma → **surgery**.
- Prostaglandin analogues → major first-line topical class.
- ROCK inhibitors → ↑ trabecular outflow.
- Trabeculectomy → major filtration procedure.

### 21.4 Secondary Glaucoma Pearls

| Clinical Clue | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| **Target sign + Fnocks** | Pseudoexfoliation |
| **Krukenberg spindle** | Pigmentary glaucoma |
| **Rubeosis iridis** | Neovascular glaucoma |
| **Swollen lens + shallow AC** | Phacomorphic |
| **Shrunken hypermature lens + deep AC** | Phacolytic |
| **Postoperative aqueous misdirection + very shallow AC** | Malignant glaucoma |
| **Mild uveitis + high IOP + open angle** | Posner-Schlossman |

### 21.5 Visual-Field Pearls

- Early characteristic defect → **paracentral scotoma**.
- **Seidel** → comma/sickle-shaped.
- **Bjerrum** → arcuate.
- **Roenne** → nasal step.
- **Temporal island = last remaining field.**

### 21.6 Congenital Glaucoma Pearls

- Buphthalmos
- Corneal oedema
- Haab striae
- Blepharospasm
- Photophobia
- Lacrimation
- **Earliest sign = hazy cornea/corneal oedema**
- **Haab striae = horizontal Descemet tears**
- Treatment fundamentally **surgical**
- **Goniotomy when cornea is clear**

## 22. Management Logic

### 22.1 Open-Angle Glaucoma

1. Confirm glaucoma using:
   - IOP
   - Optic-disc/RNFL structure
   - Visual field
2. Establish **target IOP**.
3. Start topical therapy.
4. **Prostaglandin analogues** are the principal first-line class emphasized.
5. Additional options:
   - Beta blockers
   - Alpha agonists
   - CAIs
   - Pilocarpine
   - ROCK inhibitors
6. **Laser trabeculoplasty** for open-angle disease.
7. Resistant/uncontrolled disease:
   - Trabeculectomy
   - Non-penetrating surgery
   - Drainage devices
   - MIGS

### 22.2 Angle-Closure Glaucoma

1. Identify mechanism:
   - Pupillary block
   - Plateau iris
   - Other secondary causes
2. Acute attack → emergency pressure reduction.
3. **IV mannitol ± acetazolamide**.
4. **Pilocarpine after IOP falls**.
5. **LPI = definitive treatment for pupillary block**.
6. Plateau iris → **laser iridoplasty**.
7. Consider prophylactic LPI in fellow eye.

### 22.3 Congenital Glaucoma

- Definitive management = **surgery**.
- Trabeculotomy/trabeculectomy emphasized.
- **Goniotomy preferred when cornea is clear.**

## 25. Core Glaucoma Mental Model

### 25.1 Disease Concept

**Glaucoma = characteristic optic neuropathy + irreversible visual-field loss**

**IOP may be raised or normal.**

### 25.2 Aqueous Pathway

**Ciliary processes → posterior chamber → pupil → anterior chamber → trabecular or uveoscleral outflow**

### 25.3 Open-Angle Glaucoma

**Angle anatomically open → trabecular drainage defective → treat outflow/aqueous dynamics**

Main options:

- Prostaglandins
- Beta blockers
- Alpha agonists
- CAIs
- Pilocarpine
- ROCK inhibitors
- Trabeculoplasty
- Trabeculectomy/MIGS

### 25.4 Angle-Closure Glaucoma

**Iris blocks drainage angle → restore access to angle**

Pupillary block:

**Lens–iris contact → pressure gradient → iris bombe → angle closure**

Treatment:

**Pressure lowering → pilocarpine when responsive → LPI**

Plateau iris:

**Persistent angle crowding → iridoplasty**

### 25.5 Optic Nerve Progression

**Neural tissue loss → rim thinning → vertical cupping → laminar/vessel changes → characteristic field loss**

### 25.6 Visual-Field Progression

**Paracentral → Seidel → Bjerrum/arcuate → double arcuate/ring → Roenne nasal step → central loss → temporal island**

### 25.7 Acute Angle Closure

**Predisposed eye + trigger → pupillary block → iris bombe → angle closure → IOP 40–60 mmHg**

Typical picture:

- Painful red eye
- Corneal oedema
- Severe headache
- Vomiting
- Halos
- Fixed mid-dilated pupil
- Rock-hard eye

Treatment:

**IV mannitol ± acetazolamide → pilocarpine after IOP falls → definitive LPI**

### 25.8 Congenital Glaucoma

**Trabeculodysgenesis → impaired aqueous outflow → raised IOP → buphthalmos + corneal oedema + Haab striae**

**Treatment = surgery**

### 25.9 Secondary Glaucoma Pattern Recognition

#### Pseudoexfoliation

**Target sign + Fnocks**

#### Pigmentary Glaucoma

**Young myope + Krukenberg spindle + Sampaolesi line**

#### Neovascular Glaucoma

**PDR/ischaemic CRVO → VEGF → rubeosis → angle neovascularization → closure**

#### Phacomorphic Glaucoma

**Swollen lens + shallow AC**

#### Phacolytic Glaucoma

**Hypermature/shrunken lens + deep AC + protein-loaded macrophages**

#### Malignant Glaucoma

**Postoperative + aqueous misdirection + very shallow AC → atropine**

#### Posner-Schlossman Syndrome

**Mild anterior uveitis + markedly raised IOP + open angle**

---

## 26. Five Rules to Remember

1. **Glaucoma is not simply high IOP** — normal-tension glaucoma exists.
2. **Goldmann is the gold-standard tonometer** — thin cornea falsely lowers and thick cornea falsely raises the reading.
3. **Open angle → trabeculoplasty; closed angle → iridotomy.**
4. **Acute angle closure → lower IOP first → pilocarpine after IOP falls → definitive LPI.**
5. **Congenital glaucoma → buphthalmos + corneal oedema + Haab striae → surgical treatment.**
