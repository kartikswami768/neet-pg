Carcinoma Larynx

2.63% of all cancers are carcinoma larynx.

Usually presents with change in voice, pain while speaking, >40-50 years of age with smoking history and weight loss.

# Vocal Cord Palsy

Vocal Cord Palsy Vocal cord paralysis means vocal cords are not working, due to nerve damage which supplies the vocal cords. Personal Notes

## Vagus Nerve

Arises from medulla, travels via jugular foramen along with glossopharyngeal (9th) nerve + spinal accessory nerve.
Supplies the ear, all muscles of the pharynx except stylopharyngeus, larynx, cardiac fibers where it regulates the heart beats between 60-80 bpm, increases gastric emptying and acid production. 2 ganglions: 1. Superior ganglion - Arnold's nerve (supplies posterior wall of external auditory canal), also known as auricular branch of vagus nerve. 2. Inferior ganglion - Superior laryngeal nerve (SLN).
Superior laryngeal nerve arises from 4th arch, it divides into: 1. Internal LN: sensory supply to supraglottis (It cuts through the thyro-hyoid membrane where anesthesia block can be given). 2. External LN: supplies cricothyroid (only intrinsic muscle lying outside).
ELN accompanies superior thyroid artery.
Left recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN) loops near the arch of aorta, gives one branch to heart and one to the esophageal plexus.
It has a longer course compared to right RLN, hence more prone to injury (mediastinum area).

## Ortner'S Syndrome

Also known as cardiovascular hoarseness (Cardiovagal syndrome).
An enlarged left atrium compresses the left RLN (in case of Mitral stenosis/pulmonary hypertension), causing cough and hoarseness of voice.

# Stridor and Laryngotracheal Stenosis

Stridor and Laryngotracheal Stenosis
Stridor means noisy breathing, occurring due to narrowing.
Narrowing can be because of: mass, swelling, growth, soft tissues. Personal Notes

## 3 Types Of Stridor

Inspiratory : supraglottis level.
Biphasic : vocal cords level or 1 cm below it (subglottic space).
Expiratory : remaining subglottis and trachea. Vocal cord level is the narrowest part of the windpipe.
Pharynx & supraglottis Inspiratory stridor
Glottis, subglottis & cervical trachea Biphasic
Thoracic trachea Expiratory stridor & bronchi
Causes of supraglottic or inspiratory stridor:
Laryngomalacia: lying down supine worsens the stridor, on lying down prone it improves, omega-shaped sign.
Epiglottitis: thumb sign seen, inspiratory stridor seen, which improves in prone position.
Croup: laryngo-tracheal bronchitis, barking cough present, in X-ray steeple sign seen. First inspiratory stridor occurs, which eventually becomes biphasic stridor. Note: most characteristic stridor in the croup is biphasic.
Ca larynx: supraglottic cancer, most common site is epiglottis (drains to deep cervical lymph nodes especially antero-superior parts).
Foreign body Causes of glottic or biphasic stridor:
Croup

![[images/laryngotracheal-stenosis-01.jpg]]

Vocal cord lesions: hemangioma, polyps, large nodules, reinke's edema.
1 cm subglottic space stenosis (subglottic stenosis : < 3mm subglottic diameter, just below vocal cords). Causes of subglottic or expiratory stridor:
Laryngotracheal stenosis: X ray - hourglass shape.

## Treatment Of Stridor

In case of emergency: cricothyroidotomy - gives you 48 hrs of time. Cricothyroid/ cricovocal membrane is burst by a small pen or pipe.
In case of a foreign body: heimlich's maneuver.
Tracheostomy: secure the airway, then treat the cause.

# Congenital Diseases of the Larynx

Congenital Diseases of the Larynx

## Laryngomalacia

Congenital softening of the tissues of the larynx above the vocal cords.
Classical omega shaped epiglottis: short aryepiglottic fold.
No redness or inflammation.
Supine position-condition worsens.
Prone position-condition improves.
Self limiting condition. Elastic cartilage is found in: epiglottis, cuneiform, corniculate, upper part of arytenoids. Hyaline cartilage is found in: cricoid, thyroid, lower part of arytenoids. Treatment:
Conservative and symptomatic, ensure no stridor occurs, anti-inflammatory drugs.

## Congenital Vocal Cord Paralysis

If stridor occurs, tracheostomy is to be done.
Corrective surgery needed. Causes:
Mass present on nuclei.
Bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.

![[images/laryngomalacia-01.jpg]]

Vagus nerve problem.
Posterior cricoarytenoid is the only abductor - any congenital condition compromising its nervous supply.

## Subglottic Stenosis

Biphasic stridor occurs.
1 cm below true vocal cords.
Commonly seen in intubated children for > 6 days and adults too, even if no cuff present. Common causes:
Prolonged intubation.
Wegener's granulomatosis (causes cartilaginous problems like nasal septum perforations)
Laryngeal web:
Congenital presence of a membranous web.
Sometimes as the larynx grows, it normally becomes alright, or remains as a small remnant.

# Neck Dissection and Tracheostomy

Neck Dissection and Tracheostomy

## Ca Larynx

Most common: Glottic cancer-no lymph node spread, good prognosis.
Supraglottic cancer: most common subsite is epiglottis, drains in deep cervical nodes (antero - superior part: level II and III).
Subglottic cancer: most rare, drains in deep cervical nodes (postero-inferior quadrant: level III and IV). Lymph nodes in neck: I A: submental nodes I B: submandibular II: upper deep cervical nodes III: middle deep cervical nodes IV: lower deep cervical nodes V: posterior triangle or supraclavicular VI: pre-tracheal nodes VII: mediastinal nodes

## Associated Structures In Neck

Sternocleidomastoid muscle
Internal jugular vein
Spinal accessory nerve
Omohyoid muscle
Parotid tail
Submandibular salivary glands Radical neck dissection (RND): removal of level I to V lymph nodes + all associated structures in the neck. Modified neck dissection (MND): removal of level I to V lymph nodes + all structures removed, except three: sternocleidomastoid muscle or internal jugular vein or spinal accessory nerve.
Type I MND: spinal accessory nerve preserved, rest all removed.
Type II MND: spinal accessory nerve + internal jugular vein preserved, rest removed.
Type III MND: spinal accessory nerve + internal jugular vein + sternocleidomastoid muscle preserved.
Meanings of surgical terms:
Tomy : cut
Ectomy : remove
Plasty : repair

# Thyroidectomy and RLN Palsy

Thyroidectomy and RLN Palsy

## Introduction

Thyroid gland: produces T3 and T4, under influence of TSH from pituitary gland.
Hypothyroidism is more common.
Enlarged thyroid gland is visible on neck while swallowing.
Thyroglossal cyst (between thyroid and tongue) doesn't move on swallowing, but moves upon protruding the tongue.
Physiological enlarged thyroid gland is seen during puberty, which subsides later on. Hyperthyroidism:
Exophthalmos seen, anxiety, palpitations, sweating , decreased weight, intolerance to heat.
TSH should be controlled in between 1.5 to 2 mmol.
Thyroxine tablets are given as replacement between 25 micrograms to 100 micrograms. Hypothyroidism:
Proximal myopathy develops: sitting and standing up becomes very difficult.
Weight gain: moon face.
Intolerance to cold.
Decreased appetite, lethargy.
Hairfall, dry skin. Common causes of hypothyroidism: 1. Iodine deficiency is called goiter (multinodular/single lobed/diffuse). 2. Hashimoto's thyroiditis: autoimmune condition where body produces a lot of anti-thyroid antibodies or thyroid immunoglobulins. Note: bull hump is typical for corticosteroid usage, bull neck seen in lymphadenopathy. Unilateral swelling/mass on thyroid gland: 1. Check thyroid profile: TSH, T3, T4, antibodies. 2. USG - guided fine needle aspiration cytology is done:
Papillary carcinoma thyroid: more common, single lobectomy done.
Follicular carcinoma thyroid: more dangerous, total thyroidectomy done. 3. MRI contrast enhanced imaging is better for thyroid gland.
