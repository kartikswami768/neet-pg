## Pathophysiology Of Parotid Disorders

Parotid gland produces 95% of saliva through the stenson's duct 0.5-1.5L/day.
When the stenson's duct gets blocked by stones or by dried secretions, the backflow
and pressure causes infection and inflammation of the parotid gland.

**Clinical Features:**
Visible swelling in the face → in front of ear above the angle of mandible
Dehydration → most common reason leading to parotid infection
Dehydration can be due to decreased fluid intake, diuretics, alcohol, pregnancy
Pain increases on eating food

**Note:**
Saliva production is increased while eating food from 0.1ml/min to 1-3 ml/min
Bilateral parotid gland involves → parotitis
Unilateral parotid gland involved → usually stones

## Sialolithiasis/Salivary Calculi

Most common gland for the formation of stones: Submandibular gland
Calculi may form in the ducts of submandibular or parotid glands.
They are formed by the deposition of calcium phosphate on the organic matrix of
mucin or cellular debris.
About 80% of the stones are radio-opaque and can be seen on appropriate X-rays.
Treatment is sialolithectomy-removal of stone.

## Vocal Cord Nodules

Also known as Teacher’s nodule, Screamer’s nodule or Singer’s nodule.
There is thickening of the vocal cords due to increased friction while screaming or singing.
Presents with hoarseness of voice, voice fatigue and pain after speaking.
Managed conservatively, that is voice rest.

## Intubation Granuloma

Caused due to intubation.
Intubation should be converted to tracheostomy after 6 days.
It can also be caused due to voice abuse which is called Contact Pachyderma.
If it is due to LPR then it is called Peptic Granuloma.
It is also managed conservatively if the granulation is small and if it is bigger in size then
it can be excised with the help of surgery.

## Juvenile Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis

A dreadful and repeated condition in childrens.
Occurs due to HPV 6 & 11.
Seen before the age of 12.
Even after excision it reoccurs back.
Steroidal injections, laser excisions, antiviral drugs and microlaryngeal surgery can be done.

## Adult Respiratory Papillomatosis

Usually it doesn’t reoccur.
The treatment here is also the same.
