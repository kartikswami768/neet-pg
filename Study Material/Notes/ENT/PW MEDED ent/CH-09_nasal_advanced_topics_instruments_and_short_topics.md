## Congenital Cochlear Abnormalities and Temporal Bone Angles

### Cochlear Embryology

The cochlea develops in the **3rd week of gestation** at the level of the rhombencephalon.

The embryology illustration labels the following stages and structures:

- **3rd–4th week:** otic placode, ectoderm, mesenchyme, otic pit, and otocyst.
- **4th week:** otocyst.
- **5th week:** pars utriculovestibularis, endolymphatic duct, and pars sacculocochlearis.
- **6th week:** semicircular canals, vestibule, and endolymphatic sac.
- **8th week:** superior, posterior, and lateral semicircular canals, cochlea, and cochlear aqueduct.

![[images/cochlear-embryology.png|450]]

### Michel's Aplasia

- Failure of development of the otic placode at or before the 3rd week of gestation.
- Autosomal dominant.
- Thalidomide usage history.
- No hearing (unilateral/bilateral).
- **Diagnosis:** HRCT, BERA.
- **Treatment:** cochlear implant (after 12 months) or brainstem implant.

The HRCT illustration labels an absent cochlea, absent vestibular aqueduct, and dysplastic vestibule.

![[images/michel-aplasia-hrct.png|400]]

### Mondini Aplasia

- Developmental defect of the otic capsule.
- Autosomal dominant.
- Most common ear deformity.
- **1.5 or 1.75 turns** of the cochlea.
- Highly associated with meningitis.
- Syndromes associated with Mondini aplasia:
  - **Pendred syndrome:** deficiency of pendrin protein.
  - **CHARGE syndrome.**
  - **DiGeorge syndrome:** chromosome 22 absent.

The image labels 1.5 turns of the cochlea with a cystic apex.

![[images/mondini-aplasia-hrct.png|450]]

### Alexander's Aplasia

- Aplasia of the cochlear duct.
- Cannot be detected on CT scan because these are membranous deformities.
- MRI is preferred.

### Scheibe Aplasia

- Cochleosaccular aplasia.
- Cannot be detected on CT scan because these are membranous deformities.
- MRI is preferred.

- **Aplasia:** no formation.
- **Dysplasia:** deformation.

### Trautmann's Triangle

![[images/trautmann-triangle-temporal-bone.png|400]]

## Nystagmus, Vestibular Reflexes, VEMP

### Vestibulo-Ocular Reflex

The illustrated sequence is:

1. Detection of rotation.
2. Inhibition of extraocular muscles on one side.
3. Excitation of extraocular muscles on the other side.
4. Compensating eye movement.

![[images/vestibulo-ocular-reflex.png|400]]

### Nystagmus

Abnormal movement of the eyeballs.

**Forms of movement**

1. Jerking movement.
2. Pendular movement.
3. Rotational movement.

**Types**

1. **Central:** horizontal, rotational, or vertical.
2. **Peripheral:** horizontal plus torsional; acute; more severe; lasts for a few hours.

### Ewald's Laws

1. The axis of nystagmus is parallel to the semicircular canal.
2. Ampullopetal movement causes more severe nystagmus than ampullofugal movement.

### Tullio's Phenomenon

- Sounds cause vertigo or nystagmus.
- Associated with superior semicircular canal dehiscence or fistula.

### Unterberger's Test / Fukuda's Stepping Test

- The patient closes the eyes and stretches the arms out in front.
- The patient walks on the spot for one minute.
- The knees are raised as high as possible.
- Patients with vertigo start to turn their axis in a particular direction.
- This is a closed-eye test used to diagnose peripheral vertigo.

![[images/unterberger-fukuda-stepping-test.png|350]]

### VEMP

**Vestibular evoked myogenic potential**

- **oVEMP:** utricle and superior vestibular nerve.
- **cVEMP:** saccule and inferior vestibular nerve.

## Ossicular Discontinuity and Meatoplasty

### Ossicular Discontinuity

#### Austin's Classification

The notes list four common types and state that the incus is absent in all cases and tympanic membrane (TM) reconstruction is required in all cases.

| Type | Malleus | Stapes superstructure | Ossicular-chain defect described |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | M+ | S+ | Loss of part of the incus or total loss of the incus. |
| B | M+ | S− | Loss of incus and stapes superstructure, but the malleus handle is still present. |
| C | M− | S+ | Loss of incus and malleus, but the stapes superstructure is still present. |
| D | M− | S− | Loss of incus, malleus, and stapes superstructure, but the mobile footplate is still present. |

#### Austin/Kartush Classification: Ossicular Status

| Type | Ossicular-chain status |
|---|---|
| 0 | M+ I+ S+ |
| A | M+ S+ |
| B | M+ S− |
| C | M− S+ |
| D | M− S− |
| E | Ossicular head fixation |
| F | Stapes fixation |

### Related Surgical Terms

- **Ossiculoplasty:** repair of an ossicle.
- **Tympanoplasty:** repair of the eardrum.
- **Canaloplasty:** repair of the tympanic canal.
- **Meatoplasty:** repair of cartilage.
- **Otoplasty:** repair of the pinna.

### Meatoplasty

Repair of the cartilaginous and bony parts.

**Most common indications listed**

- Modified radical mastoidectomy.
- Canal wall down mastoidectomy.
- Stenosis.
- Tumour.
- Glomus.

**Complications**

- Stenosis.
- Wide cosmetic deformity.

#### Surgical Technique: Endaural Incision Approach

- **Lempert I incision:** semicircular incision from the 12 o'clock to 6 o'clock position in the posterior meatal wall at the bony-cartilaginous junction.
- **Lempert II incision:** starts from the first incision at 12 o'clock and then passes upward in a curvilinear direction between the tragus and crus of the helix through the incisura terminalis.
