# Guideline for this Document

**Updated:** 20 September 2026  
**Purpose:** A focused revision backlog generated from the Pediatrics project history, with **special weight given to the 19 September 2026 practice paper and its post-paper error analysis**.

> **Status key**
> - 🔴 **High priority:** demonstrated weakness / repeated error / major gap; needs deliberate re-teaching + retrieval + application.
> - 🟠 **Revision priority:** known or probable weakness; needs targeted revision/testing.
> - 🟡 **Maintenance:** concept is known but should be revisited through mixed questions rather than full re-teaching.
>
> This is **not a new syllabus**. It is the list of material that should receive future attention before being considered secure.

## Evidence used

1. **19 Sept 2026 practice paper:** 72 questions; Q9, Q32 and Q65 were left blank. The post-paper analysis identified weaknesses in developmental milestones, nutrition/malnutrition, metabolic/genetic disorders, neonatology, cystic fibrosis, genetic syndromes, renal topics, growth parameters and epilepsy.
2. **Earlier project assessments:** specific gaps included cerebral-palsy subtype recognition, pyridoxine/B6-related sideroblastic anemia, inherited bilirubin disorders, FGF23/hypophosphatemic versus nutritional rickets, SAM admission/management, and Turner vs Noonan vs Klinefelter.
3. **Earlier planning/teaching:** neonatal respiratory distress (TTN vs RDS vs MAS), neonatal jaundice, developmental milestones, Down syndrome, infectious-rash differentiation, Kawasaki disease, and growth assessment were already identified as areas requiring repair.
4. **Recent self-assessment:** congenital heart disease was explicitly identified as an area needing more question-based testing even though fetal circulation and the broad cyanotic/acyanotic framework are partly known.
5. **Important distinction:** a topic appearing here does **not** mean the entire chapter is weak. The listed subtopics are the parts to attack first.

# How this backlog should be used

This document should **not** become another chapter to read cover-to-cover.

For each 🔴 topic:

**Recall → 3–10 targeted questions → correct errors → one concise summary/algorithm → spaced re-test.**

For 🟠 topics:

**Rapid recall → mixed clinical question → repair only the gap.**

For 🟡 topics:

**Do not reread routinely. Revisit through cumulative/mixed testing or when an error reappears.**

## Mastery rule

A topic moves out of the active backlog only after there is evidence of:
- correct recall without cues,
- correct interpretation/application,
- ability to distinguish common confounders,
- and acceptable performance on a later mixed question.

**Reading it again is not evidence of mastery.**
