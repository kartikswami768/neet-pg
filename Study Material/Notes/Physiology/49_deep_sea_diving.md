# Deep Sea Diving

## Relationship of pressure to sea depth

### Pressure changes underwater

- For every descent of **33 feet (10 metres)**, pressure increases by **1 atm**.
- At 33 feet, total pressure = **2 atm**.
- At 100 feet, total pressure = **4 atm** (1 atm at the surface + 3 atm from the water column).

### At a depth of 100 feet

- Inside a submarine, the lungs are unaffected because pressure is maintained internally.
- During apnea diving, external pressure (4 atm) compresses lung volume.
- At an external pressure of 4 atm, the source calculates lung volume as **6/4 = 1.5 L**.
- The source states that despite increased inspired PO₂, oxygen utilisation reduces PO₂ and further increases PCO₂; these changes stimulate ventilation and create an intense urge to breathe, prompting ascent to the surface. The source notation for the intermediate PO₂/PCO₂ changes is unclear in the extracted text; the sequence is retained here in readable form.

## SCUBA system

**SCUBA** = self-contained underwater breathing apparatus.

- Purpose: prevent lung compression by equalising internal and external pressures using a breathing apparatus connected to a gas cylinder.
- The breathing apparatus contains high-pressure gases.
- A valve system regulates gas delivery (e.g. 4 atm at 100 feet).
- Components:
  - **First-stage valve:** controls the pressure at which gases are delivered.
  - **Demand valve:** connected to the mask; opens during inspiration and closes during expiration.
  - Mask, hose/nozzle and air cylinders.
- Allows controlled breathing in high-pressure environments.

## Effects of high-pressure gases on the body

The source states that prolonged exposure to high-pressure gases can cause normally occurring gases to have toxic effects on the body.

### Oxygen toxicity

- High-pressure oxygen → production of oxidising free radicals → damage to membrane fatty acids (myelin) and cellular enzymes.
- Symptoms: muscle twitching, dizziness, visual disturbances, irritability and disorientation.

### Carbon dioxide toxicity

- Normal PCO₂: **40 mmHg**.
- Tolerable PCO₂: **40–80 mmHg**, which stimulates ventilation.
- Above **80 mmHg**: depression of the respiratory centre, causing respiratory acidosis.

### Nitrogen toxicity and nitrogen narcosis

- High N₂ dissolves in tissues, especially fat (60%) and body water (40%), and alters ionic conductance, increasing excitability (nitrogen narcosis), as described by the source.
- Characteristics resemble alcohol intoxication.
- Also called **“raptures of the depth”** or the **“Martini Effect.”**
- Symptoms intensify with depth.

### Sudden decompression

- Ascent should occur step by step.
- Rapid ascent causes sudden decompression.
- Dissolved gases form bubbles (nitrogen, oxygen and CO₂); the source states that nitrogen forms the most bubbles.
- After excessive exposure, significant quantities of N₂ bubbles develop in body fluids. Minute bubbles can block the smallest vessels, producing decompression sickness.

### Decompression sickness

Also called **Caisson disease**, **compressed-air sickness**, **diver’s paralysis**, **dysbarism** or **the bends**.

- Bends: **80–90%**.
- CNS manifestations: **5–10%**.
- Chokes: **2%**; symptoms include shortness of breath and dry cough.
- Cutaneous: mottled or marbled skin, pitting oedema.
- Audiovisual: hearing loss and visual abnormalities.
- N₂ bubbles block blood vessels in muscles and bones → joint and muscle pain → flexion of the joint.
- Other listed symptoms: dizziness, unconsciousness, collapse, paraesthesia, amnesia, paralysis, and urinary and faecal incontinence.


### Heliox for SCUBA diving

- Heliox is a breathing-gas mixture of helium and oxygen.
- The source gives **79% He and 21% O₂**, with other mixtures of **70/30** and **60/40**.
- Advantages of helium, according to the source:
  - Narcotic effect is **one-fifth** that of N₂.
  - Dissolved gas content is half that of dissolved N₂.
  - Decreases airway resistance.

## MCQs

**1. All of the following are seen in decompression sickness except?** (INICET 2024)

- a. Haemodilution
- b. Hearing loss
- c. Coagulopathy
- d. Vision loss

**Answer: a. Haemodilution**

**2. Nitrogen narcosis is due to** (AIIMS 2011)

- a. Nitrogen inhibits dismutase enzyme
- b. Increased production of nitrous oxide
- c. Increased solubility of nitrogen in the nerve-cell membrane
- d. Decrease in oxygen free radicals

**Answer: c. Increased solubility of nitrogen in the nerve-cell membrane**
