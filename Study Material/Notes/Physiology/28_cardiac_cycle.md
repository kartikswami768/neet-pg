### Last Rapid Filling Phase (Lrfp)

- Once the contraction begins, blood is pushed into the ventricle again
- The rapid blood flow and filling of the ventricle start once again
- This rapid filling is known as the Last rapid filling phase of the ventricle
- This is the only phase where the atrium is contracting
- LRFP coincides with the atrium systole or atrium contraction
- The ventricular filling does not occur in the isovolumic relaxation phase because all the valves are closed
- The filling only occurs during the first rapid filling phase, diastasis, and the last rapid filling phase
- The atrial systole is responsible for 25-30% of ventricular filling
- It is known as "Active filling" of the ventricle due to active contraction of the atrium
- A combination of the first rapid filling and the diastasis phases is responsible for the rest of the 70- 75% filling
- This is known as the "Passive filling" of the ventricle, as there is no contraction

### First Heart Sound

- The first heart sound is the onset of ventricular systole. Isovolumic contraction starts with depolarization
- When the action potential is in phase O, it corresponds to the QRS complex
- The action potential is the electrical event, whereas the contraction & relaxation are mechanical events
- The electrical event happens first, and then the mechanical event
- The first heart sound means ventricular contraction has to start
- However, the Q wave does not cause full ventricular contraction, as it is septal depolarization only
- After the Q wave generation, when the R wave starts, the ventricle causes full contraction
- The first heart sound will not appear during the Q wave; instead, it appears when the R wave is going on,
particularly at the peak of the R wave

### Second Heart Sound

- In the case of the second heart sound, the electrical relaxation has to happen, that is, the repolarization,
and then the ventricular muscles will fully relax
- At the end of the T wave, i.e., repolarization, the second heart sound is heard

### Third & Fourth Heart Sounds

- Both the third & fourth heart sounds occur due to the vibration of the ventricular wall during diastole
- The third heart sound occurs during the first rapid filling phase (FRFP)
- The T-P segment denotes the 3" HS
- The fourth heart sound occurs during the last rapid filling phase (LRFP) due to atrial contraction/atrial
systole
- Occurs at the end of the P wave
- The duration of the third & fourth heart sounds is 0.1 seconds for each
- The frequency of both is approximately 10 Hz, making these sounds inaudible
- However, there are certain conditions when audible sound is produced
- This happens when the frequency increases to 25 -45Hz

### Aortic Ventricular Pressure Curve

- IVC phase:
- On contraction, the ventricular pressure rapidly rises
- The aortic pressure remains the same, and at a certain point, the ventricular pressure rises above the
aortic pressure, thus opening the semilunar valve
- Ejection phase:
- Blood is ejected out through semilunar valves while ventricular pressure continues to rise during contraction
- Aortic pressure T but remains lower than the ventricular pressure
- When the ejection continues, the ventricular pressure will start falling, & the aortic pressure will also
start falling proportionately
- However, now the aortic pressure is more than the ventricular pressure, but the ejection continues
- Now, the ventricular pressure is lower than the aortic pressure
- This is when the blood will try to fall back from the arch of the aorta
- The immediate closure of the aortic valve prevents this
- Isovolumic relaxation phase:
- The ventricle relaxes increasingly & pressure thus falls to near 0 mmHg
- This is the point where the filling of the ventricle will start again
- During the closure of the aortic valve, the aortic pressure rises & falls a while later & then remains
constant, which is the diastolic pressure
- The mitral & tricuspid valves open, the ventricular filling starts again, & the pressure will rise again
