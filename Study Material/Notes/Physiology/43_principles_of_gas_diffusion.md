# Principles of Gas Diffusion

## Respiratory unit and membrane

- Respiratory unit = exchange zone.
- Exchange zones consist of:
  - Respiratory bronchioles (generations 17, 18 and 19).
  - Alveolar duct (generations 20, 21 and 22).
  - Alveoli (generation 23).
- Inner lining contains surfactant.
- 95% of the surface area of alveoli is covered by type I pneumocytes.
- The respiratory membrane (0.6 micrometre thick) consists of:
  1. Water and surfactant layer.
  2. Type I pneumocyte and basement membrane.
  3. Interstitial lung tissue.
  4. Endothelial cells and pulmonary capillary basement membrane.

## Diffusion capacity

- Gas exchange in the lung occurs by simple diffusion.
- **Fick's law:**

  $$
  D = \frac{\Delta P \times A \times K}{d}
  $$

  - ΔP: partial-pressure difference.
  - A: surface area.
  - d: membrane thickness.
  - K: diffusion constant.

**Average values given in the source**

- ΔP for O₂ = 11 mmHg.
- ΔP for CO₂ = 1 mmHg.

## Factors affecting diffusion

**Surface area**

- The source lists an increase in asthma (hyperinflation).
- Decrease with alveolar rupture (emphysema and ageing).

**Thickness (d)**

- Normal = 0.6 μm.
- Increased in:
  - Lung fibrosis (fibrous tissue deposition).
  - Pulmonary oedema (water in interstitial tissue).
- Increased thickness reduces diffusion capacity.

**Diffusion constant (K)**

- Depends on solubility (S) and molecular weight (MW).
- K = S / √MW.

## Diffusion constants and clinical implication

- Assuming K for O₂ is 1:
  - CO₂ is 20.3, so the source states that CO₂ diffuses 20.3 times faster than O₂.
  - CO is 0.81.
- The source states: in most respiratory diseases, hypoxia is much greater than hypercapnia because CO₂ diffusion is 20.03 times faster than O₂.

## Diffusion equilibrium

- Transit time for an RBC to cross the alveolar zone of a capillary = 0.75 s.
- Diffusion equilibrium of O₂ = 0.25 s:
  - One-third of transit time.
  - The source states that no diffusion occurs in the remaining two-thirds of transit time.
- Diffusion equilibrium of CO₂ = 0.25 s.
- Average partial-pressure difference between alveolar and capillary blood:
  - ΔP for O₂ = 11 mmHg.
  - ΔP for CO₂ = 1 mmHg.

## Diffusion-limited and perfusion-limited gases

![[images/ch43_p313_diffusion_perfusion_limited_graph.png]]

- The source states: “Diffusion can be increased only by increasing fresh blood flow → diffusion-limited / perfusion-limited gases.” The classification bullets below are retained as printed.

**Flow-limited gases**

- Nitrous oxide (equilibrates rapidly).
- Oxygen.

**Diffusion-limited gas**

- Carbon monoxide (CO).
  - Diffusion is the limiting factor, not perfusion.
  - The partial pressure of CO will not increase during transit time because it is chemically bound to haemoglobin.
  - Diffusion can be increased by increasing inhalation of CO.
- Oxygen is also listed as diffusion-limited in hypoxia.

## Diffusion capacity of gases

- CO is used to measure lung diffusion capacity as an index gas for diffusion calculation.
- DᴸCO = 17 mL/min/mmHg.
- DᴸO₂ = (17 × 1.23) = 21 mL/min/mmHg.
  - ΔP for O₂ = 11 mmHg.
- Total amount of oxygen diffused in the lung = (21 × 11) = 230 mL/min.
- The source gives DᴸCO₂ = 21 × 20 = 420 (400–450) mL/min.

## Alveolar gas equation

- Inhaled air enters the conducting zone of the lung.
- Functions of the conducting zone:
  - Warms inhaled air.
  - Humidifies atmospheric air.
- The source describes PO₂ as decreasing from atmospheric air to the conducting zone, decreasing further on entering alveoli, and decreasing further at the capillary.

### Atmospheric air

- Barometric pressure (pB) = 760 mmHg.
- pB = pN₂ + pO₂ + pCO₂.
- Composition as printed:
  - N₂ = 72%.
  - O₂ = 21%.
  - CO₂ = 0.04%.
- Partial pressures as printed:
  - pN₂ = 760 × (79/100) ≈ 600 mmHg.
  - pO₂ = 160 mmHg.
  - pCO₂ = 0.03 mmHg.
- Partial pressure of water vapour at 37°C = 47 mmHg.

### Inspiratory air

- Atmospheric pO₂ = 160 mmHg.
- Water vapour is added in the conducting zone.
- pB = pH₂O + pN₂ + pO₂ + pCO₂.
- pO₂ = (pB − pH₂O) × 21/100 = PiO₂, the partial pressure of O₂ in inspiratory air.
- PiO₂ = (760 − 47) × 21/100 = 149–150 mmHg.

### Alveolar air

- Partial pressure of O₂ in alveoli (PaO₂) is presented as PiO₂ minus O₂ consumption.
- RQ = CO₂ production / O₂ consumption.
- PaO₂ = PiO₂ − (O₂ production / RQ) = PiO₂ − (PaCO₂ / RQ), as printed.
- PaO₂ = (pB − pH₂O) FiO₂ − (PaCO₂ / RQ).
- Normal values given:
  - FiO₂ = 21%.
  - FeO₂ = 16%.
- Example: PaO₂ = [(760 − 47) × (21/100) − (40/0.8)] = 104 mmHg ≈ 100 mmHg.

## Physiological shunt

- Capillary blood partial pressure of O₂ = PaO₂, as stated in the source.
- Deoxygenated blood from the bronchial vein mixes with oxygenated blood at the end of the pulmonary capillary.
- The source further describes deoxygenated blood from the bronchial vein mixing with blood at the end of the pulmonary capillary, decreasing arterial blood PO₂.
- The amount of venous blood is stated as 2% of total cardiac output.
- Hence, PaO₂ = 95–98 mmHg.

![[images/ch43_p315_physiological_shunt_diagram.png]]

## O₂ cascade

- Step-by-step decrease in partial pressure of O₂ is called the O₂ cascade.
- Partial pressure of O₂ at each level, as listed in the source:
  1. Atmospheric air (dry air) = 160 mmHg.
  2. Inspiratory air (PiO₂) = 149 mmHg.
  3. Alveolar air (PaO₂) = 100–104 mmHg.
  4. Arterial blood (PaO₂) = 95–98 mmHg.
  5. Tissue level = 23 mmHg (range 5–40 mmHg).
  6. Venous blood PvO₂ = 40 mmHg.

## MCQs

**1. Gas X is four times as soluble and four times as dense as gas Y. What is the ratio of diffusion rates of X to Y?**

- a. 16
- b. 8
- c. 4
- d. 2

**Answer: d. 2**

**2. At an altitude of 13,000 ft, barometric pressure is 447 mmHg. Calculate PiO₂ of moist air.** (JIPMER, December 2018)

- a. 100 mmHg
- b. 93 mmHg
- c. 84 mmHg
- d. 149 mmHg

**Answer: c. 84 mmHg**

**3. Calculate the alveolar–arterial oxygen gradient from the following information:** (JIPMER, December 2018)

- PaO₂ = 49 mmHg.
- PaCO₂ = 48 mmHg.
- Respiratory quotient = 0.8.

Options: a. 1 mmHg; b. 60 mmHg; c. 41 mmHg; d. 12 mmHg.

**Answer: c. 41 mmHg**
