# 36 — Tubular Functions

## 23. Tubular Functions

## Basic concept: TF/P ratio and tubular-fluid tonicity

The graph is plotted between the length of the nephron and the TF/P ratio, where TF is tubular-filtrate osmolality and P is plasma osmolality. A value of 1 on the y-axis indicates isotonic fluid.

![[images/ch36_p269_tubular_filtrate_plasma_ratio_graph.png]]

| Nephron segment | Tonicity and source description |
|---|---|
| Glomerulus (filtration) | Isotonic; the same as plasma osmolality. |
| Proximal tubule | Isotonic; solute reabsorption matches water reabsorption; TF/P = 1. |
| Descending thin segment of loop of Henle (LOH) | Hypertonic; only water is reabsorbed, without solute reabsorption; TF/P > 1. |
| Thick ascending limb of LOH | Described as isotonic; only solute is reabsorbed, without water reabsorption; TF/P = 1. |
| Distal convoluted tubule (DCT) | Hypotonic; more solute is reabsorbed, with negligible water reabsorption; TF/P < 1. |
| Collecting duct (CD), with ADH | Water reabsorption exceeds solute reabsorption → hypertonic urine; TF/P > 1. |
| Collecting duct (CD), without ADH | The source says there is no water reabsorption but solute is reabsorbed → hypotonic urine; TF/P < 1. |

![[images/ch36_p269_nephron_segment_tonicity_table.png]]

### Minimum and maximum tonicity stated in the source

| Condition | Minimum tonicity | Maximum tonicity |
|---|---|---|
| With ADH | DCT | End of collecting duct > tip of LOH |
| Without ADH | End of collecting duct | Tip of LOH |

## Glomerulotubular balance

- Maintains a balance between glomerular filtration and tubular reabsorption.
- Increased filtration leads to increased reabsorption.
- The percentage reabsorbed stays constant, but the number of reabsorbed molecules increases proportionally.
- Example:
  - 100 molecules of Na⁺ filtered → 70% reabsorbed (70 molecules).
  - If 200 molecules are filtered, proportionally 140 molecules are reabsorbed.

![[images/ch36_p270_glomerulotubular_balance_schematic.png]]

### Mechanism

- Increased filtration → higher protein concentration in peritubular capillaries (PTC) → higher oncotic pressure → enhanced reabsorption.
- Increased filtration → lower water content → lower hydrostatic pressure → facilitates water reabsorption.
- Low hydrostatic pressure aids water reabsorption.
- High oncotic pressure retains water and solutes in the PTC.

## Sodium transport

### Sodium reabsorption by nephron segment

- Proximal tubule: 67%.
- Thick ascending limb: 25%.
- DCT and cortical collecting duct (CCD): 5%.
- Medullary collecting duct (MCD): 3%.
- The source states that 100% is filtered and 99.6% is reabsorbed.
- Sodium is reabsorbed in every segment except the descending thin segment (DTS) of the loop of Henle.
- Sodium reabsorption is both active and passive; the source identifies the ascending thin segment (ATS) as the only site of passive reabsorption.

### Sodium reabsorption in the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)

- Tight junctions permit passive Na⁺ reabsorption through paracellular pathways.
- Luminal Na⁺ reabsorption mechanisms listed in the source:
  - SGLT: Na⁺ with glucose (5%).
  - Cotransporters: Na⁺ with amino acids and citric acid (45%).
  - NHE (sodium–hydrogen exchanger): 50%.
- H⁺ secreted into the lumen is neutralized by HCO₃⁻; the source states there is no acidification of urine at the PCT.
- NHE is stimulated by angiotensin II.
- Basolateral Na⁺ transport:
  - Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump: main active transport mechanism.
  - NBC: sodium–bicarbonate cotransporter.

![[images/ch36_p270_proximal_tubule_sodium_reabsorption_schematic.png]]

### Proximal straight tubule (PST)

- Slight increase in Cl⁻ concentration.
- Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are reabsorbed.

### Descending thin segment of the loop of Henle (DTS)

- No sodium reabsorption; impermeable to solute.

### Ascending thin segment (ATS)

- Na⁺ and Cl⁻ are reabsorbed passively through the paracellular pathway.

### Thick ascending limb (TAL)

- Sodium transport occurs through the NKCC channel (Na⁺–K⁺–2Cl⁻): 1 Na⁺, 1 K⁺ and 2 Cl⁻ are transported.
- NKCC is inhibited by furosemide.
- Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump: reabsorbed sodium exits the cell through this pump.
- ClC-Kb channel: chloride exits through this channel; the Barttin protein assists this process.
- ROMK channel: the source says potassium is reabsorbed into the lumen, maintaining electroneutrality.
- Reabsorption of Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺:
  - Positive luminal charge repels Ca²⁺ (25%) and Mg²⁺ (70%) into the paracellular space for reabsorption.
  - Maximum Mg²⁺ reabsorption occurs at the TAL.

![[images/ch36_p271_thick_ascending_limb_transport_schematic.png]]

### Calcium and magnesium reabsorption

| Ca²⁺ reabsorption | Mg²⁺ reabsorption |
|---|---|
| PCT: 65% | PCT: 15% |
| TAL: 25% | TAL: 70% |
| DCT: 8% (active reabsorption) | DCT, CCD, MCD: 10% |
| MCD: 1% | Urine: 5% of filtered load |
| Urine: 1% of filtered load | — |

The source labels the PCT/TAL Ca²⁺ handling as “Passive > Active” and separately labels DCT reabsorption as active.

![[images/ch36_p271_calcium_magnesium_reabsorption_table.png]]

### Bartter's syndrome

- Type 1: NKCC2 mutation.
- Type 2: ROMK mutation.
- Type 3: ClC-Kb mutation.
- Type 4: Barttin protein mutation.
- Type 5: extracellular calcium-sensing receptor and genes encoding chloride-channel subunits ClC-Ka and ClC-Kb.

## Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)

### NCC channel

- NCC is the sodium–chloride cotransporter.
- Thiazides inhibit NCC and promote calcium reabsorption:
  - Thiazide inhibits NCC → decreased Na⁺ in the cytoplasm → increased NCX transporter activity → increased Ca²⁺ reabsorption.
- The source calls thiazides the drug of choice for idiopathic hypercalciuria.
- NCC mutation is associated with Gitelman's syndrome.

### TRPV5/6 channel

- Reabsorbs calcium from the lumen into the cytoplasm.
- Stimulated by PTH.
- Calbindin binds calcium and transports it from the luminal to the basolateral membrane; the source describes this as increasing cytoplasmic calcium.
- NCX transports Ca²⁺ from the basolateral membrane to the PTC in exchange for Na⁺ from the PTC, as phrased in the source.
- NCX and calbindin are stimulated by vitamin D₃.

![[images/ch36_p272_distal_convoluted_tubule_calcium_transport_schematic.png]]

## Collecting duct (CD)

### Principal (P) cells

- ENaC channel reabsorbs Na⁺.
- ENaC is inhibited by ANP and amiloride (K⁺-sparing).
- ROMK secretes K⁺ back into the lumen.
- Aquaporin-2 mediates water reabsorption regulated by ADH.

### Intercalated (I) cells

- H⁺-ATPase and H⁺–K⁺-ATPase.
- Secrete H⁺ into the lumen and acidify urine.
- H⁺–K⁺-ATPase exchanges K⁺ for H⁺.

![[images/ch36_p272_collecting_duct_cell_transport_schematic.png]]

### Important information from the source

- In the collecting duct, whenever Na⁺ is reabsorbed, either K⁺ or H⁺ is secreted into the lumen.
- Secretion depends on the level of K⁺/H⁺ ions in blood.
- Example given: acidosis (increased H⁺ in blood) → secretion of H⁺ ions → K⁺ remains in the blood → hyperkalemia.

### Aldosterone action

- Stimulates ENaC, ROMK and H⁺-ATPase.
- Excess aldosterone (Conn's syndrome), as described in the source:
  - Increased Na⁺ reabsorption → hypertension.
  - Increased K⁺ excretion → hypokalemia.
  - Decreased H⁺ → alkalosis.

### ENaC mutations

| Liddle's syndrome | Pseudohypoaldosteronism type 1 |
|---|---|
| Mutation of β and γ subunits. Gain-of-function mutation (ENaC hyperfunction) → high aldosterone effects. | Loss-of-function mutation involving α, β and γ subunits → impaired Na⁺ reabsorption and impaired aldosterone effects. |

## Glucose transport

- The proximal tubule absorbs 100% of filtered glucose in the source description.
- It is divided into PCT and PST in the source diagram.

![[images/ch36_p273_glucose_transport_in_pct_and_pst_schematic.png]]

| Transporter | PCT | PST |
|---|---|---|
| Luminal transporter | SGLT-II: absorbs 1 Na⁺ + 1 glucose molecule | SGLT-I: absorbs 2 Na⁺ + 1 glucose molecule |
| Basolateral transporter | GLUT-2: facilitated diffusion of glucose into blood | GLUT-1: facilitated diffusion of glucose into blood |

- The source diagram assigns 90% of glucose transport to PCT/SGLT-II and 10% to PST/SGLT-I.
- Dapagliflozin and canagliflozin inhibit SGLT-II → glucose excretion and decreased blood glucose.
- The source states that these drugs are antidiabetic without affecting SGLT-I.

![[images/ch36_p273_glucose_transporters_table.png]]

### Transport maximum (Tmax) of glucose

- Tmax is the maximum amount of glucose reabsorbed by the proximal tubules before saturation.
- The source gives Tmax = 375 mg/min (tubular load) or 300 mg/dL (plasma level).
- Theoretical concept: an ideal scenario in which all nephrons function fully and equally; the source says this is practically incorrect.

### Renal threshold

- Theoretical Tmax: 375 mg/min.
- The source gives the actual renal threshold as **200 mg/dL or 250 mg/min**.
- Glucose appears in urine when the renal threshold exceeds 200 mg/dL.
- The source labels the renal-threshold account “practically correct.”

![[images/ch36_p273_glucose_tmax_and_renal_threshold_table.png]]

### Splay effect and Tmax curve

- The source defines the splay effect as the difference between Tmax (375 mg/min) and renal threshold (250 mg/min), attributed to uneven nephron function.
- The Tmax curve depicts the splay effect.
- The ideal curve assumes 100% nephron efficiency.
- The actual curve deviates at the renal threshold because of uneven nephron function; the gap between the curves is the splay effect.

![[images/ch36_p274_glucose_splay_curve.png]]

## Water transport

- Proximal tubule: reabsorbs 65% of water.
- Descending thin segment (DTS): reabsorbs 15% of water.
- Aquaporin-1 is present on luminal and basolateral membranes for water reabsorption.

### Effect of ADH

| Obligatory reabsorption | Facultative reabsorption |
|---|---|
| ADH-independent. Occurs in PCT and DTS. | ADH-dependent. Occurs in DCT and collecting duct (CD). With ADH, 15% water is reabsorbed; without ADH, 2% water is reabsorbed, leading to water loss. |

- Maximum water reabsorption, whether ADH is present or absent, occurs in the PCT.

![[images/ch36_p274_obligatory_vs_facultative_water_reabsorption_table.png]]

### Mechanism of facultative water reabsorption

- Aquaporin-2 channels on the DCT/CD enhance reabsorption.
- Aquaporins 3 and 4 reabsorb water from the cell cytoplasm.
- V2-receptor activation by ADH increases cAMP levels and mobilizes aquaporin-2 to the luminal surface.

## Potassium transport

Potassium can be reabsorbed or secreted depending on blood levels.

| Low K⁺ state — reabsorption | High K⁺ state — secretion |
|---|---|
| PCT: 80% of K⁺ | PCT: 80% |
| TAL: 10% | TAL: 10% |
| DCT and cortical CD: 2% | DCT and cortical CD: “20 + 180%” as printed in the source |
| Medullary CD: 6% | Medullary CD: 20–40% |
| Urine: 2% | Urine: 10–150% of K⁺ |

![[images/ch36_p274_potassium_transport_low_vs_high_states_table.png]]

## MCQs

### 1. Fluid leaving the PCT in the absence of ADH is:

(a) Isotonic; (b) Hypotonic; (c) Hypertonic; (d) Depends on fluid intake.

**Answer: (a)** *(INICET 2022)*

### 2. In the PCT of the kidney, luminal concentration of all of the following will decrease, except:

(a) Chloride; (b) Bicarbonate; (c) Glucose; (d) Amino acids.

**Answer: (a)** *(INICET 2020–2021)*

### 3. Concentration of which substance is decreased at the end of the PCT?

(a) Urea; (b) Glucose; (c) Creatinine; (d) Chloride.

**Answer: (b)** *(INICET 2021)*

### 4. The percentage of filtered load of different substances in different parts of the nephron is shown in the graph. What is label D?

(a) PAH; (b) Inulin; (c) Bicarbonate; (d) Glucose.
