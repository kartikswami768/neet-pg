# 35 — Renal Clearance

## 22. Renal Clearance

### Clearance

Clearance is the volume of plasma that is completely cleared of a substance by the kidneys per unit of time.

## Clearance of creatinine

### Creatinine

- An endogenously produced substance.
- Freely filterable.
- Plasma (700 mL/min in the source example) flows through glomerular capillaries and distributes creatinine homogeneously.
- Plasma, including creatinine, is filtered at the glomerular capillaries.

### Filtration and secretion

- Creatinine is filtered into Bowman's capsule and then partially secreted in the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT).
- Creatinine remaining in the blood flows through the peritubular capillaries; the source describes it as secreted at the level of the PCT.
- Urine contains creatinine filtered into Bowman's capsule and creatinine secreted at the PCT.

![[images/ch35_p264_creatinine_filtration_and_secretion_schematic.png]]

### Clearance equation

- Clearance is the total plasma volume cleared of a substance at the level of the kidney.
- Measurement described in the source: 24-hour urine analysis.

\[
\text{Creatinine clearance}=\frac{\text{Amount of creatinine in urine (mg/min)}}{\text{Plasma concentration of creatinine (mg/mL)}}
\]

The resulting unit is mL/min, a volume per unit time.

\[
\text{Amount in urine (mg/min)}=V\times U
\]

- \(V\): urine flow rate (mL/min).
- \(U\): urine concentration of the substance (mg/mL).
- \(P\): plasma concentration of the substance (mg/mL).

\[
C=\frac{UV}{P}
\]

Sources of substances in urine:

1. Filtration.
2. Secretion.
3. Reabsorption.

\[
C=\frac{\text{Filtration} + \text{Secretion} - \text{Reabsorption}}{\text{Plasma concentration}}
\]

- Creatinine is not reabsorbed.

## Clearance of inulin

- Inulin is freely filterable.
- It is neither secreted nor reabsorbed; therefore, it must be injected to measure its clearance.

\[
C_{\text{inulin}}=\frac{UV}{P}=\frac{\text{Filtration}}{\text{Plasma concentration}}=\frac{\text{Filtration volume}}{\text{Time}}=\text{GFR}
\]

- \(C_{\text{inulin}}=\text{GFR}=125\text{ mL/min}\).
- Inulin clearance is identified as the **gold standard for measuring GFR**.

### Measured GFR and estimated GFR

| Measured GFR | Estimated GFR (eGFR) |
|---|---|
| Gold standard: inulin clearance. | Based on endogenous filtration markers in blood. |
| Other clearances listed: iothalamate, iohexol, ⁹⁹ᵐTc-DTPA, ⁵¹Cr-EDTA. | Creatinine (most common) and cystatin C (alternative). |
| Precise and accurate. | Recommended by clinical practice guidelines. |
| Costly and time-consuming. | — |

## Clearance and GFR

### Clearance of a substance secreted in the nephron

\[
C=\frac{UV}{P}=\frac{\text{Filtration} + \text{Secretion}}{\text{Plasma concentration}}
\]

- Clearance is much greater than GFR: \(C\gg\text{GFR}\).

### Clearance of a substance reabsorbed in the nephron

\[
C=\frac{UV}{P}=\frac{\text{Filtration} - \text{Reabsorption}}{\text{Plasma concentration}}
\]

- Clearance is much less than GFR: \(C\ll\text{GFR}\).

![[images/ch35_p265_clearance_compared_with_gfr_table.png]]

The relative clearances shown in the source are:

\[
C_{\text{PAH}}\gg C_{\text{creatinine}}\gg C_{\text{inulin}}\gg C_{\text{urea}}\gg C_{\text{sodium}}\gg C_{\text{glucose}}
\]

- The greater the secretion, the higher the clearance.

### Clearance of potassium

- Potassium is both secreted and reabsorbed.
- Hypokalemia → potassium is reabsorbed → \(C_{\text{inulin}}\gg C_{\text{potassium}}\).
- Hyperkalemia → potassium is secreted → \(C_{\text{potassium}}\gg C_{\text{inulin}}\).

### Clearance of glucose in a non-diabetic person

- Amount of glucose in urine = 0.

\[
C=\frac{UV}{P}=\frac{0}{P}=0
\]

## Clearance of para-aminohippurate (PAH)

- PAH is freely filterable.
- It is secreted at the PCT.
- PAH clearance \((C_{\text{PAH}})\) is used to estimate renal plasma flow (RPF).
- PAH is not endogenous; it is injected into the blood to measure clearance.

### PAH extraction by the kidney

- First clearance: on reaching the glomerular capillary, some PAH is filtered into Bowman's capsule (20%).
- Second clearance: at the PCT, PAH is secreted (80%).
- 20% + 80% ≈ 100% of plasma is cleared through the kidney; this is related to RPF.

![[images/ch35_p266_pah_clearance_schematic.png]]

- The source states that PAH clearance does not give the full RPF, but instead gives effective RPF (approximately 625 mL/min).
- \(C_{\text{PAH}}\) is stated as approximately 90% of plasma.
- The source notes that not 100% of blood passing through the kidney passes through the afferent arteriole and states that 10% passes through the “renal capsule.”
- Actual RPF is given as:

\[
\text{Actual RPF}=\frac{\text{ERPF}}{0.9}\approx700\text{ mL/min}
\]

### Transport maximum of PAH

- Injecting large amounts of PAH increases plasma concentration; the source states that 20% is filtered into Bowman's capsule and 80% moves into peritubular capillaries.
- Increased PAH levels in peritubular capillaries cause incomplete secretion at the PCT because carrier proteins become saturated.
- Clearance equation:

\[
C_{\text{PAH}}=\frac{\text{Amount in urine}}{\text{Plasma concentration}}=\frac{\text{Filtration} + \text{Secretion}}{\text{Plasma concentration}}
\]

- At high PAH concentration, secretion becomes constant because of carrier-protein saturation. The amount in urine is then stated as constant relative to the source's explanation, and clearance becomes low.
- Injecting a high amount of PAH can therefore produce a falsely low RPF measurement.

![[images/ch35_p266_pah_transport_maximum_schematic.png]]

### Clearance curves

- X-axis: concentration of the substance.
- Y-axis: clearance.

![[images/ch35_p266_renal_clearance_curves.png]]

| Curve | Description in source |
|---|---|
| PAH clearance | At PAH concentration ≤ 10 mg/dL, \(C_{\text{PAH}}\) = RPF (625 mL/min). Above 10 mg/dL, \(C_{\text{PAH}}\) decreases, producing a falsely low RPF value. |
| Inulin clearance curve | A straight line because inulin is freely filterable; equal to 125 mL/min (GFR). |
| Creatinine clearance curve | Lies just above the inulin clearance curve because creatinine clearance is greater than inulin clearance. |
| Glucose/amino-acid clearance curve | Initially zero (\(C_{\text{glucose}}=0\)); later becomes positive and approaches the inulin clearance curve. |

## MCQs

### 1. In the formula for urea clearance (UV/P), what does “U” signify?

(a) Urine volume; (b) Serum urea in mg/dL; (c) Urine urea in g/24 hour; (d) Urine urea in mg/mL.

**Answer: (d)** *(INI-CET 2021; AIIMS 2016)*

### 2. Which of the following is not required for the calculation of creatinine clearance?

(a) Urine creatinine; (b) Serum creatinine; (c) Volume of urine in 24 hours; (d) Volume of serum in the kidney in 24 hours.

**Answer: (d)** *(AIIMS 2020)*
