# 03 — Cellular Physiology

> **Source:** Prep XI Physiology notes, printed pages 17–22 (PDF pages 17–22). Original source page images are embedded to preserve labelled illustrations and detailed junction diagrams.


### Source page 17

#### Cell

The source cell diagram labels: cell membrane, Golgi apparatus, nucleolus, nucleus, smooth endoplasmic reticulum, rough endoplasmic reticulum, cytoplasm, lysosome, microtubule, mitochondria, centrosome, ribosome, and vacuole.

#### Cell membrane

- Lipid bilayer (phospholipid).
- Thickness of the cell membrane: **7.5 nm**.
- Proteins are embedded in the layers.
- **Fluid Mosaic Model (1972):** Singer and Nicolson; proteins are present in the fluid phase.
- A phospholipid has a hydrophilic phosphate head facing outward and two hydrophobic tails facing inward.

#### Composition of the cell/plasma membrane

| Component | Proportion and details |
|---|---|
| Lipid | 42%: phospholipids 25%, cholesterol 13%, triglyceride 0% (as stated in the source) |
| Protein | 55%: integral/intrinsic membrane proteins span the membrane; peripheral/extrinsic proteins attach to intrinsic membrane proteins or to the cell-membrane surface |
| Carbohydrates | 3%, in the form of glycoproteins or glycolipids |

### Source page 18

#### Protein-to-lipid ratio

- The protein:lipid ratio varies across membranes.
- Highest ratio listed: inner mitochondrial membrane (**3:2**) and sarcoplasmic reticulum membrane (**2**).
- Lowest ratio listed: myelin sheath (**0.23**).

#### Cell cytoskeleton

- Maintains cell size and shape.
- The source groups microtubules and microfilaments as a **dynamic cytoskeleton** and intermediate filaments as a **static cytoskeleton**.
- Diameters in the source: microtubules **25 nm**, microfilaments **7 nm**, intermediate filaments **10 nm**.

| Cytoskeletal element | Properties and functions stated in the source |
|---|---|
| **Microtubules** | Hollow tubular structure; composed of α-tubulin and β-tubulin. A total of 13 tubulin subunits form the periphery/wall. Dynamic structure: growing end is the (+) end; non-growing end is the (−) end. Cell growth involves tubulin polymerization, using GTP as the energy source. γ-tubulin is also present at the centrosome. Functions: intracellular transport; components of cilia and flagella. |
| **Microfilaments** | Composed of actin; globular subunits are G-actin. The source identifies actin as the most abundant intracellular protein in mammalian cells. Further details continue on p. 19. |
| **Intermediate filaments** | Presented as a static element; details continue on p. 19. |

### Source page 19

#### Cytoskeleton (continued)

**Microfilaments**

- Individual subunits are globular **G-actin**.
- Multiple G-actin molecules attach to form a long filament, **F-actin**.
- F-actin also helps in muscle contraction; two F-actin filaments intertwine.
- Dynamic cytoskeleton: the end that grows by polymerization is the positive end; the other, non-growing end, is the negative end.
- **ATP** is the energy source stated for microfilament assembly.

**Intermediate filaments**

- Fixed structure.
- Cell-specific; used as cell markers to identify different cell types.
- Connect the nuclear membrane and cell membrane, helping the cell withstand external pressure.

| Cell marker | Cell type listed in the source |
|---|---|
| Cytokeratin | Epithelial cell |
| Vimentin | Mesenchymal cell |
| Desmin | Skeletal muscle cell |
| Glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) | Astrocytes |

#### Drugs and cytoskeleton

- **Colchicine and vinblastine:** act on microtubules, preventing assembly (polymerization) and blocking mitosis.
- **Paclitaxel (Taxol):** binds to microtubules and stabilizes them; the source states that organelles cannot move, mitotic spindles are not formed, cell division is blocked, and cells die.

#### Intercellular junctions — overview

- **Tight junction:** seals the gap between epithelial cells; occluding junction.
- **Adherens junction:** connects actin-filament bundles.
- **Desmosome:** connects intermediate filaments.
- **Gap junction:** allows passage of small water-soluble molecules; channel-forming junction.
- **Cell–matrix anchoring junctions:** focal adhesions and hemidesmosomes attach to the basal membrane.

### Source page 20

#### Intercellular junctions — location and proteins

| Position on cell | Interaction/junction | Proteins and source notes |
|---|---|---|
| Apical portion | Tight junction (TJ), occluding junction, zona occludens | Claudin, occludin, junctional adhesion molecule (JAM). “Tight” TJ: no molecule penetrates. “Leaky” TJ: molecules can penetrate via paracellular transport. |
| Below tight junction | Adherens junction (AJ), zona adherens | Cadherin and catenin |
| Below adherens junction | Desmosome | Desmoglein and cadherin (as written in the source) |
| Below desmosome | Gap junction/channel-forming junction | Connexon; passes small molecules between cells, including Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺, and H₂O; impermeable to proteins |
| Lowermost part | Cell–matrix junction attached to basal membrane (BM) | Integrin. Two types: focal adhesions and hemidesmosomes. Focal adhesions are labile and help cells move from one place to another. |

**Intracellular attachments**

- Adherens junctions and focal adhesions attach to actin filaments.
- Desmosomes and hemidesmosomes attach to intermediate filaments.

#### Gap-junction structure

- The source diagram labels presynaptic/postsynaptic cytoplasm, the normal extracellular space, a channel formed by membrane pores, connexin/connexon subunits, and dimensions of 3.5 nm and 120 nm.
- Six connexin subunits form one connexon (hemichannel).
- Each of the six connexins has four membrane-spanning regions; the diagram also labels cytoplasmic loops for regulation and extracellular loops for homophilic interactions.

### Source page 21

#### Gap junction — structural details

- Gap between two plasma membranes generally: **20 nm**.
- Gap between plasma membranes at a gap junction: **3.5 nm**.
- A gap junction is formed by two hemiconnexon molecules.
- One connexon protein is composed of six subunits (connexin molecules).
- One connexin molecule has four membrane-spanning domains.
- Pore diameter: **0.8–1.4 nm**.
- Permeability: up to **1000 Da molecular weight**.
- The source states that 20 different genes form connexin proteins.

#### Connexins in disease

The source associates connexin mutations with approximately 20 human diseases:

- **Clouston syndrome:** connexin 30 defect; ectodermal dysplasia.
- **Erythrokeratoderma variabilis:** Cx30.3 and Cx31.
- **Cataract:** Cx46 and Cx50.
- **Idiopathic atrial fibrillation:** Cx40.
- **X-linked Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease:** Cx32.

### Source page 22

#### MCQs

**Q1.** A research scholar studying the cell cytoskeleton finds stable strands of fibrous proteins that distribute tensile strength across tissue. Which structure best fits the description? (NEET 2021)

- A. Microfilament
- B. Microtubules
- C. Intermediate filaments
- D. All of the above

**Answer: C — Intermediate filaments**

**Q2.** Which type of amino acids are present in the transmembrane part of a membrane receptor? (INI-CET 2020)

- A. Amphipathic
- B. Hydrophobic
- C. Hydrophilic
- D. None

**Answer: B — Hydrophobic**
