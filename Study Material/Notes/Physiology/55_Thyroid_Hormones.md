# 55. Thyroid Hormones

## Thyroid gland: histology

- The thyroid gland consists of follicles lined by cuboidal epithelial cells, enclosing colloid.
- Parafollicular/C cells lie adjacent to follicles and produce calcitonin.

**Thyroid follicle histology**

![[images/ch55_p381_thyroid_follicle_histology.png]]
- **Thyroglobulin (Tg):**
  - A large protein with multiple tyrosine residues.
  - Synthesized by follicular cuboidal cells and secreted into colloid.
  - Serves as the backbone for thyroid-hormone synthesis.
- The follicular cells facilitate T3, T4 and reverse T3 (rT3) production; the source says the actual synthesis occurs in colloid.
- T3, T4 and rT3 are stored in colloid, with reserves stated to sustain thyroid function for 2–3 months.

### Inactive vs active thyroid gland

| Inactive gland (not stimulated by TSH) | Active gland (stimulated by TSH) |
|---|---|
| Abundant colloid | Small follicles |
| Large follicles | Columnar cells |

## Thyroid hormone synthesis

### Structure
- T4 (tetraiodothyronine): iodine is attached at positions 3, 5, 3′ and 5′.

![[images/ch55_p382_t4_structure.png]]

### Steps of thyroid-hormone synthesis

1. **Uptake of iodide from blood**
   - Iodine trapping: iodide enters follicular cells through the Na⁺/I⁻ symporter (NIS).
   - This concentrates iodide in the thyroid gland to 20–30 times the level in blood.
   - The Cl⁻/I⁻ antiport / pendrin transporter, present at the apical portion, transports concentrated iodide into colloid.

2. **Thyroglobulin synthesis**
   - Thyroglobulin is synthesized by the rough endoplasmic reticulum of thyroid follicular cells and is stimulated by TSH.

3. **Oxidation of iodine**
   - Thyroid peroxidase (TPO), present at the apical membrane, oxidizes iodide (I⁻) to nascent iodine (the source labels this as nascent iodine, I⁰).

4. **Organification of iodine**
   - One iodine molecule attached to the 3-position of a tyrosine amino acid forms monoiodotyrosine (MIT).
   - Two iodine molecules attached at the 3- and 5-positions form diiodotyrosine (DIT).
   - TPO aids oxidation and organification.

5. **Coupling reactions**
   - Thyroglobulin folds, bringing MIT and DIT close for coupling:
     - MIT + DIT = T3.
     - DIT + DIT = T4.
     - DIT + MIT = rT3.
   - T3, T4 and rT3 remain bound to thyroglobulin after coupling.

6. **Endocytosis**
   - Thyroid follicular cells extend pseudopodia to engulf colloid by endocytosis, forming endocytic vesicles.
   - These vesicles contain Tg with T3, T4, MIT and DIT.

7. **Lysosomal degradation**
   - Lysosomes fuse with endocytic vesicles.
   - Lysosomal proteases break down Tg.
   - T3, T4, rT3, MIT and DIT are separated from Tg.

8. **Release of thyroid hormones**
   - T3, T4 and rT3 are secreted into capillary blood.
   - Being lipophilic, they cross the cell membrane to enter circulation.
   - Iodine from MIT and DIT is released and returns for thyroid-hormone synthesis.
   - Tyrosine residues also return for reuse.

### Additional information
- NIS is also present in placenta, salivary gland, mammary gland and GIT; the source states these are not stimulated by TSH.
- **Wolff–Chaikoff effect:** a large iodine dose prevents organification; the source says it is usually done before thyroid surgery to reduce thyroid size.
- Megalin protein can also cause endocytosis.

## Thyroid-hormone binding proteins

Thyroid hormones require binding proteins for transport in blood because of their lipophilic nature.

| Protein | Plasma concentration (mg/dL) | T4 bound (%) | T3 bound (%) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Thyroxine-binding globulin (TBG) | 2 | 67 | 46 |
| Transthyretin (thyroid-binding prealbumin) | 15 | 20 | 1 |
| Albumin | 3500 | 13 | 53 |

### Fluctuations in TBG

**Increased TBG (↑TBG) — agents listed**
- Estrogen.
- Methadone.
- Heroin.
- Antipsychotic drugs, including clofibrate.

**Decreased TBG (↓TBG) — agents listed**
- Glucocorticoids.
- Androgens.
- Danazol.
- Asparaginase.

### Total and free hormone
- Total hormone = protein-bound hormone + free hormone (biologically active).
- The source explains that an increase in protein-bound hormone reduces the free form and negative feedback to the pituitary, increasing TSH secretion; this normalizes the free form while total and protein-bound hormone rise.
- In conditions with increased or suppressed TBG, total thyroid-hormone estimation may be misleading.
- Free thyroid-hormone estimation is preferred for accurate assessment in such cases.

## Peripheral conversion

- Deiodinases I and II (5′-deiodinases) convert T4 into 3,5,3′-triiodothyronine (T3), the most active form.
  - 5′-deiodinase removes iodine from the 5′ position of T4.
- Deiodinases I and III (5-deiodinases) convert T4 into 3,3′,5′-triiodothyronine (reverse T3/rT3), the inactive form.
  - 5-deiodinase removes iodine from the 5 position of T4.
- 5-deiodinase converts T3 to T2 (3,3′-diiodothyronine).
- 5′-deiodinase converts rT3 to T2.
- T2 (3,3′-diiodothyronine) is labelled as the most inactive form.

### Secretion and conversion amounts shown in the source
- The thyroid gland also secretes T3 (4 µg/day) and rT3 (2 µg/day).
- Total T3 in blood: 87% from T4 and 13% from the thyroid.
- Total rT3 in blood: 95% from T4 and 5% from the thyroid.

## Difference between T4 and T3

| Parameter | T4 | T3 |
|---|---|---|
| Secretion | More (93% of total) | Less (7% of total) |
| Plasma concentration | 8 µg/dL | 0.15 µg/dL |
| Protein-bound | 99.98% | 99.8% |
| Free (as transcribed in the source table) | 0.02% | 0.2% |
| “Total Free %” row as printed | 2 ng/dL | 0.3 ng/dL |
| Half-life (t½) | 7 days | 1 day |
| Action | Slower | Faster |
| Potency | Less | More (3–5 times) |
| Binding to receptor | Low affinity | High affinity |

## Functions of thyroid hormones

| System / action | Effects listed in the source |
|---|---|
| **Cardiovascular system** | Increased heart rate and contractility → increased systolic blood pressure (SBP); reduced total peripheral resistance (TPR) → decreased diastolic blood pressure (DBP). |
| **CNS** | Maturation of CNS; deficiency is associated in the source with mental retardation. Hyperthyroidism → hyperexcitability and irritability. |
| **Skeletal muscle** | Increased protein catabolism and effects on myosin heavy-chain (MHC) gene expression in skeletal muscle. Muscle weakness in hyperthyroidism (thyrotoxic myopathy). |
| **Growth** | Synergistic with GH and somatomedins to promote bone formation; stimulates bone maturation through ossification and fusion of growth plates. In thyroid-hormone deficiency, bone age is lower than chronological age. |
| **Calorigenic action** | Increases BMR in every tissue except gonads, lymph nodes, spleen and brain (as listed in the source). |
| **Metabolism** | Increases glucose absorption from GIT; increases glycogenolysis, gluconeogenesis and glucose oxidation; increases lipolysis; increases LDL receptors in liver and hepatic removal of cholesterol; increases protein synthesis and degradation. Overall effect is described as catabolic. |

### Effect of diet on conversion of T4 to T3
- T4 is converted into:
  - T3 (active) → increases BMR.
  - rT3 (inactive).
- In starvation, most T4 is converted to rT3, with a proportional decrease in T3 (symbol direction preserved from the source text).

### Hypothyroidism

| Type | Primary site | Pattern / causes as given in the source |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Thyroid gland | Decreased T4 → loss of negative feedback → increased TSH and TRH. Causes listed: Hashimoto's thyroiditis (goitrous thyroiditis; antibodies against TPO and Tg) and atrophic hypothyroidism. |
| **Secondary** | Pituitary gland | Decreased T4, decreased TSH and increased TRH . Causes listed: hypopituitarism and isolated TSH deficiency (rare). |
| **Tertiary** | Hypothalamus | Low TRH, low TSH and low T4 as indicated in the source. Causes listed: hypothalamic tumours, trauma, infiltrative disorders and idiopathic causes. |

### Hyperthyroidism

| Type | Primary site | Pattern / causes as given in the source |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Thyroid gland | Increased T4, decreased TSH and decreased TRH. Causes: Graves' disease (antibodies against TSH receptor / thyroid-stimulating antibody), toxic multinodular goitre and toxic adenoma. |
| **Secondary** | Pituitary | Increased TSH, increased T4 and decreased TRH; pituitary TSHoma. |
| **Tertiary** | Hypothalamus | Increased TRH, TSH and T4, as indicated in the source. |

**Classification of hyperthyroidism**

![[images/ch55_p386_hyperthyroidism_classification.png]]

- **Thyrotoxicosis:** signs and symptoms arising from an increased level of thyroid hormones.
- **Hyperthyroidism:** results from excessive thyroid function.


## Clinical features

| Hyperthyroidism | Hypothyroidism |
|---|---|
| Weight loss | Weight gain |
| Heat intolerance, sweating | Cold intolerance, somnolence |
| Palpitations (↑THR), tremor | Bradycardia |
| Irritability, emotional lability | Dry skin, dry hair |

| Amenorrhoea / oligomenorrhoea | Menorrhagia |

### Low TSH that improves after TRH
**Question:** A person presents with puffy skin and lethargy. TSH levels are low and improve after administration of TRH. What is the probable diagnosis? (NEET 2020)

- a. Diffuse thyroid enlargement
- b. Hypothyroidism because of a hypothalamic defect
- c. Hyperthyroidism because of a hypothalamic defect
- d. Hypothyroidism because of a pituitary defect

**Answer as printed:** (b).

- b. Hashimoto's disease
- c. Pituitary adenoma
- d. Hypoparathyroidism

**Answer as printed:** (b) Hashimoto's disease.
