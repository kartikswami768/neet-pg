# 24. Counter Current System

## Criteria

### Prerequisites for a counter-current system

- Two parallel tubes must be present.
- Fluids in the tubes flow in opposite directions.
- The tubes should be closely placed.
- Selective permeability is required. For example, if the left-hand side is permeable to water, the right-hand side is impermeable to water.

![[images/ch37_p277_selective_permeability_schematic.png]]

### Sites

- Intestinal villi
- Skin
- Testes
- Kidney

## Counter-current system

The counter-current system is divided into:

- **Counter-current multiplier:** generation of medullary hypertonicity.
- **Counter-current exchanger:** maintenance of medullary hypertonicity.

![[images/ch37_p277_countercurrent_system_tree.png]]

### Counter-current system in the kidney

- **Aim:** To maintain a hypertonic medulla, facilitating water reabsorption and concentrating urine.
- It operates in juxtamedullary nephrons, involving:
  - Loop of Henle (LOH)
  - Peritubular capillaries

| Counter-current multiplier | Counter-current exchanger |
|---|---|
| Creates medullary hypertonicity. | Maintains medullary hypertonicity. |
| Key sites: Loop of Henle (LOH), especially the thick ascending limb (TAL; main site); the ascending thin segment has the least role; medullary collecting duct. | Component: vasa recta (peritubular capillaries). |

![[images/ch37_p277_multiplier_vs_exchanger_table.png]]

## Counter-current multiplier

- Glomerular filtrate at the corticomedullary junction is isotonic (approximately 300 mOsm/L).
- The thick ascending limb (TAL) reabsorbs Na⁺ and Cl⁻, increasing medullary osmolarity.
- Water remains in the TAL because it is impermeable to water.
- Urea reabsorption in the medullary collecting ducts is enhanced by ADH.
- Hypertonicity increases from the cortex to the medulla due to sodium, chloride and urea.
- Water moves from the low-solute concentration (300 mOsm/L) in the descending thin segment to the higher-solute concentration (500 mOsm/L) in the medullary interstitium.
- **Tonicity of the medulla:**
  - Lowest: cortex.
  - Highest: inner medullary region.
  - Maximum: 1400 mOsm/L.
- Generation of the corticomedullary osmotic gradient is performed by the counter-current multiplier.
- **Sodium chloride and urea contribution:**
  - NaCl: 50–60% (approximately 600 mOsm/L).
  - Urea: 40–50% (approximately 600 mOsm/L).
- The source also states: **maximal medullary tonicity approximately 1200 mOsm/L**.
- Single most important substance for medullary hypertonicity: urea.
- Most important substance for medullary hypertonicity in combination: sodium chloride.

![[images/ch37_p278_countercurrent_multiplier_schematic.png]]

### Urea transporters

- **UT-A**
  - UT-A1: apical membrane of the medullary collecting duct (MCD).
  - UT-A3: basolateral membrane of the MCD.
  - UT-A2: descending thin segment (DTS) of the Loop of Henle.
  - The source groups these UT-A transporters under “stimulated by ADH.”
- **UT-B:** present at the level of red blood cells (RBCs).

### Steps of the counter-current multiplier

1. **Initial tonicity:** Fluid in the descending thin segment (DTS) starts isotonic at 300 mOsm/L.
2. **Action in the thick ascending limb (TAL):**
   - Na⁺, Cl⁻ and K⁺ are reabsorbed.
   - K⁺ diffuses back into the lumen.
   - This reabsorption decreases lumen tonicity and increases medullary interstitial tonicity, initiating the counter-current multiplier.
3. **Osmosis in the DTS:**
   - Water is reabsorbed from the DTS by osmosis, increasing lumen tonicity and decreasing interstitial tonicity.
   - Equilibrium is reached when both reach equal tonicity (for example, 400 mOsm/L).
4. **Cycle repeats:**
   - Fresh fluid (300 mOsm/L) from the proximal tubule pushes the 400 mOsm/L solution downward to the TAL.
   - At the TAL, Na⁺, Cl⁻ and K⁺ are absorbed again, increasing medullary interstitial tonicity to approximately 500 mOsm/L.
   - The process continues, maintaining a 200 mOsm/L gradient between the TAL and interstitial tonicity.
   - The cycle repeats 4–6 times, reaching a final tonicity of 1200–1400 mOsm/L.

## Counter-current exchanger

- Blood vessels: **vasa recta**.
- **Role:** Maintains medullary hypertonicity.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Solutes absorbed in the medullary interstitium correct the solute concentration of the vasa recta capillary.
  - Solutes recirculate back into the interstitium at the ascending limb of the LOH, preventing washout.
  - Blood flow in the vasa recta is slow, ensuring solute reabsorption and maintaining hypertonicity.

![[images/ch37_p279_vasa_recta_exchanger_diagram.png]]

### Vasa recta

- **Straight tube:** If straight instead of looped, solutes are washed into the renal vein, resulting in hypotonic urine.
- **Slow blood flow:** Increased blood-flow velocity causes solute washout and decreased medullary tonicity.

## MCQs

### 1. Counter-current mechanism is not seen in which of the following? *(AIIMS 2016)*

- a. Gut
- b. Eye
- c. Kidney
- d. Testis

**Answer: (b)**

### 2. The counter-current mechanism is present in which of the following parts of the body? *(INI-CET 2022)*

- A. Eye
- B. Testes
- C. Kidney
- D. Gut
- E. Lungs

**Options:**

- a. A and B are correct
- b. A, B and C are correct
- c. A, B, C, D and E are correct
- d. B, C, D and E are correct

**Answer: (d)**

### 3. The prime driving force for the counter-current multiplier system is. *(AIIMS)*

- a. Active reabsorption of Na⁺ in the thick ascending limb
- b. Action of ADH via aquaporin channels
- c. Urea recycling
- d. Medullary hyperosmolarity

**Answer: (a)**

### 4. Which of the following is not a component of the counter-current multiplier mechanism?

- a. Thick ascending Loop of Henle
- b. Collecting duct
- c. Vasa recta
- d. Thin descending Loop of Henle

**Answer: (c)**
