# 57. Hormonal Regulation of Calcium Balance

## Calcium distribution

- Total calcium in the body: approximately **1.1 kg**.
- **Skeletal calcium: 99%**.
- **Non-skeletal calcium: 1%**, divided into:
  - Intracellular storage: 0.9%.
  - Extracellular fluid: 0.1%.
- Extracellular calcium maintains a plasma concentration of **10 mg/dL**, **5 mEq/L**, or **2.5 mmol/L**.
- ECF calcium distribution:
  - Ionized calcium: 50% (1.2 mmol/L).
  - Protein-bound calcium: 41% (1.0 mmol/L).
  - Calcium complexed to anions: 9% (0.2 mmol/L).
- Ionized calcium is the biologically active form.
- Normal plasma calcium: 10 mg/dL.

## Hypocalcemia and tetany

- Tetany occurs when total blood calcium is **less than 6 mg/dL**.
- It is characterized by persistent contraction of skeletal muscle due to hyperexcitability of neuronal membranes.
- Neuronal action potentials depend on voltage-gated sodium channels.
- Normally, extracellular calcium blocks the outer mouth of these channels and reduces sodium permeability.
- Low calcium reduces this block, increasing sodium permeability and neuronal excitability, producing tetany.

### Hyperventilation and tetany

- Hyperventilation → excess CO₂ loss from the body → alkalosis.
- Albumin (negatively charged) binds both Ca²⁺ and H⁺ (positively charged).
- H⁺ competes with Ca²⁺ for albumin binding.
  - **Acidosis:** excess H⁺ binds albumin → more free calcium → less tetany.
  - **Alkalosis:** low H⁺ → more calcium binds albumin → decreased free calcium → hyperexcitability (tetany).

## Bone physiology

### Types of bone

- Compact/cortical/lamellar bone: **80%**.
- Trabecular/cancellous/spongy/medullary bone: **20%**.
- Bone composition includes matrix, minerals, cells and blood vessels.

**Cortical and cancellous bone; bone matrix**

![[images/ch57_p395_bone_types_matrix.png]]

### Bone matrix

| Matrix component | Description | Contents / process |
|---|---|---|
| **Unmineralized matrix (osteoid)** | Soft, uncalcified part of bone | Secreted by osteoblasts; collagen I, chondroitin sulphate, osteocalcin and osteonectin |
| **Mineralized matrix** | Deposition of minerals in the soft matrix | Calcium and phosphate deposition; hydroxyapatite crystal; hard/calcified bone |

- Hydroxyapatite formula as printed: Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂.

### Osteoid and osteocytes

- Osteoblasts lie beneath the lining epithelium and form osteoid.
- Osteocytes are osteoblasts entrapped within the matrix during mineralization.
- Osteocytes are connected by cytoplasmic processes.
- Once entrapped, an osteocyte cannot return to the surface.

**Osteoid and mineralized bone structure**

![[images/ch57_p396_osteoid_bone_structure.png]]

### Bone remodelling

- Bone continuously renews itself through resorption of old bone and formation of new bone.
- Osteoblasts lie below the lining epithelium.
- Collagen molecules are secreted, and osteocalcin and osteonectin are deposited between collagen molecules.
- Ca²⁺ and PO₄³⁻ from blood enter the soft osteoid matrix.
- Hydroxyapatite forms and is deposited between the soft osteoid matrix to form hard matrix.
- Some osteoblasts become trapped in the matrix and remain connected by cytoplasmic processes, forming osteocytes.

### Formation and resorption

- Osteocytes function as mechanosensory cells, detecting mechanical stress on bone.
- Mechanical stress → activation of osteocytes → signals to surface osteoblasts.
- Osteoblasts secrete macrophage colony-stimulating factor (M-CSF), recruiting hematopoietic stem cells (HSCs).
- Monocyte–macrophage series activation: blood monocyte → macrophage.
- RANK is expressed on the surface of pro-osteoclasts.
- RANK-L (ligand) is secreted by osteoblasts.
- RANK binds RANK-L; single-nucleated pro-osteoclasts become multinucleated osteoclasts.
- Osteoclasts attach to the bone surface and secrete HCl/H⁺, creating an acidic environment in bone.
- Hydroxyapatite crystals break down into free calcium and phosphate ions in blood.
- Old bone is resorbed by osteoclasts while new bone is formed by osteoblasts continuously.

**Bone-remodelling and osteoclast-formation pathways**

![[images/ch57_p396_bone_remodeling_signaling.png]]

### Bone remodelling unit (BMU)

Sequence: **Activation → Resorption → Reversal → Formation → Quiescence**.

- During reversal, all osteoclasts disappear.
- Osteoblastic cells are recruited; pre-osteoblasts become mature osteoblasts.
- Quiescence corresponds to a resting bone surface.
- There are at least 1–2 million microscopic BMUs.
- 5–10% of bone is remodelled every year.

### Applied bone disorders

| Disorder | Features listed |
|---|---|
| **Osteoporosis** | Reduction in total bone mass, with equal loss of bone mineral and organic matrix; imbalance between resorption and formation; the osteoblast cannot refill the resorption lacuna, producing net bone loss with each remodelling event |
| **Osteomalacia/rickets** | Inadequate mineralization of new bone matrix; bone softening; reduced ratio of bone mineral to matrix; reduced bone strength; bones distort under mechanical loads |
| **Paget's disease** | Disordered bone remodelling; excessive breakdown followed by disorganized new bone formation; bone pain, deformities and fracture |
| **Osteopetrosis (“stone bone”)** | Osteoclasts are defective (their number may be variable) and cannot resorb bone while deposition is normal; increased bone density, nerve compression and haematologic abnormalities |

## Hormones for calcium regulation

### Parathyroid hormone (PTH)

- There are four parathyroid glands.
- Removal of two glands: no effect.
- Removal of three glands: transient hypocalcemia.
- A single gland can perform the function.
- **Chief cells:** secrete PTH.
- **Oxyphil cells:** listed as having no function.

**Parathyroid glands and cells**

![[images/ch57_p398_parathyroid_gland.png]]

### PTH secretion

| Stimuli for secretion | Inhibitors of secretion |
|---|---|
| Hypocalcemia | Hypercalcemia |
| Decrease in plasma Mg²⁺ (mild) | 1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D |
| Hyperphosphatemia | Severe hypomagnesemia |
| Catecholamines | Continued decrease in magnesium and calcium |

### Actions of PTH

- **Bone — fastest action of PTH**
  - PTH and vitamin D have receptors on osteoblasts.
  - Osteoblast signalling through M-CSF activates osteoclasts.
  - Increased bone resorption increases calcium and phosphate in blood.
- **Kidney — PCT and DCT**
  - **PCT:** stimulates 1α-hydroxylase, producing active vitamin D₃; inhibits phosphate reabsorption.
  - **DCT:** increases calcium reabsorption.
- **Intestine:** no direct action. Indirectly, active vitamin D₃ synthesised in the PCT acts on the intestine and enhances calcium and phosphate reabsorption.

### Vitamin D₃

- Cholecalciferol (D₃) is synthesised under the skin by UV light.
- Dietary sources listed:
  - D₃: animal sources.
  - Ergocalciferol (D₂): plants/diet.
- Vitamin D is delivered to the liver and hydroxylated to **25-(OH) vitamin D**, the major storage and circulating form.
- Low calcium: 25-(OH) vitamin D moves to the kidney; PTH activates 1α-hydroxylase → **1,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D**, the active form.
- High calcium stimulates 24α-hydroxylase → further hydroxylation to **24,25-(OH)₂ vitamin D**, the inactive form.

**Vitamin D metabolism**

![[images/ch57_p399_vitamin_d_metabolism.png]]

### Actions of vitamin D₃

- **Bone**
  - Normal level: bone deposition.
  - High levels: bone resorption.
  - With low calcium, vitamin D activates osteoblasts, which activate osteoclasts → bone resorption → more calcium and phosphate in blood.
- **Kidney**
  - DCT: calcium reabsorption.
  - PCT: phosphate reabsorption, enhanced by stimulation of the sodium–phosphate cotransporter.
- **Intestine:** increases calcium and phosphate reabsorption.
- PTH inhibits phosphate reabsorption by inactivating/stopping stimulation of the sodium–phosphate cotransporter.

### Molecular mechanism of calcium and phosphate transport

- **Kidney (DCT):** calcium is reabsorbed by TRPV5/6 channels, activated by PTH.
- **Intestine:** the mechanism is the same as in the DCT, except PTH cannot stimulate transport at the intestinal level.
- Calcium enters the cell → binds calbindin (activated by vitamin D₃) → is transported toward the basolateral membrane → enters blood through calcium ATPase (activated by vitamin D₃) and the sodium–calcium exchanger.
- Vitamin D₃ activates TRPV5/6, calbindin, the calcium ATPase transporter and the sodium–calcium exchanger.

### Calcitonin

- Synthesised and secreted by parafollicular C cells of the thyroid.
- Stimuli listed: serum calcium ≥9.5 mg%, CCK, glucagon, estrogen, secretin, dopamine and gastrin.
- Main action: inhibits bone resorption by inducing osteoclast apoptosis.

### Summary of calcium-regulating hormones

| Feature | PTH | Vitamin D | Calcitonin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stimulus for secretion | Low serum Ca²⁺; high serum phosphate | Low serum Ca²⁺; low serum phosphate | High serum Ca²⁺ |
| Bone | High resorption | High resorption | Low resorption |
| Kidney | ↓ PO₄ reabsorption; ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption | ↑ PO₄ reabsorption; ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption | — |
| Intestine | ↑ Ca²⁺ absorption (indirect) | ↑ PO₄ reabsorption; ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption | — |
| Overall effect on serum Ca²⁺ | ↑ | ↑ | ↓ |
| Overall effect on serum PO₄ | ↓ | ↑ | No arrow is shown in the source table |
