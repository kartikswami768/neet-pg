# 29 — Cardiac Output

## 16. Cardiac Output
### CARDIAC OUTPUT (CO)
- Cardiac output (CO) - the amount of blood pumped by each ventricle in 1 minute
- Stroke volume x Heart rate (HR) = Cardiac output
- The normal cardiac output value is 5.4 L/min for a 70-kg adult
- The cardiac index is cardiac output divided by a person's body surface area (BSA)
  - For a normal 70 kg adult: cardiac index = CO/BSA = 5.4/1.7 m² = 3.2 L/min/m² of body surface area
- The ejection fraction percentage is calculated as stroke volume / end-diastolic volume × 100.
  - The normal ejection fraction value is 65%, although the normal range is 55-70%
### DETERMINANTS OF CARDIAC OUTPUT
Stroke Volume (SV) × HR = CO; CO × Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR) = Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)  
- Heart rate is influenced by two systems, the Sympathetic & Parasympathetic (dominant) systems
- Two major reflexes control heart rate
  - The Bainbridge reflex
  - The Baroreceptor reflex
- Three factors that change stroke volume are Preload, Contractility & Afterload
  - Preload & Contractility positively correlate with stroke volume
  - Afterload is negatively correlated with stroke volume.
### PRELOAD
- "Pre" (before) & "load" (tension)
- The load (diastolic) on the ventricular wall before the onset of contraction
- Initial stretch on ventricle
- Preload depends on the volume of blood at the end of diastole (End-diastolic volume (EDV) of blood)
- The best index of preload is EDV/end-diastolic pressure
  - Right ventricle EDP: 1-7 mmHg; left ventricle EDP: 4-12 mmHg
- The best determinant of preload is the Venous return
### FRANK–STARLING LAW (FS law)
- ↑ EDV → ↑ stretch on ventricular muscle → ↑ ventricular muscle length → ↑ muscle contraction force.
- This is due to the length–tension relationship in cardiac muscle:
  - If the length of cardiac muscle increases, tension increases.
  - Cross-bridge formation at the sarcomere level increases.
  - Sensitivity of troponin C to calcium increases.
- The notes describe this as preload increasing the contractility of the heart (Frank–Starling law).

### CONTRACTILITY

| Increased contractility | Decreased contractility |
|---|---|
| Sympathetic system stimulation | Parasympathetic system stimulation |
| Drugs such as digoxin | Hypoxia, hypercapnia and acidosis |
| Bowditch effect | — |

### BOWDITCH'S EFFECT
- The source describes Bowditch's effect as the “forced frequency relationship” of the myocardium (source wording retained).
- ↑ HR → ↑ Contractility
- It is due to the molecular mechanism of contraction & calcium release during myocardial contraction
  - Increase in Ca²⁺ in myocardium due to lagging of Na⁺–K⁺ ATPase pump
### AFTERLOAD
- Load (systolic) on ventricles after the onset of contraction
- Systolic ventricular wall pressure
- The best index of afterload is Mean Aortic Pressure (MAP)
  - MAP is primarily determined by Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR)
- The best determinant of afterload is Total Peripheral Resistance (TPR)
- Equation: MAP = CO × TPR, where CO is treated as fixed and TPR as variable in this explanation.
### ANREP EFFECT
- ↑ Afterload leads to ↑ contractility.
- Afterload is inversely proportional to stroke volume.

**Acute response to increased afterload:**
1. ↑ Afterload
2. ↓ Stroke volume
3. ↑ EDV in the next cardiac cycle
4. ↑ Contraction by the Frank–Starling law

**Chronic response:**
- After 15–20 minutes, increased release of calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum occurs with high afterload — the late effect of Anrep.

| Heterometric regulation of CO | Homometric regulation of CO |
|---|---|
| A change in end-diastolic volume (EDV)/myocardial length, or preload, changes cardiac output. | Increasing or decreasing afterload and increasing or decreasing contractility changes cardiac output. |


### FRANK–STARLING CURVE
- X-axis: length; Y-axis: tension.
- The Frank–Starling curve is the length–tension relationship curve of cardiac muscle.
- The resting operating point is marked on the graph.
- As cardiac-muscle length increases, tension increases up to a physiological limit. Beyond that limit, further lengthening decreases contractility.
- **Decreased contractility curve:** the green curve below normal indicates factors that reduce myocardial contractility, e.g., parasympathetic activity, hypoxia, acidosis and hypercapnia.
- **Increased contractility curve:** the red curve indicates factors that increase myocardial contractility.
- **Heart-failure curve:** the source graph shows a flatter curve.
- For the second graph, the X-axis is end-diastolic pressure/volume (EDP/EDV), and the Y-axis is stroke volume (SV)/cardiac output.
- Initially, increasing EDV increases tension and SV; the curve later flattens. The source states that beyond the limit, SV/CO may not rise despite increasing EDP/EDV, indicating heart failure.
- The source gives the normal left-ventricular end-diastolic pressure as 4–12 mmHg and refers to EDP rising above 15 and 25 mmHg without a rise in SV/CO as indicating heart failure.
- Let point A represent the steady-state condition in heart failure. The source describes the following treatment-related shifts:

| Shift | Treatment | Change shown/described in the source |
|---|---|---|
| A → B | Digoxin | SV rises without a change in EDP; digoxin increases contractile force. |
| A → C | Diuretics | EDP/EDV decreases without a rise in SV; volume reduction decreases venous return. |
| A → D | ACE inhibitor | EDP decreases and SV rises; ACE inhibitor counters angiotensin II effects. |

- In heart failure, the sympathetic system is described as overactive and stimulating renin secretion and high angiotensin II levels.
- Angiotensin II is described as a potent vasoconstrictor that can cause venoconstriction and arteriolar constriction.
- ACE inhibitors reduce angiotensin II production, causing venodilation and arteriolar dilation. Venodilation allows more pooling of blood in the veins; venous return and EDV decrease.

![[images/ch29_p222_frank_starling_curves.png]]

- In arterioles, a decrease in TPR reduces afterload; therefore, SV increases.
- ACE inhibitors reduce angiotensin II levels and decrease ventricular preload and afterload:
  - If preload decreases, EDP decreases.
  - If afterload decreases, stroke volume increases.
  - The curve therefore shifts leftward and upward.

### VENTRICULAR PRESSURE–VOLUME (PV) LOOP
- Ventricular volume is plotted on the X-axis and pressure on the Y-axis.
- **A → B: ventricular filling.** Volume increases with minimal pressure change. This includes the first rapid-filling phase, diastasis and the final rapid-filling phase; the ventricle is relaxed.
- At point B (end of filling), EDV is present in the ventricle.
- **B → C: isovolumic contraction.** Volume remains constant while pressure increases. Ventricular pressure must exceed aortic pressure to open the semilunar valve; the green dotted line in the source diagram indicates afterload.
- At point C, ventricular pressure exceeds aortic pressure, the semilunar valve opens and ejection begins.
- **C → D: ejection/auxotonic contraction.** Ventricular volume decreases; pressure rises and then falls during ejection.
- At point D, ventricular pressure falls below aortic pressure and the semilunar valve closes.
- **D → A: isovolumic relaxation.** Pressure decreases while volume remains constant. The remaining volume is the end-systolic volume (ESV).
- At point A, ventricular pressure is low and atrial pressure is high; the mitral valve opens and filling begins.
- At the end of filling, the volume at point B is the end-diastolic volume (EDV), after which contraction begins and the mitral valves close. The isovolumic contraction phase begins after the atrioventricular valves close.
- The source states that the first heart sound occurs at point B and the second at point D; it places the third and fourth heart sounds between A and B, respectively.
- **Source wording requiring caution:** the source also states, “C to B is the ejection phase of the ventricle via isovolumic contraction.” This is retained as a source statement, although it appears inconsistent with the phase labels in the same diagram.

![[images/ch29_p223_ventricular_pressure_volume_loop.png]]

- Stroke volume = EDV − ESV (shown between points B and A in the source notes).
- Ejection fraction = SV/EDV.
- Cardiac output = HR × SV.
### PV LOOP SHIFTING
- The normal PV loop is shown in black in the source diagram.
- **Increased EDV/preload:** point B shifts to a higher volume; the loop shifts rightward (red in the source diagram). The source assumes no change in aortic pressure for this example.
- **Increased contractility:** ejection continues for longer; point A/ESV shifts leftward while EDV remains consistent, increasing SV. This is shown as a leftward shift (blue).
  - A tangential line from the end-systolic point to the origin indicates the slope of the end-systolic relationship.
  - Increased contractility increases this slope.
  - The end-diastolic slope is the line connecting point B and the origin.
- **Increased afterload:** EDV does not change. The ventricle must overcome higher aortic pressure; ejection is shorter, ESV rises and SV decreases. The PV loop shifts upward (green), while the end-systolic slope remains the same.

| Change | PV-loop effect given in the source |
|---|---|
| Increased preload | Right shift |
| Increased contractility | Left shift and increased end-systolic slope |
| Increased afterload | Upward shift; end-systolic slope unchanged |

### PV LOOP IN VALVULAR DISEASE
| Condition | Description/transcription of the source notes |
|---|---|
| **Aortic regurgitation** | Blood returns from the aorta and right atrium to the ventricle; EDV increases, producing a right shift and upward shift. The source attributes an increase in aortic pressure to increased stroke volume (greater afterload). |
| **Mitral regurgitation** | Blood regurgitates into the left atrium during systole. Increased venous return leads to a higher EDV in the next cycle, producing a right-shifted curve. |
| **Aortic stenosis** | Increased afterload. Acute stenosis produces an upward shift. In chronic stenosis, the source describes ventricular hypertrophy and increased contraction force (high contractility) by the Anrep effect, resulting in an upward and leftward shift (counter-clockwise movement of the PV loop). |
| **Mitral stenosis** | Ventricular filling is reduced. The source states that ESV and EDV decrease and the PV loop shifts left. |

### PV LOOP IN CARDIOMYOPATHIES
- **Dilated cardiomyopathy:** enlargement of the left and/or right ventricle with impaired systolic function. EDV and ESV are higher than normal, with a large rightward shift and a decrease in SV or CO.
- **Restrictive cardiomyopathy:** the source describes myocardial infiltration or scarring of the “endometrium,” resulting in restriction of ventricular filling. EDP is very high during filling, the filling curve shifts upward, and EDV decreases (leftward shift).
- **Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy:** disproportionate left-ventricular hypertrophy involving the septum more than the wall. The source states that EDV and ESV decrease because hypertrophy decreases chamber volume and contractility increases; the PV curve shifts leftward and upward.
- The term “endometrium” is transcribed as it appears in the source; it has not been silently replaced.

### MEASUREMENT OF CARDIAC OUTPUT

| Method | Explanation as given in the source |
|---|---|
| Fick principle | Best method for low-cardiac-output states; invasive method. |
| Dye dilution and thermodilution (Stewart–Hamilton method) | The source says the principles were described by Stewart and Hamilton. Both methods are based on the same principle. Dye dilution (indicator dilution) is the older version and was replaced by thermodilution. Thermodilution involves inserting a catheter into the pulmonary artery (pulmonary artery catheter method) and injecting cold saline into a heart chamber. The source calls thermodilution the gold-standard method, says it works best for high-cardiac-output states, and identifies it as invasive. |
| Echocardiography | Noninvasive and the most common method of measuring CO; the source also calls it the least accurate method. |
| Impedance cardiography / thoracic bioimpedance | Electrodes are placed on the chest surface and a low-amplitude current is passed. The source gives an error percentage of 20%. |
| Velocity-encoded phase-contrast MRI | The source calls this the most accurate method for measuring CO and says it is currently used for research rather than clinically. |


### FICK'S PRINCIPLE

Cardiac output = O₂ absorption at the lung (mL/min) ÷ (arterial O₂ content − venous O₂ content).

- Oxygen consumption is measured using a spirometer.
- Blood samples are taken from an artery and a vein; oxygen content is measured.
- The mixed venous blood sample is taken from the pulmonary artery.
- Brachial artery blood is taken for the arterial sample.

### DYE DILUTION METHOD

CO = [amount of dye (mg) × 60] ÷ [time (s) × average dye concentration (mg/mL)].

Dye is injected into a heart chamber → dye circulates through systemic circulation → dye concentration is measured in multiple arterial blood samples at regular intervals.
- After a certain point, the dye concentration in the artery becomes zero.
- The time interval is measured.
- The average concentration of dye in arterial blood is measured.
- The amount of dye injected is measured.
- Since cardiac output is measured per minute, the calculation is multiplied by 60.

**Q1. Cardiac output can be calculated from which of the following?** (INICET 2022 Nov)
- a. Echocardiography
- b. Ventilation-perfusion scan
- c. Thermodilution technique
- d. Fick's principle
- **Answer: (a, c, and d)**

**Q2.** Myocardial contractility was found to have increased in a person. What will be its effect on the pressure-volume curve of the heart? (INICET 2021 Nov)
- a. Decrease in the slope of the end-diastolic pressure-volume curve
- b. Increase in the slope of the end-systolic pressure curve
- c. Left shift of pressure-volume curve without change in slope
- d. Left shift of pressure-volume curve with decrease in end-systolic slope
- **Answer: (b)**

**Q3.** Stroke volume depends on end-diastolic volume. This is best represented as which of the following? (INICET 2021 June)
- a. Length-velocity relationship
- b. Length-efficiency relationship
- c. Length-tension relationship
- d. Length-force relationship
- **Answer: (c)**
