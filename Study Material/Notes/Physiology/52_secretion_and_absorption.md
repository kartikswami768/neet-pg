# Secretion and Absorption

## General principles

Several types of glands provide different secretions:

- Surface epithelium and goblet cells → mucus.
- Crypts of Lieberkühn (Paneth cells) → antibacterial substances (defensin, lysozyme).
- Tubular glands in the stomach → acid and pepsinogen.
- Complex glands include the salivary glands, pancreas and liver.

## Secretion of saliva

### Major salivary glands and secretions

- **Parotid gland:** serous secretion.
- **Submandibular and sublingual glands:** serous plus mucin secretion.
- Saliva contains two major types of protein secretion:
  - Serous secretion contains **ptyalin (α-amylase)**.
  - Mucus secretion contains **mucin**.

### Formation of saliva

- Acinar cells produce ptyalin, mucus and water as a primary, isotonic secretion.
- This isotonic secretion passes through the salivary duct cells.
- In duct cells:
  - Na⁺ reabsorption exceeds K⁺ secretion; both are described in the source as active processes.
  - Na⁺ reabsorption makes luminal membrane voltage **−70 mV**, causing passive Cl⁻ reabsorption and HCO₃⁻ secretion (active and passive).
- Final secretion is **hypotonic**.

### Regulation of salivary secretion

- **Parasympathetic stimulation:** increases volume, producing more watery secretion.
- **Sympathetic stimulation:** also increases secretion, but produces more mucous secretion.
- At high flow rate, the source lists increases in osmolarity, pH, Na⁺, Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻, with a decrease in K⁺.
- At low flow rate, the source lists increased K⁺ and decreases in osmolarity, pH, Na⁺, Cl⁻ and HCO₃⁻.

## Stomach secretion

### Types of gastric glands

- **Main gastric glands (fundus and body):**
  - Parietal (oxyntic) cells: H⁺ and intrinsic factor (IF).
  - Chief (peptic) cells: pepsinogen.
- **Cardiac tubular glands:** soluble mucus.
- **Pyloric (antral) glands:** mucus cells, G cells (gastrin), D cells (somatostatin), and chief cells; the source states there are no parietal cells.

## Acid secretion in the parietal cell

- CO₂ from blood enters the parietal cell and reacts with H₂O to produce carbonic acid (H₂CO₃).
- H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻.
- The H⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump secretes H⁺ into the lumen while K⁺ is absorbed from the lumen.
- An anion exchanger secretes HCO₃⁻ into the blood while Cl⁻ enters the parietal cell.
- Cl⁻ is secreted into the lumen through a chloride channel.
- K⁺ absorbed from the lumen via the H⁺/K⁺-ATPase pump diffuses back into the lumen.
- **Postprandial alkaline tide:** temporary rise in blood pH after a meal due to HCO₃⁻ secretion into the bloodstream during gastric HCl production by parietal cells.

### Control of the parietal cell

Three main receptors are present on the basolateral membrane:

- **M3 receptor:** parasympathetic ACh → M3 receptor → IP₃/DAG second-messenger pathway → increased intracellular Ca²⁺ → stimulation of H⁺/K⁺-ATPase → increased acid secretion.
- **CCK-B2 receptor:** gastrin from G cells → CCK-B2 receptor → IP₃/DAG pathway → increased intracellular Ca²⁺ → stimulation of H⁺/K⁺-ATPase → increased acid secretion.
- **H₂ receptor:** histamine from ECL cells → H₂ receptor → cAMP pathway → stimulation of H⁺/K⁺-ATPase → increased acid secretion.

Cross-stimulation described in the source:

- The parasympathetic system also stimulates G cells via **GRP**, increasing gastrin and acid secretion.
- Gastrin stimulates ECL cells to produce histamine, increasing acid secretion.
- Somatostatin and prostaglandins inhibit acid secretion; the source associates this with reduced cAMP.

### Phases of gastric secretion

- **Cephalic phase: 20–30%.** Triggered by the sight, smell or thought of food; a feed-forward control system mediated by the vagus nerve.
- **Gastric phase: 60–70%.** Triggered by stomach distension, which stimulates gastrin secretion and activates a local vagovagal reflex; ACh is produced and stimulates acid secretion.
- **Intestinal phase: 10%.** The source states that protein-digestion products (amino acids) in the duodenum stimulate acid secretion.

## Pancreatic secretion

- Exocrine pancreatic glands resemble salivary glands.
- **Acinar cells:** produce exocrine pancreatic enzymes and H₂O.
- **Duct cells:** secrete HCO₃⁻ and reabsorb Cl⁻ (secondary active transport); Cl⁻ is secreted back into the pancreatic duct lumen through a chloride channel.
- The source states that pancreatic secretion contains HCO₃⁻ and Cl⁻ in comparison with plasma.
- Hormonal/neural regulation:
  - **CCK:** stimulates acinar cells to produce enzymes.
  - **Secretin:** acts on duct cells to produce bicarbonate.
  - **ACh:** stimulates both acinar and duct cells.

## Absorption of nutrients in the GIT

### Carbohydrate absorption

- Carbohydrate digestion produces the monosaccharides glucose, galactose and fructose.
- At the luminal membrane:
  - **Fructose:** absorbed via **GLUT-5** by facilitated diffusion.
  - **Glucose and galactose:** absorbed with Na⁺ via **SGLT-1** (secondary active transport).
- At the basolateral membrane, **GLUT-2** facilitates diffusion of glucose, galactose and fructose into the bloodstream, as described in the source.

### Protein absorption

- Luminal protein digestion produces free amino acids and small peptides of different lengths.
- At the luminal membrane:
  - Na⁺-dependent secondary active transporters absorb amino acids into the cytoplasm.
  - **PepT1**, a secondary active transporter, absorbs dipeptides and tripeptides into the cytoplasm.
- Within the cell, di- and tripeptides are converted to amino acids by peptidases.
- At the basolateral membrane, amino acids enter the bloodstream (**75%** in the source); some di- and tripeptides (**25%**) may be absorbed directly into blood.
- Both Na⁺-dependent and Na⁺-independent transporters are present.

### Fat absorption

- The source describes absorption as occurring by simple diffusion.
- Lipolytic by-products are solubilised in micelles in the intestinal lumen.
- Micelles diffuse across the enterocyte brush border into the cytoplasm.
- Inside the cell, triglycerides are resynthesised into chylomicrons.
- Chylomicrons exit by exocytosis, enter lymph through lacteals and reach the bloodstream via the thoracic duct.

### Regional absorption and water handling

- Carbohydrates, proteins and lipids: maximum absorption in **duodenum > jejunum > ileum**, according to the source.
- Folate and iron: absorbed in the duodenum (maximum) and proximal jejunum.
- Bile salts and cobalamin: maximally absorbed in the distal ileum.
- Daily fluid load: approximately **9 L** in normal adults.
  - Small-intestinal absorption: **8.8 L**.
  - Jejunum: **5.5 L/day**.
  - Ileum: **2.0 L/day**.
  - Colonic absorption: **1.3 L/day**.
  - Stool: **100 mL (1%)**.
- The source diagram also labels regional absorption for calcium, iron, folate, carbohydrates, proteins and lipids: duodenum, jejunum and ileum are shown as distinct sites.

**1. Which of the following is correct of salivary secretion?** (INICET 2021 Nov)

- a. Active Na⁺ absorption, passive Cl⁻ absorption, active K⁺ secretion, active and passive secretion of HCO₃⁻
- b. Passive Na⁺ absorption, passive Cl⁻ absorption, active K⁺ secretion, active and passive secretion of HCO₃⁻
- c. Active Na⁺ absorption, active Cl⁻ absorption, active K⁺ secretion, active and passive secretion of HCO₃⁻
- d. Active Na⁺ absorption, passive Cl⁻ absorption, passive K⁺ secretion, active and passive secretion of HCO₃⁻

**Answer: a.**

**2. All of the following will increase gastric acid secretion except:** (INICET 2021 Nov)

- a. Histamine
- b. Serotonin
- c. Acetylcholine
- d. Gastrin

**Answer: b. Serotonin**

**3. Acid-secreting cell in the stomach:** (AIIMS 2016 May)

- a. Parietal cell
- b. D cell
- c. Peptic cell
- d. G cell
