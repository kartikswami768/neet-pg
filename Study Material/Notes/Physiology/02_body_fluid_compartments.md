#### Distribution of body fluid

- **Total body water (TBW):** 60% of total body weight.
- **Intracellular fluid (ICF):** 2/3 of TBW; approximately 40% of body weight.
- **Extracellular fluid (ECF):** 1/3 of TBW; approximately 20% of body weight.
  - **Interstitial fluid (ISF):** 3/4 of ECF; approximately 15% of body weight.
  - **Plasma:** 1/4 of ECF; approximately 5% of body weight.
- Blood is approximately 8% of body weight: 5% plasma + 3% total cell volume.

#### Change in body fluid with age

The source graph shows TBW decreasing with age. Values stated under the age groups are:

| Stage | TBW as a percentage |
|---|---:|
| Intrauterine life | 95% of total body weight |
| Preterm infants | 90% of birth weight |
| Term infants | 75% of birth weight |
| Later age | Decreases with age (as shown in the source graph) |

**ICF and ECF changes**

- In intrauterine life, ICF is initially less than ECF.
- ICF gradually increases with organogenesis while ECF decreases.
- At 3–4 months after birth, ICF and ECF become equal (ratio 1:1).
- By 1 year of age, ICF and ECF approach adult levels.
- At puberty, percentage and distribution of body fluids reach adult proportions.

#### Measurement of body water

- To measure the exact amount of total body water (TBW), the source uses the **indicator-dilution principle** (volume-of-distribution method).
- The diagram distinguishes water inside cells (ICF), water outside cells (ISF), water inside capillaries (plasma), and plasma + ISF (ECF).
- A substance such as **Q mg of inulin** is injected into a capillary. It distributes in plasma, passes through the capillary into ISF, and is impermeable to the cell membrane.

#### Indicator-dilution calculations

- Inulin distributes throughout the entire ECF, and equilibrium is reached between plasma and ISF concentrations.
- Plasma inulin concentration is measured using:

\[
	ext{Volume of distribution} = rac{Q}{C}
\]

where **Q** is the total amount injected and **C** is the concentration in plasma.

- For substances that are easily metabolized, metabolism and excretion must be considered:

\[
	ext{Volume} = rac{Q-e}{C}
\]

where **e** is the excreted or metabolized part of the injected substance.

- A substance permeable to capillaries reaches ISF.
- A substance also permeable to cell membranes reaches ICF and measures TBW.
- A substance impermeable to capillaries measures only plasma volume.

#### Indicators for body-fluid measurement

| Compartment/measurement | Indicator used in the source | Notes stated in the source |
|---|---|---|
| Total body water | D₂O, tritium oxide, antipyrine | Freely permeable to capillary and cell membranes |
| ECF volume | Inulin (best), sucrose, ²²Na, ¹²⁵I-iothalamate, mannitol | Freely permeable to capillary, impermeable to cell membrane |
| ICF volume | TBW − ECF | Requires two substances: one for TBW and one for ECF |
| Plasma | ¹²⁵I-albumin (best), Evans' blue | Impermeable to capillaries |

- The source states that ISF has slightly more negatively charged ions than plasma.

- Total osmotic pressure of plasma as calculated in the source: 19.3 × 282 = **5441 mmHg** (approximately 5500 mmHg).

### Source page 13

#### Osmolar gap (continued)

- Measured osmolality is greater than calculated osmolality.
- **Osmolar gap = measured osmolality − calculated osmolality.**
- Normal value stated: **10**.
- Increased osmolar gap is listed with:
  - Alcohol poisoning
  - Sorbitol
  - Huge amount of protein
  - Hyperlipidaemia

#### Osmolarity and tonicity

- **Osmolarity:** concentration of osmotically active particles (osmoles) in one litre of solution.
- **Osmolality:** osmoles per kilogram of water.
- Difference between osmolarity and osmolality stated in the source: **1%**.
- **Tonicity** predicts the effect of a solution on cell volume at equilibrium and depends on impermeant solutes.
- If cell osmolarity is 300 mOsm/L and the solution is 500 mOsm/L, the solution is hyperosmotic relative to the cell. Without knowing the concentration of impermeant solutes, hypertonicity, hypotonicity, or isotonicity cannot be determined from osmolarity alone.

**Examples in the prose**

- 200 mOsmol/L NaCl (impermeable) + 300 mOsmol/L urea (permeable) → described as **hyperosmotic but hypotonic** compared with the cell; a hypotonic solution increases cell volume at equilibrium.
- 300 mOsmol/L NaCl (impermeable) + 200 mOsmol/L urea (permeable) → described as **isotonic** compared with the cell; cell volume remains the same.

**Source figure labels**

- Hyperosmotic but hypotonic: **200 mOsmol/L NaCl + 200 mOsmol/L urea** (the figure's urea value differs from the prose).
- Hyperosmotic but isotonic: **300 mOsmol/L NaCl + 100 mOsmol/L urea** (the figure's urea value differs from the prose).
- Hyperosmotic and hypertonic: **400 mOsmol/L NaCl**.
- The graphs show cell volume over time: swelling after the hypotonic example, return to normal/isotonic volume in the isotonic example, and shrinkage in the hypertonic example.

> **Source discrepancy preserved:** the prose and the figure give different urea concentrations for the first two examples. See QA report; neither value has been silently replaced.

### Source page 14

#### Isotonic solutions

The source lists:

- 5% mannitol
- 5% dextrose — isotonic *in vitro*
- 0.9% NaCl (normal saline)
- Ringer's lactate solution / lactated Ringer's / Hartmann's solution
- Ringer's solution — described as having the same constituents as plasma but not containing sodium lactate

Once infused into plasma, glucose is rapidly taken up by cells, leaving behind water and making the solution hypotonic.

#### Darrow–Yannet diagram

- Illustrates changes in ECF and ICF volume after administration of solutions of different tonicity.
- At equilibrium, ICF and ECF osmolality are equal; value stated: **280 mOsmol/L**.

| Gain/loss of fluid | Explanation in the source |
|---|---|
| Gain of isotonic fluid | ECF volume increases without a change in osmolarity. Example: 0.9% NaCl infusion. |
| Loss of isotonic fluid | Hemorrhage, urinary loss, acute vomiting, or diarrhoea: isotonic fluid loss decreases ECF volume without altering osmolarity. |
| Gain of hypotonic fluid | Examples: 0.45% NaCl, polydipsia, SIADH. ECF volume expands and ECF osmolarity decreases; because ICF osmolality remains higher, the body is not at steady state. Hypotonic ECF moves into ICF by osmosis, expanding ICF volume and decreasing osmolarity. The source warns that hypotonic fluid therapy can cause brain swelling and herniation. For 1 L infused: 1/3 (333 mL) enters ECF and 2/3 (667 mL) enters ICF. |

### Source page 15

#### Darrow–Yannet diagram (continued)

| Gain/loss of fluid | Explanation in the source |
|---|---|
| **Loss of hypotonic fluid** | In diabetes insipidus or with inadequate water intake, excess water is excreted (insufficient ADH). ECF volume decreases. Because water is lost while solutes remain, ECF osmolarity and tonicity increase, making ECF hypertonic relative to ICF. Water moves from ICF to ECF, decreasing ICF volume and increasing its osmolarity. The source concludes that both ICF and ECF volume decrease while the osmolarity of both compartments increases. |
| **Gain of hypertonic fluid** | Infusion of 3% NaCl expands ECF volume and increases ECF osmolarity. ECF becomes hypertonic relative to ICF, so water moves out of cells; ICF volume decreases and ICF osmolarity increases. |
| **Loss of hypertonic fluid** | In adrenal insufficiency, solute is lost in excess of water. Hypertonic-fluid loss decreases ECF volume and makes ECF hypotonic relative to ICF. Water moves from ECF to ICF, and ICF volume increases slightly. The final source bullet says this results in reduced ECF volume, ICF volume, and decreased tonicity in both compartments at a new steady state. |

> **Source inconsistency preserved:** for loss of hypertonic fluid, the source first states that ICF volume increases slightly, then states that ICF volume is reduced in the concluding bullet. Both statements are retained as printed and flagged in QA.

### Source page 16

#### MCQs

**Q1.** A research fellow measured Na⁺ = 10 mEq/L and K⁺ = 140 mEq/L in a body-water sample. Which compartment is represented? (NEET 2021)

- A. ECF
- B. ICF
- C. ISF
- D. Plasma

**Answer: B — ICF**

**Q2.** Calculate blood volume for a patient weighing 60 kg with hematocrit 45%. (JIPMER 2019)

- A. 4.8 L
- B. 5.0 L
- C. 5.45 L
- D. 6 L

**Answer: C — 5.45 L**

**Q3.** 100 mg of sucrose is injected into a 70 kg man. Plasma sucrose after mixing is 0.01 mg/mL. If 5 mg was metabolized during this period, what is the ECF volume? (AIIMS 2020)

- A. 9.5 L
- B. 14 L
- C. 17.5 L
- D. 10 L

**Answer: A — 9.5 L**
