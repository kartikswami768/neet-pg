# 11. Smooth Muscle

## Structure of smooth muscle

- Smooth-muscle cells are **spindle-shaped** and **single-nucleated**.
- They are supplied by the **autonomic nervous system (ANS)**.
- Nerve fibres form multiple swellings called **varicosities**, which contain neurotransmitters. Each varicosity has multiple contact points with a muscle fibre, forming a synapse/neuro-effector junction called **synaptic en passant** (a nerve passing through a smooth-muscle cell provides the synapse).

![[images/ch11_p074_smooth_muscle_varicosities.png]]

### Types based on gap junctions

| Single-unit smooth muscle | Multi-unit smooth muscle |
|---|---|
| Smooth-muscle cells are connected to one another; gap junctions are present between cells. | Each smooth-muscle cell behaves as a single unit. Different nerve fibres supply the cells; few or no gap junctions are present, with little or no connection between cells. |
| Stimulation of one muscle cell → Ca²⁺ release → Ca²⁺ penetrates through each cell → contraction of smooth-muscle cells as a unit, i.e. simultaneous/syncytial contraction. | Examples: iris muscles, ciliary muscles of the eye and piloerector muscles of the skin. Function: fine or controlled movements. |
| Described as required for strong contraction. Examples: visceral smooth muscle of the gallbladder, uterus and GIT. | |

## Molecular structure

![[images/ch11_p074_dense_body.png]]

| Structure/protein | Properties |
|---|---|
| **T-tubules** | Absent in smooth muscle. Small indentations of the plasma membrane of smooth-muscle cells are called **caveolae**. |
| **Z-line** | Absent. A spherical structure beneath the plasma membrane, the **dense body**, is the functional equivalent of Z-lines; actin fibres radiate from dense bodies. |
| **Actin and myosin** | Present, but not highly organised. Sarcomeres are not well organised; there are no dark and light bands, so smooth muscle is non-striated. Myosin is present between actin fibres. |
| **Troponin** | Calcium-binding protein; absent in smooth muscle. **Calmodulin**, another calcium-binding protein, is present. |
| **Tropomyosin** | Present; increased actin–myosin interaction is indicated in association with tropomyosin. |
| **Calponin and caldesmon** | Present; prevent actin–myosin interaction and result in relaxation. |
| **Nebulin and titin** | Absent in smooth muscle. |

## Sources of Ca²⁺ for smooth-muscle contraction

### 1. Ca²⁺ entry through CaV1.2 (from ECF)

- Ca²⁺ source: **extracellular fluid (ECF)**.
- Caveolae in smooth-muscle cells contain voltage-gated L-type calcium channels. The **CaV1.2** subtype is listed for cardiac and smooth muscle.
- An action potential depolarises the smooth-muscle membrane → voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open → extracellular Ca²⁺ enters the cytoplasm → contraction.

![[images/ch11_p075_ecf_calcium_entry.png]]

### 2. Ca²⁺ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum through RyR3

- Ca²⁺ source: **intracellular fluid (ICF)**; the SR in smooth muscle is described as rudimentary and as a calcium store.
- Ca²⁺ entering from ECF through the voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channel stimulates **RyR3**, releasing more Ca²⁺ into the cytoplasm and causing contraction.
- This is **calcium-induced calcium release (CICR)**.

![[images/ch11_p075_sr_ryr3_calcium_release.png]]

### 3. Store-operated calcium entry/release (SOCR/SOCE)

Store-operated calcium handling is labelled both “SOCR” and “SOCE”.

- Ca²⁺ source: ECF.
- A calcium channel is present on the surface of the smooth-muscle cell membrane.
- Examples listed: **Orai-family calcium channels** and **transient receptor potential (TRP) calcium channels**.
- Depletion of the SR Ca²⁺ store causes Ca²⁺ channels to open; more calcium enters to replace cytoplasmic and SR stores.
- SOCE is described as independent of membrane depolarisation/action potentials and as **pharmaco-mechanical coupling** of smooth muscle.

![[images/ch11_p076_store_operated_calcium_entry.png]]

### 4. SR Ca²⁺ release mediated by IP₃ receptors

- Ca²⁺ source: ICF.
- G-protein-coupled receptors on the smooth-muscle surface are stimulated by hormones, neurotransmitters, drugs, etc.
- Activated GPCRs form a second-messenger system that generates **diacylglycerol (DAG)** and **inositol triphosphate (IP₃)**.
- The IP₃ receptor on the SR surface is a Ca²⁺ channel. IP₃ stimulates this receptor → the channel opens → calcium is released into the cytoplasm.
- IP₃-mediated Ca²⁺ release is described as independent of membrane depolarisation/action potentials and as **pharmaco-mechanical coupling** of smooth muscle.

![[images/ch11_p076_ip3_receptor_pathway.png]]

## Mechanism of smooth-muscle contraction

- At rest, the angle between the myosin head and neck is acute.
- For contraction, a 90° angle between the myosin head and neck should occur.
- Myosin ATPase activity is very low in the resting condition.

Sequence:

1. Ca²⁺ is present inside the smooth-muscle cytoplasm.
2. Ca²⁺ binds to calmodulin, forming the **Ca²⁺–calmodulin complex**.
3. The complex activates **myosin light-chain kinase (MLCK)** and other kinases.
4. MLCK phosphorylates the myosin light chain at the myosin neck. Other kinases phosphorylate calponin and caldesmon, so their inhibition is lost.
5. The myosin head–neck angle and myosin ATPase activity increase.
6. Myosin interacts with actin and cross-bridges form → muscle contraction.

![[images/ch11_p076_myosin_head_angle.png]]

![[images/ch11_p077_smooth_muscle_contraction_sequence.png]]

## Relaxation and latch-bridge phenomenon

- Activation of **myosin light-chain phosphatase (MLC-phosphatase)** causes dephosphorylation of the myosin light chain.
- The myosin head detaches from actin (myosin–actin interaction ends) → relaxation.
- Sustained/tonic contraction is associated with the **latch-bridge phenomenon (latch state)**. Even after dephosphorylation, the myosin head remains attached to actin.
- This mechanism is described as useful in the GIT and gallbladder, with **low ATP utilisation** and a **high-force condition**.

## Smooth-muscle contraction without an action potential

- Some single-unit smooth muscles contract without an action potential and require graded depolarisation.
- An action potential usually does not occur in multi-unit smooth muscle; local depolarisation (junctional potential) is required.
- Some smooth muscles contract in response to agonist drugs: drug-induced IP₃ release produces second-messenger-mediated Ca²⁺ release. This is called pharmaco-mechanical coupling of smooth muscle.

## MCQs

**1. Which one of the following increases the release of Ca²⁺ from the endoplasmic reticulum?** (AIIMS 2004)

- a. Inositol triphosphate
- b. Decreased parathyroid hormone
- c. 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol
- d. Diacylglycerol

**Answer:** (a)

**2. Which statement is true regarding excitation–contraction coupling in smooth muscle?** (AIIMS 2002)

- a. Presence of troponin is essential
- b. Sustained contraction occurs with high calcium concentration
- c. Phosphorylation of actin is required for contraction
- d. Presence of cellular Ca²⁺ is essential to cause muscle contraction

**Answer:** (d)

**3. The mechanism responsible for sustained vascular smooth-muscle contraction is:** (JIPMER 2018)

- a. Sustained calcium release from the SERCA pump
- b. Latch-bridge mechanism
- c. Sustained calcium release from the RyR channel
- d. Calcium entry from ECF

**Answer:** (b)
