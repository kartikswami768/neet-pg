### Operant Conditioning                                                                                     00:16:14
- Example of associative learning
EXPERIMENT Speaker Signal lights Pellet dispenser Lever Dispenser tube To shock generator Food cup Electric grid
- Hungry rat is placed in a Skinner box.
- Floor of the box is made up of electric grid which is connected with shock generator
- On pressing the lever, the rat gets the pellet from the pellet dispenser → Rewarding stimulus
- Now the rat has learned that pressing the lever results in a reward, so it changes its behaviour accordingly
- Now Skinner placed the same rat in another chamber, in which the lever is connected with shock generator.
- Therefore, the rat gets electric shock on pressing the lever → Painful stimulus
- After pressing for 3 or 4 times he learned that on pressing lever he gets shock → And avoids pressing the lever

### Operant Conditioning
- Law of effect:
- o Behaviour which is reinforced tends to be repeated (i.e., strengthened)
- Behaviour which is not reinforced tends to die out- or be extinguished (i.e., weakened)
- Discovered by Edward Thorndike and systematically studied by B.F. Skinner and others
- Classical conditioning: Relationship between two stimuli
- Operant conditioning: Relationship between an action and an outcome (behaviour)

- Change in strength of synapse in response to repeated exposure
- 3 processes of synaptic plasticity → Post-tetanic potentiation: Lasts up to 60 seconds → Long term potentiation (LTP) → Long term depression (LTD) POST TETANIC         Presynaptic neuron is stimulated by tetanizing stimulus (repeated stimulation)
POTENTIATION Presynaptic Ca" channels open Tetanizing Ca*    Ca** stimulus             Huge amount of Ca" enters into the presynaptic terminal INT                   Ca* causes release of neurotransmitters from synapse
- Neuron is repeatedly stimulated because of huge calcium influx by tetanizing stimulus
