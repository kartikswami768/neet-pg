# 10. Mechanism of Muscle Contraction

Skeletal-muscle cells are composed of three layers:

- Sarcolemma
- Sarcotubular system (T-tubules and L-tubules)
- Myofibril

## Myofibrillar proteins

| Group | Proteins listed |
|---|---|
| Contractile proteins | Actin, myosin |
| Regulatory proteins | Troponin, tropomyosin |
| Anchoring proteins | Desmin, alpha-actinin/Cap Z protein, titin/connectin, nebulin, tropomodulin, myomesin |

![[images/ch10_p066_myofibril_protein_hierarchy.png]]

## Sarcomere

- Structural and functional unit of skeletal muscle.
- A sarcomere is the distance between two Z-lines.
- Resting sarcomere length: **2–2.2 µm**.
- Maximum sarcomere length: **3.6 µm**.
- Minimum sarcomere length: **1.6 µm**.
- Actin is the thin filament attached to the surface of the Z-line (Zwischenscheibe); one actin filament is **1 µm** long.
- Myosin is the thick filament between two Z-lines; one myosin filament is **1.6 µm** long.
- One myosin is surrounded by six actin molecules in a hexagonal pattern.

![[images/ch10_p066_sarcomere_structure.png]]

### Bands and lines

- **A band (anisotropic band):** region where myosin is present; dark region and thick-filament band.
- **H band (Heller):** part of the A band not overlapped by actin; a lighter region within the dark band.
- **Pseudo-H band:** region in the centre of myosin where the head portion is absent; **0.2 µm** long and lighter than the H band.
- **M line (Mittelscheibe):** at the centre of the pseudo-H band, elevated regions of myosin contact the centremost elevated portions of adjacent myosin to form a continuous line.
- **I band (isotropic band):** area between two A bands where myosin is absent; thin-filament band composed of actin, troponin and tropomyosin. The band is described as involving two nearby sarcomeres.

![[images/ch10_p067_pseudo_h_sarcomere_bands.png]]

## Sliding-filament theory

Sliding-filament theory is attributed to **Hodgkin and Huxley**.

During contraction, the thin filament (actin) moves over the thick filament (myosin):

- Length of the A band remains the same.
- Length of the H band decreases or disappears.
- Length of the I band decreases or disappears.
- The M line becomes prominent.

![[images/ch10_p067_sarcomere_micrograph.png]]

## Regulatory proteins

### Tropomyosin: gatekeeper molecule

- Filamentous protein arranged spirally around actin.
- Covers the active sites on actin.
- Prevents actin–myosin attachment in the relaxed state; described as the **gatekeeper molecule**.

### Troponin

- Spherical molecule attached to tropomyosin at regular intervals.
- Has three subunits: **T, I and C**.

| Subunit | Properties |
|---|---|
| **Troponin T** | Attached to tropomyosin. |
| **Troponin C** | Calcium-binding subunit. Four calcium-binding sites are present: two regulatory calcium-binding sites and two sites where calcium is permanently attached. |
| **Troponin I** | Inhibitory subunit. Described as a spiral molecule with a tendency to unwind from actin; acts as a wedge, holds tropomyosin in place and prevents actin–myosin interaction. |

Mnemonic: **Trop T** remains attached to tropomyosin; **Trop C** is the calcium-binding subunit; **Trop I** helps tropomyosin act on myosin interaction.

![[images/ch10_p067_troponin_tropomyosin.png]]

## Mechanism of contraction and the cross-bridge cycle

- Contraction requires removal of the inhibitory action of troponin I.
- Calcium binds to the troponin C subunit, causing tropomyosin to move away.
- Myosin heads form a cross-bridge with actin at a **90° angle**.
- During the power stroke, the myosin head moves from **90° to 45°** on both sides of the centre and pulls actin towards the centre.
- One power stroke produces **10–11 nm** of actin shortening towards the centre.
- The cycle continues until Ca²⁺ is removed from the cytoplasm; this is called the **cross-bridge cycle of muscle contraction**.

Sequence: free cytoplasmic Ca²⁺ → calcium binds troponin C → tropomyosin moves away → myosin head attaches to actin at 90° with ADP + Pi → cross-bridge formation → power stroke (90° to 45°) → ADP release → ATP binds, detaching the myosin head from actin → ATP hydrolysis/cocking returns the head towards the 90° position for another cycle.

## Anchoring proteins

| Protein | Properties |
|---|---|
| **Desmin** | Attached from the Z-line to the sarcolemma; an intermediate filament in skeletal muscle. |
| **Alpha-actinin / Cap Z protein** | Z-line protein; attaches actin to the Z-line. Mutation of alpha-actinin causes nemaline myopathy. |
| **Nebulin** | Attaches from the tip of actin to the Z-line and regulates actin length. Nebulin mutation also causes nemaline myopathy. |
| **Titin (connectin)** | Elastic protein present from M-line to Z-line; holds myosin in the centre of the sarcomere. Described as the largest protein in human cells, with molecular weight “30 Lac Dalton” and 25,000–27,000 amino acids. Mutation is associated with dilated cardiomyopathy and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. |
| **Tropomodulin** | Present only at the tip of actin; regulates actin length. |
| **Myomesin** | Helps titin remain connected to the M-line; an M-line protein. |

## Length–tension relationship

The X-axis represents sarcomere length and the Y-axis represents tension generated by muscle.

| Sarcomere length | Relationship |
|---:|---|
| **1.6 µm** | One actin overlaps the other; tension is less. Force/tension is directly proportional to the number of cross-bridges. |
| **2.0 µm** | Tips of actin filaments touch; actin length is 1 µm; cross-bridge formation increases and maximum tension is generated. |
| **2.2 µm** | Actin moves apart; number of cross-bridges remains the same; there are no heads in the centre part of myosin; tension remains maximal. |
| **3.6 µm** | Only terminal heads remain attached to actin; tension is very low and the sarcomere is stated to rupture. This is the maximum sarcomere length. |

![[images/ch10_p069_sarcomere_length_diagrams.png]]

- The graph represents the **active-tension curve** of muscle.
- **Active tension** is due to cross-bridge formation.
- **Passive tension** is due to elasticity; it is zero up to a sarcomere length of 2–2.2 µm.
- **Total tension curve** is the addition of active and passive curves.
- Optimum sarcomere length is **2–2.2 µm**. Optimum length is equated with maximum force production and resting length of skeletal muscle.

![[images/ch10_p070_sarcomere_length_cases.png]]

![[images/ch10_p070_length_tension_curve.png]]

Fibres are arranged in a parallel pattern. | Operating point is point B on the rising part of the curve, signifying room for stretch and greater force. The passive-tension curve starts earlier. Cardiac fibres are arranged in a basket-weave pattern and recoil with considerable force when stretched. |

## Simple muscle twitch

Stimulation of a nerve fibre supplying skeletal muscle produces muscle contraction.

- **Latent period (LP):** period with no contraction; time required for the action potential to travel.
- **Contraction period (CP):** from the end of the latent period to the peak.
- **Relaxation period (RP):** from the peak to the end.

![[images/ch10_p072_simple_muscle_twitch_phases.png]]

For a typical gastrocnemius muscle:

- LP: 10 ms
- CP: 40 ms
- RP: 50 ms
- Total duration of a simple muscle twitch: 100 ms

## Multiple stimulation, treppe and tetanus

- The height of the second contraction is greater than that of the first because Ca²⁺ takes time to be reabsorbed into the SR by SERCA.
- A step-ladder increase in contraction height is the **treppe/staircase phenomenon**.
- Stimulation is stated to be given after complete relaxation of the previous stimulus for this pattern.

![[images/ch10_p073_treppe_staircase_graph.png]]

| Incomplete tetanus generation | Complete tetanus generation |
|---|---|
| More frequent stimulation is given before complete relaxation. | More frequent stimulation occurs with no relaxation in between; multiple contractions fuse with one another. |

**Tetanizing frequency (TF)** = 1 second / contraction-period duration in milliseconds = **1000 ms / CP (ms)**. Frequency is measured in hertz (cycles per second).
