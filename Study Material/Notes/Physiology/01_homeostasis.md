# 01 — Homeostasis

## Homeostasis (*00:00:40*)

- **Homeostasis:** maintenance of near-constant conditions in the internal environment.
- The concept of the **“milieu intérieur”** (internal environment) was introduced by **Claude Bernard**. He stated: “the stability of the internal environment (*milieu intérieur*) is the condition for a free & independent life”.
- Homeostasis depends on interstitial fluid (ISF), a component of extracellular fluid (ECF).
- The term **homeostasis** was coined by **Walter B. Cannon**.
  - According to Walter B. Cannon, homeostasis refers to various physiological arrangements that restore the normal state once it has been disturbed.
- Mechanisms maintaining the internal environment are called **control systems**.

## Control system (*00:05:47*)

![[images/ch01_p004_control_tree.png]]

*Control-system classification: feedback and feedforward control.*

A control system may operate by **feedback** or **feedforward** control. Feedback control is divided into **positive** and **negative** feedback.

### Feedback control system

- If hormone A stimulates production of hormone B, which in turn stimulates production of hormone C (the output), feedback occurs when output C returns as input at the level of A or B.
- **Positive feedback:** stimulating feedback. A stimulates B → B stimulates C → C stimulates A.
- **Negative feedback:** inhibitory feedback.

![[images/ch01_p004_feedback_loops.png]]

*Feedback-loop schematic and the blood-pressure/baroreceptor example.*

## Gain of a control system

- Measures the effectiveness of the control system.

\[
\text{Gain} = \frac{\text{Correction}}{\text{Error}}
\]

**Example — blood-pressure control**

- Original/normal BP: **100 mmHg**.
- Raised BP: **160 mmHg**.
- Corrected BP (baroreceptor-mediated): **120 mmHg**.
- Error: 120 − 100 = **20 mmHg**.
- Correction: 160 − 120 = **40 mmHg**.
- Reported gain: (40/20) = **−2**.
- The notes explain that BP is raised and the baroreceptor corrects it in the opposite direction; therefore, gain is negative.

**Normal gains**

- Baroreceptor control system: **−2**.
- Thermoreceptor control system: **−33**.

**Infinity-gain control system:** a control system achieving 100% correction with zero error, giving infinite gain. Example: the kidney regulating BP or blood volume.

> **Source inconsistency retained:** the printed example gives correction/error as 40/20 but reports the gain as −2. The sign is reproduced as printed; it has not been silently reconciled.

## Regulation factor (*00:16:33*)

- Measures how much a system reduces the deviation from normal.

\[
R = \frac{\text{Change with regulation}}{\text{Change without regulation}}
\]

**Example — blood-pressure control**

- Original/normal BP: **100 mmHg**.
- Raised BP: **160 mmHg**.
- Corrected BP (baroreceptor-mediated): **120 mmHg**.
- Change in BP without regulation/control: 160 − 100 = **60 mmHg**.
- Change in BP with regulation/control: 120 − 100 = **20 mmHg**.
- Regulation factor: 20/60 = **1/3**.
- Accuracy of a control system is inversely related to the regulation factor.

## Positive-feedback control system (*00:19:00*)

Positive-feedback control systems may be beneficial during specific physiological processes.

### Blood clotting

- Thrombin generated → positive feedback to factors **Va and VIIIa** → activation of factor X (to factor Xa) → conversion of prothrombin to thrombin → clot formation.

![[images/ch01_p005_clotting_feedback.png]]

*Positive-feedback loop in blood clotting.*

### LH surge just before ovulation

- During the menstrual cycle, estrogen normally exerts a negative-feedback effect on LH secretion.
- Just before ovulation, estrogen switches to a positive-feedback effect → LH surge → ovulation.

### Uterine contraction during labour — Ferguson reflex

- Increased uterine contractions push the fetal head further, promoting childbirth.
- The fetal head stretches the cervix.
  - **Local neural pathway:** cervix → uterus → contractions of the body of the uterus.
  - **Long neural pathway:** oxytocin secretion from the hypothalamus → stronger uterine contractions.
  - The contractions further stretch the cervix, continuing the positive-feedback cycle until delivery.

![[images/ch01_p006_ferguson_reflex.png]]

*Ferguson reflex: cervical stretch, the local uterine pathway, and the oxytocin pathway.*

### Lactation

- The baby suckles near the nipple and areolar region of the breast → activates a long neural pathway → oxytocin secretion → milk ejection.

### Generation of a nerve action potential

- Influx of positively charged sodium ions → depolarization of the cell membrane → opening of additional voltage-gated sodium channels → further depolarization → further opening of channels.

### Important information — potassium channels

- Potassium-channel opening is described as a negative-feedback mechanism.
- Opening of K⁺ channels → K⁺ efflux → hyperpolarization → closure of K⁺ channels.

## Feedforward control system (*00:29:10*)

- Also called an **anticipatory control system**.
- When a control system predicts an impending change, corrective measures are taken before the change occurs.

### Examples

1. **Thermoregulation system**
   - Both feedback and feedforward components are present.
   - Thermoreceptors are present in the skin (peripheral receptors) and the anterior hypothalamus (central receptors).
   - A decrease in ambient temperature decreases skin temperature. Peripheral receptors are activated and send input to the hypothalamus, triggering the heat-production system and increasing core body temperature.
2. **Heart rate and respiratory rate before exercise**
   - Heart rate and respiratory rate increase even before exercise starts.
   - Cause: psychic stimulation of the brain.
3. **Cephalic phase of gastric secretion**
   - The sight, smell, or thought of food triggers acid secretion in the stomach as an anticipatory response.
4. **Receptive relaxation of the stomach**
   - Food in the mouth that begins to be swallowed triggers relaxation/dilation of the stomach.
5. **Cerebellum**
   - The printed diagram shows granular cells (cerebellum) → basket and stellate cells (**labelled “stomach” in the diagram**) → Purkinje cells (cerebellum), with positive and negative signs as shown.
   - Purkinje cells have no effect at the level of basket or granular cells.
   - The signal is unidirectional and moves forward.
   - Controls various movements of the body.

![[images/ch01_p007_cerebellar_signal.png]]

*Cerebellar signal diagram; the label is retained as printed.*

### Important information — adaptive relaxation and thermoregulation

- **Adaptive relaxation of the stomach:** once food enters the stomach, the stomach stretches and dilates to accommodate increased volume. It is **not** a feedforward control system.
- **Negative-feedback thermoregulatory system:** a decrease in ambient temperature → decrease in core body temperature → decrease in blood temperature → central receptors activated → heat-production system activated → increase in core body temperature.

## MCQs

**Q1.** In negative feedback, feedback gain is infinite in which of the following? (AIIMS 2020)

- A. Temperature control in hypothalamus
- B. Blood-volume control by kidney
- C. Blood-pressure control by baroreflex
- D. Infinite feedback gain is not possible

**Answer: B.**

**Q2.** A patient's systolic BP decreases by 10 mmHg upon standing and recovers by only 8 mmHg, with an error of 2 mmHg. What is the gain? (AIIMS 2020)

- A. 2
- B. 4
- C. 8
- D. 10

**Answer: B.**
