### Source page 4

#### Homeostasis

- **Definition:** Maintenance of near-constant conditions in the internal environment.
- The concept of the **milieu intérieur** (internal environment) was introduced by **Claude Bernard**. He stated: “The stability of the internal environment (*milieu intérieur*) is the condition for a free & independent life.”
- Homeostasis depends on interstitial fluid (ISF), a component of extracellular fluid (ECF).
- The term **homeostasis** was coined by **Walter B. Cannon**.
- According to Walter B. Cannon, homeostasis comprises the various physiological arrangements that restore the normal state once it has been disturbed.
- Mechanisms maintaining the internal environment are **control systems**.

#### Control system

The source diagram classifies control systems into:

- **Feedback:** positive feedback and negative feedback.
- **Feedforward:** anticipatory control.

#### Feedback control system

- If hormone A stimulates production of hormone B, which in turn stimulates production of hormone C (the output), the output may feed back to the level of A or B to provide input.
- The source diagram labels the feedback-control pathway and uses blood pressure/baroreceptor notation in the negative-feedback example.

| Positive feedback control system | Negative feedback control system |
|---|---|
| Stimulating/positive feedback. A stimulates B → B stimulates C → C stimulates A. | Inhibitory/negative feedback. Example: increased BP activates baroreceptors to lower BP toward normal. |
| Example of a positive-feedback or “vicious” cycle: childbirth (Ferguson reflex). | Stability of a control system is maintained by negative feedback. |
| Uterine contractions → oxytocin release → stronger contractions → more oxytocin; the cycle continues until delivery. |  |
| Responsible for instability of the control system. |  |

#### Gain of a control system

- Measures the effectiveness of a control system.

\[
	ext{Gain} = rac{	ext{Correction}}{	ext{Error}}
\]

**Example: blood-pressure regulation**

| Item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Original/normal BP | 100 mmHg |
| Raised BP | 160 mmHg |
| Corrected BP (baroreceptor-mediated) | 120 mmHg |
| Error: 120 − 100 | 20 mmHg |
| Correction: 160 − 120 | 40 mmHg |
| Gain reported in the source | −2 |

- BP is raised; the baroreceptor response corrects it in the opposite direction, so the gain is negative.
- Normal gains stated in the source: baroreceptor control system **−2**; thermoreceptor control system **−33**.
- **Infinite-gain control system:** a system achieves 100% correction with zero error, giving infinite gain. Example stated: the kidney regulating BP or blood volume.

#### Regulation factor

- Measures how much a system reduces deviation from normal.

\[
R = rac{	ext{Change with regulation}}{	ext{Change without regulation}}
\]

**Example: blood-pressure regulation**

- Original/normal BP: 100 mmHg.
- Raised BP: 160 mmHg.
- Corrected BP (baroreceptor-mediated): 120 mmHg.
- Change without regulation: 160 − 100 = 60 mmHg.
- Change with regulation: 120 − 100 = 20 mmHg.
- Regulation factor: 20/60 = **1/3**.
- Accuracy of a control system is inversely related to the regulation factor.

#### Positive feedback control system

Positive-feedback control systems can be beneficial in selected physiological processes. The source begins with blood clotting:

- Thrombin generated provides positive feedback to factors **Va and VIIIa**.
- This activates factor X to factor Xa, which converts prothrombin to thrombin; a clot is formed.

#### Further examples of positive feedback

| Example | Explanation in the source |
|---|---|
| **LH surge just before ovulation** | During the menstrual cycle, estrogen normally exerts negative feedback on LH secretion. Just before ovulation, estrogen switches to positive feedback → LH surge → ovulation. |
| **Uterine contraction during labour (Ferguson reflex)** | Contractions push the fetal head further; the fetal head stretches the cervix. This activates a local neural pathway from cervix to uterus and a long neural pathway that promotes oxytocin secretion from the hypothalamus. Uterine contractions are strengthened. |
| **Lactation** | The baby suckles near the nipple and areolar region → long neural pathway is activated → oxytocin secretion → milk ejection. |
| **Generation of a nerve action potential** | Influx of positively charged sodium ions → membrane depolarization → opening of additional voltage-gated sodium channels → further depolarization → further channel opening. |

**Important information — potassium channels**

- Potassium-channel opening is described as a negative-feedback mechanism.
- Opening of K⁺ channels → K⁺ efflux → hyperpolarization → closure of K⁺ channels.

#### Feedforward control system

- An **anticipatory control system**.
- When a control system predicts an impending change, corrective measures are taken before the change occurs.

**Examples**

1. **Thermoregulation:** both feedback and feedforward components are present. Thermoreceptors are located in the skin (peripheral receptors) and anterior hypothalamus (central receptors). A decrease in ambient temperature decreases skin temperature; peripheral receptors send input to the hypothalamus, triggering heat production and increasing core body temperature.
2. **Heart rate and respiratory rate:** both increase even before exercise begins. The source attributes this to psychic stimulation of the brain.

### Source page 7

#### Further feedforward examples

3. **Cephalic phase of gastric secretion:** the sight, smell, or thought of food triggers gastric acid secretion as an anticipatory response.
4. **Receptive relaxation of the stomach:** food in the mouth, beginning to be swallowed, triggers relaxation/dilation of the stomach.
5. **Cerebellum:** the source diagram labels granular cells (cerebellum), basket and stellate cells, and Purkinje cells (cerebellum). The accompanying source statements say that Purkinje cells have no effect at the level of basket or granular cells, that the signal is unidirectional (moves forward), and that the cerebellum controls various body movements.

**Important information**

- **Adaptive relaxation of the stomach:** once food enters the stomach, the stomach stretches and dilates to accommodate the increased volume. The source explicitly says this is **not** a feedforward control system.
- **Negative-feedback thermoregulatory system:** decrease in ambient temperature → decrease in core body temperature → decrease in blood temperature → central receptors are activated → heat-production system is activated → core body temperature rises.

#### MCQs

**Q1. In negative feedback, feedback gain is infinite in which of the following?** (AIIMS 2020)

- A. Temperature control in hypothalamus
- B. Blood-volume control by kidney
- C. Blood-pressure control by baroreflex
- D. Infinite feedback gain is not possible

**Answer: B**

**Q2. A patient's SBP decreased by 10 mmHg upon standing and recovered by only 8 mmHg, with an error of 2 mmHg. What was the gain?** (AIIMS 2020)

- A. 2
- B. 4
- C. 8
- D. 10

**Answer: B**
