# 27. Ventilation Perfusion

## Ventilation

| Pulmonary ventilation / minute ventilation | Alveolar ventilation |
|---|---|
| PV = tidal volume (TV) × respiratory rate (RR). The source describes TV here as air entering the lung in 1 minute. Example: 500 mL × 12 = **6 L/min**. | AV = (TV − dead space) × RR. TV − dead space is the air reaching the alveoli for gas exchange. Example: (500 − 150) mL × 12 = **4.2 L/min**. |

## Perfusion

- Blood flow in the lungs = cardiac output of the right ventricle = **5.4 L/min**.

## Ventilation/perfusion (V/Q) ratio

- Average V/Q in the middle portion of the lung = 4.2/5.4 = **0.8**.

## V/Q in the upright posture

### Resting condition

- In an upright posture, the volume of the basal and apical intrapleural spaces differs due to gravitational pull on the lungs.
  - Basal intrapleural space: compressed.
  - Apical intrapleural space: stretched.
- **Variation in intrapleural pressure:**
  - Apex: −10 cm H₂O.
  - Base: −2.5 cm H₂O.
- Alveolar size decreases from apex to base:
  - Largest at the apex.
  - Smallest at the base.

![[images/ch40_p292_upright_lung_pressure_gradient.png]]

### Inspiration

- Inspiration expands the thoracic cavity, making IPP more negative and producing greater alveolar expansion at the base than at the apex (as stated in the source).
- Most of the tidal volume enters alveoli with greater expansion.
- Ventilation: **base >> apex**.

### Perfusion

- Gravity causes more blood to flow to the base of the lungs than to the apex.
- Perfusion: **base >> apex**.

## V/Q ratio across the lungs

| Region | Ventilation (V) | Perfusion (Q) | V/Q ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Apex | 2 L/min | 0.6 L/min | 3.3 (highest) |
| Middle | 4 L/min | 5 L/min | 0.8 |
| Base | 6 L/min | 10 L/min | 0.6 (lowest) |

- At the apex, reduction in perfusion is greater than reduction in ventilation; therefore V/Q ratio increases.

### Regional ventilation: apex

- Apical alveoli are large but have a low blood supply.
- V/Q ratio = 3.3; ventilation is 3.3 times higher than perfusion.
- Excess ventilation increases O₂ in the alveoli, saturating capillary blood.
- Excess O₂ remains in the alveoli due to disproportionate perfusion, increasing the stated PaO₂.
  - Apical alveolar pO₂: highest (132 mmHg).
- **Important information:**
  - Apical alveoli are more prone to tuberculosis (TB).
  - TB bacilli are aerobic and thrive in high oxygen tension.
- Venous blood brings ↓CO₂ to the apex due to ↓blood flow.
- CO₂ is washed out due to hyperventilation.
  - Apical alveolar PaCO₂: lowest (28 mmHg).
- The source states that venous blood loses CO₂ quickly due to lower PaCO₂; pH of capillary blood leaving the apex is 7.51 (slightly alkaline).
- Ventilation is wasted due to high V/Q.
- **Alveolar dead space:** air entering alveoli but not being exchanged.

### Regional ventilation: middle

- PaO₂ = 100–104 mmHg.
- PaCO₂ = 40 mmHg.
- pH = 7.4.

### Regional ventilation: base

- V/Q ratio = 0.6.
- Ventilation is less than perfusion → ↓O₂ entry → easier O₂ delivery to venous blood.
- PaO₂ = 89 mmHg (lowest).
- PaCO₂ = 42 mmHg (highest), attributed to high venous blood flow and reduced CO₂ exit.
- Capillary blood leaving basal alveoli has a pH of 7.31 (slightly acidic).
- Incomplete oxygenation of blood at basal alveoli is described as perfusion wastage.
- The source says that venous blood enters the arterial side, causing shunting at basal alveoli.

## V/Q ratio: summary

| V/Q | PaO₂ | PaCO₂ |
|---|---|---|
| V/Q > 1 | High | Low |
| V/Q < 1 | Low | High |

## V/Q in pathology

| Obstruction (tumour/foreign body) | Embolism |
|---|---|
| Complete obstruction of a bronchiole. Ventilation = 0, so V/Q ratio = 0. O₂ and CO₂ exchange continues briefly until equilibrium is reached and alveolar gas composition matches venous blood (PvO₂ = 40, PvCO₂ = 46). Subsequently, no further gas exchange occurs. Shunting of blood. | Ventilation is present; perfusion = 0, so V/Q ratio = ∞. Inspiratory air entering the alveoli does not undergo gaseous exchange. pO₂ of inspiratory air = 149 mmHg; pCO₂ = 0.3 mmHg. PaO₂ = 149 mmHg and PaCO₂ = 0.3 mmHg, matching inspiratory air. Alveolar dead space. |

## Characteristics of pulmonary circulation

- **Dual circulation:**
  - Pulmonary artery from the right ventricle.
  - Bronchial artery from the descending thoracic aorta, supplying up to the respiratory bronchiole.
- Low-pressure, low-resistance and high-capacitance system.
- Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) is approximately 1/8 to 1/10 of systemic vascular resistance.
- PVR decreases as cardiac output (CO) increases.
  - Example: exercise → increased CO → increased blood flow in the lung → increased diameter of lung vessels → decreased PVR.

## Zones in pulmonary circulation

| Zone | Pressure relationship | Flow/features stated in the source |
|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 | Pᴬ > Pₐ > Pᵥ (alveolar > arterial > venous) | Area of zero blood flow; not present in normal lung; may appear in pathological lung; no blood flow because capillaries are compressed. |
| Zone 2 | Pₐ > Pᴬ > Pᵥ | Intermittent blood flow; blood flow only during systole, with no flow during diastole (waterfall effect); apex to middle part. |
| Zone 3 | Pₐ > Pᵥ > Pᴬ | Continuous blood flow; located at the base of the lungs, where perfusion is largest due to gravity. |

## MCQs

### 1. V/Q is infinity when which of the following applies? *(NEET 2020)*

- a. Pressure of oxygen in alveolar air is less than dead-space volume
- b. Partial pressures of oxygen and carbon dioxide are equal
- c. PaO₂ is 159 mmHg and PaCO₂ is 40 mmHg
- d. No exchange of CO₂ and O₂ occurs

**Answer: (d)**

### 2. Ventilation/perfusion ratio is maximum at which location? *(AI 1998)*

- a. Apex of lung
- b. Base of lung
- c. Posterior lobe of lung
- d. Middle of the lung

**Answer: (a)**
