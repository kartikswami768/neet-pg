### First Aid additions: electrolyte and acid–base effects

- **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors:** ↓HCO₃⁻ reabsorption → **metabolic acidosis**.
- **K⁺-sparing diuretics:** aldosterone blockade prevents K⁺ and H⁺ secretion; hyperkalemia can promote intracellular K⁺/H⁺ exchange and contribute to acidosis.
- **Loop diuretics and thiazides:** produce **metabolic alkalosis** through volume contraction → ↑angiotensin II → ↑Na⁺/H⁺ exchange and HCO₃⁻ reabsorption, together with K⁺ loss and enhanced H⁺ secretion.
- In hypokalemia, H⁺ rather than K⁺ is exchanged for Na⁺ in the cortical collecting tubule → alkalosis with **paradoxical aciduria**.

![[p647_osmotic_ca_loop_diuretics.png|700]]

### Loop-diuretic details

- **Furosemide, bumetanide, torsemide:** sulfonamide loop diuretics that inhibit the **Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter** in the thick ascending limb.
- The loop diuretics abolish medullary hypertonicity, preventing concentration of urine.
- **Ethacrynic acid:** non-sulfonamide inhibitor of the same cotransporter; used for diuresis in patients with sulfa allergy. It is particularly ototoxic.

![[p648_thiazide_k_sparing.png|700]]

### Thiazide and potassium-sparing details

- **Thiazides:** hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone, metolazone; inhibit NaCl reabsorption in the early DCT.
- **Potassium-sparing drugs:** spironolactone, eplerenone, amiloride, triamterene.
- Spironolactone and eplerenone are competitive aldosterone-receptor antagonists in the cortical collecting tubule; amiloride and triamterene block Na⁺ channels there.
- A useful mnemonic from First Aid is **“Keep your SEAT”** for the potassium-sparing group.

## Drugs used for Preservation of Renal Function

### Angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors

**Examples:** captopril, enalapril, lisinopril, ramipril.

**Mechanism:** Inhibit ACE → ↓angiotensin II → ↓GFR by preventing constriction of efferent arterioles. ↑renin occurs because of loss of negative feedback. ACE inhibition also prevents inactivation of **bradykinin**, a potent vasodilator.

**Clinical uses:** hypertension; heart failure (↓mortality); proteinuria; diabetic nephropathy. In chronic kidney disease such as diabetic nephropathy, ↓intraglomerular pressure and slowing of GBM thickening are renoprotective. Also prevent unfavorable cardiac remodeling associated with chronic hypertension.

**Adverse effects:** dry nonproductive cough; angioedema (both related to ↑bradykinin; C/i in C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency); teratogenicity with fetal renal malformations; ↑creatinine from ↓GFR; hyperkalemia; hypotension. Use with caution in **bilateral renal artery stenosis** because further reduction in GFR can precipitate renal failure.

> **Mnemonic:** **Captopril’s CATCHH.**

### Angiotensin II receptor blockers

**Examples:** losartan, candesartan, valsartan.

**Mechanism:** Selectively block angiotensin II binding to the **AT1 receptor**. Effects are similar to ACE inhibitors, but ARBs do **not** increase bradykinin.

**Clinical uses:** hypertension, heart failure, proteinuria, and chronic kidney disease (eg, diabetic nephropathy), particularly when ACE inhibitors are not tolerated because of cough or angioedema.

**Adverse effects:** hyperkalemia, ↓GFR, hypotension, and teratogenicity.

### SGLT-2 inhibitors
- This drugs inhibit Na⁺-Glucose Co-transporter type 2 in the PCT. 
- Increase NaCl delivery to thick ascending limb → trigger tubulo-glomerular feedback → Decrease glomerular hypertension. 
### GLP-1 receptor agonist
