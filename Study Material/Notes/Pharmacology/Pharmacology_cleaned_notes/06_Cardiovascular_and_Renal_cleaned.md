# Cardiovascular Pharmacology
## 3. RENAL: DIURETICS
### 3.1 General Definition
Diuretics are drugs that increase excretion of sodium and/or water in urine.
Major diuretic classes by nephron site:
1. Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors
2. Osmotic diuretics
3. Loop diuretics
4. Thiazide/thiazide-like diuretics
5. Potassium-sparing diuretics
### 3.2 Carbonic Anhydrase Inhibitors
#### Mechanism of action
- Act at the **proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)**.
- Inhibit **carbonic anhydrase**.
- Reduce bicarbonate reabsorption.
- Increase urinary loss of:
 - Na+
 - HCO3-
 - water
- These agents are **noncompetitive and reversible inhibitors** of carbonic anhydrase.
- They are **sulfonamide derivatives**.
#### Drugs
- **Acetazolamide**
- **Methazolamide**
- **Dichlorphenamide**
#### Clinical uses
**[Mnemonic: GAME Plan]**
##### G - Glaucoma
- Reduce aqueous humor production.
- Lower intraocular pressure.
##### A - Alkalinization of urine
- Increased urinary bicarbonate causes urine alkalinization.
-
 - Useful for increasing urinary excretion of some weak acids.
 - Mentioned with **uric acid and cystine stones**.
- Also an increased risk of **calcium phosphate stones**.
##### M - Metabolic acidosis
- Bicarbonate loss can produce a metabolic acidosis.
- This is associated with increased H+ concentration with respiratory-center stimulation.
##### E - Epilepsy
- Acetazolamide can be used as an adjunct in selected seizure disorders.
- **Mnemonic continuation: ** “Plan: Periodic muscle paralysis.”
- Acetazolamide may be used in **hyperkalemic or hypokalemic periodic paralysis**.
##### Mountain sickness
- **Acetazolamide** is a standard preventive treatment for acute mountain sickness.
- It promotes renal bicarbonate loss and produces a mild metabolic acidosis that stimulates ventilation.
#### Adverse effects
**[Mnemonic: BASE]**
- **B - Bone marrow suppression**
 - Rare but important sulfonamide-related hematologic toxicity.
- **A - Ammonia elevation**
 - This warrants caution because about use in liver disease because impaired ammonia handling may aggravate encephalopathy.
- **S - Stones**
 - Calcium phosphate nephrolithiasis.
- **E - Electrolyte/metabolic effects**
 - Hypokalemia
 - Hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis
- The key clinical use in altitude illness is well established.
- Acetazolamide can worsen metabolic acidosis and may contribute to nephrolithiasis.
- The wording “contraindicated in liver disease” is too absolute for every clinical context; severe hepatic disease with risk of encephalopathy is a major caution because acetazolamide can reduce urinary acid excretion and may alter ammonia handling.
- “Bone marrow suppression” is uncommon, not the typical adverse effect.
### 3.3 Osmotic Diuretic: Mannitol
#### Drug
- **Mannitol**
- IV **20% osmolar solution**.
#### Mechanism
- Raises plasma osmolality.
- Pulls water from tissues into the intravascular space.
- Reduces:
 - CSF production → lowers ICP
 - Aqueous humor production → lowers IOP
#### Clinical uses
##### Cerebral edema / raised intracranial pressure
- Mannitol is used to lower CSF volume and intracranial pressure.
- Decreases CSF volume and intracranial pressure.
##### Acute glaucoma
- Reduces intraocular pressure by osmotic dehydration of the eye.
##### Dialysis disequilibrium syndrome
- Maintains plasma osmolality and may reduce the risk of cerebral edema in dialysis disequilibrium.
#### Contraindications / major cautions
- Heart failure
 - Increased intravascular volume can worsen preload/congestion.
- Renal failure/anuria
 - Inability to excrete the osmotic load can worsen volume status.
- “Mannitol excretes metals”
- “Lithium toxicity (treatment of choice)”
- “Cisplatin toxicity”
Do **not** use this as a current lithium-poisoning algorithm.
- Mannitol is not standard first-line treatment for lithium toxicity.
- Initial management generally involves stopping lithium and appropriate fluid/electrolyte management.
- **Hemodialysis** is the definitive extracorporeal therapy for severe lithium poisoning when clinical criteria are met.
- “Forced diuresis” has no routine modern role comparable to hemodialysis.
- Mannitol is not a general routine treatment for cisplatin nephrotoxicity.
Osmotic diuresis occurs predominantly in the **proximal tubule and thin descending limb**, depending on the filtered osmole and water permeability.
### 3.4 Loop Diuretics
#### Site of action
- **Thick ascending limb (TAL) of the loop of Henle**
#### Mechanism
1. Inhibit the **Na+-K+-2Cl- (NKCC2) cotransporter** in the TAL.
2. Increase urinary NaCl and water excretion.
3. Also increased prostaglandin synthesis and renal vasodilation/blood flow.
#### Major characteristics
- **Most effective / high-ceiling diuretics.**
#### Drugs
##### Mercury-containing
Historical only:
- **Mersalyl**
 - Very ototoxic.
  - Permanent deafness can occur.
##### Sulfonamide-containing
- **Furosemide**
- **Torsemide**
- **Bumetanide**
Key points:
- Torsemide: longer acting than furosemide.
- Bumetanide: more potent per mg.
- Ototoxicity can occur.
- Sulfonamide allergy is a consideration.
##### Non-mercury, non-sulfonamide
- **Ethacrynic acid**
This is the main loop option classically used when a non-sulfonamide loop is desired.
#### Clinical uses
- Pulmonary edema - A common first-line choice.
- Severe fluid overload.
- Hypertensive crisis: loop diuretics are useful when significant volume overload/congestion is present; otherwise treatment depends on the acute target-organ injury.
Loop diuretics are especially useful when hypertension coexists with significant volume overload or renal dysfunction, but they are **not a generic first-line drug for all hypertensive emergencies**. Blood-pressure control in emergency depends on the specific acute target-organ injury.
#### NSAID interaction
**Aspirin + loop diuretic**
- Aspirin is an NSAID.
- NSAIDs inhibit prostaglandin synthesis.
- Reduced renal prostaglandins can reduce renal blood flow and blunt the natriuretic response.
This interaction is pharmacologically plausible and clinically relevant, especially with high-dose NSAIDs and in patients at risk of kidney injury or sodium retention.
### 3.5 Thiazide and Thiazide-like Diuretics
#### Site
- **Distal convoluted tubule (DCT)**
#### Mechanism
- Inhibit the **Na+-Cl- symporter (NCC)**.
#### Drugs
##### Simple thiazides
- **Chlorothiazide**
- **Hydrochlorothiazide**
##### Thiazide-like drugs
- **Chlorthalidone**
 - Potent, long-acting thiazide-like drug.
- **Metolazone**
 - Retains diuretic utility at reduced GFR.
- **Indapamide**
#### Clinical uses — HORD
##### H - Hypertension
- Thiazides are first-line antihypertensive options.
- Loop diuretics are mainly used for volume overload and selected hypertensive emergencies with congestion.
##### O - Osteoporosis
- Reduced urinary Ca2+ loss may help preserve bone mineral density.
##### R - Renal stones
- Particularly **calcium stones** associated with hypercalciuria.
##### D - Diabetes insipidus
- Thiazides paradoxically reduce urine volume in **nephrogenic DI**.
#### Low-GFR statement
> “Only thiazide safe in renal failure, when GFR <45” for metolazone.
This wording should **not** be memorized as an absolute rule.
- Thiazide-like agents, especially **metolazone and chlorthalidone**, can retain antihypertensive and/or diuretic effects at reduced GFR.
- The choice depends on the clinical objective, kidney function, volume status, and electrolyte risk.
- There is no contemporary universal statement that metolazone is the “only thiazide safe” below a specific GFR.
### 3.6 Adverse Effects of Loop + Thiazide Diuretics
- Hyponatremia
- Hypotension
- Hypokalemia
	- Increased risk of **digoxin toxicity**
	- They ↓ K⁺ in Pancreatic β cell → ↓ insulin secretion → worsen diabetic control
- Hypomagnesemia
- ==Hyperglycemia==
- Hyperlipidemia
- Hyperuricemia
#### Contraindication statement
The loop/thiazide diuretics are contraindicated in:
- Diabetes mellitus
- Gout
- Dyslipidemia
### 3.7 Calcium Handling: Loop vs Thiazide
| Aspect | Loop diuretics | Thiazide diuretics |
|---|---|
| Effect on Ca2+ | **Lose calcium in urine** | **Retain calcium** |
| Blood Ca2+ | Can lower serum calcium | Can increase serum calcium |
| Classic clinical use | Loop: hypercalcemia | Thiazide: hypercalciuria |
| Renal stone point | Less useful for prevention of calcium stones | Useful in recurrent calcium stones with hypercalciuria |
- Calcium-retaining effects reduce urinary calcium and are useful in recurrent calcium stones with hypercalciuria; stone risk depends on the clinical context.
### 3.8 Potassium-Sparing Diuretics
#### Site
- **Collecting duct**
#### Basic mechanism
- Reduce Na+ reabsorption in the collecting duct.
- Reduce K+ secretion.
- Therefore they are **potassium-sparing**.
#### Main types
| Type | Drugs | Site/target |
|---|---|
| Aldosterone receptor antagonists / MRAs | Spironolactone, Eplerenone, Finerenone | Mineralocorticoid receptor |
| ENaC inhibitors | Amiloride, Triamterene | Epithelial sodium channel (ENaC) |
#### Important exam distinction
> Potassium-sparing diuretics are the major diuretic class associated with **hyperkalemia**.
This remains a useful exam pearl.
#### 3.8.1 Aldosterone physiology
Aldosterone binds its intracellular receptor in principal cells of the collecting duct and promotes:
- ENaC expression/activity
- Na+ reabsorption
- K+ secretion
Therefore, an aldosterone antagonist causes:
- Less Na+ reabsorption
- Less K+ secretion
- More K+ retention
It is more precise to describe the **mineralocorticoid receptor** as an intracellular receptor rather than saying the drug acts literally “from the basolateral side.” ENaC is the apical/luminal channel that is directly targeted by amiloride/triamterene.
#### 3.8.2 Spironolactone
##### Pharmacology
- Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist.
- Active metabolite: **canrenone**.
##### Adverse effects / special features
- **Gynecomastia**
- Sexual adverse effects
- Hyperkalemia
- Antiandrogenic effects
##### Clinical uses
- Chronic heart failure
- Cirrhotic ascites
- Hyperaldosteronism
- Hirsutism/PCOS in selected patients
Spironolactone is a guideline-supported MRA in appropriate HFrEF patients and is also widely used in resistant hypertension and hyperaldosteronism.
#### 3.8.3 Eplerenone
- More selective mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist.
- Less antiandrogenic effect than spironolactone.
- Therefore:
 - **Lower risk of gynecomastia**
 - Sexual adverse effects may be less prominent.
Key point: lower gynecomastia risk and lower hyperkalemia risk than spironolactone.
The hyperkalemia risk is **not absent** and is still clinically important with eplerenone.
#### 3.8.4 Finerenone
- Newer MRA.
- Cardioprotective.
- Nephroprotective.
- Used in diabetic nephropathy.
Finerenone is a **nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist** with important evidence in **type 2 diabetes + CKD**, especially in patients with albuminuria, to reduce cardiorenal outcomes when used appropriately. It is not simply another high-volume “diuretic.”
Current CKD guidance places finerenone among important kidney/cardiovascular risk-reducing therapies in appropriately selected patients.
#### 3.8.5 ENaC inhibitors
##### Amiloride
- Acts from the **luminal side**.
- Blocks ENaC.
##### Uses
###### Liddle syndrome
- Genetic gain-of-function/excess ENaC activity.
- **Amiloride** blocks ENaC.
###### Lithium-induced nephrogenic DI
- Amiloride can reduce lithium entry into collecting-duct principal cells through ENaC.
- This can improve lithium-induced nephrogenic DI.
Amiloride is particularly useful in lithium-induced nephrogenic DI because of this mechanism and is often favored over simply escalating thiazide therapy.
##### Triamterene
- ENaC inhibitor.
- Potassium-sparing.
### 3.9 Edema: Diuretic of Choice
Edema treatment by type:

| Type of edema | listed diuretic of choice |
|---|---|
| Heart failure | Loop diuretic |
| Pulmonary edema | Loop diuretic |
| Renal failure | Loop diuretic |
| Periorbital edema | Loop diuretic |
| Liver failure / ascites | Spironolactone |
| Cerebral edema / raised ICP | Mannitol |
#### Edema pearl
- Loop diuretics can be used in essentially **all major forms of clinically significant volume-overload edema**.
- Spironolactone is particularly important in **cirrhotic ascites** because of secondary hyperaldosteronism.
#### Mannitol caution
- Mannitol should not be used indiscriminately in patients with heart failure or severe renal failure because the initial intravascular volume expansion can be harmful.
## 4. ANTIDIURETIC HORMONE (ADH) / VASOPRESSIN
### 4.1 Vasopressin Agonists
#### 4.1.1 Natural arginine vasopressin
- “Presses” blood vessels.
- Administration: injection.
- Major outcome: vasoconstriction and increased blood pressure.
- Vasopressin is used as a vasopressor adjunct in selected resistant vasodilatory shock.
 - Resistant shock, including shock resistant to epinephrine/norepinephrine.
Vasopressin is a vasopressor adjunct in selected forms of vasodilatory shock. Exact use depends on the clinical setting and contemporary ICU/critical-care protocol.
#### 4.1.2 Terlipressin
- V1 agonist.
- Injection.
- Vasoconstriction.
- Listed uses:
 - Bleeding esophageal varices
 - Hepatorenal syndrome
Terlipressin is a vasopressin analogue with strong V1a-mediated vasoconstrictor activity and has established use in selected patients with variceal bleeding and **hepatorenal syndrome / HRS-AKI**, depending on jurisdiction and protocol.
#### 4.1.3 Desmopressin (DDAVP)
- Primarily **V2 agonist**.
- Oral or intranasal administration.
- Major uses:
 - Neurogenic/central DI
 - Nocturnal enuresis
 - Hemophilia A in selected patients
- V2 stimulation increases release of **factor VIII**.
Desmopressin also increases **von Willebrand factor** and is used in selected patients with type 1 von Willebrand disease and other appropriate bleeding disorders.
### 4.2 Vasopressin Antagonists - Vaptans
#### Mechanism
- Block ADH/vasopressin receptors.
- Promote free-water excretion (**aquaresis**).
#### Drugs
- **Conivaptan**
 - IV
 - V1a/V2 antagonist
- **Tolvaptan**
 - Oral
 - Selective V2 antagonist
#### Main indication
- **SIADH**
#### SIADH pathophysiology
Excess ADH → increased water reabsorption → dilutional hyponatremia.
listed causes:
- Head injury
- Brain tumors
- Heart failure
- ADPKD
The list is not exhaustive. SIADH can occur with pulmonary disease, malignancy, drugs, CNS disease, postoperative states, and many other conditions.
#### Demeclocycline
- Tetracycline antibiotic.
- Used in SIADH because it reduces renal responsiveness to ADH.
Demeclocycline is no longer a routine first-line SIADH treatment in many modern practices because of nephrotoxicity and availability/preferences for other strategies. It is best remembered as an older pharmacologic option.
### 4.3 Nocturnal Enuresis
#### Age
- Treatment generally considered when the child is **>6 years** and bedwetting remains clinically significant.
#### First-line non-drug management
- Education/training.
- Restrict excessive fluid intake immediately before sleep.
- **Alarm therapy**
 - Alarm triggers when urine is passed.
 - Trains arousal to bladder filling.
#### Pharmacotherapy
##### Desmopressin
- **Drug of choice**
- Decreases nocturnal urine production.
- Nasal route mentioned for small children.
Intranasal desmopressin is not generally preferred for routine enuresis because of safety concerns; oral formulations are more commonly used, with careful fluid restriction to reduce hyponatremia risk.
##### Oxybutynin
- Anticholinergic.
- Relaxes bladder detrusor.
- Usually considered in selected children with bladder dysfunction and not as the universal first pharmacologic choice.
##### Imipramine
- Tricyclic antidepressant.
- Anticholinergic effect.
- Can relax the bladder.
- Its adverse-effect profile and overdose toxicity limit routine use.
### 4.4 Diabetes Insipidus (DI)
#### Basic pathophysiology
Deficient ADH effect → reduced water reabsorption → increased free-water loss.
Clinical pattern:
- Polyuria
- Dilute/low-osmolality urine
#### 4.4.1 Central (Neurogenic) DI
##### Cause
- Deficient ADH production/release from hypothalamic-neurohypophyseal pathways.
##### Drug of choice
- **Desmopressin**
##### Other listed drugs
- Carbamazepine
- Chlorpropamide
These may increase endogenous ADH effect/secretion in selected patients, but are not first-line modern replacements for desmopressin.
#### 4.4.2 Nephrogenic DI
##### Cause
- Kidney is resistant to ADH.
##### Treatment
- Thiazide diuretics
- NSAIDs (e.g., indomethacin)
- Amiloride
##### Mechanistic idea
Thiazide-induced mild extracellular volume contraction → increased proximal sodium/water reabsorption → less fluid reaches the collecting duct → lower final urine volume.
##### Lithium-induced DI
Thiazides can increase lithium serum concentration and therefore require substantial caution, lithium dose adjustment, and close monitoring.
- Thiazides can **increase lithium serum concentration** and therefore require substantial caution, lithium dose adjustment, and close monitoring.
- **Amiloride** is particularly useful in lithium-induced nephrogenic DI because it limits lithium entry through ENaC.
- Thiazides are not simply an absolute contraindication in every patient; they are a medication-interaction problem requiring careful management.
### 4.5 Drugs Causing SIADH / Excess ADH Effect
#### Mnemonic
- **C - Carbamazepine**
- **C - Chlorpropamide**
- **V - Vincristine**
- **S - SSRIs**
Also::
- Carbamazepine → hyponatremia via SIADH
- Chlorpropamide → hyponatremia via SIADH
#### Drugs causing an ADH-deficiency/nephrogenic-DI picture
**Mnemonic**
- **L - Lithium**
- **A - Alcohol** [listed]
- **P - Phenytoin** [listed]
- **D - Demeclocycline**
The lithium association is the major high-yield drug-induced nephrogenic DI association.
## 5. ANTIHYPERTENSIVE DRUGS - RAAS INHIBITORS
### 5.1 RAAS Pathway
Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system:
```text
Angiotensinogen
      |
      | renin (kidney JG cells)
      v
Angiotensin I
      |
      | ACE
      v
Angiotensin II
      |
      | AT1 receptor
      v
Vasoconstriction
+
Aldosterone release
+
Na+ retention
+
K+ loss
      |
      v
Increased BP
```
#### Drug classes
1. **Direct renin inhibitor**
 - Aliskiren
2. **ACE inhibitors**
 - names end in **-pril**
3. **Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs)**
 - names end in **-sartan**
#### Pharmacologic effect of RAAS inhibition
- Arterial vasodilation.
- Reduced aldosterone.
- Reduced sodium retention.
- Potassium retention.
- Lower blood pressure.
### 5.2 ACEI vs ARB: Adverse Effects and Contraindications
#### Pregnancy
- ACEIs and ARBs are contraindicated in pregnancy because of fetal renal toxicity and related adverse fetal outcomes.
This remains a major contraindication. Current hypertension-in-pregnancy guidance also excludes other RAAS-modifying drugs in pregnancy.
#### Bilateral renal artery stenosis
- GFR may already depend on angiotensin II-mediated efferent arteriolar constriction.
- RAAS blockade can sharply reduce intraglomerular pressure.
- AKI/oliguria can result.
This is a classic high-risk situation, not an instruction to label every renal artery stenosis as an absolute contraindication. The degree of stenosis, kidney function, indication for therapy, and monitoring matter.
#### Hyperkalemia
- ACEI/ARB can increase serum K+.
- Risk is higher with:
 - CKD
 - MRA therapy
 - potassium supplements
 - other potassium-raising drugs.
#### ACEI-specific adverse effects
ACE inhibition reduces bradykinin breakdown.
Therefore:
- **Dry cough** - common
- **Angioedema** - uncommon but potentially life-threatening
Management of significant ACEI-associated angioedema:
- Stop ACE inhibitor.
- Treat the airway/emergency syndrome appropriately.
- ARB use requires individualized consideration.
Do not list **aliskiren** as the routine automatic replacement for ACEI angioedema.
### 5.3 Nephroprotective Drugs in CKD
#### Mnemonic: SAF
- **S - SGLT2 inhibitors**
- **A - ACE inhibitor / ARB**
- **F - Finerenone**
#### SGLT2 inhibitors
listed examples:
- Canagliflozin
- Dapagliflozin
- Empagliflozin
Also used in diabetes mellitus.
Current CKD guidance supports SGLT2 inhibitors for appropriate patients with CKD, with or without diabetes depending on the indication and level of kidney function. They are kidney/cardiovascular risk-reducing therapy, not merely “diabetes drugs.”
#### ACEI/ARB
Current KDIGO guidance places particular emphasis on **ACEI or ARB in CKD with albuminuria when indicated**, especially in diabetic or non-diabetic albuminuric CKD.
Do **not** routinely combine ACEI + ARB.
#### Finerenone
- Nonsteroidal MRA.
- Particularly relevant to type 2 diabetes + CKD with albuminuria when potassium and eGFR criteria are appropriate.
### 5.4 Special Properties of Selected ARBs
#### Losartan
- Lowers uric acid.
- The **uric-acid-lowering effect** is a recognized distinguishing property of losartan.
#### Telmisartan
- Partial **PPAR-gamma agonist** activity.
- May improve insulin sensitivity.
This pharmacologic distinction is recognized, although it does not make telmisartan a substitute for a dedicated insulin-sensitizing drug.
#### Olmesartan
- Risk of sprue-like enteropathy / chronic diarrhea.
Olmesartan-associated sprue-like enteropathy is a recognized adverse effect.
## 6. CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS (CCBs)
### Mechanism
Block **L-type calcium channels** in vascular smooth muscle and/or myocardium.
### 6.1 Classification
| Class | Examples | Major action |
|---|---|
| Dihydropyridines (DHP) | Amlodipine, Nifedipine, Nicardipine, Clevidipine, Nimodipine | Predominantly vascular |
| Non-DHP | Verapamil, Diltiazem | Cardiac conduction + vascular smooth muscle |
#### DHPs
- Primarily arterial dilators.
- Lower systemic vascular resistance.
- Lower BP.
#### Non-DHPs
- Slow SA-node automaticity.
- Slow AV-node conduction.
- Reduce heart rate.
- Also produce some vasodilation.
### 6.2 Clinical Applications
#### Verapamil
- Used for supraventricular tachycardias.
For an acute **regular narrow-complex tachycardia**, vagal maneuvers and adenosine are commonly first steps; IV diltiazem/verapamil are options in selected stable patients.
#### Diltiazem
- Angina
- SVT
Key point: reduced heart rate and oxygen demand.
#### DHP CCBs
- Common first-line BP-lowering agents.
- Amlodipine has a long oral duration.
#### Nicardipine
- IV drug for severe hypertension.
- IV nicardipine is a titratable option for hypertensive emergency; choice depends on the acute target-organ injury.
IV nicardipine is a widely used titratable agent for hypertensive emergency, but the choice should match the organ injury (e.g., aortic dissection, acute coronary syndrome, pulmonary edema, stroke) and local protocol.
#### Nimodipine
- Crosses the BBB.
- Used in subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- Nimodipine reduces the risk of delayed cerebral ischemia and poor neurologic outcome after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage.
In aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage, nimodipine is used to reduce the risk of **delayed cerebral ischemia and poor neurologic outcome**. It is not best described simply as a generic “vasospasm-prevention” drug.
### 6.3 Key Adverse Effects
#### Non-DHP
- Bradycardia
- AV block
- Negative inotropy
#### DHP
- Reflex tachycardia (more prominent with short-acting agents)
- Peripheral edema, especially with amlodipine
- Flushing
- Headache
#### Major interaction
> Do not combine verapamil/diltiazem with beta blockers.
**Reason:**
- Excessive AV-nodal suppression
- Bradycardia
- AV block
- Negative inotropy
This is a useful exam and clinical caution, especially with verapamil.
### 6.4 CCB Adverse Effects Mnemonic
**“Hypertensives Usually FORGeT CCB”**
- **H - Headache**
- **U - Urinary retention**
- **F - Flushing**
- **O - Edema**
 - Amlodipine → ankle edema
- **Ge - GERD**
  - Lower esophageal sphincter relaxation may aggravate GERD.
- **T - Tocolytic action**
- **CCBs - Constipation**
#### CCB toxicity
- Calcium gluconate is used.
Calcium is an important therapy for severe CCB poisoning, but modern toxicology management can also include **high-dose insulin/euglycemia**, vasopressors, and other advanced measures depending on the clinical syndrome. “Calcium gluconate” is therefore better remembered as **one important treatment**, not the sole antidote.

### 7.1 Classification

#### Minoxidil
- Potassium-channel opener.
- Used in resistant hypertension.
- Can cause hypertrichosis.
- Topical minoxidil is also used for androgenetic alopecia.
#### Diazoxide
- Potassium-channel opener.

 - Hydralazine
 - Isoniazid

### 7.3 Nicorandil
- Potassium-channel opener.
- Coronary vasodilator.
- Used for angina.
Nicorandil also has an **NO-donor** component in addition to KATP-channel activation.
## 8. NITRATES AND NITROPRUSSIDE
### 8.1 Nitric Oxide / cGMP pathway
```text
NO
 |
 v
Soluble guanylyl cyclase
 |
 v
↑ cGMP
 |
 v
Smooth-muscle relaxation
 |
 v
Vasodilation
```
### 8.2 Sodium Nitroprusside
#### Mechanism
- Direct nitric-oxide donor.
- Enzyme-independent in the sense that it does not require the same enzymatic bioactivation pathway as organic nitrates.
- Produces **arterial + venous dilation**.
- Rapidly lowers BP.
#### Clinical use
- **Hypertensive emergency**
- Especially useful when an easily titratable balanced vasodilator is desired.
#### Light sensitivity
- Protect from light / use opaque or light-protective handling.
#### Major toxicity: cyanide
Key point:
- Hepatic metabolism can contribute to cyanide generation.
- Cyanide toxicity is the major classic toxicity.
#### Treatment
- Hydroxocobalamin (vitamin B12 form)
- Sodium nitrite
- Amyl nitrite
- Sodium thiosulfate
Current sodium nitroprusside labeling emphasizes stopping the drug and use of **sodium nitrite + sodium thiosulfate** in cyanide toxicity. **Hydroxocobalamin** is also used clinically as a cyanide antidote because it binds cyanide to form cyanocobalamin.
### 8.3 Organic Nitrates
#### Major drugs
- **GTN / nitroglycerin**
- **Isosorbide dinitrate (ISDN)**
- **Isosorbide mononitrate (ISMN)**
- **Pentaerythritol tetranitrate (PETN)**
#### Key pharmacology
- Predominantly **venodilate at lower doses**.
- Reduce venous return → reduce preload → reduce myocardial oxygen demand.
- At higher concentrations, arterial and coronary effects become more prominent.
#### High-yield route
Sublingual:
- GTN
- ISDN
#### Oral bioavailability
Key point:
- **ISMN** has high oral bioavailability.
- ISDN undergoes first-pass metabolism and forms active metabolites, including ISMN.
### 8.4 Nitrate Tolerance
- Continuous exposure → reduced therapeutic effect.
- Particularly prominent with continuous transdermal exposure.
#### Mechanism of tolerance
 - down-regulation/reduced efficiency of nitrate bioactivation pathway. ALDH2 contributes to organic-nitrate bioactivation.
#### Management
- **Nitrate-free interval**
A common transdermal nitroglycerin regimen provides about **10-12 hours nitrate-free each day** to limit tolerance. The exact interval is formulation-dependent.
### 8.5 Uses of Nitrates
- Angina
- Selected acute coronary syndromes when clinically appropriate
- Acute pulmonary edema / acute LV failure in selected patients because of preload/afterload reduction
#### Right ventricular infarction
- Contraindication because preload may be critical.
This remains an important bedside pearl: nitrates can markedly reduce preload and may produce hypotension in preload-dependent states.
#### Colicky pain
- useful for abdominal colicky pain
- not useful for renal colic
This is not a standard modern general indication for organic nitrates and should not be overgeneralized.
## 9. HYPERTENSION MANAGEMENT
### 9.1 Essential Hypertension
Options include first-line classes:
- CCB
- ACEI/ARB
- Thiazide diuretics
This broad class structure remains consistent with contemporary major hypertension guidance.
#### Combination therapy
Current practice often uses combination therapy when BP is substantially above goal, frequently with:
- ACEI/ARB + CCB
- ACEI/ARB + thiazide-type diuretic
- triple therapy with all three classes when necessary
### 9.2 Resistant Hypertension
- BP not controlled on 3 drugs in combination.
- DOC:
 - Spironolactone or eplerenone.
- Newer drug:
 - Aprocitentan.
The **2025 AHA/ACC hypertension guideline** defines resistant hypertension as BP above goal despite **3 complementary antihypertensive agents at maximally tolerated doses, one being a diuretic**, or BP controlled only with **4 or more drugs**.
- Spironolactone is a preferred fourth-line option when appropriate.
- Eplerenone is an alternative MRA.
- Aprocitentan, a dual endothelin-receptor antagonist, is a newer option for resistant hypertension.
Important safety issues for MRAs include:
- Hyperkalemia
- Reduced kidney function
### 9.3 Hypertension with Comorbidities
| Clinical setting                                                       | preferred class                                |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| CKD / microalbuminuria / diabetes / stroke / retinopathy / scleroderma | ACEI or ARB                                    |
| Stable angina / HOCM / aortic dissection / hyperthyroidism / migraine  | Beta blocker                                   |
| BPH                                                                    | Prazosin                                       |
| Osteoporosis                                                           | Thiazide                                       |
| Post-menopausal hot flushes                                            | Clonidine                                      |
| Raynaud's / asthma                                                     | DHP CCB such as amlodipine                     |
| Pheochromocytoma                                                       | Phenoxybenzamine                               |
| “Cheese reaction”                                                      | Phentolamine                                   |
| Pregnancy / preeclampsia                                               | Labetalol, hydralazine, methyldopa, nifedipine |
##### BPH
Alpha-1 blockers can improve BPH symptoms, but **prazosin is not a standard universal first-line antihypertensive drug**. Hypertension should usually be treated according to cardiovascular/renal indications rather than selecting an alpha blocker solely because BPH is present.
##### Beta blockers
Beta blockers are not universal first-line antihypertensives. They are especially useful when there is another compelling indication.
##### Asthma
- Nonselective beta blockers can worsen bronchospasm.
- Cardioselective agents may sometimes be used cautiously when strongly indicated; nonselective beta blockers can worsen bronchospasm.
##### Pregnancy
ACE inhibitors, ARBs, and direct renin inhibition are contraindicated.
Commonly used pregnancy-compatible agents include:
- Labetalol
- Nifedipine
- Methyldopa
Hydralazine is used in selected acute severe-hypertension settings.
### 9.4 Hypertensive Crisis
- BP >180/120 mmHg
#### “Urgency” vs emergency
- Urgency: no organ damage.
- Emergency: end-organ damage such as:
 - hemorrhage
 - stroke
 - aortic dissection
Contemporary guidance increasingly avoids treating “hypertensive urgency” as a mandate for rapid pharmacologic BP lowering. The critical distinction is:
- **Severe hypertension without acute target-organ injury**
- **Hypertensive emergency with acute target-organ injury**
In hypertensive emergency, IV titratable therapy and controlled reduction are used according to the injured organ.
### 9.5 Drugs for severe BP elevation
Mnemonic:
- **N - Nicardipine**
- **N - Nitroprusside**
- **C - Clonidine**
- **A - ACE inhibitor (enalaprilat)**
- **N - Nifedipine / clevidipine**
- **H - Hydralazine**
- **E - Esmolol / labetalol**
- **L - Loop diuretic**
- **P - Phentolamine**
#### Clinical use
- The correct IV agent depends on the emergency subtype.
- **Esmolol/labetalol** are especially relevant in aortic pathology and certain catecholamine-driven states.
- **Nicardipine/clevidipine** are commonly used titratable arterial vasodilators.
- **Nitroprusside** is potent but is not universally preferred because of toxicity and specific clinical considerations.
- **Loop diuretics** are useful when acute pulmonary edema/volume overload is present.
- **Phentolamine** is useful in catecholamine excess.

## 11. ANGINA
### 11.1 Definition
Angina is ischemic chest pain due to myocardial oxygen supply-demand mismatch, usually related to coronary artery disease but not necessarily requiring complete coronary occlusion.
### 11.2 Types of Angina
#### Stable / classical angina
- Fixed coronary narrowing, commonly atherosclerotic.
- Symptoms occur with exertion or increased oxygen demand.
- Improve with rest or antianginal therapy.
#### Unstable angina
- Atherosclerotic plaque rupture.
- Platelet deposition/thrombus.
- Part of the acute coronary syndrome spectrum.
#### Vasospastic (Prinzmetal / variant) angina
- Transient coronary artery vasospasm.
- Can occur at rest.
#### Acute coronary syndrome
- **Unstable angina + myocardial infarction**
### 11.3 Treatment Framework
#### Stable chronic angina
Key point:
- Beta blockers
- CCBs
- Nitrates
Current chronic coronary disease guidance supports **beta blocker, CCB, or long-acting nitrate** as antianginal options, with therapy individualized to heart rate, BP, LV function, comorbidities, and tolerance.
Long-term beta-blocker therapy is **not automatically required for outcome improvement in every stable coronary-disease patient** if there is no recent MI, reduced LVEF, or another compelling indication.
#### Vasospastic angina
- **CCB is a key first-line therapy.**
- Long-acting nitrates can be added when needed.
- Beta blockers are generally not preferred for vasospasm.
#### Acute anginal attack
- **Sublingual nitroglycerin**.
### 11.4 Organic Nitrates
#### Drugs
- GTN / nitroglycerin
- ISDN
- ISMN
- PETN
#### Sublingual
- GTN
- ISDN
#### Mechanism
- Predominant venodilation → reduced preload → reduced myocardial oxygen demand.
### 12. SECOND-LINE / ADD-ON ANTIANGINAL DRUGS
Mnemonic: **No DRIFT**
#### N - Nicorandil
- KATP-channel opener.
- Coronary vasodilator.
- Antianginal.
#### D - Dipyridamole
- Coronary vasodilator.
- Dipyridamole is not a standard antianginal drug; coronary steal may worsen ischemia in susceptible settings.
Do not memorize “banned” as a global pharmacologic rule. Dipyridamole is not a standard antianginal drug and can produce coronary vasodilation that may worsen ischemia in susceptible settings (“steal” concept), but this does not make the drug globally “banned.”
#### R - Ranolazine
- Blocks the **late Na+ current**.
- Reduces intracellular Na+ and secondary Ca2+ overload.
- Decreases ventricular wall tension and myocardial oxygen demand.
Additional benefit:
- Favorable glycemic effect / may modestly lower HbA1c.
#### I - Ivabradine
- Blocks the **If (“funny”) current** in the SA node.
- Lowers heart rate.
- Reduces myocardial oxygen demand.
Ivabradine has a role in selected patients with chronic stable angina or HFrEF with appropriate sinus rhythm/heart-rate criteria. It is not a generic add-on for every angina patient.
#### F - Fasudil
- Rho-kinase inhibitor.
- Coronary vasodilator.
Fasudil is region-specific/limited and is **not a standard routine antianginal recommendation in major contemporary general guidelines**.
#### T - Trimetazidine
- Metabolic modulator.
- Shifts myocardial energy use from fatty-acid oxidation toward glucose oxidation.
- Glucose oxidation requires less oxygen for ATP production per amount of ATP generated.
Trimetazidine inhibits **3-ketoacyl-CoA thiolase**, reducing long-chain fatty-acid oxidation and favoring glucose oxidation.
### 12.1 Antianginal Combination Precautions
#### Beta blocker + verapamil/diltiazem
- Avoid because of excessive bradycardia/AV block and negative inotropy.
#### Nitrate + PDE5 inhibitor
- Sildenafil
- Tadalafil
Mechanism:
```text
Nitrate → ↑ cGMP
PDE5 inhibitor → ↓ cGMP breakdown
                 |
                 v
       Marked cGMP accumulation
                 |
                 v
      Severe vasodilation / hypotension
```
Typical minimum withholding intervals:
- Sildenafil → 24 h
- Tadalafil → 72 h
These are useful exam-time minimum intervals, but exact clinical withholding intervals depend on the particular PDE5 inhibitor, dose, patient factors, and guideline/label.
### 13. ACUTE CORONARY SYNDROME (MI)
#### 13.1 Historical Mnemonic: MONAS
- **M - Morphine**
- **O - Oxygen**
- **N - Nitroglycerin**
- **A - Aspirin 325 mg**
- **S - Statins**
Then:
- DAPT:
 - Aspirin + clopidogrel/prasugrel/ticagrelor
- High-intensity statin:
 - Atorvastatin 80 mg
 - Rosuvastatin 40 mg
- Beta blockers
- Reperfusion
#### 13.2 Current ACS pharmacotherapy
Current ACS pharmacotherapy:
#### Antiplatelet therapy
- Aspirin loading then maintenance.
- Add an oral P2Y12 inhibitor:
 - Ticagrelor
 - Prasugrel
 - Clopidogrel when appropriate
For ACS patients undergoing PCI, ticagrelor or prasugrel are generally preferred over clopidogrel when not contraindicated.
#### Anticoagulation
- Parenteral anticoagulation is indicated in appropriate ACS/revascularization pathways.
#### High-intensity statin
- Atorvastatin 80 mg or rosuvastatin 40 mg are classic high-intensity choices.
#### Oxygen
- **Do not give oxygen routinely to every normoxemic MI patient.**
- Oxygen is generally reserved for hypoxemia, respiratory distress, or other appropriate indication.
#### Morphine
- Not routine for every ACS patient.
- Can be reserved for severe refractory ischemic pain.
- Opioids may delay absorption of oral P2Y12 inhibitors.
#### Nitroglycerin
Useful when clinically appropriate, but avoid in major nitrate contraindications such as:
- Suspected right ventricular infarction with preload dependence
- Significant hypotension
- Recent PDE5 inhibitor exposure
#### 13.3 Reperfusion
- Reperfusion within 12 hours.
- Thrombolytics:
 - Streptokinase
 - Alteplase
 - Tenecteplase
- Fibrinolysis is for **STEMI**, not NSTEMI.
- PCI:
 - Balloon angioplasty/stenting
 - Preferred option
 - Can be used in STEMI and NSTEMI according to clinical strategy.
For STEMI:
- **Primary PCI** is the preferred reperfusion modality when it can be performed within an appropriate time window.
- If timely PCI cannot be achieved and there are no contraindications, **fibrinolysis** is an appropriate option for eligible STEMI patients, especially when presenting within the early window.
- After fibrinolysis, transfer to a PCI-capable center is generally part of the strategy, with rescue PCI for failed reperfusion.
NSTEMI:
- Fibrinolysis is **not** routine therapy.
- Invasive/revascularization strategy is determined by ischemic risk and clinical status.
## 14. CHRONIC HEART FAILURE (CHF)
### 14.1 Basic concept
- Myocardial damage, often post-MI.
- Reduced cardiac output → reduced organ perfusion.
#### Drug categories
1. **Cardioprotective drugs/ ↓ Mortality**
	- Improve survival / reduce disease progression.
	- ARNI
	- ACEI
	- ARB
	- β blocker
	- SGLT 2
	- Mineralocorticoid Receptor Antagonist (all three drugs)
	- HIDN (Hydralazine + IDN) → more effective in Black people 
2. **Symptomatic drugs**
	- Diuretics
		- Furosemide
		- SGLT 2 inhibitor
		- MRA
		- Add on Metolazone
	- Nitrates
	- Nesiritide [historical/limited use]
3. **Decrease Heart Rate**
	1. β blocker
	2. Ivabradine
4. **Inotropes**
	- Digoxin → can be given on and off
	- Acute IV inotropes in selected decompensated patients

5. **New Drugs**
	- Vericiguat → guanyl cyclase ↑ ↑ → ↑ cGMP
### 14.2 HFrEF: Contemporary Core Pharmacotherapy
The four foundational HFrEF classes are:
#### Four major foundational classes (“four pillars”)
1. **ARNI**
 - Sacubitril/valsartan
2. **Evidence-based beta blocker**
 - Carvedilol
 - Metoprolol succinate
 - Bisoprolol
3. **MRA**
 - Spironolactone
 - Eplerenone
4. **SGLT2 inhibitor**
 - Dapagliflozin
 - Empagliflozin
#### ACEI/ARB
- Important alternatives when ARNI is not feasible/tolerated.
- ==Not generally combined with an ARNI due angioedema risk and overlapping RAAS inhibition.== So, if switching from ACEi to ARNI always maintain a gap of 2 days.
### 14.3 Sacubitril/Valsartan (ARNI)
- Sacubitril = neprilysin inhibitor.
- Combined with valsartan.
- ARNI is generally preferred when feasible for HFrEF.
#### Mechanism
Neprilysin degrades:
- Natriuretic peptides
- Other vasoactive peptides
Neprilysin inhibition → increased beneficial natriuretic-peptide signaling.
Valsartan blocks AT1.
Therefore:
- Reduced vasoconstriction
- Reduced sodium retention
- Reduced pathologic remodeling
ARNI is a core therapy for HFrEF and is generally preferred when feasible.

##### Adverse Effect
Angioedema
So if switched from ACE inhibitor → gap of 48 hours to be done
### 14.4 Beta Blockers in HFrEF
- Nebivolol
- Bisoprolol
- Metoprolol succinate
- Carvedilol
#### Current evidence-based mortality drugs
The three beta blockers with the strongest guideline-supported mortality benefit in HFrEF are:
- **Carvedilol**
- **Metoprolol succinate (extended release)**
- **Bisoprolol**
Nebivolol has supportive evidence in selected older patients in some populations, but it should not be substituted for the three core HFrEF evidence-based agents in a high-yield “three drugs” question.
#### Key timing point
- Beta blockers are used in **stable, compensated chronic HFrEF**.
- Do not initiate or aggressively uptitrate during uncontrolled acute decompensation until the patient is stabilized.
### 14.5 SGLT2 Inhibitors in HF
Examples:
- Canagliflozin
- Dapagliflozin
- Empagliflozin
Dapagliflozin and empagliflozin have strong HF evidence and are used in HFrEF irrespective of diabetes status when otherwise appropriate.
SGLT2 inhibitors provide both **cardiorenal** benefit.
### 14.6 Hydralazine
- Hydralazine has a role in selected HFrEF patients when combined with isosorbide dinitrate.
- In modern HF care, the important evidence-based combination is **hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate** in selected patients, including certain patients who cannot receive RAAS inhibition and selected Black patients with persistent symptomatic HFrEF despite standard therapy.
### 14.7 Ivabradine in HF
- Listed for angina and CHF.
- Slows sinus rate through If-current blockade.
In HFrEF, ivabradine is used selectively, typically in patients in **sinus rhythm with elevated resting heart rate despite maximally tolerated evidence-based beta blockade**, according to guideline criteria.
### 14.8 Calcium Channel Blockers in HFrEF
The key issue is **HFrEF / reduced systolic function** and negative inotropy.
- Verapamil/diltiazem can worsen systolic HF.
- This should not be generalized to every form of “CHF” or every preserved-EF HF phenotype.
### 14.9 Natriuretic Peptides
Natriuretic peptides:
- Vasodilate.
- Reduce preload.
- Reduce afterload.
- Promote natriuresis.
#### Nesiritide
- Synthetic **BNP** analogue.
- Nesiritide is a synthetic BNP analogue; it is not a core modern guideline-directed chronic HF therapy.
Nesiritide is **not a core modern guideline-directed chronic HF therapy** and is not routinely used as a standard survival-improving intervention.

- K+ channel opener

### 15.4 Omecamtiv mecarbil

This is a cardiac myosin activator studied extensively in HFrEF, but it should **not** be memorized as a routine universally available standard therapy.
## 16. DIGOXIN
### 16.1 Source and class
- Source: **Digitalis** plant / foxglove.
- Cardiac glycoside.
- Digoxin is the clinically used cardiac glycoside emphasized here.
- Other historical plant glycosides:
	- Digitoxin
	- Ouabain
### 16.2 Mechanism
1. **Primary mechanism**
	- Inhibits **Na+/K+-ATPase** in cardiac myocytes.
	- Increased intracellular Na+ reduces Na+/Ca2+ exchanger-mediated Ca2+ extrusion.
	- Intracellular Ca2+ rises.
	- Increased SR Ca2+ availability → **positive inotropy**.
2. **AV-node effect**
	- Increased vagal tone.
	- **Slows**:
 - SA-node rate
 - **AV-node conduction**
Therefore:
- **Negative chronotropy**
- **Negative dromotropy**
### 16.3 Clinical uses
- Chronic heart failure → due to positive inotropy
- Atrial flutter/fibrillation / SVT → due to its AV-node effect
	- Selected patients with **HFrEF** who remain symptomatic despite guideline-directed therapy.
	- **Rate control in atrial fibrillation**, particularly when other rate-control agents are unsuitable or when HF limits choices.
	- It does not replace catheter ablation or other rhythm-control strategies when those are indicated.
### 16.4 Digoxin Adverse Effects
#### Gastrointestinal
- Nausea
- Vomiting
- Appetite loss
#### Visual
- Yellow vision / **xanthopsia**
- Other color-vision disturbances
>- Sildenafil → blue-green color disturbance (not a routine toxicity pearl)
>- Ethambutol → red-green color disturbance
#### Arrhythmias
Digoxin toxicity may produce almost any arrhythmia.
- Ventricular bigeminy
- Ventricular fibrillation
#### Gynecomastia / hormonal effects
Gynecomastia / hormonal effects (DISCO):
- Digoxin
- Isoniazid
- Spironolactone
- Cimetidine
- Ketoconazole
- Omeprazole
### 16.5 Digoxin Pharmacokinetics
#### Narrow therapeutic window
- Lower serum digoxin concentrations are generally preferred; toxicity can occur even below 2 ng/mL.
#### Volume of distribution
- High Vd.
- Distributed into tissues.
- Therefore **hemodialysis is not effective** for significant digoxin poisoning.
#### Renal elimination
- Important renal clearance.
- P-glycoprotein transport is relevant.
#### Drug interactions
- ==Rifampicin== → P-gp induction → lower digoxin exposure.
- ==Erythromycin/clarithromycin== → P-gp inhibition → higher digoxin exposure.
#### Renal impairment
- Marked concern.
Renal failure is **not an absolute contraindication**.
Instead:
- Reduce dose.
- Monitor renal function.
- Monitor serum digoxin concentration when clinically indicated.
- Monitor K+ and Mg2+.
### 16.6 Electrolytes and Digoxin Toxicity
- **Hypokalemia** → increased digoxin toxicity.
- Thiazide/loop diuretics can therefore indirectly raise digoxin toxicity risk.
Also clinically important:
- Hypomagnesemia increases arrhythmia susceptibility.
- Hypercalcemia may increase toxicity risk in certain settings.
### 16.7 Antidote for Digoxin Poisoning
- **Digibind**
Modern terminology:
- **Digoxin-specific antibody fragments (digoxin immune Fab)**
### 16.8 Digoxin-induced Ventricular Arrhythmia
> Life-threatening unstable ventricular arrhythmias still require appropriate electrical therapy; digoxin-specific Fab is critical when indicated.
- Digoxin-specific Fab is central treatment for severe digoxin toxicity.
- Lidocaine or other selected antiarrhythmics may be used for ventricular arrhythmias depending on the clinical situation and toxicology guidance.
## 17. WOLFF-PARKINSON-WHITE (WPW)
### Mechanism
Accessory AV pathway (classically the **Bundle of Kent**) bypasses the AV node.
ECG:
- Short PR interval
- Delta wave
- Wide QRS complex in classic pre-excitation
### Digoxin
- Avoid in WPW.
### Flecainide

## 18. HYPERTROPHIC OBSTRUCTIVE CARDIOMYOPATHY (HOCM)
- Digoxin → increases contraction → can worsen outflow obstruction.
- Beta blockers → reduce contractility and symptoms.
- Verapamil is another negative inotrope used in selected patients.
- Mavacamten is a newer myosin inhibitor.
The core pathophysiologic pearl is correct:
> **Avoid unnecessary positive inotropy in obstructive HCM because increasing contractility can worsen LV outflow obstruction.**
Mavacamten is a cardiac myosin inhibitor used in selected adults with symptomatic obstructive HCM under appropriate specialist oversight.
## 19. ANTI-ARRHYTHMIC DRUGS
### 19.1 Vaughan-Williams framework
| Class | Main target | Examples |
|---|---|
| I | Na+ channel blockers | IA, IB, IC |
| II | Beta blockers | Metoprolol, esmolol, etc. |
| III | K+ channel blockade / repolarization prolongation | Amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide, ibutilide |
| IV | Ca2+ channel blockers | Verapamil, diltiazem |
| Miscellaneous | Other mechanisms | Adenosine, digoxin, magnesium; atropine as bradycardia therapy |
#### Important terminology
“Class V” is **not a formal universally accepted modern Vaughan-Williams category**; these agents are grouped as **miscellaneous/other antiarrhythmic or adjunctive agents**.
### 20. CLASS I - SODIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS
#### 20.1 Class IA
##### Drugs
Mnemonic: **QUPD**
- **Quinidine**
- **Procainamide**
- **Disopyramide**
##### Electrophysiology
- Block fast Na+ channels → reduce phase 0 slope.
- Also block K+ currents → prolong repolarization.
- Increase action-potential duration and QT.
- Therefore increase risk of **torsades de pointes**.
##### 20.2 Procainamide
- Included in SHIP drug mnemonic.
- Drug-induced lupus.
- Sulfonamide-derived amide group.
- Agranulocytosis / bone marrow suppression risk.
Procainamide is indeed associated with:
- Drug-induced lupus
- Agranulocytosis
- QT prolongation / torsades risk
It remains clinically useful in selected arrhythmia settings, including some forms of pre-excited atrial fibrillation and stable wide-complex tachycardia.
##### 20.3 Disopyramide
- Class IA.
- Strong negative inotrope.
- Anticholinergic adverse effects.
#### 21. CLASS IB
##### Drugs
Mnemonic: **Leave PMT**
- **L - Lidocaine / lignocaine**
- **P - Phenytoin**
- **M - Mexiletine**
- **T - Tocainide**
##### Mechanism
- Na+ channel blockade.
- Lidocaine:
 - IV.
 - Used for ventricular arrhythmias.
- Perioral numbness due to local-anesthetic-type Na+ channel blockade.
Class IB drugs are best understood as **Na+ channel blockers with relatively little K+ blockade** and a tendency to **shorten or minimally affect ventricular action-potential duration**, especially in ischemic/depolarized tissue.
##### Lignocaine toxicity
- **20% IV lipid emulsion** as antidote for local-anesthetic toxicity (e.g., lidocaine/bupivacaine).
This is a major toxicology principle for severe local-anesthetic systemic toxicity.
#### 22. CLASS IC
##### Drugs
Mnemonic: **Famous PM**
- **Flecainide**
- **Propafenone**
##### Pharmacology
- Strong Na+ channel blockade.
- Marked slowing of conduction.
- Little direct effect on action-potential duration compared with IA/IB.
##### Major warning
- Increased mortality.
- Contraindicated in MI/structural heart disease.
This remains a major high-yield rule for flecainide/propafenone in patients with prior MI or significant structural heart disease because of proarrhythmic risk.
### 23. CLASS II - BETA BLOCKERS
#### General rule
Drug names commonly end in:
- **-lol**
Examples:
- Propranolol
- Metoprolol
- Atenolol
#### Ultra-short acting IV agents
- **Esmolol**
- **Landiolol**
Landiolol is ultra-short acting.
#### Uses
- Sympathetic tachycardia
- Rate control in selected SVT/AF/flutter
- Perioperative/intraoperative tachyarrhythmias
- Hyperthyroid-associated tachycardia
#### Adverse effect
- **Bradycardia**
#### Beta-blocker overdose
- Glucagon
Glucagon is a classic rescue therapy for beta-blocker poisoning, but severe toxicity may require a broader toxicology protocol including high-dose insulin/euglycemia, vasopressors, calcium, lipid emulsion in selected situations, and advanced circulatory support.
### 24. CLASS IV - CALCIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS
#### Main drug
- **Verapamil**
#### Mechanism
- L-type Ca2+ channel blockade.
- Slows:
 - SA-node rate
 - AV-node conduction.
#### Indications
- SVT
- Selected AF rate control
#### Contraindications / cautions
- Never use in VF/VT because of increased mortality.
- Never combine with beta blockers.
The clinically important rule is:
- Verapamil/diltiazem are **not treatments for ventricular tachycardia** and can be dangerous in some wide-complex rhythms.
- In stable regular narrow-complex tachycardia, they are possible options after appropriate initial therapy.
- In **HFrEF**, non-DHP CCBs are generally avoided because of negative inotropy.
### 25. CLASS III - POTASSIUM CHANNEL BLOCKERS
#### mnemonic: DIVAS
- **D - Dofetilide**
- **I - Ibutilide**
- **V - Vernakalant**
- **A - Amiodarone**
- **S - Sotalol**
- **Sotalol** has both beta-blocking (class II) and potassium-channel-blocking (class III) actions.
#### Mechanism
- Delayed repolarization.
- Prolonged action-potential duration.
- Prolonged QT in many agents.
#### Torsades risk
Many class III drugs can increase QT and torsades risk.
Exception:
- **Amiodarone** prolongs QT but has a relatively lower torsades propensity than many other class III drugs.
#### 25.1 Dofetilide
- Potassium-channel blocker.
- Used for rhythm control in selected AF/flutter patients.
- QT prolongation risk.
#### 25.2 Ibutilide
- IV class III antiarrhythmic.
- Used for pharmacological cardioversion of AF/flutter in appropriate patients.
- QT prolongation/torsades risk.
#### 25.3 Vernakalant
Vernakalant is an atrial-selective antiarrhythmic used for pharmacological cardioversion of recent AF where approved.
#### 26. AMIODARONE
##### 26.1 Mechanism
- Predominantly class III action.
- Also has:
 - Na+ channel blockade
 - Ca2+ channel blockade
 - beta-adrenergic blocking properties
###### Electrophysiology
- Prolongs repolarization/action-potential duration.
- Prolongs QT.
- “Most effective anti-arrhythmic drug.”
##### 26.2 Iodine Content
- About **37% iodine by weight**.
Clinical consequence:
- Significant thyroid effects.
##### 26.3 Major Adverse Effects
###### 1. Pulmonary toxicity
- Interstitial pneumonitis/fibrosis.
- **most dangerous** adverse effect.
###### 2. Thyroid
Can cause:
- Hypothyroidism
- Hyperthyroidism
Hypothyroidism is often more common, but both occur.
###### 3. Skin
- Blue-gray / blue-purple skin discoloration.
- “Purple man syndrome”
###### 4. Cornea
**Corneal verticillata / vortex keratopathy**
- Whorl-like / flower-like epithelial deposits.
- Usually reversible after discontinuation.
- Often asymptomatic.
###### 5. Hepatotoxicity
- Liver enzyme elevation
- Clinically significant liver injury in some patients.
###### 6. QT prolongation
- Prolongs QT.
- Torsades is possible but less frequent than expected for the extent of QT prolongation.
#### 27. DRONEDARONE
- Modified derivative of amiodarone.
- Lacks iodine.
- Therefore:
	- Fewer thyroid toxicities
	- Less blue-gray skin deposition[]
> [!note] “PURPLE SYNDROMES”
> ### Purple man
> - **Amiodarone**
> - Blue-gray/blue-purple skin discoloration from chronic tissue deposition and photosensitivity-related changes.
> ### Purple toe
> - **Warfarin**
> - Classic association with **cholesterol microembolization / cholesterol crystal emboli**.
> ### Purple glove
> - **Phenytoin**

## 30. ECG / ACUTE ARRHYTHMIA MANAGEMENT

- Ventricular tachycardia
- Torsades de pointes
The visual sequence is more clinically useful when expressed as an emergency algorithm.
### 30.1 Regular Narrow-Complex Tachycardia / PSVT
The image depicts a regular narrow-complex tachycardia, with atrial activity difficult to see or occurring close to the QRS.
#### Modern algorithm
```text
Regular narrow-complex tachycardia
            |
            v
Assess hemodynamic stability
       /             \
 unstable stable
 | |
 v v
Synchronized Vagal maneuvers
cardioversion |
                        v
                    Adenosine
                        |
                 persistent rhythm
                        |
                 diltiazem/verapamil
                        |
                or synchronized
                  cardioversion
```
This reflects contemporary AHA adult advanced-life-support principles. The exact sequence depends on rhythm diagnosis, stability, and contraindications.
### 30.2 Wide-Complex Tachycardia
#### Unstable
- Immediate **synchronized cardioversion** for an unstable monomorphic wide-complex tachycardia when synchronization is feasible.
- If the rhythm is polymorphic/sustained, synchronization is unreliable → unsynchronized shock.
#### Stable monomorphic wide-complex tachycardia
Appropriate specialist/ACLS options include:
- IV amiodarone
- IV procainamide
- IV sotalol in selected situations
#### Important “do not”
- Do **not** give verapamil/diltiazem empirically to undifferentiated wide-complex tachycardia.
### 30.3 Torsades de Pointes
#### ECG concept
Torsades is a form of **polymorphic VT associated with prolonged QT**, often showing changing QRS amplitude and axis around the baseline.
#### Management
```text
Sustained polymorphic VT
          |
          v
Immediate unsynchronized shock
          |
          v
Check QT / reversible causes
          |
          +-→ Long QT / torsades
          |       |
          |       +-→ IV magnesium for recurrent episodes
          |       +-→ correct K+ / Mg2+
          |       +-→ remove QT-prolonging cause
          |
          +-→ Normal QT
                  |
                  +-→ search for ischemia/other cause
```
## 31. EXAM-ORIENTED CROSS-TOPIC PEARLS
### Diuretics
- **Loop:** TAL, NKCC2, lose Ca2+, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia.
- **Thiazide:** DCT, NCC, retain Ca2+, useful in hypercalciuria.
- **MRA/ENaC blockers:** potassium sparing → hyperkalemia.
- **Acetazolamide:** PCT, bicarbonate loss, metabolic acidosis.
- **Mannitol:** osmotic diuresis, raised ICP/IOP.
### Vasopressin
- **V1** → vasoconstriction.
- **V2** → renal water reabsorption.
- **Desmopressin** → central DI, enuresis, factor VIII/vWF.
- **Vaptans** → aquaresis.
### RAAS
- ACEI → ↓ angiotensin II + ↓ aldosterone + ↑ bradykinin.
- ACEI cough/angioedema → bradykinin.
- ARBs block AT1 without increasing bradykinin.
- Both can produce hyperkalemia and reduce GFR in susceptible states.
- Pregnancy → avoid ACEI/ARB/direct renin inhibition.
### CCBs
- DHP → vessels.
- Verapamil/diltiazem → AV node + heart.
- DHP → edema/flushing/headache.
- Verapamil → constipation, bradycardia.
- Avoid non-DHP + beta blocker combination.
### Nitrates
- Predominantly venous dilation at lower doses.
- Reduce preload.
- Nitrate + PDE5 inhibitor → severe hypotension.
- Continuous nitrate exposure → tolerance.
- Nitrate-free interval is required.
### ACS
- Aspirin + P2Y12 inhibitor is foundational antiplatelet therapy.
- High-intensity statin.
- Oxygen is selective, not routine in normoxemia.
- Primary PCI preferred for eligible STEMI when timely.
- Fibrinolysis is for appropriate STEMI when timely PCI is unavailable; not for NSTEMI.
### HFrEF
Modern four pillars:
1. ARNI / ACEI / ARB strategy
2. Evidence-based beta blocker
3. MRA
4. SGLT2 inhibitor
### Digoxin
- Na+/K+-ATPase inhibition → positive inotropy.
- Vagal effect → AV nodal slowing.
- Hypokalemia → more toxicity.
- Renal impairment → dose reduction/monitoring.
- Digoxin immune Fab → severe toxicity.
- Avoid AV-node blockers in acute pre-excited AF/WPW.
### Antiarrhythmics
- IA → Na block + K block → prolonged QT.
- IB → ventricular Na blockade, shortens/minimally changes APD.
- IC → strong Na blockade, conduction slowing; avoid in prior MI/major structural disease.
- II → beta blockade.
- III → repolarization prolongation.
- IV → verapamil/diltiazem.
