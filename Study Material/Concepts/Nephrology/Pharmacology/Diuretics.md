## Mannitol

### Mechanism
- Osmotic diuretic. 
- Increases serum osmolality, promoting fluid shift from interstitium to intravascular space. 

### Site of Action
- **PCT and descending limb of the loop of Henle** (and other water-permeable segments).
- **Normal physiology:** 
	- In the proximal tubule, ~65–70% of filtered Na⁺ and water is reabsorbed isosmotically via Na⁺/K⁺‑ATPase, Na⁺/H⁺ exchangers, and aquaporins. 
	- In the descending limb, water is reabsorbed passively through aquaporins driven by medullary hypertonicity. 
- **MECHANISM OF ACTION:** Mannitol increases tubular fluid osmolarity, reducing passive water reabsorption in these segments and increasing water and Na⁺ delivery to the distal nephron.

### Clinical Use
- Drug overdose, 
- elevated intracranial/intraocular pressure, 
- augmenting diuresis.

### Adverse Effects
- Dehydration, hypo- or hypernatremia, ==pulmonary edema==.
- ==Contraindicated in anuria, heart failure (HF).==

## Acetazolamide

### Mechanism
- Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor. 
- Causes self‑limited NaHCO₃ diuresis and reduces total body HCO₃⁻ stores. 
- Alkalinizes urine.

### Site of Action
- **PCT.**
- **Normal physiology:** 
	- The proximal tubule reabsorbs ~85% of filtered bicarbonate. 
	- Luminal H⁺ (secreted by Na⁺/H⁺ exchanger) combines with filtered HCO₃⁻ to form H₂CO₃; luminal carbonic anhydrase converts this to CO₂ and H₂O. CO₂ diffuses into the cell, where intracellular carbonic anhydrase regenerates H⁺ and HCO₃⁻; HCO₃⁻ exits basolaterally.
	- The net effect is HCO₃⁻ is absorbed and Na⁺ follows. 
- Acetazolamide inhibits carbonic anhydrase, reducing HCO₃⁻ and Na⁺ reabsorption, leading to bicarbonaturia and alkaline urine.

### Clinical Use
- Glaucoma, 
- metabolic alkalosis, 
- altitude sickness (by offsetting respiratory alkalosis), 
- idiopathic intracranial hypertension (pseudotumor cerebri).

### Adverse Effects
- Proximal renal tubular acidosis (type 2 RTA), 
- paresthesias, 
- NH₃ toxicity, 
	- Normally, acidic urine converts ammonia into its charged form which is less diffusible across membrane.
	- Acetazolamide alkalinizes the urine which promotes its gaseous state. Since gas is much more lipid soluble, it diffuses across the membrane.
	- So acetazolamide reduces ammonia trapping.
- sulfa allergy, 
- hypokalemia.
	- Since sodium follows negatively charged bicarbonate ion, which is now in the tubule, delivery of sodium is increased in the distal tubules. 
	- The distal tubules exchange potassium for sodium and thus excretes more of potassium.
- Promotes calcium phosphate stones (insoluble at high urine pH).

> [!tip] Mnemonic
> "Acid"azolamide causes acidosis.

## Loop Diuretics

**Drugs:** **Furosemide, Bumetanide, Torsemide, Ethacrynic Acid**


### Normal Physiology
- **Thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle.**
- **Normal physiology:** 
	- The thick ascending limb reabsorbs ~25% of filtered Na⁺ via the apical Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻ cotransporter (NKCC2). 
	- K⁺ is recycled via ==ROMK== channels, and Cl⁻ exits basolaterally. 
	- ==Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺== are reabsorbed paracellularly driven by the positive luminal potential. 
	- This segment is ==impermeable to water==, diluting the tubular fluid and ==contributing to medullary hypertonicity== via countercurrent multiplication. 
- Loop diuretics inhibit NKCC2, reducing Na⁺ reabsorption, abolishing medullary hypertonicity, and preventing urine concentration.

### Mechanism (Furosemide, Bumetanide, Torsemide)
- ==Sulfonamide== loop diuretics. 
- Inhibit **co-transport system (Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻)** of **thick ascending limb of loop of Henle.**
- Abolish hypertonicity of medulla, preventing concentration of urine.
- Associated with increased PGE (vasodilatory effect on afferent arteriole); inhibited by NSAIDs.
- ==Increases Ca²⁺ excretion. "Loops lose Ca²⁺."==

### Mechanism (Ethacrynic Acid)
- ==Nonsulfonamide== inhibitor of the same cotransport system (Na⁺/K⁺/2Cl⁻) in the thick ascending limb of loop of Henle.


### Clinical Use
- **Furosemide, Bumetanide, Torsemide:** 
	- Edematous states (HF, cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome, pulmonary edema), 
	- hypertension, 
	- ==hypercalcemia==.
- **Ethacrynic Acid:** Diuresis in patients allergic to sulfa drugs.

### Adverse Effects
- **Furosemide, Bumetanide, Torsemide:** 
	- Ototoxicity, 
	- ==Hypokalemia==, 
	- ==Hypomagnesemia==, 
	- Dehydration, 
	- Allergy (sulfa), 
	- metabolic ==Alkalosis==, 
	- Nephritis (interstitial), 
	- ==Gout==.
  > [!tip] Mnemonic
  > OHH DAANG!

- **Ethacrynic Acid:** Similar to furosemide, but more ototoxic and no sulfa allergy risk.
  > [!tip] Mnemonic
  > Loop earrings hurt your ears.

## Thiazide Diuretics

**Drugs:** **Hydrochlorothiazide, Chlorthalidone, Metolazone**

### Mechanism
- Inhibit NaCl reabsorption in early DCT, reducing diluting capacity of nephron. Reduces Ca²⁺ excretion.

### Site of Action
- **Early distal convoluted tubule.**
- **Normal physiology:** The early distal convoluted tubule reabsorbs ~5–10% of filtered Na⁺ via the apical Na⁺/Cl⁻ cotransporter (NCC). Ca²⁺ reabsorption is regulated by PTH and occurs via apical Ca²⁺ channels and basolateral Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchange. This segment is impermeable to water and continues dilution of the tubular fluid. Thiazides inhibit NCC, reducing Na⁺ reabsorption and diluting capacity, while enhancing Ca²⁺ reabsorption.

### Clinical Use
- Hypertension, HF, idiopathic hypercalciuria, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, osteoporosis.
- Potentiates loop diuretics in refractory volume overload.

### Adverse Effects
- Hypokalemic metabolic alkalosis, hyponatremia, hyperglycemia, hyperlipidemia, hyperuricemia, hypercalcemia. Sulfa allergy.
> [!tip] Mnemonic
> Hypergluc.

## Potassium-Sparing Diuretics

**Drugs:** **Spironolactone, Eplerenone, Amiloride, Triamterene**

### Mechanism
- Spironolactone and eplerenone are competitive aldosterone receptor antagonists in cortical collecting tubule.
- Triamterene and amiloride block Na⁺ channels at the same part of the tubule.

### Site of Action
- **Cortical collecting tubule / collecting duct (principal cells).**
- **Normal physiology:** Principal cells reabsorb Na⁺ via apical epithelial Na⁺ channels (ENaC) and secrete K⁺ via ROMK/BK channels, driven by basolateral Na⁺/K⁺‑ATPase. Aldosterone increases ENaC expression, Na⁺/K⁺‑ATPase activity, and K⁺ secretion. Spironolactone and eplerenone block aldosterone receptors, while amiloride and triamterene directly block ENaC, reducing Na⁺ reabsorption and K⁺ secretion.

### Clinical Use
- Hyperaldosteronism, HF, hepatic ascites (spironolactone), nephrogenic DI (amiloride), antiandrogen (spironolactone).

### Adverse Effects
- Hyperkalemia (can lead to arrhythmias), endocrine effects with spironolactone (e.g., gynecomastia, antiandrogen effects), metabolic acidosis.

## Angiotensin-Converting Enzyme (ACE) Inhibitors

**Drugs:** **Captopril, Enalapril, Lisinopril, Ramipril**

### Mechanism
- Inhibit ACE, reducing Angiotensin II (AT II). This reduces GFR by preventing constriction of efferent arterioles.
- Increases renin due to loss of negative feedback.
- Inhibition of ACE also prevents inactivation of bradykinin, a potent vasodilator.

### Site of Action
- **Renal efferent arteriole / glomerulus (not primarily tubular).**
- **Normal physiology:** Angiotensin II preferentially constricts the efferent arteriole, helping to maintain glomerular capillary pressure and GFR, especially in states of low renal blood flow. ACE inhibitors reduce angiotensin II, dilating the efferent arteriole and lowering intraglomerular pressure, which reduces proteinuria and slows progression of chronic kidney disease.

### Clinical Use
- Hypertension, HF (reduces mortality), proteinuria, diabetic nephropathy.
- Prevent unfavorable heart remodeling as a result of chronic hypertension.
- In chronic kidney disease (e.g., diabetic nephropathy), reduces intraglomerular pressure, slowing GBM thickening.

### Adverse Effects
- Cough (dry, nonproductive), Angioedema (both due to increased bradykinin; contraindicated in C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency), Teratogen (fetal renal malformations), increased Creatinine (reduced GFR), Hyperkalemia, and Hypotension.
- Used with caution in bilateral renal artery stenosis because ACE inhibitors will further reduce GFR, leading to renal failure.
> [!tip] Mnemonic
> Captopril’s CATCHH.

## Angiotensin II Receptor Blockers (ARBs)

**Drugs:** **Losartan, Candesartan, Valsartan**

### Mechanism
- Selectively block binding of angiotensin II to AT₁ receptor.
- Effects similar to ACE inhibitors, but ARBs do not increase bradykinin.

### Site of Action
- **Renal efferent arteriole / glomerulus (not primarily tubular).**
- **Normal physiology:** Angiotensin II binds AT₁ receptors on the efferent arteriole, causing vasoconstriction and maintaining glomerular filtration pressure. ARBs block this effect, dilating the efferent arteriole and reducing intraglomerular pressure, similar to ACE inhibitors.

### Clinical Use
- Hypertension, HF, proteinuria, or chronic kidney disease (e.g., diabetic nephropathy) with intolerance to ACE inhibitors (e.g., cough, angioedema).

### Adverse Effects
- Hyperkalemia, reduced GFR, hypotension; teratogen.

## Aliskiren

### Mechanism
- Direct renin inhibitor, blocks conversion of angiotensinogen to angiotensin I.
> [!tip] Mnemonic
> Aliskiren kills renin.

### Site of Action
- **Juxtaglomerular cells / renal efferent arteriole (via reduced angiotensin II).**
- **Normal physiology:** Renin is secreted by juxtaglomerular cells and converts angiotensinogen to angiotensin I, which is then converted to angiotensin II. Angiotensin II constricts the efferent arteriole, maintaining glomerular filtration pressure. Aliskiren inhibits renin, reducing angiotensin I and II production, leading to efferent arteriolar dilation and reduced intraglomerular pressure.

### Clinical Use
- Hypertension.

### Adverse Effects
- Hyperkalemia, reduced GFR, hypotension, angioedema.
- Relatively contraindicated in patients already taking ACE inhibitors or ARBs and contraindicated in pregnancy.
