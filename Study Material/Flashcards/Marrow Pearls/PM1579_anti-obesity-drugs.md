# Anti-obesity drugs

Centrally acting anti-obesity drugs: The biologic effect of these agents on appetite regulation is produced by augmentation of the neurotransmission of three monoamines, norepinephrine, serotonin (5-HT) and dopamine.

- **Phentermine** — Sympathomimetic (Phentermine is usually used in combination with topiramate).
- **Bupropion/naltrexone** — Centrally acting combination anti-obesity drug.
  - Bupropion: Dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor
  - Naltrexone: Opioid receptor antagonist

### Peripherally acting medications

- **Orlistat** is a synthetic hydrogenated derivative of a naturally occurring lipase inhibitor, lipostatin, that is produced by the mold *Streptomyces toxytricini*.

### Glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists

These agents activate intestinal incretin receptors (GLP-1) that stimulate insulin secretion, inhibit glucagon, delay gastric emptying, and enhance satiety, contributing to weight loss and improved glycemic control.

- **Liraglutide** — Glucagon-like peptide 1 receptor agonist.
- **Semaglutide** — Long-acting glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonist (administered once weekly).
- **Tirzepatide** — Dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and GLP-1 receptor agonist.
- **Orforglipron** — Oral small-molecule, non-peptide, GLP-1 receptor agonist.

### Anti-obesity drugs in development

- **Retatrutide** — Triple agonist targeting GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon receptors.
- **Survodutide** — GLP-1/glucagon co-agonist.
