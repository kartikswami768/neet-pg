# Autonomic Nervous System — 60 Flashcards

These cards are derived from the ANS notes and focused on NEET-PG/INI-CET active recall.

#flashcards/Pharmacology/Autonomic-Nervous-System

### ANS-001 — ANS regulation · Neurotransmitters · fact · receptor: none · difficulty: 1
What is the major neurotransmitter of the postganglionic sympathetic nervous system?::Norepinephrine (NE).

### ANS-002 — ANS regulation · Exceptions · fact · receptor: none · difficulty: 1
Where does the sympathetic system use acetylcholine instead of the usual norepinephrine at its target?::At sweat glands; the adrenal medulla also receives acetylcholine from preganglionic sympathetic fibers.

### ANS-003 — ANS regulation · Dopamine · receptor · receptor: D1 · difficulty: 1
What is the important renal vascular action of dopamine via D1 receptors in the notes?::Renal vasodilation, producing increased renal blood flow/diuresis.

### ANS-004 — Cholinergic receptors · Nicotinic · receptor · receptor: Nn · difficulty: 1
Where are Nn nicotinic acetylcholine receptors classically located?::Autonomic ganglia and the adrenal medulla.

### ANS-005 — Cholinergic receptors · Nicotinic · receptor · receptor: Nm · difficulty: 1
Where are Nm nicotinic acetylcholine receptors located?::At the neuromuscular junction of skeletal muscle.

### ANS-006 — Cholinergic receptors · Muscarinic · receptor · receptor: M1 · difficulty: 2
What is the G-protein coupling and major high-yield role of the M1 receptor?::M1 is Gq-coupled; it is associated with higher cognition, enteric activation and increased exocrine secretion.

### ANS-007 — Cholinergic receptors · Muscarinic · receptor · receptor: M2 · difficulty: 1
What is the G-protein coupling and major effect of M2 receptors?::M2 is Gi-coupled; it decreases heart rate, AV-node conduction and atrial contractility.

### ANS-008 — Cholinergic receptors · Muscarinic · receptor · receptor: M3 · difficulty: 2
What are the major high-yield effects of M3 receptor activation?::Increased exocrine secretion, gut peristalsis and bladder contraction; bronchoconstriction; miosis and accommodation; and vasodilation via vascular endothelium.

### ANS-009 — Adrenergic receptors · Receptors · receptor · receptor: α1 · difficulty: 1
What is the G-protein coupling and core effect of α1 receptors?::α1 is Gq-coupled and promotes vascular and visceral smooth-muscle contraction, including vasoconstriction and mydriasis.

### ANS-010 — Adrenergic receptors · Receptors · receptor · receptor: α2 · difficulty: 2
What is the G-protein coupling and key inhibitory role of α2 receptors?::α2 is Gi-coupled; it reduces sympathetic outflow and neurotransmitter release, insulin release and lipolysis, and reduces aqueous-humor production.

### ANS-011 — Adrenergic receptors · Receptors · receptor · receptor: β1 · difficulty: 1
What is the G-protein coupling and main effects of β1 receptors?::β1 is Gs-coupled; it increases heart rate and contractility and increases renin release.

### ANS-012 — Adrenergic receptors · Receptors · receptor · receptor: β2 · difficulty: 1
What are the major high-yield effects of β2 receptor activation?::Bronchodilation, vasodilation, increased insulin/glycogenolysis and uterine relaxation (tocolysis).

### ANS-013 — Adrenergic receptors · Receptors · receptor · receptor: β3 · difficulty: 1
Where is β3 especially important and what does it do?::It is important in adipose tissue and the bladder detrusor; it increases lipolysis and relaxes the detrusor.

### ANS-014 — Dopaminergic receptors · Receptors · comparison · receptor: D1, D2 · difficulty: 2
How do D1 and D2 receptors differ in G-protein coupling and the striatal pathways listed in the notes?::D1 is Gs-coupled and associated with the direct striatal pathway; D2 is Gi-coupled and associated with the indirect pathway and presynaptic transmitter modulation.

### ANS-015 — Adrenergic receptors · Presynaptic feedback · mechanism · receptor: α2 · difficulty: 2
What is the role of the presynaptic α2 autoreceptor?::It provides negative feedback by reducing norepinephrine release from the presynaptic terminal.

### ANS-016 — Cholinomimetics · Direct agonists · classification · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
What are the main direct muscarinic agonists emphasized in the notes?::Methacholine, bethanechol, pilocarpine and cevimeline; acetylcholine is the prototype that stimulates both muscarinic and nicotinic receptors.

### ANS-017 — Cholinomimetics · Direct agonists · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
Which direct cholinergic drug is used for a bronchial challenge test for asthma?::Methacholine.

### ANS-018 — Cholinomimetics · Direct agonists · drug-use · receptor: M3 · difficulty: 1
What are the classic uses of bethanechol in the notes?::Urinary retention/bladder atony and postoperative ileus; it is a direct muscarinic agonist with mainly M3 effects.

### ANS-019 — Cholinomimetics · Direct agonists · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
What are the two major exam associations for pilocarpine?::Glaucoma and xerostomia.

### ANS-020 — Cholinomimetics · Direct agonists · drug-use · receptor: M3 · difficulty: 1
Which muscarinic agonist is associated with xerostomia in Sjögren syndrome?::Cevimeline, an M3-preferring muscarinic agonist.

### ANS-021 — Anticholinesterases · Mechanism · mechanism · receptor: Muscarinic + Nicotinic · difficulty: 1
What happens at cholinergic synapses when acetylcholinesterase is inhibited?::Acetylcholine increases at both muscarinic and nicotinic receptors; effects depend on where the drug distributes, including the CNS if it crosses the BBB.

### ANS-022 — Anticholinesterases · Distribution · drug-property · receptor: AChE · difficulty: 1
Which reversible AChE inhibitor crosses the blood-brain barrier and is important for anticholinergic toxicity?::Physostigmine.

### ANS-023 — Anticholinesterases · Distribution · comparison · receptor: AChE · difficulty: 1
Which important reversible AChE inhibitors in the notes are quaternary and do not cross the BBB?::Neostigmine and pyridostigmine.

### ANS-024 — Anticholinesterases · Clinical uses · drug-use · receptor: AChE · difficulty: 1
Which AChE inhibitors are used for Alzheimer disease in the notes?::Donepezil, rivastigmine and galantamine.

### ANS-025 — Anticholinesterases · Clinical uses · drug-use · receptor: AChE · difficulty: 2
What are the main exam uses of neostigmine?::Myasthenia gravis, postoperative ileus, urinary retention and reversal of nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockade.

### ANS-026 — Anticholinesterases · Clinical uses · drug-use · receptor: AChE · difficulty: 1
Which AChE inhibitor is emphasized as the long-acting treatment for myasthenia gravis?::Pyridostigmine.

### ANS-027 — Anticholinesterases · Myasthenia gravis · drug-use · receptor: AChE · difficulty: 2
Which short-acting AChE inhibitor was classically used for the Tensilon test?::Edrophonium; the notes label its myasthenia-diagnosis use as historical.

### ANS-028 — Cholinergic toxicity · Poisoning · toxicology · receptor: AChE · difficulty: 1
What is the classic treatment combination for organophosphate poisoning?::Atropine plus pralidoxime (2-PAM); atropine reverses muscarinic effects and pralidoxime reactivates AChE when given before aging.

### ANS-029 — Antimuscarinics · Poisoning · toxicology · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
What is the DOC for severe anticholinergic poisoning such as atropine or Datura toxicity in the notes?::Physostigmine.

### ANS-030 — Antimuscarinics · Clinical uses · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
Which antimuscarinic is classically associated with prevention of motion sickness?::Scopolamine (hyoscine).

### ANS-031 — Antimuscarinics · Clinical uses · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
Which antimuscarinic is emphasized for reducing preoperative secretions and does not significantly cross the BBB?::Glycopyrrolate.

### ANS-032 — Systemic ANS effects · Pupil · physiology · receptor: M3 · difficulty: 1
What pupil effect is produced by a parasympathomimetic drug?::Active miosis.

### ANS-033 — Systemic ANS effects · Pupil · physiology · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
What pupil effect is produced by a parasympatholytic drug such as atropine or tropicamide?::Passive mydriasis, with cycloplegia when the ciliary muscle is blocked.

### ANS-034 — Systemic ANS effects · Pupil · comparison · receptor: α1 · difficulty: 2
How does phenylephrine differ from an antimuscarinic mydriatic with respect to cycloplegia?::Phenylephrine causes active mydriasis via α1 stimulation but has no cycloplegic effect.

### ANS-035 — Antimuscarinics · Ophthalmology · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
Which short-acting antimuscarinic is used for routine adult fundus examination and refractive testing?::Tropicamide.

### ANS-036 — Antimuscarinics · Ophthalmology · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
Which antimuscarinic is classically used to produce cycloplegia for refraction?::Cyclopentolate.

### ANS-037 — Systemic ANS effects · Bronchi · physiology · receptor: M3, β2 · difficulty: 1
What are the major autonomic effects on bronchi of parasympathomimetics and β2 agonists?::Parasympathomimetics cause bronchoconstriction; β2 agonists cause bronchodilation.

### ANS-038 — Antimuscarinics · Respiratory · drug-use · receptor: M3 · difficulty: 1
Which LAMA is emphasized as a DOC for COPD in the notes?::Tiotropium.

### ANS-039 — Antimuscarinics · Respiratory · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
Which inhaled muscarinic antagonist is the classic SAMA and is used in COPD?::Ipratropium.

### ANS-040 — Systemic ANS effects · Heart · physiology · receptor: M2 · difficulty: 1
What are the major parasympathetic effects on the heart?::Decreased heart rate and decreased AV conduction.

### ANS-041 — Antimuscarinics · Heart · drug-use · receptor: Muscarinic · difficulty: 1
Which antimuscarinic is a key treatment for symptomatic bradycardia and AV block in the notes?::Atropine.

### ANS-042 — Adrenergic agonists · Heart · drug-use · receptor: β1 · difficulty: 1
Which predominantly β1 agonist is used as an inotrope in acute decompensated heart failure/cardiogenic shock?::Dobutamine.

### ANS-043 — Systemic ANS effects · Bladder · physiology · receptor: M3 · difficulty: 1
What does parasympathetic M3 activation do to the bladder detrusor?::It contracts the detrusor and promotes bladder emptying.

### ANS-044 — Antimuscarinics · Bladder · drug-use · receptor: M3 · difficulty: 1
Name common antimuscarinics used for overactive bladder in the notes.::Oxybutynin, solifenacin, darifenacin, tolterodine, fesoterodine and trospium.

### ANS-045 — Adrenergic agonists · Bladder · drug-use · receptor: β3 · difficulty: 1
Which β3 agonist is used for overactive bladder by relaxing the detrusor?::Mirabegron.

### ANS-046 — Nicotinic drugs · Neuromuscular blockers · mechanism · receptor: Nm · difficulty: 1
How does succinylcholine produce skeletal-muscle paralysis?::It is a depolarizing Nm agonist that causes persistent end-plate depolarization.

### ANS-047 — Nicotinic drugs · Neuromuscular blockers · classification · receptor: Nm · difficulty: 1
Name three important nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockers emphasized in the notes.::Rocuronium, vecuronium and atracurium.

### ANS-048 — Adrenergic agonists · Blood vessels · clinical-application · receptor: α1 · difficulty: 2
In spinal-anesthesia-induced hypotension, which sympathomimetic is preferred when heart rate is normal?::Phenylephrine.

### ANS-049 — Adrenergic agonists · Blood vessels · clinical-application · receptor: α/β + indirect · difficulty: 2
In spinal-anesthesia-induced hypotension, which sympathomimetic is preferred when the patient also has a low heart rate?::Ephedrine.

### ANS-050 — Adrenergic agonists · Blood pressure · drug-use · receptor: α1 · difficulty: 1
Which α1 agonist is used for postural/orthostatic hypotension?::Midodrine.

### ANS-051 — Adrenergic agonists · Shock · drug-use · receptor: α1, α2, β1 · difficulty: 1
Which catecholamine is emphasized as the DOC vasopressor for cardiogenic, septic and neurogenic shock?::Norepinephrine.

### ANS-052 — Adrenergic agonists · Shock · drug-use · receptor: α1, α2, β1, β2, β3 · difficulty: 1
Which sympathomimetic is the DOC for anaphylactic shock?::Epinephrine.

### ANS-053 — Adrenergic agonists · Dopamine · dose-effect · receptor: D1 → β1 → α1 · difficulty: 2
What is the classic dose-dependent receptor sequence for dopamine in the notes as dose increases?::D1 → β1 → α1; low dose is associated with the D1 effect, then cardiac β1 effects, then α1 vasoconstriction.

### ANS-054 — Adrenergic agonists · Indirect sympathomimetics · interaction · receptor: Indirect NE release · difficulty: 1
What classic food-drug interaction should you associate with tyramine?::Aged/fermented foods containing tyramine can cause a hypertensive crisis when combined with MAO inhibitors.

### ANS-055 — Sympatholytics · Central α2 agonists · drug-use · receptor: α2 · difficulty: 1
What is the mechanism and one classic exam warning for clonidine?::It is a central α2 agonist that decreases sympathetic outflow; abrupt withdrawal can cause rebound hypertension.

### ANS-056 — Sympatholytics · Central α2 agonists · drug-use · receptor: α2 · difficulty: 1
Which central sympatholytic is classically used for hypertension in pregnancy?::Methyldopa, converted to α-methylnorepinephrine, an α2 agonist.

### ANS-057 — Alpha blockers · Pheochromocytoma · comparison · receptor: α1, α2 · difficulty: 1
What is the key pharmacology distinction between phenoxybenzamine and phentolamine?::Phenoxybenzamine is an irreversible nonselective α blocker; phentolamine is a reversible competitive α blocker.

### ANS-058 — Alpha blockers · BPH · drug-use · receptor: α1A · difficulty: 1
Which α1A-preferring blocker is used for BPH?::Tamsulosin; silodosin is also highly α1A-selective.

### ANS-059 — Beta blockers · Heart failure · high-yield-triad · receptor: β · difficulty: 1
Which three β-blockers in the notes have the key heart-failure outcome association?::Bisoprolol, carvedilol and metoprolol.

### ANS-060 — Beta blockers · Adverse effects · adverse-effect · receptor: β · difficulty: 2
What are the major high-yield adverse effects of β-blockers, and what antidote is listed for β-blocker toxicity?::Bradycardia/AV block, bronchospasm, masked hypoglycemia symptoms, CNS effects such as insomnia/nightmares and exercise intolerance; glucagon is listed for toxicity.
