# Endocrine System — 100 Flashcards

These cards are derived from the cleaned endocrine pharmacology notes and deliberately emphasize recurrent NEET-PG/INI-CET question patterns: mechanism, drug of choice, site of action, adverse effects, contraindications, comparisons, and clinical vignettes.

#flashcards/Pharmacology/Endocrine-System

### ENDO-001 — Diabetes · Type 1 DM · drug-use · target: β-cell replacement · difficulty: 1
What is the essential pharmacologic replacement in type 1 diabetes mellitus?::Insulin; oral glucose-lowering drugs do not replace the absolute insulin deficiency.

### ENDO-002 — Diabetes · Type 2 DM · first-line concept · target: insulin resistance · difficulty: 1
What is the classic exam answer for first-line pharmacotherapy of uncomplicated type 2 diabetes mellitus?::Metformin, unless contraindicated or a comorbidity-directed drug strategy is preferred.

### ENDO-003 — Diabetes · Type 2 DM · severe hyperglycemia · target: insulin · difficulty: 1
At approximately what glycemic level should insulin be considered promptly in type 2 diabetes because of very high glycemia?::A1C >10% or blood glucose ≥300 mg/dL, especially when symptomatic hyperglycemia or hyperglycemic crisis is present.

### ENDO-004 — Diabetes · Type 2 DM · cardiorenal disease · target: GLP-1/SGLT2 · difficulty: 2
In type 2 diabetes with established/high-risk ASCVD, heart failure or CKD, what important treatment principle is high-yield?::An SGLT2 inhibitor and/or GLP-1 receptor agonist with demonstrated outcome benefit may be appropriate irrespective of A1C and with or without metformin.

### ENDO-005 — Diabetes · Gestational diabetes · drug of choice · target: insulin · difficulty: 1
What is the preferred pharmacologic agent for gestational diabetes mellitus?::Insulin.

### ENDO-006 — Insulin · Classification · rapid acting · target: prandial control · difficulty: 1
Which insulin analogues are rapid/ultra-short acting?::Lispro, aspart and glulisine.

### ENDO-007 — Insulin · Classification · short acting · target: prandial control · difficulty: 1
Which insulin is the classic short-acting preparation, and what emergency route is important?::Regular insulin; it can be given IV in DKA and in potassium-shifting treatment of hyperkalemia.

### ENDO-008 — Insulin · Basal-bolus · comparison · target: glucose profile · difficulty: 1
What is the conceptual difference between bolus and basal insulin?::Bolus insulin controls meal-related/postprandial glucose, whereas basal insulin suppresses hepatic glucose output and fasting/intermeal hyperglycemia.

### ENDO-009 — Insulin · Long acting · classification · target: basal therapy · difficulty: 1
Which three long-acting insulin preparations are high-yield?::Glargine, detemir and degludec.

### ENDO-010 — Insulin · Flat profile · comparison · target: pharmacokinetics · difficulty: 1
Which basal insulins have a relatively flat/peakless action compared with NPH?::Glargine and degludec are classic flat-profile basal analogues.

### ENDO-011 — Insulin · Emergency · DKA · target: glucose metabolism · difficulty: 1
Which insulin is classically used intravenously for diabetic ketoacidosis?::Regular insulin.

### ENDO-012 — Insulin · Afrezza · contraindication · target: lung · difficulty: 2
What is Afrezza, and in which respiratory conditions is it contraindicated?::Afrezza is inhaled human insulin powder; it is contraindicated in chronic lung disease such as asthma or COPD because of bronchospasm risk.

### ENDO-013 — Insulin · Glargine · formulation · target: pH · difficulty: 1
Which insulin is formulated as an acidic solution and becomes prolonged after subcutaneous injection?::Insulin glargine.

### ENDO-014 — Insulin · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: glucose · difficulty: 1
What is the most important acute adverse effect of insulin therapy?::Hypoglycemia.

### ENDO-015 — Insulin · Adverse effects · injection site · target: adipose tissue · difficulty: 1
What injection-site complication can result from repeatedly using the same insulin injection site, and how is it prevented?::Lipohypertrophy/lipoatrophy can occur; rotate injection sites.

### ENDO-016 — Insulin · Hypoglycemia · treatment · target: glucose · difficulty: 1
What are the classic pharmacologic treatments for significant insulin-induced hypoglycemia?::Glucose/dextrose, with glucagon as an option when oral/IV glucose cannot be promptly given.

### ENDO-017 — Sulfonylureas · Mechanism · receptor/target: KATP channel · difficulty: 1
How do sulfonylureas increase insulin secretion from pancreatic β-cells?::They close ATP-sensitive K+ channels, depolarize the β-cell membrane, open voltage-gated Ca2+ channels and increase insulin exocytosis.

### ENDO-018 — Sulfonylureas · Adverse effects · safety · target: insulin secretion · difficulty: 1
What are the two classic adverse effects of sulfonylureas?::Hypoglycemia and weight gain.

### ENDO-019 — Sulfonylureas · Chlorpropamide · adverse-effect · target: ADH/alcohol metabolism · difficulty: 1
Which sulfonylurea is classically associated with SIADH/hyponatremia and a disulfiram-like reaction with alcohol?::Chlorpropamide.

### ENDO-020 — Meglitinides · Comparison · mealtime control · target: KATP channel · difficulty: 1
Which meglitinides are high-yield, and what is their main practical advantage?::Repaglinide and nateglinide; they are short-acting insulin secretagogues suited to mealtime glucose control.

### ENDO-021 — Incretins · Physiology · comparison · target: GLP-1/DPP-4 · difficulty: 1
What two major glucose-lowering effects are produced by incretin signaling?::It increases glucose-dependent insulin secretion and suppresses glucagon release.

### ENDO-022 — GLP-1 receptor agonists · Mechanism · target: GLP-1 receptor · difficulty: 1
What are the major actions of GLP-1 receptor agonists?::Increase glucose-dependent insulin, decrease glucagon, reduce appetite and slow gastric emptying, producing weight loss.

### ENDO-023 — GLP-1 receptor agonists · Drugs · classification · target: GLP-1 receptor · difficulty: 1
Name four high-yield GLP-1 receptor agonists from the notes.::Exenatide, liraglutide, dulaglutide and semaglutide.

### ENDO-024 — GLP-1 receptor agonists · Semaglutide · route/formulation · target: GLP-1 receptor · difficulty: 1
What is an important formulation distinction for semaglutide?::It is available in both oral and injectable formulations; the injectable form is commonly given weekly.

### ENDO-025 — GLP-1 receptor agonists · Combination · comparison · target: incretin pathway · difficulty: 1
Should a GLP-1 receptor agonist routinely be combined with a DPP-4 inhibitor for extra glucose lowering?::No. Concurrent GLP-1 RA plus DPP-4 inhibitor therapy is not recommended because meaningful additional benefit is not expected.

### ENDO-026 — DPP-4 inhibitors · Mechanism · target: DPP-4 enzyme · difficulty: 1
How do DPP-4 inhibitors lower glucose?::They inhibit incretin degradation, increasing endogenous GLP-1 activity and thereby increasing glucose-dependent insulin and decreasing glucagon.

### ENDO-027 — DPP-4 inhibitors · Renal dosing · comparison · target: kidney · difficulty: 1
Which DPP-4 inhibitor is classically remembered as requiring no renal dose adjustment?::Linagliptin.

### ENDO-028 — DPP-4 inhibitors · Heart failure · adverse-effect · target: cardiac risk · difficulty: 2
Which DPP-4 inhibitors deserve particular caution regarding heart-failure risk?::Saxagliptin and alogliptin have specific heart-failure safety concerns.

### ENDO-029 — Metformin · Mechanism · target: liver/AMPK · difficulty: 1
What is the principal glucose-lowering action of metformin?::It reduces hepatic gluconeogenesis and hepatic glucose output, with effects linked to AMPK-related pathways and improved insulin sensitivity.

### ENDO-030 — Metformin · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: GI/B12/lactate · difficulty: 1
What are the three high-yield adverse-effect associations of metformin?::GI symptoms such as diarrhea, vitamin B12 deficiency with long-term use, and rare but serious lactic acidosis.

### ENDO-031 — Metformin · Renal impairment · contraindication · target: eGFR · difficulty: 1
At what eGFR is metformin contraindicated according to the notes/current standards?::eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m².

### ENDO-032 — Pioglitazone · Mechanism · target: PPAR-γ · difficulty: 1
Which nuclear receptor is activated by pioglitazone, and what is the major metabolic result?::It is a PPAR-γ agonist that increases insulin sensitivity in adipose tissue and other insulin-sensitive tissues.

### ENDO-033 — Pioglitazone · Adverse effects · safety · target: fluid balance · difficulty: 1
What adverse effects make pioglitazone an important exam caution?::Fluid retention/edema, weight gain and potential worsening of heart failure; bladder-cancer risk is also a labeling concern.

### ENDO-034 — Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors · Mechanism · target: intestinal brush border · difficulty: 1
How do acarbose and voglibose reduce postprandial glucose excursions?::They inhibit intestinal α-glucosidases, delaying carbohydrate breakdown and glucose absorption.

### ENDO-035 — Alpha-glucosidase inhibitors · Hypoglycemia · treatment · target: disaccharidase activity · difficulty: 1
If a patient taking acarbose develops hypoglycemia, why should glucose/dextrose be given instead of sucrose?::Acarbose inhibits sucrose breakdown, so glucose/dextrose is absorbed directly and corrects the hypoglycemia.

### ENDO-036 — Pramlintide · Mechanism · amylin analogue · target: glucagon/gastric emptying · difficulty: 1
What are the main actions of pramlintide?::It is an amylin analogue that decreases glucagon, slows gastric emptying and increases satiety.

### ENDO-037 — Pramlintide · Type 1 DM · clinical role · target: insulin adjunct · difficulty: 1
Can pramlintide replace insulin in type 1 diabetes?::No. It is only an adjunct to mealtime insulin in selected patients.

### ENDO-038 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Site of action · target: PCT/SGLT2 · difficulty: 1
Where do SGLT2 inhibitors act, and what transporter do they block?::They act in the proximal convoluted tubule and inhibit SGLT2-mediated sodium-glucose reabsorption.

### ENDO-039 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Drugs · classification · target: SGLT2/SGLT1 · difficulty: 1
Name three major SGLT2 inhibitors and the dual SGLT1/2 inhibitor in the notes.::Canagliflozin, dapagliflozin and empagliflozin are SGLT2 inhibitors; sotagliflozin inhibits SGLT1 and SGLT2.

### ENDO-040 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Mechanism · renal physiology · target: filtered glucose · difficulty: 1
Approximately what fraction of filtered glucose reabsorption is mediated by SGLT2 versus SGLT1?::About 90% by SGLT2 and about 10% by SGLT1.

### ENDO-041 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: GU/ketones/volume · difficulty: 1
What are the major adverse-effect associations of SGLT2 inhibitors?::Genital mycotic infection, GU infection, volume depletion, euglycemic/atypical DKA and rare Fournier gangrene.

### ENDO-042 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Cardiorenal benefit · clinical use · target: heart/kidney · difficulty: 1
Why are SGLT2 inhibitors important even beyond glucose lowering?::They provide cardiovascular and kidney outcome benefits in appropriate patients with heart failure and/or CKD.

### ENDO-043 — SGLT2 inhibitors · Perioperative care · safety · target: ketogenesis · difficulty: 2
When should SGLT2 inhibitors generally be avoided or held in high-risk clinical settings?::During severe acute illness, ketonemia/ketonuria, prolonged fasting and around surgery because of euglycemic DKA risk.

### ENDO-044 — Diabetic complications · Gastroparesis · drug-use · target: motilin receptor · difficulty: 1
Which drug can be used as a prokinetic option in diabetic gastroparesis by stimulating motilin receptors?::Erythromycin.

### ENDO-045 — Diabetic complications · Diarrhea · drug-use · target: α2 receptor · difficulty: 1
Which drug is associated with treatment of diabetic diarrhea through an α2-mediated reduction in intestinal secretion?::Clonidine.

### ENDO-046 — Diabetic retinopathy · Anti-VEGF · drug-use · target: VEGF · difficulty: 1
Which anti-VEGF drugs are commonly used intravitreally for diabetic macular edema/retinopathy?::Ranibizumab, aflibercept and bevacizumab are common ophthalmic anti-VEGF agents.

### ENDO-047 — Obesity pharmacotherapy · GLP-1/GIP · comparison · target: incretin receptors · difficulty: 1
Which drug in the notes is a dual GIP + GLP-1 receptor agonist used for obesity/weight loss?::Tirzepatide.

### ENDO-048 — Epalrestat · Diabetic neuropathy · mechanism · target: aldose reductase · difficulty: 1
What enzyme does epalrestat inhibit in diabetic neuropathy?::Aldose reductase, reducing conversion of glucose to sorbitol.

### ENDO-049 — Hyperkalemia · Cardiac protection · drug-use · target: cardiac membrane · difficulty: 1
What is the role of IV calcium gluconate in hyperkalemia?::It stabilizes the cardiac membrane and reduces arrhythmia risk; it does not lower serum potassium.

### ENDO-050 — Hyperkalemia · Intracellular shift · mechanism · target: Na/K ATPase · difficulty: 1
How does IV regular insulin acutely lower serum potassium in hyperkalemia?::It shifts potassium into cells, largely by increasing Na+/K+-ATPase-mediated cellular uptake; glucose is given as needed to prevent hypoglycemia.

### ENDO-051 — Hyperkalemia · β2 agonist · drug-use · target: β2 receptor · difficulty: 1
Which inhaled β2 agonist is used as an adjunct to insulin/glucose for potassium shifting in hyperkalemia?::Nebulized salbutamol (albuterol).

### ENDO-052 — Hyperkalemia · Potassium removal · management · target: total body K+ · difficulty: 1
Which therapies actually remove potassium from the body rather than merely shift it into cells?::Loop diuretics when appropriate, potassium binders and dialysis.

### ENDO-053 — Thyroid drugs · NIS inhibitors · mechanism · target: NIS · difficulty: 1
Which agents inhibit the sodium-iodide symporter and therefore iodide uptake into thyroid cells?::Perchlorate and thiocyanate are classic pharmacologic examples; pertechnetate is used in nuclear medicine as an NIS-handling analogue.

### ENDO-054 — Thionamides · Mechanism · target: TPO · difficulty: 1
What three thyroid-hormone synthesis steps are reduced by TPO inhibition?::Iodide oxidation, organification and coupling.

### ENDO-055 — Thionamides · Prodrug · comparison · target: methimazole · difficulty: 1
Which common thionamide is a prodrug converted to methimazole?::Carbimazole.

### ENDO-056 — PTU · Peripheral conversion · mechanism · target: deiodinase/T4→T3 · difficulty: 1
Which thionamide also inhibits peripheral conversion of T4 to T3?::Propylthiouracil (PTU).

### ENDO-057 — Thionamides · Adverse effects · safety · target: marrow/liver · difficulty: 1
What two serious adverse effects are classically associated with thionamides?::Agranulocytosis and hepatotoxicity; severe hepatotoxicity is particularly important with PTU.

### ENDO-058 — Hyperthyroidism in pregnancy · First trimester · drug of choice · target: thyroid synthesis · difficulty: 1
Which thionamide is generally preferred during early pregnancy when antithyroid therapy is required?::PTU, commonly through about week 16.

### ENDO-059 — Hyperthyroidism in pregnancy · Later pregnancy · comparison · target: antithyroid therapy · difficulty: 1
After early pregnancy, which thionamide is generally preferred and why?::Methimazole/carbimazole is generally preferred after the first trimester because PTU carries important hepatotoxicity risk.

### ENDO-060 — Iodides · Mechanism · acute effect · target: thyroid hormone release · difficulty: 1
What is the classic acute effect of high-dose iodide on the thyroid?::It rapidly suppresses thyroid hormone release and organification through the Wolff–Chaikoff effect.

### ENDO-061 — Thyroid storm · Sequence · management · target: synthesis/release/conversion · difficulty: 2
What is the high-yield pharmacologic sequence in thyroid storm?::β-blockade → thionamide → iodide after the thionamide → glucocorticoid.

### ENDO-062 — Thyroid storm · Iodide timing · mechanism · target: hormone synthesis/release · difficulty: 1
Why should iodide be given after the thionamide in thyroid storm?::Giving the thionamide first blocks new hormone synthesis; iodide is then used to acutely inhibit hormone release without supplying substrate before synthesis blockade.

### ENDO-063 — Radioactive iodine · I-131 · mechanism · target: thyroid tissue · difficulty: 1
How does radioactive iodine treat hyperthyroidism?::I-131 is concentrated by thyroid tissue and emits radiation that destroys thyroid follicular cells.

### ENDO-064 — Radioactive iodine · Contraindication · safety · target: pregnancy · difficulty: 1
What major reproductive contraindication should be remembered for radioactive iodine therapy?::Pregnancy; breastfeeding also requires avoidance/appropriate interruption according to nuclear-medicine guidance.

### ENDO-065 — Radioactive iodine · Adverse effects · thyroid eye disease · target: salivary gland/orbit · difficulty: 2
What complications are classically associated with radioiodine therapy?::Sialadenitis/parotitis can occur, and Graves ophthalmopathy may worsen in susceptible patients.

### ENDO-066 — Thyroid hormone replacement · Levothyroxine · drug-use · target: T4 receptor signaling · difficulty: 1
What is the standard chronic thyroid-hormone replacement drug?::Levothyroxine, synthetic T4.

### ENDO-067 — Myxedema coma · Thyroid hormone · emergency · target: T4/T3 replacement · difficulty: 1
Which thyroid hormone preparations can be used IV in severe myxedema coma?::IV levothyroxine is standard; liothyronine may be added in selected protocols.

### ENDO-068 — Thyroid eye disease · Teprotumumab · mechanism · target: IGF-1R · difficulty: 1
What is the mechanism and major indication of teprotumumab?::It targets IGF-1 receptor (IGF-1R) signaling and is used for thyroid eye disease, particularly active disease with clinically significant manifestations.

### ENDO-069 — Medullary thyroid carcinoma · Vandetanib · mechanism · target: tyrosine kinases/RET · difficulty: 1
Which drug in the notes is a tyrosine kinase inhibitor used for advanced medullary thyroid carcinoma?::Vandetanib.

### ENDO-070 — Osteoporosis · Bisphosphonates · mechanism · target: FPPS/mevalonate pathway · difficulty: 1
How do nitrogen-containing bisphosphonates inhibit osteoclasts?::They inhibit farnesyl pyrophosphate synthase in the mevalonate pathway, impairing osteoclast function and promoting osteoclast death.

### ENDO-071 — Bisphosphonates · Uses · mnemonic · target: bone resorption · difficulty: 1
What three high-yield uses can be remembered by the mnemonic HOP for bisphosphonates?::Hypercalcemia, osteoporosis and Paget disease.

### ENDO-072 — Bisphosphonates · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: bone/kidney/esophagus · difficulty: 1
What are the classic adverse-effect associations of bisphosphonates?::Renal toxicity, osteonecrosis of the jaw, atypical/subtrochanteric femoral fractures and esophagitis.

### ENDO-073 — Bisphosphonates · Zoledronic acid · route · target: IV infusion · difficulty: 1
What is the correct route for zoledronic acid in osteoporosis treatment?::Intravenous infusion; it is not an IM drug.

### ENDO-074 — Teriparatide · Mechanism · target: PTH receptor/osteoblast · difficulty: 1
Why does intermittent teriparatide increase bone formation whereas persistent PTH excess promotes bone resorption?::Intermittent PTH receptor stimulation preferentially stimulates osteoblast activity and bone formation; sustained PTH excess favors resorption.

### ENDO-075 — Teriparatide · Clinical role · safety · target: bone formation · difficulty: 2
In which type of osteoporosis patient is teriparatide particularly considered, and what major safety concept is high-yield?::It is used in patients at very high fracture risk; avoid/caution in conditions associated with increased baseline osteosarcoma risk or hypercalcemia.

### ENDO-076 — Calcitonin · Uses · comparison · target: osteoclast/Ca2+ · difficulty: 1
What are the classic route/use associations for calcitonin?::Parenteral calcitonin can rapidly lower calcium in acute hypercalcemia, whereas intranasal calcitonin has a limited role in osteoporosis.

### ENDO-077 — Denosumab · Mechanism · target: RANKL · difficulty: 1
How does denosumab reduce bone resorption?::It binds RANKL and prevents RANKL-mediated osteoclast formation and activation.

### ENDO-078 — Denosumab · Renal disease · safety · target: calcium homeostasis · difficulty: 2
Why can denosumab be used when renal impairment limits bisphosphonate choices, yet still require caution in advanced CKD?::It is not renally cleared like bisphosphonates, but severe CKD increases the risk of clinically important hypocalcemia after denosumab.

### ENDO-079 — Denosumab · Discontinuation · rebound phenomenon · target: bone turnover · difficulty: 2
What important problem can occur if denosumab is stopped or delayed without follow-up antiresorptive therapy?::Rebound bone turnover with increased risk of vertebral fractures.

### ENDO-080 — Cinacalcet · Mechanism · target: CaSR · difficulty: 1
How does cinacalcet reduce PTH secretion?::It activates the calcium-sensing receptor on parathyroid cells, increasing sensitivity to extracellular calcium and thereby reducing PTH secretion.

### ENDO-081 — Romosozumab · Mechanism · target: sclerostin · difficulty: 1
What is the molecular target of romosozumab and the major skeletal effect?::It inhibits sclerostin, increasing bone formation and also reducing bone resorption to some extent.

### ENDO-082 — Burosumab · Mechanism · indication · target: FGF23 · difficulty: 1
What is the classic disease association of burosumab?::X-linked hypophosphatemia and related approved FGF23-mediated hypophosphatemic disorders; it is not a routine osteoporosis drug.

### ENDO-083 — Hypercalcemia · Initial management · target: volume status · difficulty: 1
What is the core initial treatment of significant hypercalcemia?::Isotonic saline volume repletion.

### ENDO-084 — Hypercalcemia · Diuretics · comparison · target: renal calcium handling · difficulty: 1
Why are thiazides generally avoided when treating hypercalcemia?::Thiazides increase renal calcium reabsorption and can worsen hypercalcemia.

### ENDO-085 — Hypercalcemia of malignancy · Rapid effect · target: calcitonin · difficulty: 2
Which drug can provide rapid, short-term calcium lowering in severe hypercalcemia of malignancy, often together with an antiresorptive?::Calcitonin, because it has a rapid onset; its use is limited by tachyphylaxis.

### ENDO-086 — Hyperphosphatemia · Drug of choice · target: intestinal phosphate · difficulty: 1
What is the classic drug of choice for hyperphosphatemia in the notes?::Sevelamer.

### ENDO-087 — Phosphate binders · Alternatives · comparison · target: intestinal phosphate binding · difficulty: 1
Name two established non-calcium phosphate binders besides sevelamer.::Lanthanum and other non-calcium binders; the notes specifically emphasize lanthanum as an established alternative.

### ENDO-088 — GH axis · Physiology · sequence · target: GHRH/GH/IGF-1 · difficulty: 1
What is the high-yield sequence of the GH–IGF-1 axis?::Hypothalamic GHRH → anterior pituitary GH → liver IGF-1 → growth-promoting effects.

### ENDO-089 — Growth hormone · Somatropin · indication · target: GH receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the classic indication for somatropin, and when is it most effective for linear growth?::Growth hormone deficiency and other approved indications; for linear growth it must be given before epiphyseal closure.

### ENDO-090 — Growth hormone · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: GH/IGF-1 · difficulty: 1
What adverse effects are classically associated with excess pharmacologic growth hormone exposure?::Edema/arthralgia, carpal tunnel syndrome, hyperglycemia and, in children, effects related to growth plates.

### ENDO-091 — Mecasermin · Mechanism · indication · target: IGF-1 · difficulty: 1
What is mecasermin, and in which deficiency state is it used?::Recombinant IGF-1 used for severe primary IGF-1 deficiency.

### ENDO-092 — Mecasermin · Adverse effect · high-yield · target: glucose · difficulty: 1
What important adverse effect of mecasermin should be remembered?::Hypoglycemia.

### ENDO-093 — Pituitary hormones · Comparison · mechanism · target: GH/prolactin · difficulty: 1
Which hypothalamic hormones have the classic pituitary-inhibitory effects of decreasing GH and prolactin, respectively?::Somatostatin decreases GH; dopamine decreases prolactin.

### ENDO-094 — Acromegaly · Treatment · drug-use · target: GH/IGF-1 · difficulty: 1
What is an important first-line treatment for a surgically resectable GH-secreting pituitary adenoma?::Transsphenoidal surgery.

### ENDO-095 — Acromegaly · Somatostatin analogues · drugs · target: somatostatin receptor · difficulty: 1
Which somatostatin analogues are high-yield treatments for acromegaly?::Octreotide and lanreotide; pasireotide is another somatostatin-pathway option in selected patients.

### ENDO-096 — Acromegaly · Pegvisomant · mechanism · target: GH receptor · difficulty: 1
How does pegvisomant treat acromegaly?::It antagonizes the GH receptor, reducing IGF-1 production.

### ENDO-097 — Prolactinoma · Drug of choice · target: D2 receptor · difficulty: 1
What is the major drug of choice for prolactinoma?::Cabergoline.

### ENDO-098 — Prolactinoma · Pregnancy · comparison · target: D2 receptor · difficulty: 1
Which dopamine agonist has the classic pregnancy-associated role in prolactinoma?::Bromocriptine.

### ENDO-099 — Dopamine agonists · Adverse effects · high-yield · target: vascular/valvular system · difficulty: 2
What adverse effects should be remembered for dopamine agonists used in prolactinoma?::Nausea, orthostatic hypotension, headache and dopaminergic neuropsychiatric effects; high cumulative doses of cabergoline are associated with valvular heart-disease concern.

### ENDO-100 — Reproductive/endocrine · Integrated PYQ sweep · comparison · target: GnRH/sex steroids · difficulty: 2
What are the three most important paired distinctions: continuous GnRH agonist vs GnRH antagonist, tamoxifen vs raloxifene, and finasteride vs androgen-receptor blockers?::Continuous GnRH agonist causes an initial flare followed by gonadotropin suppression, whereas GnRH antagonists suppress immediately without flare; tamoxifen is anti-estrogenic in breast but estrogenic in endometrium/bone, whereas raloxifene does not stimulate the endometrium; finasteride/dutasteride inhibit 5α-reductase and lower DHT, whereas drugs such as enzalutamide/bicalutamide directly block androgen-receptor signaling.
